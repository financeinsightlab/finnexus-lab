# G1 · Grammar for Interviews Foundations: The Sentence Core

> *Interviewers forgive nervous pauses. They do NOT forgive "I am agree" or "he don't know SQL" — one broken sentence and your "communication skills" checkbox gets a cross. Grammar in interviews is not about Shakespeare; it's about 10 small rules that never break under pressure. This module bulletproofs the sentence core: agreement, questions, negatives — the three places interviews punish instantly.*

## 🎯 Objectives
Why grammar errors cost offers (the unfair truth) · subject-verb agreement drilled to instinct · the Do/Does/Did question machine · negative sentences without double-negatives · the 7 most-damaged Indian-English patterns with fixes · your error fossil-hunting method.

## 📘 1.1 The unfair truth — and the good news

Harsh reality: panels HEAR grammar before they hear intelligence. "He don't know DAX" makes them hear "not corporate-ready". The good news: interview grammar is a CLOSED SET — the same 10–15 patterns repeat in every answer you give ("tell me about yourself", "your strengths", "why this company"). Fix 15 sentences PERMANENTLY and you're 90% safe. This whole course drills exactly those.

**Today you start an Error Log 📝:** a note where you capture every grammar mistake you catch yourself making + its fixed version. Fossils (old habits) die only when seen.

## 📘 1.2 Subject-Verb Agreement — the royal rule 👑

**ONE S stays with ONE partner:** either the noun or the verb, never both, never neither.

| Pattern | ❌ Broken | ✅ Fixed |
|---|---|---|
| he/she/it + verb | He **don't** know SQL | He **doesn't** know SQL |
| does/did + verb | She **didn't went** | She **didn't go** (did already carries past!) |
| I/we/you/they | I **is** ready / they **was** friendly | I **am** ready / they **were** friendly |
| Tricky singulars | The **list** of candidates **are** ready | The LIST **is** ready (list is the subject!) |
| each/everyone | Everyone **are** here | Everyone **is** here |
| news/data/feedback (uncountable!) | The data **are** clean | The data **is** clean |

**The does/did golden rule** (memorize as chant): *"After do/does/did, the verb goes home to base."* Does she go ✓ · Did you finish ✓ · He doesn't know ✓.

🔥 Interview drill — say these aloud 5× till automatic: "My manager **guides** me." / "My SKILLS **include** SQL and Power BI." / "This company **offers** growth." / "Neither of the reports **was** wrong."

## 📘 1.3 The Do/Does/Did QUESTION machine ❓

Panels ask YOU questions (surface is easy). But YOU must also ask THEM questions — and interviews are lost on clumsy ones.

**Machine:** `QUESTION-WORD + do/does/did + SUBJECT + BASE VERB?`
- ❓Where **do** you **see** this team in 2 years? (do + plural subject)
- ❓What **does** success **look** like in this role? (does + it/success)
- ❓How **did** the team **handle** the migration? (did + base!)

❌ The classic breaks: "Where you will be in 5 years?" (no helper!) · "What does the company expects?" (expect**S** after does!) · "How the data looked?" (missing did).

✏️ Grammar of ANSWERING yes/no questions: **Answer + tiny reason.**
"Do you know Python?" → ❌ dead: "Yes." → ✅ alive: "Yes, I **do** — I **use** it daily for data cleaning at ShopKart." (Notice: do — use, matching the question's grammar back.)

## 📘 1.4 Negatives — without the double-trouble 🚫

- **One negative per sentence:** "I don't have **no** experience" ❌ → "I don't have **any** experience" ✅ (double negative = doubly wrong impression!)
- not + verb needs its helper: I **am not** ready · he **does not** agree · they **did not** call.
- Contraction freeze: under pressure "don't/doesn't/isn't" come out smoother — drill the contracted form, always.
- "isn't it / no?" — Indian tag-question trap! English matches the tag to the sentence: "You're from Delhi, **aren't** you?" · "The dashboard loads fast, **doesn't** it?" — never a universal "isn't it?"

## 📘 1.5 The 7 most-damaged patterns (India edition) 🔧

| # | ❌ Panel hears | ✅ Panel wants | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I **am agree** with you | I **agree** with you | agree = verb, no 'am' |
| 2 | I am **having** 2 years of experience | I **have** 2 years of experience | possession = simple, not -ing |
| 3 | **Myself** Amit. / **Myself is** from Delhi | **I am** Amit. / **I'm** from Delhi | myself only reflects ("I did it myself") |
| 4 | He **told me** to wait. (telling confused: "He said me") | He **said** to wait. / He **told me** to wait | said ≠ told (told + object) |
| 5 | I **am working** here **since** 2023 | I **have been working** here since 2023 | since/from-past = perfect form (Module G2!) |
| 6 | I **had went** yesterday | I **went** yesterday (or: I **had gone**) | one past marker per verb |
| 7 | **More faster** / **more better** | **faster** / **better** | comparatives take -er OR more, not both |

Circle your personal demons — everyone has 2–3 from this list. Into the Error Log they go.

## 📘 1.6 The fossil-hunting method 🦴

Grammar errors are FOSSILS — habits hardened over years. Excavation method (15 min/day):
1. 📱 Record yourself answering "Tell me about yourself" (even 60 seconds).
2. 👂 Listen with pen: write down EVERY error you hear. Don't judge the content, only grammar.
3. ✍️ Write the CORRECT sentence 3× and SPEAK it 3×.
4. The fossil shrinks each time the correct version is spoken under real conditions (mirror practice → family mock → real interview).

## 🧪 LAB G1 — The sentence-core boot camp (40 min)

1. **Agreement sprint (10 min):** say aloud & fix 10: (a) "She don't like dashboards" (b) "They was impressed" (c) "The team of analysts are debating" (d) "Everyone have joined" (e) "He didn't knew the answer" (f) "My skills includes Finance GL" (g) "Neither method work" (h) "The news are good" (i) "I is ready" (j) "Data are powerful".
2. **Question machine (10 min):** build & speak 5 interview-return questions using do/does/did (culture, team size, growth, training, tools — one each). Check every verb returned to base!
3. **Your 30-sec core (15 min):** write your "Tell me about yourself" FIRST 3 sentences ONLY (name/education/current role). Grammar-check every verb against 1.2–1.5. 📱 Record. Listen. Count errors. Fix. Re-record. (This 3-sentence chunk becomes Module G2's foundation!)
4. **Error Log start (5 min):** top 3 fossils from the sprint in notes 📝: ❌ wrong → ✅ right → one new sentence using the right form.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Fix: "I am agree that more faster tools are needed, as per myself opinion."
2. Which is right and why: (a) "The batch of freshers are starting Monday." (b) "The batch of freshers is starting Monday."
3. Build the question: you want to know why the previous analyst LEFT this role. Write it 2 polite ways.
4. Convert to contraction-smooth negatives: "I do not have any concerns. He does not mind travel. They did not reject me."
5. Tag check: "You'll train me on Power BI, ___?" and "The notice period is 60 days, ___?"

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 1: "I **agree** that **faster** tools are needed, **in my opinion**." (3 fossils in 1 sentence: am-agree, more-faster, myself-opinion!)
- Ex 2: (b) — the subject is "batch" (singular); "of freshers" is decoration. Panels DO use this trap to test you mid-stress.
- Ex 3: "May I ask why the role opened up?" / "Could you share what growth path the previous analyst took?"
- Ex 5: "You'll train me on Power BI, **won't you**?" · "The notice period is 60 days, **isn't it**?" (tag mirrors the sentence's OWN helper verb!)

## ❓ Quiz
1. Why does "She didn't went" break TWO grammar locks at once?
2. Interviewers ask questions in simple words. Why should YOUR questions use the full do/does/did machine?
3. What's wrong with saying "Myself Amit from Delhi" — what does 'myself' actually mean?
4. Why is an Error Log better than a grammar textbook for interviews?

### ✅ Answers
1. "Did" already carries past; "went" carries past AGAIN — double past (should be "didn't go"). Plus many say it because they memorize chains wrong.
2. Because clumsy questions ("Why team is small?") reveal you skipped the helper-step habit — the same habit that's tested everywhere else. Smooth questions = subconscious credibility.
3. "Myself" = reflection ("I taught MYSELF SQL"). Using it as your name's intro leaves the sentence with no subject — the first 3 seconds of your interview, grammar already bleeding.
4. Textbooks teach rules you might never break in interviews; the Error Log hunts only YOUR fossils. 10 personal fixes beat 100 pages of rules.
