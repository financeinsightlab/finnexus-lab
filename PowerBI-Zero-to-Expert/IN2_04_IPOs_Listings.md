# 🎯 IN2 4 · IPOs & Listings — The DRHP Detective Manual
> An IPO is a company selling itself with the best story it will ever tell — and a 400-page DRHP where it’s legally forced to confess the parts it would rather whisper. IN2 4 teaches the listing machine (book-building, price bands, the QIB/NII/retail quotas, anchor investors, allotment odds) and then the real skill: reading a Red Herring Prospectus like an FA graduate — risks first, promoter next, numbers last. We’ll run the canon checklist on a full mock IPO and close with the statistic that ruins listing-day FOMO.

## 🎯 Objectives
- Trace the IPO pipeline: DRHP → SEBI observations → RHP + price band → bidding (3 days) → allotment → listing.
- Decode book-building mechanics: band, cut-off vs bid price, the QIB ≤50% / NII ≥15% / retail ≥35% reservation map.
- Judge anchor investors: 30-day lock-in (extended for a portion) as a signal with two opposite readings.
- Run the DRHP detective drill: risk factors, promoter history, related parties, OFS vs fresh issue, use of proceeds.
- Separate listing gains (a lottery trade) from IPO investing (owning the business) — with the long-run aftermarket record.

## 📘 Concepts

### 4.1 The pipeline — confession in stages
1. **DRHP (Draft Red Herring Prospectus):** filed with SEBI — the full tell: business, risks (30+ pages of them, legally compelled), financials (restated), promoter track, objects of the issue.
2. **SEBI observations → RHP:** regulator’s clarifications folded in; the price band (e.g., ₹280–295) and dates announced.
3. **Bidding (typically 3 working days):** book builds live on exchange screens — demand by category visible hourly.
4. **Allotment:** oversubscribed retail = computerized LOTTERY by lots (one lot max priority — applying 10 lots from one PAN wins no extra odds vs 1 lot in many designs); basis of allotment published.
5. **Listing:** T+3-ish after issue close; the stock trades, lock-ins start their clocks, and the confessions become quarterly discipline.
Investor’s golden rule: the DRHP is free and bullet-point honest for one reason — liability. Read where the liability is: risk factors, litigation, related parties.

### 4.2 Book-building & the quota map
The band’s upper end is where serious demand bids (cut-off **‘market price’** retail option = you accept the discovered price). Reservation in a standard book-built issue: **QIB ≤50%** (institutions — their land-grab signals real demand), **NII ≥15%** (HNIs applying ₹2L+ — often leveraged funding, inflating apparent demand pre-2022-era margins rules), **retail ≥35%** (≤₹2L per application). Subscription multiples by category tell different stories: QIB 80× with institutions who actually pay = muscle; NII 200× funded by borrowed money = theatre that can collapse at allotment financing. Also: **fresh issue** (money INTO the company — growth fuel) vs **OFS — offer for sale** (money OUT to promoters/PE — someone selling; not sin, but price the exit).

### 4.3 Anchors & lock-ins — signal with two faces
A day before public bidding, institutional **anchor investors** commit at the discovered price: their names (quality mutual funds vs unknown outfits) are the market’s first publicly priced opinion. Lock-ins: 30 days (with a large chunk extended to 90 by regulation) — so anchors cannot flip on listing morning. Two readings: STRONG anchor book + small float = scarcity squeeze UP (the pop factory); anchors + heavy OFS + max-band pricing = insiders professionally transferring inventory to the widest audience India has. The same document funds both — your job is telling which.

### 4.4 DRHP detective drill — the page order that matters
1. **Risk factors** (pages 20–60): the company’s own confession — customer concentration >30%? promoter litigation? “conditional” government contracts?
2. **Promoter & group:** pledging, past ventures (zombie companies dissolved quietly), other listed cousins and their histories.
3. **Objects of the issue:** “general corporate purposes” above a third of raise = vague money; debt repayment to promoter-owned lenders = FA5’s related-party smell entering through the front page.
4. **Financials (restated):** run FA1–FA5’s first pass — margins vs listed peers, CFO/PAT, leveraging INTO the IPO (beauty-parade debt paydown).
5. **Valuation vs peers:** they price at 45× while listed leaders sit at 28×? The band is a hypothesis; the peer table is the referee.
Canon checklist line: an IPO application is a BUY DECISION with a 3-day deadline — the FA verdict must exist before the UPI mandate does.

### 4.5 The aftermarket truth — pops vs portfolios
Listing-day pops are real and RANDOM-ish across cycles (hot seasons pop, cold seasons list −20%) — that trade is a lottery with decent grade lottery odds in bull years. IPO INVESTING is a different asset: across cohorts, a meaningful share of IPOs trade below issue price within 1–3 years, and the beat-the-index minority is dominated by companies whose DRHPs were boring-good (clean cash engine, specific objects, sane pricing). Structural reason: the seller CHOOSES the season and the story; the buyer inherits both. Treat the listing pop as entertainment income (strictly sized, sell discipline pre-written) and IPO investing as FA7 verdicts with a subscription button.

## 🧪 LAB — Mock IPO: Verdant Agro (10 min)
Data: band ₹340–360, 10% fresh + 90% OFS (promoter+PE), one customer = 41% of revenue, promoter’s 2016 venture delisted after governance issues, QIB book 3× by day-3 (mostly one fund family), NII 190×, retail 12×, priced top of band, GMP ₹55.
1. Verdict FIRST: apply for listing pop / for long-term / skip — with the three heaviest flags named.
2. Read the QIB 3× vs NII 190× split: which number is muscle and which is theatre? What probably funds the NII?
3. OFS 90% means listing night the promoter-PE camp receives ~₹? per ₹1,000 Cr issue
4. The 41% customer — write the two risk-factor lines the DRHP itself must carry, and the valuation haircut logic vs peers.
5. GMP ₹55 (grey market premium): explain what it is, who guarantees it (precisely: nobody), and the sizing rule if you play pops at all.

**Why this matters:** this exact data-shape (huge OFS, single customer, delisted sibling, NII-leveraged subscription theatre) recurs with alarming regularity in hot IPO seasons — the lab is a vaccine.

**🔑 Lab answers:** (1) **skip for long-term; pop-only if strictly sized** — the flags: 90% OFS (insiders leaving at YOUR price), 41% single customer (one renegotiation = thesis death), promoter’s delisted-2016 history (governance genetics) — listing scarcity pop is possible, ownership case is absent (2) NII 190× is largely **borrowed-margin theatre** (IPO financing pyramids, unwind at allotment) while modest REAL QIB money (3×, and one family = concentration, not consensus) is the honest read: demand is borrowed, conviction is shallow (3) **~₹900 Cr of the ₹1,000 Cr** accrues to selling shareholders — “IPO” here is an exit counter with confetti (4) “Company derives 41% of revenue from a single customer; loss or repricing of this relationship would materially and adversely affect results” + the mandatory second line on receivable-days strength of that customer; haircut: peers at 28× deserve at MINUS concentration discount ~20–30% → honest band ≈ 20–22× the equivalent earnings — a 360 price at 45× is retail subsidizing the gap (5) GMP is an unofficial street quote on expected listing premium — no exchange, no clearing corp, NO guarantee, manipulated cheaply via tiny unofficial volumes; the rule — pop-trades from IPO plans are pre-sized lottery tickets (≤5% of portfolio sleeve, sell-by-listing-day discipline), and GMP is NEVER a fundamental input.

6. Listing-day playbook: you’re allotted one lot at ₹360, GMP says ₹55, listing opens +14%: write the exact pre-committed exit rule a disciplined desk uses (time/price conditions) — and the psychology trap it defends against (BF2’s house-money effect!).
7. DRHP speed-read: given 40 minutes with a 400-page DRHP, list your page-by-page sequence with minutes allotted — ending with the three red-flag types that auto-veto regardless of story.

## ❓ Quiz
1. OFS-heavy IPOs (90% offer-for-sale) primarily mean:
   (a) the company gets growth capital
   (b) insiders are exiting at the discovered price — 90 PAISE of every bid-rupee goes to promoter/PE pockets; not automatically evil, but the long-term thesis must justify buying what officers of the company are professionally selling at maximum-band optimism
   (c) SEBI rejected the fresh issue
2. NII subscription of 190× mostly reflects:
   (a) 190× genuine demand
   (b) leveraged IPO financing that unwinds at allotment — paper demand theatre; the muscle metric is REAL QIB money (slow, fully-paid, held), and confusing the two is how allotment-day financing crunches turn pops into cliff-dives
   (c) smart retail
3. A disciplined listing-pop trade requires:
   (a) conviction in the brand
   (b) pre-sized lottery framing (≤5% sleeve) with a pre-written exit rule (sell at pop or hard stop at issue) — because the pop is scarcity engineering plus season luck; riding it without an FA7-grade thesis converts a lottery win into an unexamined position at peak crowd
   (c) highest possible application size

### ✅ Answers
1. **(b)** — follow the money: fresh issue feeds factories, OFS feeds founders. Read whose.
2. **(b)** — subscriptions built on 3-day financing are weather, not climate.
3. **(b)** — discipline before the open; (c) maximizes the exact variance you cannot pre-commit through.
