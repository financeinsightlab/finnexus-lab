## 📘 Concepts

### 5.1 The fraction-substitution speed kit (upgrade of PC1 arsenal)

| Given | Substitute | Given | Substitute |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12.5% | ÷8 | 37.5% | ×3/8 |
| 16.67% | ÷6 | 62.5% | ×5/8 |
| 33.33% | ÷3 | 66.67% | ×2/3 |
| 14.29% | ÷7 | 87.5% | ×7/8 |
| 11.11% | ÷9 | 9.09% | ÷11 |

**Demo:** 37.5% of ₹64,000 = 64,000 × 3/8 = 8,000 × 3 = **₹24,000** — vs 0.375 column work. Bank-SSC percentages are DESIGNED around these fractions (numbers \"6,400\", \"72,000\" appear for ÷8/÷9 reasons). When the given number divides the fraction's denominator cleanly, the exam is whispering the shortcut.

### 5.2 Option-aware elimination (MCQ martial art)

1. **Bounds first:** answer must be > the change (reverse-question) or < the max portion — kill 1–2 crazy options instantly.
2. **Sign & size sanity:** +x% then cut must land BELOW peak — options above the peak value die.
3. **Mid-option back-plug** (AV6/BR5 inheritance): test option (b/c): multiplier-check forward; too high → aim low, too low → aim high.
4. **Fraction echo:** options like 1,500 / 15,000 / 150,000 differ by unit — loud alarm to re-check the base ×-unit (percent of WHAT and HOW MANY).

### 5.3 The five percentage traps (name → vaccine)

1. **Base Flip** — \"more than\" vs \"less than\" share the gap, not the % (PC4). *Vaccine: circle the base noun; flip with 100x/(100+x).*
2. **Additive Hallucination** — +20+10 ≠ +30 (PC3). *Vaccine: never add percents; formula or multiplier ladder only.*
3. **Reverse Overreach** — adding x% back after a cut (PC2). *Vaccine: ÷ the surviving multiplier; forward-check the loop.*
4. **Points-vs-Percent** — share rose 21→23.6%: +2.6 POINTS = +12.4% relative (DI6 vaccine-2, now permanent). *Vaccine: when both numbers are %, whisper \"points or percent?\"*
5. **The Inclusive-Tax/Discount Double-Count** — stripping 18% from a ₹59,000 inclusive bill as \"−18%\" = 48,380 ❌ instead of ÷1.18 = 50,000 ✔ (PC2 LAB). *Vaccine: inclusive → ÷(1+r); exclusive → ×(1+r).*

### 5.4 The 2-pass pacing plan

```
PASS 1 (bank):  direct %-of, conversions, single change, simple elections  (~25–35 s each)
PASS 2 (grinders): reverse-engineering, successive chains, double-statement classics (~60–80 s)
Skip rule: >100 s on any % question → mark & move (these are EQUAL marks!)
Rough-sheet header: base? · ÷ survivor · × multiplier · never add %
```

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** \"62.5% of 48,000\" — the champion's compute line is:
- (a) 0.625 × 48,000 column multiplication
- (b) **48,000 × 5/8 = 30,000 — fraction-substitution: the given number (48,000) divides the denominator (8) cleanly, which is the exam whispering the shortcut**
- (c) 48,000 × 62.5 then ÷ 100
- (d) 48,000 ÷ 62.5

**Q2.** The 'Reverse Overreach' trap means:
- (a) Reversing too many questions
- (b) **Adding x% BACK after a cut — \"2,100 + 30% = 2,730\" — instead of dividing by the surviving multiplier (÷0.7 = 3,000); % of the reduced ≠ % of the original**
- (c) Solving in reverse order
- (d) Using too many multipliers

**Q3.** Your pacing plan for a 10-question mixed % mini-section should be:
- (a) Strict 1→10 order
- (b) **2-pass: bank direct %-of/conversions/single-change first (~30 s each), reverse/chain grinders second; skip-rule >100 s; rough-sheet header: 'base? ÷ survivor · never add %'**
- (c) Hardest first
- (d) All-in on the first five

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — clean division by the fraction's denominator is the design signal. 5/8 × 48,000 beats any decimal column. Speed kit installed = free 30 seconds per set.
2. **(b)** — the only legal reverse is ÷surviving-multiplier (then forward-check to close the loop). Adding back over-shoots every time — bases again!
3. **(b)** — equal marks, unequal costs: cheap ones first. The written skip-rule stops the \"just one more minute\" bleed that wrecks sections.
