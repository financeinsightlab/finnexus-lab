## 📘 Concepts

### 7.1 The ShopKart P&L as a DI table (FY25, ₹ lakh)

| Line item | FY24 | FY25 | Δ ₹ | Δ % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 236.0 | 280.0 | +44.0 | **+18.6%** |
| COGS | 168.0 | 198.8 | +30.8 | +18.3% |
| **Gross profit** | 68.0 | 81.2 | +13.2 | +19.4% |
| Rent | 28.3 | 30.8 | +2.5 | +8.8% |
| Salaries | 24.8 | 28.0 | +3.2 | +12.9% |
| Marketing | 7.1 | 9.8 | +2.7 | +38.0% |
| Other/admin | 5.2 | 5.88 | +0.68 | +13.1% |
| **Net profit** | **2.66** | **6.72** | **+4.06** | **+152.6%** |

DI reflexes at work: totals-first (revenue total = the anchor 100%); cross-tab (each cost as % of revenue); rank (net profit % growth towers — +152.6%); small-base law (net's huge % partly because FY24 base ₹2.66L was tiny — the 'small-base caveat' your GL story always repeats).

### 7.2 Margin math (statement cross-tab)

- **Gross margin** = gross profit ÷ revenue: FY25 = 81.2/280 = **29.0%** · FY24 = 68/236 = 28.8% → +0.2 POINTS (vaccine-2 wording!) — vendor terms holding.
- **Net margin** = 6.72/280 = **2.4%** (FY24: 2.66/236 = 1.13%) → +1.27 points, the QoQ/GL-net-margin story, matching the expense pie's 8.6° sliver.
- **Cost-to-revenue ratios** from the pie (COGS 71%, rent 11%…) — cross-check pie against P&L: 198.8/280 = 71% ✔. Two statements, one truth — finance DI consistency reflex.

### 7.3 Budget vs Actual: variance discipline

| FY25 head | Budget | Actual | Variance ₹L | Variance % | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 265 | 280 | +15 | **+5.7%** | Favorable ✔ |
| COGS | 187 | 198.8 | +11.8 | +6.3% | Unfavorable ✘ |
| Marketing | 12 | 9.8 | −2.2 | −18.3% | \"Favorable\" cost?? — read 7.4! |
| Net profit | 5.0 | 6.72 | +1.72 | +34.4% | Favorable ✔ |

**Variance formula:** (Actual − Budget) ÷ Budget × 100, then label direction: for revenue/income POSITIVE = favorable; for costs NEGATIVE = favorable. **The one-word verdict column** makes a table analyst-grade.

### 7.4 Finance-flavored DI traps

- **Cost under ≠ always good:** marketing −18.3% under budget while revenue beat budget — cause or coincidence? Variance flags WHERE; judgment says WHY. Never auto-celebrate under-spend on growth engines.
- **Rate vs mix:** average ticket can rise because prices rose (rate) or because the ONLINE mix grew (mix). DI shows the totals; the analyst decomposes.
- **Small-base trophy:** +152.6% net growth is REAL and REPORTABLE — with the one-line caveat (\"against a ₹2.66L FY24 base\"). Your GL story models this honesty; exams ask \"is the claim misleading?\" — answer: not wrong, but INCOMPLETE without the base.

### 7.5 CAPSTONE — The ShopKart Analyst DI Pack 🏆

**Deliverables (all five, then the spoken line):**

1. **TABLE Q:** From the P&L (7.1): \"Marketing's FY25 spend as % of revenue vs FY24 — did the RATIO rise or fall, and by how many points?\"
2. **BAR Q:** Quarter totals 58/65/76/81 (FY25) vs FY24 quarters 52/56/61/67: \"Which FY25 quarter posted the highest YoY %, and what's its value?\"
3. **LINE Q:** Online monthly (DI4): \"From Sep-FY26 onward (post-FY25), if the Sep→Oct growth pattern (20%) repeated for exactly two months starting Mar-FY25's 7.5, what would May-FY26 read?\" — compute, then state why pure repetition is an assumption.
4. **PIE Q:** Expense pie (7.1/di5): \"A 1-POINT cut in COGS share + a 0.5-point cut in rent share translates to what ₹ change in FY25 net profit?\"
5. **CASELET Q (finance costume):** \"In Q2-FY26, ShopKart invoiced Fresh Farms on Net-30 terms for supplies worth 71% of Q2 revenue. Q2 revenue = ₹71.8L. What is the total invoiced value, and how many such ₹47,250-size invoices does it approximate?\" (71% COGS share riding the quarter!)
6. **THE VERDICT (written, ≤5 lines; then spoken, 60 s):** cover revenue trajectory, margin position, the ONE cost line you'd attack, and the single data caveat.

**Capstone key:** 1) FY25: 9.8/280 = 3.5% · FY24: 7.1/236 = 3.0% → **rose +0.5 points**. 2) YoY: Q1: 58/52 = +11.5% · Q2: 65/56 = +16.1% · Q3: 76/61 = +24.6% · Q4: 81/67 = +20.9% → **Q3, +24.6%**. 3) 7.5 × 1.2 × 1.2 = **₹10.8L**; assumption: constant 20% MoM twice (seasonality ignored — name it!). 4) 1.5% × 280 = **+₹4.2L net** (≈ 62% of FY25's entire profit — the 8.6° sliver doubles with a sliver cut!). 5) 71% × 71.8 = **₹50.98L ≈ ₹51.0L invoiced**; ÷ 47,250 ≈ **108 invoices** (avg = total ÷ unit — AV1 engine). 6) e.g., *\"Revenue +18.6% YoY with Q3 the growth engine (+24.6% YoY). Net margin doubled to 2.4% but remains a 8.6° sliver — attack COGS: a 1.5-point cut = +₹4.2L, 62% of a whole year's profit. Caveat: net-growth % flatters a small FY24 base; absolute ₹4.06L improvement is the honest headline.\"* — Speak it in 60 seconds; record; replay (fluency course crossover!).

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** FY25 net margin from the P&L is computed as:
- (a) Net profit ÷ COGS
- (b) **Net profit ÷ REVENUE — 6.72 ÷ 280 = 2.4%, the whole eaten by the expense pie's 8.6° sliver; the denominator is the revenue anchor (totals-first!)**
- (c) Revenue ÷ net profit
- (d) Net growth %

**Q2.** Marketing actual ₹9.8L vs budget ₹12L (variance −18.3%). The analyst-grade verdict is:
- (a) 'Favorable, celebrate'
- (b) **'Cost-favorable on paper — FLAG WHY: if marketing drove the +5.7% revenue beat, the under-spend is a growth-risk, not a win; variance marks WHERE, judgment decides WHY'**
- (c) 'Unfavorable'
- (d) 'Must be an error'

**Q3.** For the Analyst Pack verdict, the ONE data-honesty clause that must accompany \"net profit +152.6%\" is:
- (a) 'Source: company filings'
- (b) **'…against a small ₹2.66L FY24 base' — small-base caveat: the +₹4.06L absolute gain is real, but the frothy % shrinks without naming the denominator**
- (c) 'Subject to market risks'
- (d) 'Audited by CA firm'

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — margin = profit/revenue, always the anchor denominator. FY25: 2.4% ✔. FY24: 1.13%. The statements ARE DIs — totals-first owns them.
2. **(b)** — favorable/unfavorable is a FIRST read, not a verdict. Under-budget on a growth-engine line item while revenue over-performs demands the WHY question — that's the analyst voice you're being paid for.
3. **(b)** — name the base, keep the trophy. This is precisely the GL note next to Q2's +252% — honesty sells insight better than hype, in exams and boardrooms alike.
