# 🎯 DT8 · Groups & Undistributed Profits — The Shadows Between Parents and Children

> Consolidation stitches a family of companies into one economic creature — but the tax department still bills each member separately. Between those two realities lives a whole family of deferred shadows called OUTSIDE-BASIS DIFFERENCES: gaps between the consolidated carrying amount of your investment in a subsidiary/associate/JV and its tax base (typically, original cost). Undistributed profits, revaluations inside the child, currency drift on foreign branches — sources of tax that lands only when money comes home. The standard's answer is surgical: recognize the DTL on these differences UNLESS you control the reversal calendar AND reversal is not probable in the foreseeable future. Two conditions, one parent deciding, and a ₹5cr shadow kept off the books — legitimately. This is also the modern Indian module: DDT died in 2020, so dividend taxation lives in the shareholder's hands now.

## 📘 Concepts

### 8.1 Outside-basis differences — the gap between the two ledgers

Inside consolidated books, your investment in a child equals your share of its NET ASSETS, which quietly grows as the child earns and retains profits (equity accounting for associates/JVs grows your line too). In your tax ledger, the same investment usually stays at ORIGINAL COST until you sell or liquidate. Carrying ₹60cr vs base ₹40cr → an outside-basis difference of ₹20cr. Sources: undistributed profits (the big one), fair-value step-ups from the original acquisition (DT9), foreign currency translation on foreign subsidiaries (a gap parked in OCI — DT6 tracing applies). The default instinct: taxable temporary difference → DTL. Then the standard asks two questions.

### 8.2 The two-condition exemption — the parent's prerogative

For SUBSIDIARIES: recognize the DTL on taxable outside-basis differences EXCEPT to the extent BOTH hold: **(1) the parent is able to control the timing of the reversal** (it decides dividends; repatriation only happens when it says so), and **(2) it is probable that the difference will not reverse in the foreseeable future** (no sale, no liquidation, no dividend plan on the horizon). Both genuine: the shadow stays unbooked — disclosed, not recognized. For ASSOCIATES and JVs, the FIRST condition is harder: you influence but don't dictate; joint-control agreements may hand you timing control for JVs only if the agreement says so. For consolidated BRANCHES, the parent's own ledger contains the branch directly, so timing is fully controlled — but reversal into taxable remittance may STILL be probable, cutting the exemption off at its second condition. The doctrine breath: tax follows actual repatriation likelihood under YOUR control, so the books may stay silent only where silence matches the plan.

### 8.3 The repatriation flip — JV canon (python-verified)

Your JV share: undistributed profits ₹20cr. Case A — board approves NO payout; JV agreement hands you joint timing control; no reversal probable → no DTL, disclosure only. Case B — the new holding-company model plans to upstream ₹20cr as dividends next year → reversal now PROBABLE → **recognize DTL ₹5cr** (25%), generally through P&L as the parent's repatriation-side tax cost accrues. Same ₹20cr, same rate, opposite answers — decided purely by the reversal calendar the parent genuinely governs. Japan-level subtlety avoided: the canon is governance, not geography.

### 8.4 Losses inside the child — the DTA flip-side

Deductible outside-basis differences (a consolidated carrying LOWER than tax base, or unused child-level losses usable by the group) follow the DT3 gate, hardened: recognize the DTA only when the reversal is PROBABLE (repatriation/disposal likely) AND taxable profits within the relevant jurisdiction will absorb it. Two conditions stacked over two legal-entity borders — child-level loss DTAs in group accounts are rarer, smaller, and heavier-documented than solo-entity ones. Expect the note to show unrecognized amounts and their why.

### 8.5 India's post-DDT reality — the shareholder's burden

India abolished Dividend Distribution Tax from FY 2020-21: domestic companies no longer pay a distribution tax; dividends are now taxed in the SHAREHOLDER's hands at their slab (with withholding at source). Architecturally this shrinks the domestic repatriation-shadow for Indian groups — upstream dividends from an Indian subsidiary no longer carry an entity-level dividend tax under the parent's roof — while foreign repatriations, withholding tax regimes abroad, and eventual sale-of-shares capital gains still generate classic outside-basis differences. Doctrinal common sense remains: count the taxes that will ACTUALLY crystallize on the path home, gate by the two conditions, disclose what's parked.

## 🧪 LAB — Gate the Shadows (10 min)

Meridian Group file. For each: DTL? Amount? Or exempt/disclosure-only? Justify in one breath (25%).

1. Wholly-owned subsidiary S: carrying ₹60cr vs tax base ₹40cr from retained profits; parent dictates its dividend policy; no payout or sale planned for the next five years.
2. Same subsidiary S, new facts: board resolution passed to upstream ₹12cr dividend in Q1 next year.
3. JV J (joint control, independent board): share of undistributed profits ₹20cr; you alone cannot force distribution; partners resist payouts.
4. JV J, new facts: the shareholders' agreement was amended — distributions require a simple majority you command; repatriation formally planned.
5. Foreign associate A: undistributed ₹8cr share; withholding tax 10% upstream; you plan to sell your stake within two years.

**Why this matters:** group-deferred-tax is where textbook meets governance — interviewers love that the answer changes on a board resolution.

**🔑 Lab answers:**
1. **Exempt** — both conditions pass (control + improbable reversal): disclose, don't book. 2. **DTL on the ₹12cr slice = ₹3cr** — reversal planned within the foreseeable window for that portion; the rest stays exempt. 3. **No DTL** — the control condition fails outright (you cannot time the reversal), though the standard's practical line splits this by agreement structure; with partners resisting, probable-reversal fails too. 4. **DTL ₹5cr** (20 × 25%) — control acquired and repatriation probable: both exemption legs collapse. 5. **Recognize the tax that will actually crystallize**: withholding + any capital-gains exposure on sale ≈ measured at the applicable facts; reversal (sale/disposal) is probable within the window, so no exemption — the note should name jurisdictions and rates.

## 💪 Exercises

1. Why does the exemption die the week the board approves a dividend, even before payment? One-dense-sentence answer.
2. A group keeps EVERY shadow off the books for all subsidiaries citing "control everywhere." What two-part audit procedure collapses the blanket claim?
3. Which Indian change shrank the domestic outside-basis DTL universe since FY 2020-21 — and which shadows survive it?
4. Consolidated profits keep rising; recognized repatriation-DTLs stay flat for four years. Give one benign and one suspicious reading.

### ✅ Selected answers

1. Condition two (reversal improbable in the foreseeable future) snaps the moment a planned payout enters the approved plan — the shadow must be recognized on the probable slice regardless of when cash moves, because accounting answers "probable," not "paid."
2. (a) Demand the dividend/repatriation POLICY evidence per child (board minutes, treasury plans) versus the blanket claim, and (b) scan actual/upstream flows — any planned dividend or disposal collapses condition two for that slice even under full control.
3. The DDT abolition (dividends now taxed in shareholders' hands, withholding at source) shrank entity-level distribution shadows for domestic upstreams — surviving: foreign subsidiaries' withholdings, sale-of-stake capital-gains exposure, foreign-branch remittances, and pre-2020 historical layers.
4. Benign: genuine reinvestment programs, board-documented, getting bigger every year. Suspicious: the exemption used as an earnings-management shelf — flat DTL while hidden repatriation plans move cash elsewhere; the policy disclosures versus cash-flow reality mismatch is the tell.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** The two-condition exemption for subsidiaries:
(a) recognize DTL always, no exceptions
(b) no DTL only if the parent can CONTROL the reversal's timing AND reversal is not probable in the foreseeable future — the ₹5cr JV shadow stays unbooked under a genuine no-payout plan and springs onto the books the moment repatriation becomes probable
(c) no DTL if the child is foreign
(d) no DTL if dividends are under ₹10cr

**Q2.** For associates vs subsidiaries, condition one differs because:
(a) associates are small
(b) a parent DICTATES a subsidiary's dividend policy but only INFLUENCES an associate's — timing control is the gateway leg; without a shareholders'-agreement mechanism giving you that control (as the amended JV agreement did), the exemption struggles at its first gate before probable-reversal is even tested
(c) associates have no tax base
(d) subsidiaries are always exempt

**Q3.** Modern Indian dividend tax architecture:
(a) DDT is payable by the distributing company at 15%
(b) dividends are taxed in the SHAREHOLDER's hands with withholding at source (DDT abolished from FY 2020-21) — shrinking domestic repatriation shadows for Indian groups, while foreign withholdings, capital gains on stake sales, and branch remittances remain classic outside-basis territory
(c) dividends are now tax-free entirely
(d) dividends face only face fine mechanics

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — control plus improbability: the parent's calendar governs the shadow.
2. **(b)** — influence is not timing control; the JV agreement amendment is the canonical unlock.
3. **(b)** — shareholder-borne now; think jurisdictions, withholdings and exits.
