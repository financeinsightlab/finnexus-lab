# 👋 SI1 · Self-Intro Foundations: The First 60 Seconds of Everything

> College first day. New team huddle. Placement interview. WhatsApp networking group. Every room asks the same terrifying question: *"So… tell us about yourself?"* — and every time, the next 60 seconds decide how the room files you. This course makes that answer a weapon. Module 1: what a self-intro actually does, the 5 context rooms, and the universal skeleton under every great one.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Explain the **3 jobs** every self-intro must do: remembered, trusted, followed-up.
- Adapt one intro across the **5 context rooms** (college, office, networking, interview, virtual).
- Build intros on the **universal skeleton**: Open → Role → Proof → Hand-off.
- Cut/stretch intros across the **4 time tiers**: 15s / 30s / 60s / 120s.
- Kill the **6 intro sins** before they happen.

## 📘 Concepts

### 1.1 What your intro ACTUALLY does (3 jobs)

Your self-intro is not a biography. It's a **trailer**. Three jobs, in order:

| Job | Meaning | The test |
|---|---|---|
| 🧠 **Be remembered** | One hook the room keeps after 50 other intros | Can someone repeat one fact about you an hour later? |
| 🤝 **Be trusted** | Sound coherent, honest, prepared | Do you sound like a person who has their act together? |
| 🚪 **Invite follow-up** | End so they want the next sentence | Do they ask "oh interesting — tell me more about X"? |

Miss job 1 → forgettable. Miss job 2 → memorable for the wrong reason. Miss job 3 → dead air. The skeleton in §1.3 is engineered to hit all three, every time, in any room.

### 1.2 The 5 context rooms

Same you, five very different rooms:

| Room | Their goal for your intro | Your length | Your flavor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🎓 **College / first day** | "Who is in my class?" | 30–45 s | Friendly + one fun fact (the professor checks confidence, not resume) |
| 🏢 **New team / office** | "What will we work on together?" | 30–60 s | Role + what you bring + what you're curious about |
| 🌐 **Networking / events** | "Is this person relevant to me?" | 15–30 s | Headline + niche + ONE specific ask or topic |
| 💼 **Interview** | "Can this person organize thought?" | 60–90 s | Headline → Present → Past → Future → Hand-off (IC2's full framework) |
| 💻 **Virtual / online** | "Is their energy + audio real?" | 30–45 s | 20% more energy, camera-eye contact, tight pace (IC3's virtual kit) |

The content chunks are the same; **the weights change**. Interview = proof-heavy. Networking = hook-heavy. College = warmth-heavy.

### 1.3 The universal skeleton: **O·R·P·H**

Every great self-intro in any room is four moves:

1. **Open** — name + greeting energy: *"Hi everyone, I'm Aarav…"* (slow down ON the name — it's their only chance to catch it)
2. **Role** — your one-line headline identity: *"…a business analytics graduate who turns messy data into decisions."* (a *profession + superpower*, not a job-title dump)
3. **Proof** — ONE story or ONE number: *"Recently I analyzed a real retail general ledger — revenue up 18.5%, and I spotted how vendor terms free up 30 days of cash."* (specific = trusted)
4. **Hand-off** — invite them in: *"…and that's the kind of problem I want to keep solving here."* / *"Excited to hear YOUR stories too."* / *"Happy to go deeper on any of that."*

> Weak: "Hello my name is Aarav. I am from Delhi. I have done B.Com. My hobbies are cricket and music. I am hardworking and passionate. Thank you." — six sentences, zero hooks, zero follow-up interest. O·R·P·H fixes it in the same 45 seconds.

### 1.4 The 4 time tiers (and the expand/cut rule)

Build ONE master intro; derive all tiers from it:

| Tier | Use | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **15 sec** | Networking round-robin, rapid intros | O + R + hand-off only |
| **30 sec** | College first day, team huddle, virtual | O + R + 1 proof LINE + hand-off |
| **60 sec** | Interview opener | Full O·R·P·H with headline beats (IC2 version) |
| **120 sec** | Story-driven settings (team intro, panel, presentations) | 60-sec + ONE mini-story with a turn/crisis-lesson |

**Expand/cut rule:** when stretching, add **examples and numbers, never adjectives**. Cutting = delete the least *specific* line first. "Hardworking and passionate" dies first in every edit. (BE5 taught you the simple intro; this course is the same skill at interview altitude.)

### 1.5 The 6 intro sins (confess now, sin never again)

1. **Resume reading** — "I did X, then Y, then Z" — dates nobody can remember. Fix: pick ONE proof, not five.
2. **Apology openers** — "Sorry, I'm a bit nervous / not good at this." Fix: nerves are handled by protocol (SI3), never announced.
3. **Adjective soup** — "dynamic, passionate, hardworking". Fix: replace every adjective with evidence or delete it.
4. **Rushed name** — your name at 300 wpm = nobody catches it. Fix: name lands at ~120 wpm with a micro-pause after.
5. **Limp ending** — "…yeah, that's about it." Fix: ALWAYS end on a hand-off that hands the mic somewhere.
6. **Same intro everywhere** — interview version at a chai networking session sounds like a robot. Fix: 5 rooms, 5 weightings (§1.2).

### 1.6 Delivering your NAME like it's worth remembering

Your name is job #1's anchor. Three moves:
- **Slow + clear + falling:** "I'm AA·rav." — the falling tone (PR3!) sounds like it BELONGS to you; rising sounds like you're asking permission.
- **The mnemonic gift** (optional, for unfamiliar names): "Aarav — double A — like getting an A+ twice." A memory hook is kindness to the listener.
- **Never apologize for it:** "It's difficult to pronounce, sorry…" ❌ — teach it proudly: "It's AA-rav — try it once." ✓

## 🧪 LAB — headline identities in 5 flavors (20 min)

Write your **Role line** once for each room (one sentence each):

1. College first day: warm + curious (e.g., *"…a commerce grad who thinks Excel is secretly fun."*)
2. New team: role + offering (*"…the new analyst — I make dashboards people actually open."*)
3. Networking: niche + ask (*"…aspiring finance analyst — I'd love to compare notes with anyone in FP&A."*)
4. Interview: profession + superpower (*"…an analytics graduate who turns messy ledgers into decisions."*)
5. Virtual: headline + energy (*"…the numbers person from Delhi — very happy to be on this call!"*)

Then say each aloud 2×. Which one sounds most like "you"? That flavor becomes your core; the others orbit around it.

## ❓ Quiz

1. The 3 jobs of EVERY self-intro are…
2. When stretching an intro from 30s to 120s, you add…
3. Why the micro-pause + falling tone on your name?

### ✅ Answers

1. **Remembered → trusted → invited-for-follow-up.** A trailer is not a biography; the room keeps a hook or keeps nothing.
2. **Examples and numbers — never adjectives.** Specifics stretch credibility; adjectives stretch the clock only.
3. **The name is the anchor of job #1**: slow + falling makes it sound owned and *catchable*; rushed-rising makes it optional for the listener.
