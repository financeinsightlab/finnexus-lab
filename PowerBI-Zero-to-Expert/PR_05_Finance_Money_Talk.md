## 📘 Concepts

### 5.1 The money words (say each aloud now)

**Indian money system:** lakh, crore, rupees, paise · "seventy-one point eight lakh" · "two point eight crore" (never "crores of rupees" doubling).

**Markets:** **SEN**·sex · **NIF**·ty · N·S·E / B·S·E · **BUL**·l market, **BEAR**·ish · in·**DEX** (plural **in·DI**·cees — don't fight its Latin) · **VO**·la·tile.

**Central bank & policy:** R·B·I · **RE**·po rate · re·verse RE·po · **IN**·fla·tion · mon·e·**TA**·ry pol·i·cy · **stance** ("with·DRAW·al of ac·com·mo·DA·tion" — say it five times; RP5's stance trio deserves tongue-muscles too).

**Company finance:** P&L ("pee-and-ell") · **EBIT**·DA ("e-bit-dah" — one word, never spelled) · work·ing **CA**·pi·tal · **CA**·sh flow · **MAHR**·gin · **EX**·cep·tion·al i·tems · **LAIR**·yer? no — **churn** · **CAC** (letters or "kack" — letters safer in HR rooms) · **Net·thir·ty** (net-30).

**Personal finance:** E·M·I (letters) · S·I·P (letters) · **FE**·es · in·**TRE**·st (three beats, not "in-trust") · in·VEST·ment · **LIEN**? rare: say "lee-un" if it ever appears.

**Deals & docs:** I·P·O · **D·R·H·P** (RP5's red herring doc) · a·**QUI**·si·tion · due **DIL**·i·gence.

### 5.2 The numbers readout protocol (R-S-L: round · signpost · land)

Every figure spoken to humans passes three stations:

1. **ROUND to human scale:** ₹2,79,94,469 → "about **two point eight crore**". Precision is for files; mouths serve meaning.
2. **SIGNPOST before the number:** *"The headline number…"* / *"On profitability…"* — give the listener a folder before the file arrives.
3. **LAND it:** slow to ~120 wpm for the number itself, **falling tone at the end**, then golden pause (PR3).

> Weak: "um so basically revenue was like two crore seventy-nine lakh ninety-four thousand four something percent—" 
> Readout: "On revenue — *(signpost)* — about **two point eight crore rupees**, **up eighteen point five percent** — *(landed, pause)* — growth driven by the festive season."

### 5.3 The 3 fake-calm detectors (what makes you sound nervous)

| Detector | Tell | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Number mumble** | volume/pace drop ON the number | 120-wpm rule — numbers get *more* space, not less |
| **Rising-tone figures** | "…seventy-one point eight lakh ↗?" | Land falling; the number is a claim, not a request |
| **Speed spikes** | easy words gallop, hard words stumble | Even-paced thought groups (PR3), rehearsal at fixed pace |

These three are *auditory* tells — nobody catches them watching; every interviewer catches them listening. Your PR4 recordings are where you hunt them.

### 5.4 🏆 CAPSTONE — the 90-second earnings readout

**The script (ShopKart GL — read, mark, record):**

> "ShopKart India, fiscal year twenty twenty-five results. Annual revenue: about two point eight crore rupees, up eighteen point five percent year on year. Net profit: six point seven lakh rupees — a net margin of two point four percent, up four-tenths of a percentage point over last year. In the festive quarter, revenue reached seventy-one point eight lakh, up twenty-one percent, while net profit came in at three point eight five lakh against one point zero nine lakh a year earlier. One caution from the analyst desk: the two hundred and fifty-two percent profit jump is off a very small base, and exceptional items sit in both years. Our recommendation: extend the Net-30 vendor terms across suppliers to free up working capital, before funding new dark stores. Margin first, scale second. Thank you."

**Your mission:**
1. **Mark** thought groups / stress / ↘ arrows (PR3 marks).
2. **3-take method** (PR4): cold → marked → performed. Target: one take in 80–100 seconds, zero mumbles on numbers, every landing falling.
3. **Self-score** with PR4 rubric + 3 money accuracy checks: ① points-vs-percent clean ② base-effect caveat present ③ every window named.
4. **Friend gut-vote:** play your take 3 + the model clip, ask *"which voice would you trust with investor money?"* — anonymous honesty encouraged.

## ❓ Quiz

1. R-S-L stands for… and numbers slow to ~120 wpm because…
2. "Exceptional items sit in both years" in the capstone exists to prove…
3. Saying "two point five two times" vs "up two hundred and fifty-two percent" matters because…

### ✅ Answers

1. **Round · Signpost · Land.** The 120-wpm rule gives listeners filing time for figures — numbers are the point, so they get the most space, never the least.
2. **You audit before you celebrate** — one-offs aren't repeatable, and your listener's trust compounds every time you volunteer the caveat (IC5/RP5's old friend, unprompted).
3. **Analyst language is standardized**: multiples are fluency gear ("2.5×"), but public numbers ride in percent with windows and bases — code-switching correctly marks you as bilingual between shop-floor and boardroom.
