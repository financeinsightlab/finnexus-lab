# 🎯 EC1 · Micro Foundations — Margins, Trade-offs & the Price Machine
> Every economic decision ever made — a farmer planting wheat, ShopKart pricing a SKU, you choosing this course over a movie — runs on three questions: what does it cost me (including what I give up), what does the NEXT unit add, and where do supply and demand shake hands? EC1 installs the micro operating system: opportunity cost, marginal analysis, the supply-demand machine, and elasticity — the number that tells a CFO whether a price hike is strategy or suicide. This is the physics under every finance module you've built.

## 🎯 Objectives
- Price any decision with opportunity cost — including the invisible ones (time, attention, capital parked).
- Think at the margin: sunk costs ignored, next-unit logic installed, break-even re-derived the economist's way.
- Run the supply-demand machine: shifts vs movements, equilibrium, and who pays when it breaks.
- Compute elasticity (canon: +10% price, −8% quantity → E 0.8 → revenue +1.2%) and convert it into pricing strategy.
- Spot micro logic inside finance: pricing power = low elasticity = Buffett's moat, stated economically.

## 📘 Concepts

### 1.1 Opportunity cost — the price nobody prints
Economics begins where accounting stops: every choice costs its **best rejected alternative**. That ₹10L in a 6% FD isn't "safe" — it costs the 11% index alternative: ₹5L of decade-decade invisible tuition. Your two hours tonight cost a movie, not money. CF1's capital-charging machinery (WACC, EVA) is opportunity cost wearing a CFO badge — ShopKart's capital charges 12.6% even when the bank statement shows 9%, because equity's price is the road not taken. Tattoo: **there is no "free" anything — only costs measured and costs ignored.**

### 1.2 The margin — where every smart decision lives
Decisions fail when made on AVERAGES and succeed at the MARGIN. Should ShopKart serve one more order? Compare **marginal cost** (₹38 delivery + ₹4 packaging) against **marginal revenue** (₹71) — margin ₹29, accept — even if AVERAGE cost per order is ₹55. The average lies when fixed costs smear; the margin tells the next-unit truth. Corollary: **sunk costs are dead** — the ₹2L spent on the failed app feature buys zero votes in tonight's decision; weeping over it is how businesses compound errors. Every "but we've already invested so much" is a sunk-cost fallacy with a podium.

### 1.3 The supply-demand machine — and its two gears
Equilibrium is where the plans of buyers and sellers agree. Two gearboxes, separate religions: a **movement along** a curve (price changes, quantities slide) vs a **shift of** the whole curve (income, tastes, input costs, competitor prices rewrite the map). Read markets by asking "which curve moved?": wheat supply shock (monsoon) → price springs, quantity closebehind; demand shift (festival season) → both spring. The machine never sleeps: rent ceilings create queues, price floors create gluts — suppress the price and the shortage appears in a different costume (quality cuts, black markets, waitlists). Prices aren't morality; they're MAPS — and rationing always happens, by price or by queue.

### 1.4 Elasticity — the pricing superpower, canon-computed
**Price elasticity of demand = %ΔQ ÷ %ΔP.** Canon: ShopKart tests +10% price (₹100→110) on a staple SKU; volume −8% (1,000→920). E = 8/10 = **0.8 (inelastic)** → revenue ₹1,00,000 → ₹1,01,200 **(+1.2%)**: hike succeeded. Had volume fallen 18% (E 1.8), revenue would DROP to ₹90,200 — same hike, different universe. Drivers of low elasticity: no substitutes, small wallet-share, habit/addiction, necessity. And here the finance weld: **pricing power IS low elasticity** — the moat CF courses and Buffett both kneel to, stated in its native economics. A company that can raise prices with E<1 without losing volume is printing the difference between a business and a commodity.

### 1.5 The invisible hand — and the honest asterisks
Markets coordinate billions of decisions through prices — no committee matches it (the "invisible hand", 1776 and still undefeated in peacetime). Asterisks where the hand drops objects: **externalities** (pollution costs dumped on the public ledger), **public goods** (lighthouses, national defense — free-rider economics), **information asymmetry** (used-car lemons — FA5's forensics is defensive armor against exactly this) and **monopoly power** (EC2). Knowing WHERE markets fail is what separates an economist from a pamphlet — and it's also where regulation (IN2 2's referee) gets its job description.

## 🧪 LAB — Micro gym (10 min)
1. Opportunity-cost postmortem: founder keeps ₹40L "buffer" in a current account (0%) for 2 years while his WACC clock runs at 12.6%. What did the comfort cost?
2. Margin call: delivery app average cost/order ₹61 (office included), order price ₹58 — but marginal cost ₹44. Accept the order? Two-line answer.
3. Curve-shift diagnosis: Bengaluru rents jump 15% in one year with NO new construction ban. Which curve moved, list two causes, and the one policy that addresses it without queues.
4. Elasticity council: cinema tickets E ≈ 1.9, life-saving medicine E ≈ 0.1. Which firm discounts to grow revenue, which firm sustains hikes — and name the ethical asterisk in the second case.
5. Sunk-cost courtroom: you spent ₹5L on inventory nobody wants; selling today recovers ₹1.5L, "waiting for prices to recover" costs ₹8k/month storage. Rule the decision.

**Why this matters:** item 4 is the pricing meeting you'll sit in within 3 years; item 5 is the boardroom skill that saves more money than most analyses.

**🔑 Lab answers:** (1) carrying ₹40L at 0% vs investing at even 8-10% ≈ **₹6.9L over two years (40 × 1.09² − 40)** — idle cash is a silent employee with a negative salary; the buffer belongs in T-bills/liquid funds (FI5!), never in a 0% costume (2) **accept** — marginal revenue ₹58 − marginal cost ₹44 = **+₹14 contribution**; the ₹61 average treats the office rent as if this order must pay it (it must — but from total contribution, not per-order verdicts); refuse only if capacity constraints force out full-margin orders (3) demand shifted RIGHT (tech hiring + migration inflows) while supply is stuck (construction lags years); causes: post-hiring boom, return-to-office; honest policy: enable supply (density approvals, infrastructure) — rent ceilings would convert the shortage into queues + maintenance-neglect (4) cinema discounts E 1.9 → lower price grows revenue (volume up 19% per −10%); medicine E 0.1 → hikes sail through — asterisk: elasticity near zero plus life-need is exactly where pricing power turns predatory, and regulators (and juries of public opinion) redraw the license to operate (5) **sell today** — the ₹5L is sunk (dead vote), the comparison is ₹1.5L now vs (recovery probability × future price − ₹8k/month bleed); unless recovery odds are near-certain and near-term, waiting is donating ₹8k/month to procrastination.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Trade-off ledger: list the FULL opportunity cost of your next big purchase (₹12L car): EMI + fuel + parking + the 12% index path not taken over 10 years (₹12L → ₹37.3L). Total?
2. Shift-matrix drill: for wheat, smartphones, MBAs: pick one demand shifter and one supply shifter each, and predict the equilibrium sign-change (P?, Q?) for a simultaneous double-shift — including the ambiguity rule.
3. Moat hunting, elasticity edition: name 3 Indian businesses with E<1 pricing power and 3 commodity traps with E>2, defending each in one line using substitutes/wallet-share.
4. Break-even autopsy: café sells coffee ₹150, variable ₹60, fixed ₹3L/month: break-even cups, then the MARGIN-LOGIC answer to "should we take the office-bulk order at ₹110?"
5. Externality pricing: factory pollutes ₹20 of damage per ₹100 product. Write the tax that fixes the market failure, who should collect it, and the second-order effect the producer will lobby with.
6. Behavioral cross-over: BF2's mental accounting meets opportunity cost — explain in 4 lines why "this ₹2L is gift money, it's okay to gamble it" is economically illiterate (fungibility!).
7. Interview forge: "Should Ola surge-price during rain?" — argue BOTH sides in 6 numbered lines: efficiency (rationing, supply response) vs fairness/regulation, ending with the policy you'd actually implement.

### ✅ Selected answers
1. Visible: ~₹12L + running. Invisible: ₹12L at 12% for 10y = ₹12 × 3.106 = **₹37.3L** foregone — the "₹12L car" is a ₹49L+ full-price decision against your 40-year-old self; doesn't make it wrong, makes it PRICED. Rule: buy the car with eyes that have seen the ₹37L ghost.
3. E<1 club: branded life-saving pharma (no substitute, small wallet-share), Pidilite adhesives (habit + tiny ticket + 70% shares), Microsoft-Office-class enterprise software (switching costs). E>2 traps: unbranded basmati, generic steel, economy airline seats on competitive routes — identical products, one click between suppliers, margins that live and die by ₹10 moves.
4. Break-even = 3,00,000/90 = **3,334 cups/month**; bulk at ₹110: contribution 110 − 60 = ₹50 positive → accept IF spare capacity exists and retail cannibalization < the ₹50×bulk volume; margins decide marginal orders, averages decide survival.
7. For: surge rations scarce cars to highest-need riders and CALLS supply out of bed (drivers respond to ₹) — the queue exists anyway; price just orders it honestly. Against: rain emergencies make E≈0, so surges tax distress not luxury; optics + fairness = consumer anger and regulatory risk. Implement: capped surge (1.5-2×), visible driver-incentive pass-through, and free-fee cancellation windows — keep the supply signal, blunt the distress toll. Panels love candidates who keep BOTH truths in one answer.

## ❓ Quiz
1. A founder leaves ₹40L idle for 2 years at 12.6% WACC. The honest P&L line is:
   (a) zero — cash is safe
   (b) ~₹10.1L of invisible tuition (40×1.126²−40) — opportunity cost is a real employee with a negative salary; safety belongs in yield-bearing parking (T-bills/liquid funds), never in a 0% costume
   (c) the inflation alone
2. Order price ₹58, average cost ₹61, marginal cost ₹44. Accept?
   (a) no — below average cost
   (b) yes — +₹14 marginal contribution; averages smear fixed costs across units and lie about single orders; refuse only when the order steals capacity from full-margin demand
   (c) only if the client is big
3. +10% price, −8% volume on a staple means the hike:
   (a) failed — volume fell
   (b) worked — E 0.8 < 1 makes revenue +1.2% (₹100.0k → ₹101.2k); inelastic demand is pricing power, the economic name for the moat every investor hunts
   (c) is neutral

### ✅ Answers
1. **(b)** — idle ≠ safe; the cost is the road not taken, compounded.
2. **(b)** — the margin is the courtroom; averages are hearsay.
3. **(b)** — elasticity converts pricing from gambling into measurement.
