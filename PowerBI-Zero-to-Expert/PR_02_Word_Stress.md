# 🔤 PR2 · Word Stress & Pronouncing Your World

> Wrong word stress is the single biggest "please repeat that" trigger — bigger than any accent. Say **DE**·ve·lop as de·**VEL**·op and suddenly you sound like the industry. This module gives you the stress rules covering 80% of English, the fossil-mispronunciation hitlist every Indian professional should clean once, and how to say names, brands and money like a native of the boardroom.

## 📘 Concepts

### 2.1 The 80/20 stress rules

**Rule A — suffixes steal the beat:** stress lands on the syllable *before* these:

| Suffix family | Where the beat lands |
|---|---|
| **-tion/-sion** | before it: edu·CA·tion, de·CI·sion, ex·ami·NA·tion |
| **-ic/-ical** | before it: a·NA·ly·tic, e·co·NO·mic, sta·TI·sti·cal |
| **-ity** | before it: a·bil·I·ty, re·al·I·ty, pro·duc·TI·vi·ty |
| **-graphy/-logy/-ometry** | before it: pho·TO·gra·phy, psy·CHO·lo·gy, e·con·O·me·try |

**Rule B — 2-syllable noun/verb pairs:** *noun* = beat first, *verb* = beat last:
**RE**·cord (noun) → re·**CORD** (verb) · **IM**·port → im·**PORT** · **PRE**·sent → pre·**SENT** · **CON**·tract → con·**TRACT** · **PRO**·gress → pro·**GRESS**.

> GD/IC link: "Let me **RE**·cord the session" (noun) vs "I'll re·**CORD** your answer" (verb). Interviewers notice.

**Rule C — teen vs ty:** stress the teen: fif·**TEEN**, thir·**TEEN** — but **THIR**·ty, **FIF**·ty. (BE course's tricky pairs all over again, now with the beat.) *"The call is at three·THIRTY, not thir·TEEN"* — clarity money can't buy.

### 2.3 The -ed ending rule (pronunciation carrying grammar)

Past tense endings have three outfits:

| Final sound of the verb | -ed sounds like | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| t or d | **id** (a real syllable) | want·ed, need·ed, wait·ed → WON·tid |
| voiced (b,g,v,l,m,n,r,vowels) | **d** | play·**d**, call·**d**, join·**d**, agree·**d** |
| unvoiced (k,p,s,ch,sh,f) | **t** | ask·**t**, help·**t**, watch·**t**, fix·**t**, laugh·**t** |

❗ Marketing trap: "developed" = di·VEL·**upt** (one t), not "de-ve-lop-D". Interview-anecdote gold: "I **traced** (trace-t) the pipeline and **fixed** (fix-t) it" — crisp endings sound like senior engineers.

### 2.4 Names, brands & titles — the business passport

- **Indian business names:** **TAA**·ta (long first a), Re·**LI**·ance, **IN**·fo·sys, Ad·a·ni/A·DAA·ni (own your choice), **ZO**·ma·to (nothing "tomato"-ish!), **ZEP**·to, **NY**·kaa.
- **Institutions:** **RBI**, **SEBI**, **NPCI**, UPI (say the letters — "yu-pee-eye"), **SEN**·sex, **NIF**·ty, **NSE**/**BSE** (letters), **GST** (letters).
- **Titles:** **C·E·O**, **C·F·O**, V·P (letters; never "see-yo" 😄), **HR**, **MD** (doctor-adjacent confusion avoided by context).
- **Data words:** **DAY**·ta or **DAH**·ta (both fine — pick one, stay loyal), **A**·na·lyst, **ANA**·ly·sis (noun) vs a·**NA**·ly·tic (adj) — Rule A in action, **SQL** = "ess-que-ell" or "sequel" (both professional), **DAX** (like "dax" rhyming axe), **Excel** (ex·**CEL**).

### 2.5 Numbers, prices, dates — the mouth's ATM

Rules for reading aloud without mumbling:

1. **Chunk it:** ₹47,250 = "forty-seven thousand two-fifty" ✓ (chunked, natural) vs spelling every digit ✗.
2. **Lakh/crore speak:** ₹71.8 lakh = "**seventy-one point eight** lakh"; ₹2.8 crore = "**two point eight crore**". Numbers decimal-point get said *digit·point·digit*.
3. **Prices:** ₹999 = "nine ninety-nine" or "nine hundred and ninety-nine" — both, but consistently.
4. **Dates:** 23 July 2026 = "the twenty-third of July, twenty twenty-six".
5. **Percentages:** 18.5% = "eighteen point five percent"; margin deltas: "up **0.4 percentage points**" (never "up 0.4 percent" — GD5/RP5's points-vs-percent law!).

## ❓ Quiz

1. "de-VE-LOP with three full equal vowels" sounds robotic because…
2. In noun-verb pairs like REcord/reCORD, the beat shift signals…
3. "asked" pronounced "axed" is dangerous because…

### ✅ Answers

1. **Unstressed syllables must shrink to the lazy schwa** — forcing full vowels everywhere is non-English rhythm. di·**VEL**·up: ONE beat, two lazy neighbors.
2. **Word class itself**: noun gets beat first, verb gets beat last. The stress carries the grammar (like -ed carries past tense).
3. **Axe is a different word entirely** ("axed" = fired/terminated!). On interview day, "I asked my manager" mustn't sound like "I axed my manager." Enough said.
