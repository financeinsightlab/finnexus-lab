## 📘 1.1 Why speaking about news is a career superpower

Look around any Indian office at 10 AM: someone says *"Dekha, RBI ne rate cut kiya!"* and suddenly there are two kinds of people — those who join the conversation and those who quietly sip chai. Business news is the **default small talk** of offices, interviews, MBA group discussions, and client meetings.

- **Interviews:** "So, what's happening in the market these days?" — banking, consulting and MBA admissions ask this constantly.
- **Office credibility:** the analyst who says "that stock crashed because of the new RBI circular" sounds senior, even as a fresher.
- **Your analytics career:** you build dashboards (Power BI ✅) — news gives you the *story around the numbers*. Dashboard + news sense = a complete professional.

The good news: you don't need to read everything. You need **one story a day, read well, and spoken aloud**. That's this whole course.

## 📘 1.2 Anatomy of a business article — the X-ray

Every business article — Economic Times, Mint, Moneycontrol — is built from the same 5 Lego blocks:

| Block | What it does | How long |
|---|---|---|
| **Headline** | Promises the whole story in 5–10 words | 1 line |
| **Lead** (first paragraph) | Answers the **5Ws**: Who, What, When, Where, Why | 1–2 sentences |
| **Body** | Details, background, history, numbers | Middle paragraphs |
| **Quotes** | Real people react: CEO, analyst, minister | 1–3 quotes |
| **The ending** | "What happens next" — outlook, dates to watch | Last paragraph |

💡 **Reading shortcut:** if you only ever read the *headline + lead*, you still get 80% of the news. Everything below that is evidence.

## 📘 1.3 The 3-gear news-reading method

Same gears you learned in BE5 reading — now sharper, for news:

1. **GEAR 1 — Skim (10 seconds):** headline + lead only. Ask yourself: *"Can I say this in one line?"* ("ShopKart's profit went up a lot.")
2. **GEAR 2 — Scan (20 seconds):** hunt the **numbers**: revenue, %, dates, targets. Circle them (mentally or on screen).
3. **GEAR 3 — Deep read (3–4 minutes):** only for your **one story of the day**. Read fully, note 3–5 new words, and — non-negotiable — **say a 2-line summary out loud.** Silent reading builds knowledge; speaking builds *speaking*.

## 📘 1.4 Headline grammar — decoding the compressed code

Headlines are not normal English. They're a telegram. Once you crack 4 rules, every headline opens up:

| Trick | Headline | Normal English |
|---|---|---|
| Drop *a/an/the* | "Firm opens plant in Gujarat" | **A** firm opens **a** plant in Gujarat |
| Present simple = past event | "ShopKart beats estimates" | ShopKart **beat** estimates (yesterday) |
| **to + verb** = future plan | "Flipkart to hire 5,000 staff" | Flipkart **is going to** hire… |
| Colon = "said" | "CEO: growth will double" | The CEO **said** growth will double |

✏️ Decode these aloud (answers below in exercises):
1. "RBI to hold rates, experts say"
2. "IT giant Infosys bags $1 billion deal"
3. "Markets jump as inflation cools"

⚠️ Also meet **headline verbs**: *bags* (wins), *eyes* (wants/plans), *snubs* (rejects), *hails* (praises), *woes* (problems), *tops* (exceeds). Papers love these because they're SHORT.

## 📘 1.5 Your first 45 business terms — six power families

Don't memorize randomly. Learn in families — words that show up together:

**💰 Family 1 — Money in the company**
revenue (total sales), turnover (same as revenue, British use), profit (money left after costs), loss (costs > sales), margin (profit % of sales), earnings (profit, in "quarterly earnings"), cash flow (money moving in/out).

**📈 Family 2 — Markets & ownership**
shares, stock market, index (a market scoreboard — Sensex/Nifty), blue-chip (big safe company), IPO (first public share sale), valuation (company's price tag), unicorn (startup valued $1 billion+).

**🤝 Family 3 — Deals & growth**
merger (two companies become one), acquisition (one buys another), M&A, launch, expansion, partnership, joint venture (JV), stake (a % owned).

**🇮🇳 Family 4 — The economy**
inflation (prices rising), interest rate, repo rate (RBI's master rate), GDP (country's total production), recession (economy shrinking), Budget, fiscal deficit (government overspending).

**🌧️ Family 5 — Trouble**
layoffs (job cuts), debt (borrowed money), default (failing to repay), bankrupt, fraud, probe (investigation), downgrade.

**🗣️ Family 6 — Movement verbs (the storytellers!)**
UP small: rise, grow, climb · UP big: **soar, surge, jump, rally** · DOWN small: fall, dip, slip · DOWN big: **plunge, slump, crash, tumble** · NOTHING: flat, steady, hold, unchanged.

Method: 5 words/day, always in a **spoken sentence**: *"Margins plunged this quarter."* Words you speak are words you own.

## 📘 1.6 Numbers in the news — say them without stress

Indian offices say lakh/crore; global news says million/billion. You must convert on sight:

| Indian | World | Zeros |
|---|---|---|
| ₹1 lakh | 100 thousand | 1,00,000 |
| ₹10 lakh | 1 million | 10,00,000 |
| ₹1 crore | 10 million | 1,00,00,000 |
| ₹100 crore | 1 billion | 1,00,00,00,000 |

✏️ Drill: ₹2.5 crore = **25 million** · ₹50 lakh = **5 million** · $2 billion = **₹16,600+ crore** (at ~₹83/$).

Three more number skills news loves:
- **% vs percentage POINTS:** margin rising from 2% to 3% is **+1 percentage point** but **+50%** growth. Speakers who confuse these lose trust instantly.
- **YoY vs QoQ:** YoY = vs same period last year (kills seasonality); QoQ = vs last quarter (shows momentum).
- **Fold words:** doubled (×2), tripled (×3), halved (÷2). "Profit tripled" = didn't grow *by* 300%… it *became* 300% of before. Precision = credibility.

## 📘 1.7 Your 15-minute daily news ritual (non-negotiable)

```
5 min — headlines skim: 10 headlines, say the BEST one aloud in full sentences
5 min — one story deep-read (Gear 3): note 3-5 words
5 min — SPEAK: 3 sentences about that story to the mirror
        (what happened → why it matters → what next)
```

Apps to use: any — Economic Times, Moneycontrol, Mint — plus ONE YouTube business bulletin for listening. Same ritual daily for 6 weeks and colleagues will ask, *"Yaar, tumhe sab kaise pata hota hai?"*

## 🧪 LAB BN1 — X-ray a real-style article (40–50 min)

Read this article slowly, three times (skim → scan → deep read). It's written in authentic newspaper style — with real numbers from your ShopKart Finance GL dataset:

> **SHOPKART PROFIT TRIPLES ON FESTIVE DEMAND; TO OPEN 100 DARK STORES**
>
> *NEW DELHI — Online retailer ShopKart India on Tuesday reported a 252% jump in net profit for the September quarter, beating analyst estimates, as festive-season demand and its quick-commerce push lifted sales.*
>
> *Net profit rose to ₹3.85 lakh for July–September 2025, from ₹1.09 lakh a year earlier, while revenue grew 21% year-on-year to ₹71.8 lakh. For the full financial year 2025, the company posted revenue of ₹2.8 crore, up 18.5%, with net profit of ₹6.7 lakh.*
>
> *"Tier-2 cities are our growth engine now," said CEO Meera Iyer. "Standard delivery is steady, but 10-minute delivery is exploding."*
>
> *The company said it plans to open 100 new dark stores — small delivery-only warehouses — and hire 500+ staff by Diwali 2026. Analysts warn that competition from quick-commerce giants could pressure margins, but most retain a positive outlook on the stock.*

Now do this, in order:

1. **5W hunt:** copy the lead into your notes 📝 and mark Who / What / When / Where / Why. (One sentence contains all five!)
2. **Headline decode:** the headline breaks TWO compression rules from 1.4 — which? Rewrite the headline as one full, normal sentence and SAY it aloud.
3. **Number table:** list every number in the article. Convert: ₹71.8 lakh → ? million · ₹2.8 crore → ? million · ₹6.7 lakh → ? thousand.
4. **Vocabulary harvest:** find these 6 families inside the text: one movement verb, one estimate word, one economy word, one deal word ("stake"? "expansion"?). Write each in your own spoken sentence.
5. **60-second truth test:** without looking, say aloud: what happened, why it matters, what happens next. Then re-read the lead. How close were you? (This gap IS the learning.)
6. **File it:** make a note titled "News: ShopKart Q2" — date, 5 words learned, your 2-line spoken summary. This note becomes raw material for BN2's summary lab.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Decode to full normal sentences: (a) "Tata arm to buy startup for $100 mn" (b) "Mall sales jump as inflation cools" (c) "CEO: hiring freeze ends".
2. Classify these verbs UP-big / UP-small / DOWN-big / DOWN-small / FLAT: surged, slipped, soared, dipped, crashed, held steady, tumbled, climbed, plunged, was flat.
3. Convert to the "other world": ₹35 lakh, ₹12 crore, 45 million, 2.3 billion.
4. True or false, and say WHY: "Repo rate increased from 6.5% to 7% — a 0.5% increase."
5. Find today's best business headline (any app). Write: headline → full-sentence version → your one-line answer to "so what?"

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 1: (a) A Tata company is going to buy a startup for 100 million dollars. (b) Mall sales jumped in the past, because inflation has cooled. (c) The CEO said that the hiring freeze has ended.
- Ex 2: UP-big: surged, soared, tumbled? NO — tumbled is DOWN-big! Full list: UP-big = surged, soared · UP-small = climbed · DOWN-big = crashed, tumbled, plunged · DOWN-small = slipped, dipped · FLAT = held steady, was flat.
- Ex 3: ₹35 lakh = 3.5 million · ₹12 crore = 120 million · 45 million = ₹4.5 crore · 2.3 billion = ₹230 crore.
- Ex 4: FALSE — it's a **0.5 percentage point** increase, which equals a 7.7% increase in the rate itself (0.5 ÷ 6.5). Percent ≠ percentage points — the classic interview trap!

## ❓ Quiz
1. Why does reading the lead give you 80% of an article?
2. In "ShopKart to hire 500 staff", what does "to hire" really mean in time?
3. ₹2.5 crore — how much is that in millions?
4. What's safer in a professional setting: "profit went up a lot" or "net profit rose 252% to ₹3.85 lakh"? Why?

### ✅ Answers
1. The lead compresses the 5Ws (Who, What, When, Where, Why) into 1–2 sentences — everything after it is evidence and detail.
2. A FUTURE plan — "ShopKart is going to hire 500 staff." Headlines use *to + verb* for the future, present simple for the past.
3. 25 million (1 crore = 10 million; 2.5 × 10 = 25).
4. The second — specific number + direction ("rose 252%"). News speaking is earned with precise numbers said casually; vague talk sounds like gossip.
