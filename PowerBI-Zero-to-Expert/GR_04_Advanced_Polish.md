## 📘 4.1 Conditionals — the hypothetical question engine 🔀

Case-study questions run on IF. Get the three gears:

```
GEAR 1 — REAL (likely):        If + present,   will/can + base
  "If the data shows a drop, I'll investigate the cause."
GEAR 2 — IMAGINARY (now):      If + past,      would + base
  "If I were hired, I would automate the month-end pack first."
  "If I were the CFO, I'd watch margins every week."
GEAR 3 — REGRET (past):        If + had + V3,  would have + V3
  "If we had tracked vendors earlier, we would have avoided
   the penalty."
```

🔑 **The coronation rule: with "if I" for imaginary cases → "WERE" (never "was"):** "If I **were** you…" "If I **were** hired…". It's subjunctive — the fancy word you never need to say, only to USE. 😄

Mixed career conditional (gold): "If I **hadn't joined** ShopKart, I **wouldn't have learned** finance analytics — and I **wouldn't be** sitting here today." — past IF + present result. Say it; watch the eyebrows rise.

## 📘 4.2 Articles under pressure — a / an / the warfare ⚔️

The 3 rules that cover interview sentences:

| Rule | Examples in your story |
|---|---|
| **a** + consonant SOUND / **an** + vowel SOUND | a dashboard · a CFO ("see-ef" consonant!) · **an MBA** ("em"!) · **an HR round** ("aitch") · **an hour** · a university ("yoo-ni" consonant!) |
| **the** = specific/shared knowledge | **the** CFO (that specific officer) · **the** RBI · **the** report we discussed · **the** Sensex |
| **NO the** = names/fields (institutional) | Power BI (not *the* Power BI!) · Tata Steel · Delhi University (official name — but *the* University of Delhi!) · in finance · in HR |

✏️ Drill: "I did **an** MBA at Delhi University, then joined **a** startup. **The** startup became ShopKart — now **a** mid-cap player serving **the** tier-2 market."

Traps panelists catch: "I'm looking for **an** challenging role" ❌ ("ch" = consonant → **a** challenging) · "the SQL" ❌ (languages/tools take NO the) · "an USA company" ❌ ("yoo" consonant → **a** US company).

## 📘 4.3 Prepositions — small words, giant signals 🧲

Interview-critical pairs (drill to reflex):

| Verb/Adj + prep | In your mouth |
|---|---|
| work **on** a project · work **in** a team/company | "I work **on** the finance tracker **in** a 5-member team" |
| responsible **for** | "I was responsible **for** vendor reconciliation" |
| good **at** / bad **at** | "I'm good **at** simplifying data" |
| interested **in** | "I'm interested **in** finance analytics" |
| specialize **in** / focus **on** | "I want to specialize **in** finance BI" |
| report **to** | "I report **to** the finance manager" |
| depend **on** / insist **on** | "The deadline depends **on** data quality" |
| apply **for** a role / wait **for** results | "I'm applying **for** a senior analyst role" |
| listen **to** · discuss (no 'about'!) · married **to**/engaged **to** | "We discussed the migration (NOT 'discussed about'!)" |
| since **(point)** vs for **(span)** — G2 keeps earning interest! | — |

🔥 The big-3 kills: "discussed about" ❌ · "comprise of" ❌ (comprises / is composed of ✅) · "enter into the room" ❌ (enter the room).

## 📘 4.4 Reported speech — quote your research properly 📣

Panels LOVE candidates who reference homework — if their grammar holds:

```
She said (that) she was impressed.          (shifts back: is → was)
"The JD mentioned that the role involves finance dashboards."
"I read in your annual report that ShopKart plans 100 stores."
"You said earlier that the team is expanding — tell me more?"
```

**The backshift chain:** am/is → was · will → would · can → could · have → had.
"I interviewed yesterday, and the manager **said** the team **was** growing and the role **would** open in Q3." (Said she IS? = rumor-grammar!)

Perfect with: said (that)… · told me (that)… · mentioned that… · explained that… · asked whether/if… ("She asked **whether** I could join early" — if/whether for yes/no reports!).

## 📘 4.5 Passive voice — the diplomat's tool 🕊️

Passive = focus on the ACTION, blurring the actor. Use strategically:

| Situation | Active | Diplomatic passive |
|---|---|---|
| Bad news to client | "We made a data error." | "An error was found in Monday's file and corrected." |
| Process talk | "People refresh the report nightly." | "The report is refreshed nightly." |
| Credit without spam | "I built the system." → still better ACTIVE (own wins!)| use passive for SYSTEM facts: "The system was built to auto-refresh." |

**Rule:** own your achievements ACTIVE (I built, I led); soften blame/process PASSIVE. Reverse that, and you sound either slippery or arrogant.

## 📘 4.6 The formal-humble register 🎩

Modal softeners raise you to diplomatic frequency:
- **could** (ability/possibility, gentler than can): "Could you share the team structure?"
- **would** (polite wishes + hypotheticals): "I would love to contribute to the analytics team."
- **might/may** (careful predictions — the analyst's honesty): "Margins might tighten in Q4."
- **shall** (offering next steps): "Shall I walk you through the dashboard?"

⚠️ But → **humble ≠ groveling.** "I beg to state that your humble servant shall be highly obliged" = time-machine English, and panels flinch. Modern humble: short, warm, direct.

## 🧪 LAB G4 — The pressure-polish gauntlet (50 min)

1. **Conditional forge (10 min):** write + speak ONE of each gear about your career: G1 real ("If the panel asks about Python, I'll…"), G2 imaginary ("If I were offered this role, the first thing I would…"), G3 regret ("If I had started SQL earlier, I would have…"). Bonus: one mixed conditional.
2. **Article minefield (10 min):** write a 4-line self-intro packing: MBA, US client, the RBI, a university, Power BI, an HR round. Swap with a friend (or swap with your own note after 10 min!) and hunt every article.
3. **Preposition reflex (10 min):** rapid-fire fill: responsible ___ · good ___ · interested ___ · report ___ · insist ___ · discuss ___ · applies ___ (a job) · listen ___ · specialize ___ · depends ___.
4. **Reported speech (10 min):** take 3 real findings from the ShopKart blurb (module BV2!) and report them: "I read that ShopKart **was** raising…" — backshift every verb correctly.
5. **Diplomacy rewrite (10 min):** rewrite ACTIVE→PASSIVE (blame-softening) or PASSIVE→ACTIVE (ownership) as flagged: (a→P) "Finance released the payment late." (P→a) "The quarter's targets were achieved by me." (a→P) "We discovered the leak during the audit." — 2 lines each, then speak them.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Fix: "If I was the hiring manager, I would chose you." (2 fossils!)
2. Articles sprint: "I gave ___ hour-long presentation to ___ CFO of ___ US client about ___ margins." Now adding rules: which is an/"the" and why.
3. Which sentence carries reported speech correctly? (a) "She said the team is growing fast." (b) "She said the team was growing fast." (c) "She said the team was grew." — and when is (a) actually fine?
4. Soft/blame rewrite: "You sent the wrong invoice." → diplomatic passive.
5. Strange-pairs quiz: "discussed about / discussed " · "comprise of / be composed of" · "entered into the room / entered the room" — pick the survivors.

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 1: "If I **were** the hiring manager, I would **choose** you." (subjunctive were + would takes BASE verb, not V3!)
- Ex 3: (b) is textbook; (a) is fine when the fact still stands ("The team IS growing" as general truth); (c) double-past fossil — kill.
- Ex 4: "The wrong invoice was sent on [date] — I've already issued the corrected copy."
- Ex 5: discussed ✅ · is composed of (or 'comprises' — of stays away) ✅ · entered the room ✅.

## ❓ Quiz
1. Why does "if I were" beat "if I was" at the imaginary table — and which modal always partners it?
2. When is ZERO-article correct — name 3 interview-common cases.
3. Explain the backshift in: "The recruiter told me the role would open next month."
4. When is active voice WRONG in an interview — the one scenario passive is your friend?

### ✅ Answers
1. Imaginary/present conditionals use the subjunctive — historically "were" for all persons — partnering with "would + base": "If I were hired, I would…". It's the last fossil-check panels run subconsciously.
2. (a) Tool/field names as fields: "I know SQL, Power BI, finance"; (b) most company names: "I worked at Deloitte"; (c) languages & meals-ish general nouns: "in English", "over lunch". (Watch exceptions with 'the Universities/Org names': the RBI, the University of Delhi.)
3. Told (past) pushes inner verbs one step back: will→would ("would open"), IS→was. The chain says: speaking now about what was said then.
4. When delivering shared blame or bad news you must state without finger-pointing ("the file was delayed because the source system was down") — passive removes the villain, keeps the fact. Own achievements active; soften shared messes passive.
