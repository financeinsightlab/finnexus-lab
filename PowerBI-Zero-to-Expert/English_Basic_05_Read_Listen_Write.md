# BE5 · Reading, Listening & Writing: The Three Survival Skills

> *So far you BUILD English. Now you RECEIVE it (read/listen) and SHIP it (write). Three graded texts to read, a no-subtitles listening method, and the writing machine: clean sentence → clean paragraph → clean form → clean message. Your English becomes visible — and judged by it, fairly.*

## 📘 5.1 Reading — three gears, not one speed

- **Skim (gear 1)**: eyes fast, question "what is this text about?" — title + first line of each part. 30 seconds.
- **Scan (gear 2)**: hunting ONE fact (price, time, name) — don't read everything, slide down until the fact pops.
- **Read (gear 3)**: slow, full understanding — only where it matters.
Never translate word-by-word in your head (slow death!). Unknown word? **Guess from context** first — the sentence usually explains it: "Ravi was *exhausted* after working sixteen hours" → tired, finished, done ✓. Dictionary only if the word blocks the meaning of the whole sentence.

**GRADED TEXT A (Level 1) — read 2×, aloud once:**
> Mohan is a delivery driver. He works for ShopKart. Every morning he checks his list. Today he has forty-two packets. He drives to twelve areas. At seven o'clock he finishes. "Long day," he says, "but customers are happy."

Q: How many packets? What time does he finish? (Find with SCAN!)

**GRADED TEXT B (Level 2):**
> Meena opened a small tea stall near the metro station. First week, only ten customers a day. She did not lose hope. She started a "chai + biscuit" offer for twenty rupees. Office workers loved it — cheap and fast. After three months, she serves two hundred cups daily. Her secret? Same quality, every single day.

**GRADED TEXT C (Level 3):**
> Indians buy more online during festivals. In October and November, sales jump because families shop for Diwali. Companies prepare months earlier: extra stock, new offers, faster delivery. A smart analyst always checks last year's numbers before planning the next season. History doesn't repeat exactly, but it rhymes.

Try questions on B & C: main idea (one line each), 2 facts, 1 inference ("why did the offer work?" — answers hide between lines).

## 📘 5.2 Listening — no-panic method (even without audio practice partners)

**The 4 moves**:
1. **Predict**: before listening/reading conversations, guess 5 words you'll hear (shop scene → price, change, size…).
2. **Gist first**: first listen/read for "who/where/what-topic" only — permission to miss details!
3. **Chunks, not words**: catch blocks ("a cup of tea" = one block, not four words). Reading aloud helps your ear glue blocks.
4. **Shadow**: repeat right after a slow recording, copying rhythm (phone voice recorder + free clips!).

## 📘 5.3 Writing sentences that never wobble — hygiene rules

1. One sentence = one complete thought (subject + verb!).
2. Start capital, end with a full stop / ? / !
3. Long sentences lose control: 8–15 words average is power.
4. Comma = small pause; if breath runs out mid-sentence, it was too long.
5. Its vs it's: it's = it is ("It's hot."); its = belonging ("The shop lost its keys.") — swap-test with "it is".

## 📘 5.4 The paragraph skeleton 🦴

```
TOPIC SENTENCE  → the one idea ("Rohini is a great place to live.")
SUPPORT 1,2,3   → reasons/examples (parks, metro, market)
CLOSER          → small round-up or feeling ("So I never want to move.")
```

Model **self-introduction paragraph** (your most-written English text in life — own it):

> "My name is Priya Sharma. I am twenty-three years old and I come from Jaipur. I finished my B.Com last year, and now I am learning data analytics. In my free time I like cooking and watching cricket. One day I want to work as an analyst in a big company."

Swap your details, memorize shape, never blank-page again.

## 📘 5.5 Forms & messages — English that stamps you official

- **Form fields**: SURNAME (family name), FIRST NAME (given), DOB (date of birth — write 23/07/2002 Indian style unless MM/DD asked!), NATIVE PLACE, SEX (M/F), MOBILE, SIGNATURE (script name ≠ text name field!). Print vs cursive: forms usually want BLOCK CAPITALS.
- **Chat/SMS etiquette**: short forms ok with friends (u, pls, b4, gm) — NEVER in forms/official mail; first message to a senior/customer: full words + please/thank you; one emoji max in semi-formal; "Dear Sir/Madam" still standard; sign off "Regards, [Name]".
- Phone number in English: say digits slowly in pairs/zero="oh": 9811-0xx = "nine eight double one oh…"

## 📘 5.6 Proofread like a pro (the 3-pass rule)

Pass 1: meaning (does every line SAY what I meant?) · Pass 2: grammar radar (-s of he/she/it, past forms, capitals) · Pass 3: read ALOUD — the ear catches what the eye forgives. This 30-second ritual upgrades every form, message, and exam answer you'll ever write.

## 🧪 LAB BE5 — Skills triathlon (50 min)

1. Read Text A 3× (skim, scan the 2 answers, read-aloud). Write answers + the context-guess for "exhausted"-style words.
2. Read Texts B & C; for each: 1-line main idea, 2 facts, 1 inference — total 12 lines of writing (clean handwriting counts!).
3. Screenshot-to-speech: pick one pack from BE3 dialogues, have your phone read it aloud (TTS); shadow each line 2×; note 3 words where your rhythm differed.
4. Write YOUR self-intro paragraph on the skeleton; then the 3-pass proofread (list every fix you found — proof the process works!).
5. Form-fitness: copy a mini-form (Name/Surname/DOB/Mobile/Signature) and fill it in BLOCK letters, slowly, once — observe how slow correct beats fast wrong.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Guess-from-context: "The **meagre** salary forced him to look for another job." — meaning + the context clue?
2. Skim race: from Text C extract ONLY the two numbers connected to months (Oct/Nov + 200 cups? find correctly) — compare speeds scan vs read.
3. Fix writing: "its 8 pm and meena she go to market becaus she want rice and its rains outside she cant go"
4. Write chat strings to your team-lead asking leave tomorrow (one formal SMS-style), then the same to your friend.
5. Date traps: write today's date three ways (Indian numeric, words, American words) and note which is safest in official forms and why.

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 3: "It's 8 pm and Meena is going to the market because she wants rice. But it is raining outside, so she can't go." (broken into sentences, -s fixed, tense signals read!)
- Ex 5: 23/07/2026 (Indian) · 23 July 2026 / July 23, 2026 (word forms are safest — month-names can't be flipped!).

## ❓ Quiz
1. Skim vs scan — which gear, when?
2. The best first response to an unknown word while reading?
3. "Its raining" vs "It's raining" — which, why?
4. Which parts make the paragraph skeleton complete (4)?

### ✅ Answers
1. Skim = fast gist/topic; scan = hunt a specific fact; deep read only where meaning matters.
2. Guess from CONTEXT (the sentence/paragraph usually explains) — dictionary only when the word blocks overall meaning.
3. "It's raining" — it's = it is; "its" shows ownership (its keys). Swap-test with "it is" always.
4. Topic sentence → supporting details → closer (+ my clean joining sentences holding hands).
