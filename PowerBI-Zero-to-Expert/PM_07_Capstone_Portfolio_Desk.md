## 📘 Concepts

### 7.1 Anand (25) — the engine that out-works every salary he'll earn
Inputs: ₹30k/month investable, horizon 35y, zero obligations, high volatility-tolerance-proven-by-nothing-yet (BF6 says: assume less than claimed). Build: **85/10/5** — index core 85% (NIFTY50+Next50+small satellite ≤ caps), gold 5%, debt sleeve 10% (his "crash ballast" — first −30% tape WILL test him when the corpus is real money, so the seat is bought in peacetime). Settings: SIP auto on salary+2, step-up +10%/yr (IN2 6's compounding lever), rebalance hybrid (annual review, ±5pp bands — PM6 canon), direct plans only. Projection desk-truth: ₹30k/mo flat at 12% → **₹19.3Cr nominal in 35y** (₹1.26Cr contributed); stepped +10%/yr pushes toward ₹45Cr-class; at real terms (12% nominal ≈ 7% real): the flat-SIP's honest 35y-wealth is ~₹19.3Cr nominal whose 2060-purchasing-power reads ≈ ₹3.6-4Cr of today — BOTH sentences printed in his IPS, because nominal nominalism is cruelty to 60-year-old Anand. The desk's deepest line for him: his human capital IS the bond (PM4: salary = stable cash flows) — hence 85% equity is not aggression, it's accounting.

### 7.2 Meera (40) — the bucket ballet
Goals: car ₹15L in 5y; kids' UG ₹40L in 10y; retirement ₹65L corpus + ₹60k/month engine for 18y. The desk buckets FIRST (PM4): **B1 car (5y):** glide-path bucket — current ₹6L allocated into 4y-ish TMF rungs, monthly ₹15k earmarked into laddered FD/TMF, de-risked out of equity entirely by year 4; **B2 education (10y):** ₹55k/month-heavy bucket at 65/25/10 gliding 5pp/2y toward 40/50/10 by year 8 (education costs INFLATE ~8-10% — projection uses 10%, not 6% CPI-flat); **B3 retirement (18y):** engine residual at 75/15/10 with PM6 bands, annual review. The ballet's problem is always the same: ₹60k doesn't fully fund all three at full targets — desk shows the ranked waterfall (retirement legally first — kids can borrow for UG, nobody lends for retirement), the stretch plan (step-ups eating the gap by year 7 as salary rises), and the compromise numbers printed rather than silently unfunded. Her IPS carries one custom clause: the UG bucket's SUCCESS insurance (term cover sized to ₹40L+ goal — a parent's death must not de-fund a classroom: IN2 7's pyramid logic).

### 7.3 Ramesh (60) — the 50-year runway
Corpus ₹3Cr, need ₹1L/month today, inflation 6% on his basket (medical-heavy ≈ 7%), life horizon 30y (to 90, longevity priced generously). Structures: **bucket-drawdown** — B1 (y0-4): ₹48-50L in liquid/FD/TMF rungs feeding the ₹1L/month (sleep-layer, market-immune); B2 (y4-10): ₹80L-90L in conservative hybrid/TMF ladder (debt-heavy, gliding into rungs as B1 empties); B3 (y10+): residual ₹1.6-1.7Cr in equity engines (needed: at 6% inflation, his ₹1L becomes ₹1.8L/month by year 12 — only equity buys that ladder). The desk proof (nominal-flat variant): at a blended 7% drawdown-safe portfolio, ₹3Cr feeding flat ₹1L/month **runs 50+ years** (balance still standing at month 600 — the SWP machine's durability at real-blend ≈ 4% draw rule ≈ India's version of the 4% doctrine); the honest stepped version: +6%/yr steps consume faster — solved with lower starting draw (₹85k) + B3 equity share ≥45-50% + annual rebalance + 2-year cash moat (never forced to sell equity in a bear: the 2008/2020-proof moat rule: if B3 falls >25%, draws pause from equity and run on B1's extended moat — the bucket order IS the risk management). Fraud-lock: senior-specific addendum — joint viewing of statements with son/daughter, no unsolicited "senior citizen schemes" products, SEBI-RIA-only advice channel (IN2 6's checklist, gray-haired edition).

### 7.4 The three IPS one-pagers — law before life happens
Each build is sealed by signature on: allocation + sleeves (with instruments named, direct plans, TER ceilings) · rebalancing law (hybrid: annual review ±5pp + outer rails) · flow-rules (step-up schedule, bonus-in routing %) · behavioral welds (SIP pause-lock, 48h cooling, journal-first trades) · drawdown/draw-order (Ramesh's moat rule; Meera's goal-touchdown de-risk calendar; Anand's "the 10% sleeve is my −30% psychologist") · fraud-locks (verification drill, no-products-via-DM) · review calendar (June, one page, five numbers: split, XIRR-vs-plan, glide-check, fraud-scan, signatures). Six modules, three lives, one page each. That page is the desk.

### 7.5 The compromises theory can't settle — and how the desk discloses them
Real desks carry open questions: retirees' equity share (40% vs 55%: 4%-rule research vs Indian inflation-heavier basket — desk picks 45-50% + moat, disclosed as judgment); gold's seat (5% vs 10%: crisis-manners evidence vs decades of drift-drag — desk splits by quadrant, EC7); active satellites (0% vs 20%: PM3's alpha-taxes vs process-managers' grind — desk allows ≤ 1 satellite with PM5 tribunal annual re-verdict); global equity (10% vs 30%: REER-resilience vs LRS/friction/country-overlap — desk at 10-15% for horizon ≥15y). Disclosure doctrine: every compromise is printed as a RANGE with the chosen point and the trigger that would move it — clients don't buy certainty, they buy honest machinery with dated dials.
