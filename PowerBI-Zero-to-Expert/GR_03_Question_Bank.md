# G3 · The Interview Question Bank: Grammar Inside Every Answer

## 📘 3.1 STAR — the grammar of behavioral answers ⭐

"Tell me about a time when…" = **Situation → Task → Action → Result**, and each stage owns its tense:

```
S — SITUATION:  past simple, setting scene
   "Last year, our vendor payments ran 10 days late monthly."
T — TASK:       past, YOUR job in it
   "My task was to fix the delays without extra budget."
A — ACTION:     past verbs, YOUR decisions, I not we!
   "I mapped the approval chain, built a tracker in Power BI,
    and set a 48-hour reminder rule."
R — RESULT:     past + numbers (+ perfect if impact continues)
   "Delays fell from 10 days to 2 — and the tracker is still
    running today."
```

⚠️ **The I/We suspicion trap:** "we built it" leaves the panel doubting your share. Grammar fix: honestly split — "I built the tracker; my **team** tested and deployed it." Claim your verbs!

**Action-verb ammunition (past forms — drill):** mapped, identified, redesigned, automated, negotiated, reduced, recovered, launched, mentored, simplified, escalated, documented.

## 📘 3.2 The Big-15 question bank — with sentence patterns 🗄️

| # | Question | Grammar it tests | Pattern (memorize the shape) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tell me about yourself | tense timeline (G2!) | past → since → currently → going to |
| 2 | What are your strengths? | simple present + for instance | "My core strength **is** X. For instance, at ShopKart I…" |
| 3 | What's your weakness? | present continuous (improving!) | "I **used to** struggle with X, so I'm **working on** Y — I'**ve already** improved…" |
| 4 | Why should we hire you? | comparatives + conditionals | "compared to other candidates, I bring X **and** Y — **if** you hire me, you'**ll** get…" |
| 5 | Why this company? | present simple admiration + perfect research | "I've **followed** your tier-2 strategy for a year. It **aligns** with…" |
| 6 | Why did you leave your last job? | positive past, no trash-talk | "I **left** to find bigger analytics problems — I'**m grateful for** what I learned." |
| 7 | Where do you see yourself in 5 years? | see-myself-ing + will | "I **see myself leading** a small team — I'**ll have grown** into…" |
| 8 | Describe a conflict you handled | STAR + past continuous backdrop | "The quarter **was closing** when…, so I **sat down** with…" |
| 9 | Tell me about a failure | past simple + present perfect lesson | "I **failed** to… That'**s taught** me to always…" |
| 10 | How do you handle pressure? | present simple habits + when-clauses | "**When** deadlines **stack** up, I **break** work into…" |

(11–15 are the HR/finance five — salary, notice, relocation, CTC, offers: Module G5's territory!)

## 📘 3.3 Strength & weakness grammar — honest, not harmful 💪

**Strength frame — claim + evidence (no brag-adjectives):**
❌ "I am very hardworking and passionate and dedicated."
✅ "My core strength **is** translating messy data into clean decisions. For instance, at ShopKart I **turned** 48 manual reports into one live dashboard."
➡️ Grammar: "My strength is + NOUN/GERUND", evidence in past verbs, ZERO adjective-dumping.

**Weakness frame — the 3-move pattern that panels respect:**
```
1. REAL weakness (mild, fixable): "I used to hesitate before speaking up in big meetings."
2. ACTION verb of fixing: "so I started volunteering to present our weekly numbers."
3. PROGRESS in perfect tense: "I've since presented 20+ weekly reviews — it's still
   work-in-progress, but the hesitation is gone."
```

❌ Fatal frames: "My weakness is I work too hard" (panel eyeroll) · "I have no weakness" (red flag) · negative-past with no recovery ("I was bad at Excel"). Grammar rule: weakness sentences ALWAYS travel with an 'improving' verb!

## 📘 3.4 Comparatives & superlatives — the comparison engine ⚖️

Panels love "X vs Y" questions ("Excel vs Power BI?", "you vs other candidates?"). Arm the forms:

| Structure | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| comparative adj | Adj-**er** / more + adj + **than** | "SQL is **more forgiving than** DAX at first." |
| comfort frame | more comfortable **with** X **than** Y | "I'm more comfortable **with** SQL **than** Python — but I use both." |
| preference | prefer X **to** Y / rather + verb **than** | "I'd rather build dashboards **than** clean data — though cleaning pays." |
| superlative | the + Adj-**est** / the **most** + adj | "The **most valuable** skill I've built is variance analysis." |
| as…as | as X **as** | "Python isn't **as** intimidating **as** it looks." |

Trap drill: ❌ "more better", "most fastest", "better than me" (say: "**better than I (am)**" in formal panel voice) · "more senior to" → "**senior to**" (Latin comparatives take 'to': senior/junior/superior/prior).

## 📘 3.5 Self-correction superpowers 🛠️

Perfect candidates DON'T exist. Smooth recoverers do. When your sentence derails mid-answer:

- **The catch:** "I mean—" · "Sorry, let me rephrase that—" · "What I'm trying to say is—"
- **The buy-time:** "That's a great question — let me think for a second." / "Just to make sure I understood right — you're asking about…?"
- **The honest pivot:** "I haven't faced that exact case — but a similar one at ShopKart was…"

⚠️ DON'T restart the whole answer — panels stop trusting you. Correct ONLY the broken word and keep moving: "I have went— sorry, I have **gone** through that data — and it's clear that…"

## 📘 3.6 The fluency connectors 🔗

Long answers need rails or they become "umm" soup:
**Listing:** First, … Then, … Finally, …
**Contrast:** However, … / On the other hand, … / That said, …
**Cause/Because-chain:** Because of that, … / As a result, … / Which is why …
**Adding:** Also, … / On top of that, … / More importantly, …
**Ending strong:** Overall, … / The bottom line is, … / And that's the story — happy to go deeper.

## 🧪 LAB G3 — The 10-question gauntlet (60 min)

1. **STAR forge (15 min):** pick ONE real achievement (use the ShopKart tracker story from 3.1 if stuck); write S/T/A/R in 4 labeled blocks — check: S/T = past simple scene; A = 3 past VERBS starting with 'I'; R = number + present-perfect echo. 📱 Record it once, count "umm"s only.
2. **Weakness rebuild (10 min):** write your real weakness in the 3-move pattern; say it aloud 3× until the 'improving' verb sounds natural, not memorized.
3. **Comparative relay (10 min):** answer aloud (record): (a) Excel vs Power BI for finance, (b) you vs other candidates, (c) working alone vs in a team — each with a different structure from 3.4.
4. **Pattern-match drill (10 min):** write opening lines ONLY for questions 1, 2, 5, 7, 9 from the bank (use the pattern column faithfully).
5. **Self-correction demo (5 min):** deliberately break a sentence on a recording ("I am agree—"), catch with "I mean—", repair, continue WITHOUT restart. Do 3 reps.
6. **Mock set (10 min):** family/friend asks you ANY 5 from the bank in random order — you answer with patterns + connectors. Their job: only count "umm"s and note wow-moments.

## 💪 Exercises
1. In STAR, why is "I automated the tracker" stronger than "we automated the tracker"?
2. Rebuild this weakness: "My weakness is Excel is hard for me."
3. Fix the comparatives: "SQL is more easier, and I am more senior to my colleague Rohan."
4. Which connector and why: "The tool was expensive. ___ , we recovered the cost in 4 months through time savings."
5. Write a 2-line STAR-Result for the Fresh Farms payment story (module EM5!) — include the number and the perfect-tense echo.

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 1: Panels credit VERBS to owners; "we" blurs your share. Honest split works best: "I automated the tracker; the team tested it together." — your verb claimed, team kept.
- Ex 2: "I used to find advanced Excel hard, so I started a weekend practice course. I've since improved — macros still stretch me, but pivot tables are now second nature."
- Ex 3: "SQL is **easier** (or: mere easier? NO — 'much easier' ✅), and I am **senior to** my colleague Rohan."
- Ex 4: "However" — contrast between cost pain and recovered value; "As a result" would say expense caused savings (backwards!).
- Ex 5: "Delays dropped from 10 days to 2 — and the tracker **has kept** every weekly payment on time since."

## ❓ Quiz
1. Which tense does each STAR letter carry — and why does the R often end with a perfect-form echo?
2. "My weakness is I work too hard" fails on TWO levels — name them.
3. Why must weakness sentences travel with an 'improving' verb?
4. What's the grammar difference between restarting a broken answer and repairing it — and why does it matter?

### ✅ Answers
1. S/T = past simple (scene/task in finished time); A = past action verbs claimed by 'I'; R = past result + present-perfect echo to the present ("has kept the payments on time SINCE") — the echo shows living impact, the bit panels remember.
2. (a) It's a fake humility cliché — panels hear it 10× a day; (b) it gives no 'improving' arc — no signal of self-awareness or growth.
3. A naked weakness is just a confession. The improving-verb proves the meta-skill every company hires: noticing gaps and closing them.
4. Restarting wipes credibility ("the first version was wrong"); repairing edits one word in-flight ("I have went— I mean, gone—") which signals real-time quality control. Panels trust editors, not reseters.
