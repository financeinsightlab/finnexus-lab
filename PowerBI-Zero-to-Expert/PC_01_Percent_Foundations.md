# 💯 PC1 · Percent Foundations: The Base Is King

> Welcome to the single most important topic in the 🧮 Aptitude umbrella. Percentages appear directly in every exam (5–10 questions), and secretly power three more: Profit & Loss is applied percentages, Interest is growth-percentages, and DI is percentages wearing charts. Master this course and you've built the engine room of half the quant section. Module 1 sets the two laws everything else stands on: **a percent is a fraction in a suit (per 100), and every percentage question is really a question about its BASE.**

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Define percentage honestly: \"per hundred\" — a ratio with a fixed denominator of 100.
- Convert across all three costumes: **percent ↔ decimal ↔ fraction** without hesitation.
- Own **Law of the Base**: every % statement answers \"percent OF WHAT?\" — the base changes the truth.
- Use the mirror trick: **x% of y = y% of x** — the 2-second mental swap.
- Run the anchor methods (10%-1% blocks + fraction table) for calculator-free speed.

## 📘 Concepts

### 1.1 What a percent REALLY is

\"Percent\" = Latin *per centum* = per hundred. 40% means 40 per 100 = 40/100 = 0.40 = 2/5 of the thing. That's it. Every percent question is fraction arithmetic wearing a % costume. Three costume changes you must perform mid-sprint:

| Percent | Decimal | Fraction | Memory hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50% | 0.50 | 1/2 | the half |
| 25% | 0.25 | 1/4 | the quarter |
| 20% | 0.20 | 1/5 | one-fifth |
| 33.33% | 0.3333 | 1/3 | one-third |
| 12.5% | 0.125 | 1/8 | half of a quarter |
| 10% | 0.10 | 1/10 | drop a zero |
| 5% | 0.05 | 1/20 | half of 10% |

**The exam superpower: convert to the fraction, cancel early.** 12.5% of 640 = 640 ÷ 8 = **80** (vs 0.125 × 640 column work). DI1's fraction table now gets a full course to pay rent in.

### 1.2 Law of the Base (the law that runs the whole course)

Every % statement has a hidden noun: **\"percent OF WHAT?\"** That noun is the base, and changing it changes the truth:

- ShopKart's Online vertical: 66L of 280L total → **23.6% of revenue** (base = total).
- \"Online's sales grew 20% in Q4-to-Q1\" (base = Online's OWN earlier value: 22 → 26.4).
- \"Online is 22.2% ahead of KB\" (base = KB's 54: 66/54 − 1 = 22.2%).

Same store, three percentages, three different truths — all correct. **The Law:** never compute until you've circled the base noun. Exams weaponize base confusion more than any arithmetic (PC4 is a whole module about it).

### 1.3 The mirror trick: x% of y = y% of x

Because multiplication commutes: x% × y = (x/100)×y = (y/100)×x = y% × x.

- **8% of 25?** Hard. **25% of 8 = 2.** Done.
- **17% of 50?** = 50% of 17 = **8.5**.
- **36% of 25?** = 25% of 36 = **9**.

Rule of use: whenever a \"nice percent\" (50/25/20/10/5) appears on the WRONG side, swap it to the friendly side. This converts nightmare questions into smile questions.

### 1.4 The anchor methods (full arsenal recap + extension)

From DI1 — now core curriculum:

1. **10%-1% blocks:** 10% of 4,360 = 436; 1% = 43.6; build anything: 23% = 2×436 + 3×43.6 = 872 + 130.8 = **1,002.8**.
2. **5% = half of 10%; 15% = 10% + 5%; 2.5% = half of 5%.**
3. **Fraction swap:** 12.5% = ÷8; 33.33% = ÷3; 16.67% = ÷6; 14.3% = ÷7.

**FF-221 drill (our forever invoice):** 18% GST on ₹47,250 → 10%(4,725) + 5%(2,362.5) + 3%(1,417.5) = **₹8,505**; bill = ₹55,755. You computed this in DI1 — now it should cost 4 seconds, not 15.

### 1.5 Percentages bigger than 100% (kill the fear)

150% of 80 = 1.5 × 80 = 120 — \"more than 100%\" just means MORE THAN THE BASE. \"ShopKart's FY25 revenue is 118.6% of FY24's\" = grew. \"Net profit Q2-FY26 is 352% of last year's\" = 3.52× (₹3.85L vs ₹1.09L — the 252%-growth line in a different costume!). Numbers above 100% are gradients, not errors.

## 🧪 LAB — Foundation sprints (8 min, pen only)

Use ShopKart numbers: quarters Q1 58 · Q2 65 · Q3 76 · Q4 81 (₹ lakh); Online FY 66; KB FY 54.

1. Convert all three costumes: 2/5? 0.125? 240%?
2. 8% of 25; 14% of 50; 12% of 25 — all by mirror trick.
3. Q3 as % of full year (280): 76/280 — fraction short-cut hint: 76/280 = 19/70…
4. 18% GST on Q2-FY26 revenue ₹71.8L — anchor blocks only.
5. Name the BASE in each: (a) \"Q4 is 23.8% above Q2\" (b) \"Online's share is 23.6%\" (c) \"Net margin is 2.4%\".

**Answer key:** 1) 40% / 12.5% (=1/8) / 2.4 (=12/5). 2) 2 / 7 / 3 (mirror: 25% of 8; 50% of 14; 25% of 12). 3) 27.14% (76/280; ÷4 → 19/70 ≈ 27.1%). 4) 10%(7.18)+5%(3.59)+3%(2.154) = **₹12.92L**. 5) (a) Q2 (b) total revenue (c) revenue. Law of the Base obeyed.

## 💪 Exercises

1. 24% of 75 (mirror); 65% of 20; 16% of 50.
2. Write 37.5% as decimal + fraction; write 5/8 as percent.
3. ShopKart's rent share is 11% of ₹280L. Anchor-method answer?
4. \"Sales are 140% of target.\" Target was ₹50L. Sales = ? Is 140% a problem?
5. One-line explain: why is \"10% of 60 = 6\" and \"60% of 10 = 6\" the same coin?

### ✅ Selected answers

1. 25% of 24… → 24% of 75 = 75% of 24 = **18**; 65% of 20 = 20% of 65 = **13**; 16% of 50 = **8**.
2. 0.375 = 3/8; 5/8 = **62.5%** (4/8=50%, 1/8=12.5%).
3. 10% = 28 + 1% = 2.8 → **₹30.8L** ✔ (matches the expense pie — engines agree again).
4. Sales = 1.4 × 50 = **₹70L**. 140% isn't a problem — it's +40% ABOVE plan. Good kind of surprise.
5. Commutativity: (10×60)/100 = (60×10)/100. Same product, swapped costumes — the mirror trick's whole existence proof.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** \"40% of ShopKart's revenue\" — the MOST important word for solving anything downstream is the hidden one:
- (a) \"40\" — it's the number
- (b) **\"REVENUE\" — the base. Every % statement answers 'of WHAT?' and the base decides the truth; circle it before touching arithmetic**
- (c) \"%\" — the symbol
- (d) \"of\" — the connector

**Q2.** 8% of 25 is fastest solved as:
- (a) 0.08 × 25 long multiplication
- (b) **25% of 8 = 2** — the mirror trick x% of y = y% of x, swapping the friendly percent to the friendly number
- (c) 25 × 8 then ÷ 100 twice
- (d) 8 + 25 − 100

**Q3.** 240% of ₹50L means:
- (a) An error — percents cap at 100
- (b) **₹120L — over-100% just means MORE THAN ONE BASE (2.4×)**; ShopKart at \"140% of target\" is +40% over plan, not a bug
- (c) ₹24L
- (d) ₹70L

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — Law of the Base. 40% of revenue ≠ 40% of profit ≠ 40% of last year. Same number, different universe. Circle the base noun, always.
2. **(b)** — mirror trick. Any \"nice five\" percent (50/25/20/10/5) on the wrong side gets swapped. 8% of 25, 14% of 50, 65% of 20 — all one-liners.
3. **(b)** — 240% = 2.4 bases. Fear of >100% costs marks in growth/return questions (Q2-FY26 net = 352% of last year!). Above 100% is a gradient, not an error.
