# 🎯 FA1 · The Analyst's Lens — Reading Statements in the Lethal Order
> Two people read the same annual report. One starts at the glossy P&L headline and buys; the other starts at the auditor's qualifications and the notes on related parties and saves her capital. Financial statement analysis is reading **in the lethal order**: who vouches (audit), what hides (notes), then what claims (statements). FA1 installs that order, the five-signal dashboard, and the analyst's first-pass drill you'll run on every set of accounts forever after.

## 🎯 Objectives
- Read any annual report in the professional order: auditor → notes/accounting policies → MD&A → statements → ratios.
- Distinguish audit opinions (clean, qualified, EOM, disclaimer, adverse) and price their warning levels.
- Mine the three notes sections that never lie: accounting policy changes, related parties, contingent liabilities.
- Run the five-signal first pass (GM track, CFO/PAT, debt clock, CCC, auditor continuity) on ShopKart FY25.
- Draft an analyst's one-page first-read memo with verdict ranges, never verdict adjectives.

## 📘 Concepts

### 1.1 The lethal order — why P&L-first reading is amateur hour
The statements are management's OUTPUT; the notes and audit are the factory that produced them. Sequence doctrine: (1) **Auditor's report** (who vouches? deviations?), (2) **Accounting policies & changes** (estimate edits move crores quietly), (3) **Related-party + contingent-liability notes** (the confessional pages), (4) **MD&A** (management's own story — compare against numbers later), (5) **The three statements** (now read as munition, not gospel), (6) **Ratios** (FA3–FA4 compression, already prepared). Every red flag lives earlier in this order than the page where amateurs start.

### 1.2 Audit opinions — the vouch scale
- **Clean (unqualified):** statements fairly present — the green tick, worth what your auditor's reputation is worth.
- **Emphasis of Matter (EOM):** clean BUT flag rises (going-concern cloud, litigation) — read the exact paragraph; it's the whisper that ages into a headline.
- **Qualified:** 'except for…' a specific departure (inventory uncounted! revenue disputed!) — instantly: recompute what the exception does to PAT/BS before any ratio.
- **Adverse / Disclaimer:** 'statements are wrong / we couldn't verify' — analysis continues ONLY as forensic exercise; equity stance = no-position until re-audit.
**Auditor-continuity signal:** mid-term resignations (statutory auditor leaving inside tenure) are the loudest alarm in the Indian toolkit (Satyam-taught): prices often re-rate on the resignation letter, weeks before the results page.

### 1.3 The confessional pages (notes that never lie)
- **Accounting policy changes:** inventory method tweaks, depreciation life rewrites (8y → 12y halves the charge, profit 'improves' without a single extra carton sold — AC8's machinery weaponized); policy-change year = artificial YoY comparability, restate before trending.
- **Related parties:** sales/purchases/rent/loans with sister concerns at off-market prices; the honest ones disclose clean tables, the costume ones bury classified adjectives — Satyams and Yes-era stories all left footprints here first.
- **Contingent liabilities:** guarantees to siblings group (CF3's India desk), tax disputes, litigation — provisions get booked (AC9 triad: probable+measurable), contingencies only disclose; add material contingencies back to debt in your credit lens (FA6).

### 1.4 The five-signal first pass — ShopKart FY25 in 4 minutes
1. **Gross-margin track:** 31% (FY24) → 29% (FY25): −2pp asks WHY before anything else (procurement inflation vs promo-dilution).
2. **CFO/PAT conversion:** 16.95/13.25 = **1.28** ✅ healthy; this ratio is the polygraph (AC5 canon).
3. **Debt clock:** D/E 0.30, coverage 8.7× vs floors — headroom deliberate; no rate-chase detected.
4. **CCC:** 74 days stable-ish (inventory 81 eyes roll-forward); cash isn't strangling ops ✓.
5. **Auditor continuity:** same mid-tier firm 6 years, no qualifications; unremarkable — in audit-land unremarkable is the highest grade.
Verdict range: 'fundamentals investable, margin-ramp question open, forensic sweep clean' — the memo style FA7 formalizes.

### 1.5 The first-read memo discipline
One page, six blocks: identity & auditor · three-year key trends · five-signal dashboard · notes flags (policy/parties/contingents) · MD&A-vs-numbers divergence · verdict window + the three questions for management. **Adjectives are banned: every claim carries a number and a page.** You'll draft ShopKart's in the lab, and FruitCart's (doctored) in FA7.

## 🧪 LAB — First-read drill (10 min)
1. Opinion pricing: two retail chains, identical PAT ₹18L — one clean, one qualified 'inventory not physically verified.' Same P/E multiple? Compose the 2-line discount logic.
2. Page-hunt: 'profit doubled but our depreciation policy now runs vehicles 8→12 years.' Quantify the makeover: vans ₹15L, prior 1.75/yr → new charge; flag-color this change.
3. Related-party scan: sister-entity 'marketing services' billed ₹14L (pat 13.25 context) with no rate-card disclosed. Sensitive because / question to management?
4. Contingent add-back: disclosed guarantee ₹30L to a sibling, P(outflow) low per counsel. Debt-adjusted D/E for ShopKart (base 19/63.4) on the banker's side page?
5. First-read verdict frame (no adjectives) for FY24→FY25 ShopKart using the five signals: 5 numbered lines.

**Why this matters:** item 2 is the policy-rewrite makeup the boardspray smells like; 4 is how credit officers privately read 'low-debt' balance sheets.

**🔑 Lab answers:** (1) no — the qualification hits earnings-certainty: apply a certainty-discount (P/E hair-cut 20–40% or verification-before-valuation stance): 'identical P&Ls, different birth certificates — the auditioned one prices the audit, not just the audit trail' (2) new charge = (15−1)/12 = **₹1.17L vs 1.75 ⇒ +₹0.58L artificial PBT per van per year**; across a 10-vehicle fleet that's ₹5.8L ≈ 33% of PBT — makeup at fleet scale ⇒ flag **AMBER**, legitimate only with capacity evidence, else AC8's guillotine (3) sensitivity: 14L is 106% of PAT-scale spend routed via family hands ⇒ questions: rate-card vs arm's-length tenders, renewal terms, and whether profits are being tax-shopped between entities (4) interest-coverage lens unchanged; D/E-contingent = (19+30)/63.4 = **0.77** vs 0.30 — bankers run the 'debt-equivalent of guarantees' version first; so will you now (5) sample: 1.Margin −2pp unanswered till procurement page; 2.Conversion 1.28 strong; 3.Coverage 8.7× with 0.30 D/E deliberate; 4.CCC 74d, inventory leg 81 needs roll-forward; 5.Auditor 6-year clean unremarkable = highest grade — question-queue nonempty, fundamentals window OPEN.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Draft the lethal-order reading map for any company's AR with page-sections named and the reason each precedes the next (auditor→policies→parties/contingents→MD&A→statements→ratios).
2. EOM autopsy: auditor EOM on going-concern + covenant-waiver 90 days — write the analyst stance (ratios now / later? equity call style? credit call style?) in 4 lines.
3. Policy-makeup detection kit: list 4 policy edits that cosmetically lift PAT (lives, NRV, capitalization, cut-off… ) with the mechanical effect and the counter-check for each.
4. Related-party forensics mini-case: founder rents his own building to ShopKart at ₹18L/yr (market ₹10L) — PAT impact, the disclosure duty, and the investor-pricing adjustment; take both governance seats honestly (founder's defense + minority's line).
5. Contingent-to-provision boundary: disputed excise ₹8L, counsel says 60% adverse — provision or disclosure? Quantify the balance-sheet surgery if provision triggers next year (Dr who, Cr who).
6. MD&A-vs-numbers divergence memo: MD&A says 'best year ever' while margins fell 2pp and CFO/PAT 1.28 — write the respectful 3-line reconciliation note an analyst sends the IR desk.
7. First-read sprint on FruitCart Co FY25 (seeded facts: auditor new this year; GP +4pp while sector −2; related-party advances ₹12L; contingent ₹25L): build the five-signal skeleton + verdict RANGE (no adjectives).

### ✅ Selected answers
1. Map: auditor (vouch) → policies (recipe edits) → parties/contingents (confessional) → MD&A (their story, to be penalty-kicked against numbers) → statements (the claims) → ratios (compression). Each page forewarns the next: the auditor's exceptions tell you which lines to recompute; the policy page tells you which trends are artifically spliced; the confessional tells you which 'profits' are family arrangements.
2. Stance: ratios become SCENARIO art (waiver expires = insolvency trigger): equity lens — position sized to covenant-outcome binary, not fundamentals; credit lens — freeze until waiver converts to long-term cure; annotations: 'valuation suspended pending solvency-window re-verification; DSCR at waiver-expiry is the only metric currently alive.'
3. Four edits: lives 8→12y (Charge ↓, CF8-style capacity evidence demand); NRV write-down reversals (GM boost→ aging floor vs policy bible); capitalize routine spend (EBITDA-inflation → AC8's future-benefit gate); cut-off stretch (revenue of next period booked early → receivable-days + T1 sales-spot anomalies). Each has exactly one counter-check because each is exactly one machine (AC3–AC8 modules).
4. Governance seat: founder may pick any building — duty is disclosure + arm's-length benchmarking + board approval; minority line: ₹8L/yr above-market rent = 60% of PAT transferred pre-tax ⇒ value-correct via adjusted-PAT multiples only, and demand the renewal-clause publication; both seats honest: related-party is LEGAL; opacity is the crime.
5. 60% × measurable ⇒ **provision ₹8L** must be recognized (probable) — surgery: Dr Legal/Excise expense (PAT −8×0.75 ≈ −6) / Cr Provision (CL +8); then disclose the appeal status; the boundary runs on 'probable + measurable' exactly where AC9 carved it — contingencies exposé only until probability crosses.
6. 'Revenue record noted; margins compressed 2pp with opex ramp +15% — FY26 story needs store-level unit-econ page. Conversion strong (1.28), applauded. Request: site-wise GM ramp table by quarter-end — else 'best year' upgrades to 'scale year', not 'quality year'.' Adjectives zero, questions three, relationship preserved.
7. Skeleton: (1) auditor-switch amber (why mid-tenure?); (2) GM +4pp vs sector −2 = explain-or-discount (conversion check next: CFO/PAT was 0.4 — red); (3) advances ₹12L = 90% of profit-scale routed family-side (rate-cards?); (4) contingent ₹25L on NW ₹ ~30L scale — insolvency-shaped; (5) CCC presumably stretchy. Verdict WINDOW: 'uninvestable-until-cleared: audit-resignation reason + conversion + advances rate-card must all reconcile; else forensic-file.' Range talk, no adjectives.

## ❓ Quiz
1. The lethal order reads auditor → notes → MD&A → statements before ratios because:
   - (a) auditors pay better
   - (b) statements are management's OUTPUT and their factory comes first — audit exceptions tell you which lines to recompute, policy pages which trends are spliced, related-party pages which 'profits' are family arrangements; ratio-first reading prices costumes before checking bodies
   - (c) ratios are hard
2. A statutory auditor resigning mid-tenure makes a professional analyst:
   - (a) buy the dip
   - (b) treat it as the loudest alarm in the Indian toolkit — Satyam-legacy markets re-rate on the resignation letter weeks before results; equity stance = no-position until the reason reconciles with cash-and-records; analyst asks audit-committee first, chart later
   - (c) wait for results day
3. 'Depreciation life for vehicles rewritten 8→12 years' signals:
   - (a) engineering insight
   - (b) a policy makeup that halves the annual charge and lifts PBT cosmetically (₹0.58L per ₹15L van) — legitimate only with capacity evidence; without it, it's AC8's capitalization/life-estimate machinery weaponized to manufacture margin expansion the trucks never delivered
   - (c) tax mastery

### ✅ Answers
1. **(b)** — factory first, claims second, compression last; that's the entire module.
2. **(b)** — resignation letters age into headlines; by results day the discount is already priced.
3. **(b)** — lives are estimates with receipts attached; demand the receipts or discount the profit.
