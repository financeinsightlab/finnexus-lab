# BV2 · Companies & Money: Structures, Cash & Customer Words

> *"Revenue badha, profit ghat gaya" — if you can't say that in clean business English yet, this module fixes it in one sitting. Company types (Pvt Ltd to unicorn), money-in words, money-out words, ownership words, customer words — the vocabulary of every business conversation after the small talk ends.*

## 🎯 Objectives
Company structures (proprietorship → MNC) · money-IN words (revenue, profit, margin, cash flow) · money-OUT words (cost, expense, budget, burn rate) · ownership words (share, dividend, IPO) · customer words (B2B/B2C, churn) · saying Indian money numbers fluently.

## 📘 2.1 Types of companies — shape words 🏢

| Word | Meaning | Example to SAY |
|---|---|---|
| **proprietorship** | one-owner small business | "The corner kirana is a proprietorship." |
| **partnership** | two+ owners sharing risk/profit | "Two friends run a CA firm as a partnership." |
| **LLP** | partnership with limited liability | "Many design studios register as LLPs." |
| **Private Limited (Pvt Ltd)** | registered company, shares not public | "Most Indian startups are Pvt Ltd." |
| **Public Limited (Ltd)** | can offer shares to the public | "Big Indian companies are Ltd." |
| **listed company** | shares trade on stock exchange | "TCS is listed on both BSE and NSE." |
| **startup** | young, fast-growth company | "She joined a fintech startup." |
| **unicorn** | startup valued over $1 billion | "ShopKart aims to become a unicorn by 2028." |
| **MNC** (multinational) | operates in many countries | "He works for an American MNC in Gurugram." |
| **SME / MSME** | small/medium enterprise | "MSMEs are the backbone of Indian jobs." |
| **conglomerate** | group of many businesses | "Tata is India's biggest conglomerate." |

**Group words:** parent company → **subsidiary** (child company) · **headquarters (HQ)** vs **branch** · **registered office**. Say: *"ShopKart Pvt Ltd is headquartered in Bengaluru, with five branch offices."*

## 📘 2.2 Money-IN words — cash coming home 💵

| Word | Exact meaning | Trap! |
|---|---|---|
| **revenue** | total money from sales (top line) | ≠ profit! |
| **turnover** | same as revenue (UK/India usage) | not "staff leaving" in finance talk (that meaning exists in HR!) |
| **income / earnings** | profit, in reports | informal: your salary |
| **profit (net)** | what's left after ALL costs (bottom line) | the only "real" money |
| **gross profit** | sales minus cost of goods only | before office costs |
| **margin** | profit as % of revenue | "a 2.4% margin is thin but positive" |
| **loss** | costs > revenue | "posted a loss for six quarters" |
| **break-even** | revenue = costs, zero loss | "We break even at 1,000 orders/day." |
| **cash flow** | timing of money in/out | profitable companies DIE from bad cash flow! |
| **runway** | months till cash runs out | "With ₹2 crore, we have 14 months of runway." |

✏️ **The interview-grade sentence:** *"ShopKart's FY25 revenue was ₹2.8 crore, up 18.5% YoY, with a net profit of ₹6.7 lakh — a margin of about 2.4%."* (These are YOUR Finance GL numbers — own them!)

## 📘 2.3 Money-OUT words — cash going out 💸

- **cost** (what you pay to make/buy the product) · **expense / expenditure** (day-to-day spending) · **overheads** (fixed running costs: rent, salaries, electricity)
- **CapEx vs OpEx:** capital expenditure (big one-time: machines, stores) vs operating expenditure (monthly running). CFOs LOVE this pair.
- **budget** (planned spending limit: "Marketing's budget is ₹50 lakh") · **overspend / underspend**
- **investment** (money put in for future return) · **capital** (money to run/grow the business): **equity capital** (selling shares) vs **debt** (borrowing — loans)
- **funding** rounds: **seed** → **Series A** → **Series B**… · **investor** · **venture capital (VC)** · **angel investor** (rich individual backing early startups)
- **burn rate** (cash spent per month: "burning ₹20 lakh a month") — pairs with runway: **runway = cash ÷ burn rate**
- **valuation** (what the company is "worth" on paper): "raised at a ₹500 crore valuation"

## 📘 2.4 Ownership & market words 📈

- **share / stock / equity** (piece of ownership — in business English these three overlap: "She owns 10% equity")
- **shareholder** (owner of shares) · **promoter** (Indian word: founding majority owner — "the promoters hold 60%")
- **dividend** (profit share paid to shareholders) · **IPO** (Initial Public Offering — first public share sale: "ShopKart filed for an IPO")
- **market cap** (share price × total shares = market's price tag) · **ticker** (short code on screen)
- **bull / bear / rally / correction** — you already own these from BN5! Vocabulary compounds. 💪

## 📘 2.5 Customer words — who pays you 🛒

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **customer / client** | buyer (client = service buyer) | "We onboarded 3 new clients." |
| **consumer** | the person who uses the product | "Consumers love 10-minute delivery." |
| **B2B** | business sells to business | "B2B payments are slower but bigger." |
| **B2C** | business sells to consumers | "ShopKart is a B2C company." |
| **D2C** | brand sells direct, no middleman | "D2C brands skip distributors." |
| **market share** | your % of the total market | "They hold 30% market share." |
| **target audience / segment** | the group you sell to | "Tier-2 families are our target segment." |
| **churn** | customers leaving | "Churn fell to 2% — retention is up." |
| **acquisition cost (CAC)** | cost to win one customer | "CAC rose as ads got pricier." |

## 📘 2.6 Money numbers in the mouth 🗣️

- **₹12 LPA** = "twelve lakh per annum" (LPA = Indian salary unit!) · ₹50,000/month = "fifty K a month" (informal)
- packages: "an 8-figure salary" (crore level: 1,00,00,000 = 8 digits!) · "a 7-figure business" (₹10+ lakh)
- **lakh → million, crore → ten million** (from BN1 — keep drilling): ₹12 crore = "₹120 million", ₹300 crore = "₹3 billion".
- financial years: "FY25" = April 2024–March 2025 — say: "eff-why twenty-five". Half-years: H1 = Apr–Sep, H2 = Oct–Mar. Quarters: Q1 = Apr–Jun… Q4 = Jan–Mar. (India's FY ≠ calendar year — the #1 confusion in cross-country calls!)

## 🧪 LAB BV2 — Decode the funding blurb (45 min)

1. **Read this mock blurb twice (skim → deep):**
> *BENGALURU — ShopKart India Pvt Ltd, the tier-2 focused quick-commerce startup, is in talks to raise ₹80 crore in a Series A round led by a Bengaluru-based VC fund, valuing the B2C company at about ₹400 crore. ShopKart reported revenue of ₹2.8 crore in FY25 with a net profit of ₹6.7 lakh, and says its monthly burn is comfortably funded with 18 months of runway. The newly raised capital will fund CapEx for 100 dark stores and cut dependence on debt. Promoters will retain majority equity.*

2. **Highlight & classify (15 min):** every word from modules 2.1–2.4 in the blurb → sort into COMPANY / MONEY-IN / MONEY-OUT / OWNERSHIP columns in your notes 📝. (There are 12+ — hunt them all!)
3. **Number translation (10 min):** convert aloud: ₹80 crore → ? million · ₹400 crore → ? billion · ₹2.8 crore → ? million · ₹6.7 lakh → ? thousand.
4. **The 45-second explanation (10 min):** pretend a friend asks "kya hua ShopKart ko?" — explain the WHOLE news in spoken business English using at least: Series A, valuation, runway, CapEx, debt, equity. Record it 📱.
5. **Truth-check:** re-read the blurb — did your version keep the numbers right? Fix and re-record once. That comparison is the lab's real teacher.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Revenue or profit? "Sales totalled ₹12 crore but after costs we kept ₹40 lakh." Label both numbers.
2. Match: unicorn / conglomerate / subsidiary / MNC ↔ (a) many businesses under one group (b) child company (c) operates in many countries (d) $1B+ startup.
3. A company spends ₹15 lakh/month and has ₹1.8 crore in the bank. Compute + SAY the runway in a full sentence.
4. Sort into B2B / B2C / D2C: a flour mill supplying bakeries · ShopKart app · a mattress brand selling only on its own website.
5. Say these in smooth spoken English: FY26, H2, ₹75 LPA?? (careful!) · "an 8-figure package" · ₹120 crore.

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 1: ₹12 crore = revenue (top line); ₹40 lakh = net profit (bottom line). Margin ≈ 3.3%.
- Ex 2: unicorn=(d), conglomerate=(a), subsidiary=(b), MNC=(c).
- Ex 3: "With a burn rate of about fifteen lakh a month and one-point-eight crore in the bank, the company has roughly twelve months of runway."
- Ex 4: flour mill=B2B · ShopKart=B2C · mattress brand=D2C.
- Ex 5: "eff-why twenty-six" · "aitch-two" · ₹75 LPA = "seventy-five lakh per annum" (a billionaire's salary — say it dreaming 😄) · "eight-figure" = ₹1 crore+ · ₹120 crore = "₹1.2 billion".

## ❓ Quiz
1. A shopkeeper says "my turnover is ₹1 crore". Why can't you assume he's rich?
2. What's the difference between equity capital and debt — and why do founders care?
3. Why do VCs ask about burn rate AND runway together?
4. "ShopKart is a B2C company" — decode B2C and give the opposite.

### ✅ Answers
1. Turnover = total sales only. Cost of goods, rent, salaries come out of it — profit could be tiny or even negative. Always ask: "and the margin?"
2. Equity = selling ownership (no repayment, but shared control/profits); debt = borrowing (keep ownership, but EMI + interest must be paid even in bad months). Founders balance control vs risk.
3. Burn = speed of cash going out; runway = how long the cash lasts. Together they answer the only question that matters for a startup: "how long can you survive without new money?"
4. B2C = Business to Consumer (sells to people). Opposite: B2B = Business to Business (sells to other companies).
