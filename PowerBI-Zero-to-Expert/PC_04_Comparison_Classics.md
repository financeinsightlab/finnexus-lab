# ⚖️ PC4 · Comparison Classics & Applied %

> Now percentages collide with people, prices and votes. These are the \"classic families\" every exam recycles: comparison flips (\"A is 25% more than B — so B is ?% less than A\"), elections won by vote-margins, sugar-price-rise consumption cuts, pass/fail mark gaps, and salary-split chains. Each family has a one-line engine — and they ALL run on PC1's Law of the Base. Learn the engines today, and these stop being question types and start being free marks.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Flip comparisons correctly: **x% more than = (100x/(100+x))% less than** — different bases, different numbers.
- Run **election math**: margin = share gap of total.
- Use the **consumption-cut rule**: price +x% → cut x/(100+x) to hold spend flat.
- Solve **pass/fail & marks-gap** questions.
- Split salaries into **expense/savings chains** with multipliers.

## 📘 Concepts

### 4.1 The comparison flip (the course's most-tested one-liner)

ShopKart Q2-FY26: Online 21.4L vs KB 14.83L (DI6 caselet numbers!). 

- \"Online is MORE than KB by ___%\" → base KB: (21.4 − 14.83)/14.83 = **+44.3%**.
- \"KB is LESS than Online by ___%\" → base Online: (21.4 − 14.83)/21.4 = **−30.7%**.

**Same gap, different numbers — bases!** The conversion pair (memorize as one family):

```
A is x% MORE than B   →   B is [100x ÷ (100 + x)]% LESS than A
A is x% LESS than B   →   B is [100x ÷ (100 − x)]% MORE than A
```

x = 25% more ↦ 20% less (÷1.25 = 0.8); 50% more ↦ 33.33% less; 100% more (double!) ↦ 50% less. The mirror never lies.

### 4.2 Election math (margin = gap × total)

**Classic:** \"A got 40% of valid votes and lost by 300 votes. Total valid votes?\"
B got 60% (only two candidates, no NOTA) → gap = 60 − 40 = **20% of total = 300 → total = 1,500** ✔. Engine: find the SHARE-GAP, divide the vote-gap by it. Invalid votes variant: apply valid-% to stated total first (\"2% invalid on 2,000 polled → 1,960 valid, then split\"). ShopKart office poll demo: canteen vendor vote — Team Kirana 55% vs Team QComm 45%, won by 24 votes → 10% gap = 24 → **240 votes polled**.

### 4.3 The consumption-cut rule (price up, budget flat)

Sugar price rises 25%. To keep the monthly bill unchanged, consumption must drop by **x/(100+x) = 25/125 = 20%**.
Proof by multiplier: price ×1.25, want total ×1 → quantity ×(1/1.25) = ×0.8 → **−20%** ✔.
Family table: +20% → −16.67% · +33.33% → −25% · +50% → −33.33% · +100% → −50%.
**ShopKart version:** Fresh Farms dairy input +10% — to hold the ₹-COGS line without a shelf-price hike, procurement must cut quantity/wastage by 10/110 = **9.09%** (or renegotiate — capstone material!).

### 4.4 Pass/fail marks-gap (education classics)

**Classic:** \"A student needs 40% to pass. He got 178 marks and failed by 22. Maximum marks?\"
178 + 22 = 200 = 40% of max → max = 200 ÷ 0.4 = **500**.
Engine: pass-mark = score + shortfall; then divide by pass-% . Double-fail variant: \"A scores 30%, fails by 20; B scores 42%, fails by 32\" → gap 12% = 12 marks → 1% = 1 → pass-mark… max = 100? → wait: 12% = 12 marks → 100% = 100 max. Pass% via A: 30 + … A failed by 20 → pass mark = 30%×100 + 20 = 50 ✔ (check B: 42 + 8? — B failed by 32: 42+32 = 74 ≠ 50?! — inconsistent numbers = read carefully; real exam data always reconciles. Lesson: cross-verify both candidates' data — if it doesn't reconcile, re-read the question, BR4 discipline).

### 4.5 Salary-spend chains (expense → savings)

**Classic:** \"Meena spends 65% of salary on household, 20% of the REST on kids' fees, saves the rest. Savings = ₹14,000. Salary?\"
Rest after household = 35%; fees = 20% × 35% = 7%; savings = 35 − 7 = **28% of salary = 14,000 → salary = ₹50,000** ✔.
Engine: **\"of the REST\" = multiply the surviving share**: survivors chain 0.35 × 0.8 = 0.28. Percent-of-percent = multiply — the family cousin of successive chains.

5. Rakesh draws ₹1.2L/month: 55% household, 50% of REST invested, rest kept liquid. Liquid ₹ ?

## 💪 Exercises

1. \"Q4 (81) vs Q3 (76)\" — Q4 is MORE than Q3 by ?% and Q3 is LESS than Q4 by ?% (two decimals fine).
2. Election: winner 62%, loser 38%, margin 12,000 votes → total?
3. Atta price +33.33%; flat-budget cut = ?% (fraction method: ×3/4 quantity).
4. Double-statement marks question: \"A at 25% fails by 30; pass-mark = 40% of max.\" Find max, then A's score.
5. Priya's bonus ₹60,000: 40% to savings, 35% of REST to gifts, rest on a course. Course budget?

### ✅ Selected answers

1. 5/76 = **+6.58%** (Q4 more); 5/81 = **−6.17%** (Q3 less). Same 5L, different costume — drill the flip till boring.
2. gap 24% = 12,000 → **50,000 votes**.
3. 33.33/133.33 = **25%** (×0.75 = −1/4 ✔ fraction table paying rent).
4. A's score = 25%max − … A failed by 30 → pass = 25%max + 30 = 40%max → 15%max = 30 → **max = 200**; A scored **50**.
5. REST = 60%; gifts = 35% × 60% = 21%; course = 0.6 × 0.65 = 39% × 60,000 = **₹23,400** ✔.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** \"Online sales are 44.3% MORE than KB's.\" Then \"KB is ___% LESS than Online\" equals:
- (a) 44.3% — same gap!
- (b) **30.7% — the gap divided by ONLINE now, the bigger base; the flip-formula 100x/(100+x) turns +44.3 into −30.7; bases change answers**
- (c) 55.7%
- (d) 22.15%

**Q2.** A candidate won 58% of votes, opponent 42%, margin = 64 votes. Total valid votes:
- (a) 320
- (b) **400 — the margin is the SHARE-GAP of the total: (58−42)% = 16% = 64 votes, so total = 64 ÷ 0.16 = 400**
- (c) 640
- (d) 580

**Q3.** Rice price jumps +25%. To keep the household bill EXACTLY flat, consumption must drop:
- (a) 25%
- (b) **20% — the consumption-cut rule x/(100+x) = 25/125; price ×1.25 needs quantity ×0.8 to land the product back at 1**
- (c) 12.5%
- (d) 33.33%

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — the flip. MORE-than divides by the smaller base (KB 14.83); LESS-than by the larger (Online 21.4). One gap, two percentages — the Law of the Base with a salary on it.
2. **(b)** — margin-of-votes = gap-of-shares × total. Find the gap first; total follows in one division. Election math is share-subtraction, nothing deeper.
3. **(b)** — ×1.25 price × 0.8 quantity = ×1.00 bill. x/(100+x) memorized as the cut; 100x/(100−x) memorized as the raise. Two plugs, one family.
