# 🎯 RR7 · Contract Assets, Liabilities & Costs — The Balance-Sheet Side of Revenue

> Step 5 moves revenue in time; the difference between revenue and billing has to LIVE somewhere. This module names the homes: receivable (unconditional), contract asset (conditional claim), contract liability (paid-by-customer, owed-by-you) — and then the cost side: which contract-winning and contract-fulfilling costs become assets vs expenses. By the end, the "contract balances" note of any annual report will read like a dashboard, and the telecom contract asset from RR5 will feel like an old friend.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Distinguish receivable, contract asset, and contract liability with the conditionality knife
- Apply the presentation and offset rules for contract balances
- Run impairment of contract assets under the ECL engine (the IA7 crossover)
- Capitalize incremental costs to OBTAIN a contract — and the 12-month practical expedient
- Capitalize costs to FULFIL and amortize both asset species systematically

## 📘 Concepts

### 7.1 The conditionality knife — three homes

| Instrument | Definition | Test to pass |
|---|---|---|
| **Receivable** | Unconditional right to consideration — only the passage of time is required before payment | billing done, nothing more to do |
| **Contract asset** | Right to consideration CONDITIONAL on something other than time — future performance, milestones, retention conditions | performed, but billing gated on more work or acceptance |
| **Contract liability** | Obligation to transfer goods/services for which consideration is received (or due) | customer paid first |

The knife is conditionality-vs-time. The telecom canon: delivery-day revenue ₹12,000 against ₹0 billed → **contract asset ₹12,000** (right conditioned on staying on plan/billing events); it unwinds into receivables as monthly bills catch up. Retentions in construction (5-10% held until defect-liability ends) are contract assets until the liability period clears. Prepaid annual SaaS (₹12L paid, ₹1L/month recognized) → **contract liability ₹12L bleeding down**. A single contract presents NET: offset obligations and rights within ONE contract to a single position, never both an asset and liability on the same deal.

### 7.2 Impairment — ECL rides here too (the IA7 crossover)

Contract assets are financial-asset-flavored claims: **impair them under Ind AS 109's ECL** (simplified/lifetime matrix for the general corporate book). The professional combo: credit risk lives in ECL; performance risk (will you complete the gating obligations?) lives in revenue measurement via onerous-contract lenses. A receivable rots when the customer can't pay; a contract asset ALSO rots when you can't perform what conditions the billing — auditors test both threads, and conflating them into one "bad debt" bucket is the classic internal-control finding.

### 7.3 Costs to OBTAIN a contract — the incremental knife

**Incremental costs of obtaining a contract — commissions, success bonuses — capitalize if you expect to recover them.** Knife-tests: would the cost vanish if the contract hadn't been won? Bid costs, travel, legal reviews, salaries of sales staff happen either way → expense. The 5% success commission that exists only because the deal signed → asset. **Practical expedient: if the amortization period would be 12 months or less, you may expense as incurred** — most companies expense everything sub-annual and capitalize the juicy multi-year ones (telecom dealer commissions on 24-month plans are the canon: capitalize, amortize over the expected relationship life INCLUDING expected renewals when the commission links to them).

### 7.4 Costs to FULFIL — capitalize on three criteria

Non-inventory, non-PPE, non-intangible fulfillment costs (those live in their own standards) capitalize when ALL THREE: (1) relate directly to a contract/anticipated contract (direct labor, materials, allocations); (2) generate or enhance resources used to satisfy obligations in the future; (3) are expected to be recovered from the contract price inside margin. The gate's enemies are general admin, wasted inputs, and costs of already-transferred performance — those expense immediately even on the same deal. Setup activities that aren't separate POs (RR3's onboarding) can ride this asset when the three criteria pass, amortized over the service life.

### 7.5 Amortization & impairment of cost assets

Both asset species (obtain + fulfil) amortize on a **systematic basis consistent with the transfer pattern** of the related goods/services — straight over the expected service period by default; pattern-matched where transfer curves. Impairment when carrying amount exceeds the remaining margin the contract will still yield (remaining consideration − un-run costs) — and reversals are allowed when the margin recovers. Disclosures: closing balances by category and period amortization/impairment — the note where you see a company's commission policy naked.

## 🧪 LAB — Place the Positions (10 min)

1. **Telem**: handset revenue ₹12,000 booked at delivery; bills ₹500/month from month 1. Contract position at delivery and at month 6 (assume ₹500/mo recognized service revenue on separate line)?
2. **BuildCo**: billed ₹4.2cr against cumulative revenue ₹4.0cr; customer also prepaid ₹30L mobilization on a SECOND contract not yet started. Positions?
3. **SalesCo**: signs 3-year ₹60L AMC collection; pays 6% commission day 1 to the dealer who sourced it. Capitalize or expense, and the annual flow?
4. **FitCo**: gym-chain onboarding crew spends ₹8L setting up a 3-year corporate account; crew would otherwise be idle. Criteria verdict?
5. **ConsultQ**: unbilled project work ₹18L conditioned on milestone certification next month; client in news for rating downgrades. Which engine assesses what?

**Why this matters:** position-placing at speed is the skill that turns revenue knowledge into balance-sheet fluency — and it's the one auditors actually test.

**🔑 Lab answers:**
1. Delivery: **contract asset ₹12,000**. Each monthly bill swaps a slice into receivables: month-6 contract asset ≈ 12,000 − 6×500 = **₹9,000** (unwind via billing, exactly as invoiced).
2. Contract 1 nets to **contract liability ₹0.2cr** (billing ahead of revenue); contract 2 = **contract liability ₹30L** — separate contracts can net internally but not against each other; report two liabilities here.
3. **Capitalize** — incremental (dies if unsourced) + recoverable across a 3-year recoverable stream: asset ₹3.6L amortized ₹1.2L/yr over the AMC life. (If term had been 10 months, the expedient would permit expensing.)
4. **Fails criterion (2)-shaped scrutiny**: setup crew cost doesn't generate/enhance resources to satisfy FUTURE performance when onboarding isn't a PO — expense as incurred; the RR9 canon rides the fee into the service stream instead of the cost into an asset.
5. Two engines, two threads: **ECL (Ind AS 109)** prices customer credit risk on the ₹18L contract asset; **revenue measurement (performance risk)** keeps the ₹18L unbilled until certification — audit separately, disclose separately.

## 💪 Exercises

1. Why must receivables and contract assets sit in separate lines if both are "money owed"?
2. A dealer-commission model pays ₹4,000 on plan-signing (24-month plan, historical renewal rate 50% into a second term). Sketch the capitalization verdict with the renewal wrinkle.
3. Site rework ₹9L from your own scheduling failure, capitalized "into the job" by the PM. Rule, with the gate.
4. A single contract billed ₹1.1cr, revenue recognized ₹0.9cr — and separately a contract asset ₹0.15cr mooted by the junior. Net presentation?
5. Where does an expected-fulfilment-cost impairment reverse, and why is that asymmetry (vs goodwill, IA11) tolerated?

### ✅ Selected answers

1. Conditionality is risk information: receivables carry only customer-credit risk; contract assets also carry performance/milestone risk. Lumping them hides which thread your ₹crore of claims depends on — and ECL+risk note users price the threads differently.
2. Capitalize ₹4,000 amortized over the EXPECTED relationship life — including the 50%-renewal second term when the commission's economics extend there (policy consistency required): amortization pool ≈ 24 + 12 expected months → ₹4,000/36 ≈ ₹111/month. If commissions are re-paid on each renewal, match each payment to its own term instead.
3. Expense immediately: unexpected-inefficiency costs fail the fulfilment gate (they enhance nothing; they are the absence of resource) — and padding the asset overstates both assets and margin. Audit finding + reversal required.
4. NET within the single contract: billings lead revenue by ₹0.2cr → single **contract liability ₹0.2cr**; the junior's separate asset-for-the-same-contract double-counts the position — never both sides on one deal.
5. Reversal goes to P&L (up to the original carrying amount) when the contract's remaining margin recovers — tolerated because cost assets are contract-margin instruments (recoverability floats with real margin), unlike goodwill, whose reversal games the IA11 ban closed permanently.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** The single test separating receivable from contract asset is:
(a) whether the invoice has been printed
(b) whether the right to consideration is UNCONDITIONAL — receivables wait only on time; contract assets are conditioned on future performance or milestones
(c) whether the customer dispute risk is high
(d) whether the contract is over one year

**Q2.** A 5% success commission on a 3-year contract is:
(a) always expensed as sales cost
(b) capitalized as an incremental cost of obtaining the contract and amortized over the expected relationship period — it would not exist but for the win
(c) netted against the transaction price
(d) capitalized only if the customer is a related party

**Q3.** Fulfilment costs capitalize when:
(a) they exceed ₹10L
(b) all three tests pass: direct relation to the contract, resource generation/enhancement for future performance, and expected recovery — wasted inputs and finished-performance costs never qualify
(c) the CFO certifies them
(d) the contract is long-term only

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — conditionality vs time is the knife; risk profiles follow the split.
2. **(b)** — the obtain-asset mechanics; the 12-month expedient is the only legitimate escape to expensing.
3. **(b)** — the three-test gate: relate, enhance, recover — in that order, on the same cost.
