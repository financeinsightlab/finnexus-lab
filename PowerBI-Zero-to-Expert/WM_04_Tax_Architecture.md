# 🎯 WM4 · Tax Architecture — The Legal Lakhs

> Tax is the largest single expense of your working life — bigger than rent, bigger than food — and it is also the only expense with a legal, published discount menu. Wealth managers treat the Income Tax Act as a product catalog: 80C, 80CCD(1B), 80D, HRA, capital-gains rules — this module teaches you to shop it once a year, in order, forever.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Choose old vs new regime with arithmetic, not folklore (the ₹15L case: ₹97,500 vs ₹1,95,000)
- Run the order of operations: 80C → 80CCD(1B) → 80D → HRA/home-loan
- Operate capital gains machinery: STCG 20% / LTCG 12.5% / ₹1.25L exemption (IN2 canon)
- Harvest losses and time redemptions like a desk
- Build the integrated April-March tax calendar (TLH March, regime re-check each year)

## 📘 Concepts

### 4.1 Regime choice — run the number, skip the debate

**New regime (FY2025-26 style):** slabs 0-4% nil up to ₹4L, then 5/10/15/20/25/30%; ₹75k standard deduction; **rebate 87A to ₹12L taxable → salaried up to ₹12.75L pay ~zero.** Old regime: 5/20/30% slabs with deductions (80C, HRA, 80D, home-loan interest). The ₹15L canon: NEW = ₹97,500 (75k std ded, no other breaks needed); OLD with ₹2L deductions = ₹1,95,000. **New wins by ~₹97.5k at ₹15L — and old only catches up when deductions exceed roughly ₹3.5-4L combined** (big HRA + big home-loan interest families). The professional move: **compute both every April** (salary changes, rent changes — the winner flips; it's a 10-minute spreadsheet, not a worldview). *Rules change at every budget — re-verify with current tables or a CA.*

### 4.2 The order of operations — where the menu money is

1. **80C ₹1.5L** — EPF (auto), PPF, ELSS, term premium, principal part of home EMI. At 30% slab + 4% cess: **saves ₹46,800/yr**. (Children's tuition counts here; don't double-count what's automatic.)
2. **80CCD(1B) ₹50k NPS** — stacks ON TOP of 80C: **+₹15,600 saved**. Combined 80C+NPS = **₹62,400/yr at 30% slab** — a free ₹5,200/month, forever, from two lines in a form.
3. **80D health premiums** — ₹25k self+family (+₹50k parents 60+): saves ₹7.8k-23k — and triggers the WM5 cover conversation anyway.
4. **HRA (old regime)** — least-of-three: actual HRA received; rent − 10% basic; 50%/40% basic (metro/other). Home-loan interest ₹2L (self-occupied, old regime) + principal under 80C.

The order matters because Indians chronically claim (1) wrongly (ignoring EPF auto-fill) and skip (2) entirely. ₹62,400/yr at 12% for 25y = factor 1,893-ish monthly equivalent ≈ **₹1.0-1.1Cr of retirement bought with paperwork** (dose ₹5,200/mo × 1,893 = ₹98.4L). The Act is paying a 12% of salary pension to anyone who reads it.

### 4.3 Capital gains machinery — the equity side (IN2 5 canon)

- **Equity (listed/MF-equity):** STCG (<12m) 20%; LTCG (>12m) **12.5% above ₹1.25L/yr** — the exemption renews annually, so *laddering redemptions across March-April* banks 2× harvested gains.
- **Debt funds (post-Apr-2023 buys):** slab rate, no indexation — hence WM3's VPF/PPF-first rule for taxed debt.
- **Gold:** SGB held to maturity = tax-free; ETF/physical = LTCG 12.5% after 12m (post-2024 rules) without indexation.
- **Property:** holding 24m+ = LTCG 12.5% (no indexation choice for post-July-2024 sales), else slab; Section 54 reinvestment escape exists.

### 4.4 Tax-loss harvesting — the desk move, domesticated

Losses combine against gains (short-term losses against both STCG/LTCG; long-term only against LTCG). Desk drill: on a March review, (1) sell losers to book losses → offset booked gains → rebuy if the thesis lives (watch wash-sale caveats — India has no explicit wash-sale rule, but SEBI frowns on artificial losses; keep genuine redeployment logic); (2) **harvest gains within the ₹1.25L LTCG exemption** annually ("reset-and-rebuy the winners"): a ₹10L equity book with ₹1.25L embedded gains harvested yearly saves ~₹15.6k/yr (12.5%×1.25L) — ₹15.6k SIP over 25y at 12% = ~₹30L. TLH hygiene: run it inside the March window with settlement-days care (T+1 canon), and never let the tax tail wag the allocation dog (WM2 bands rule over tax optimization).

### 4.5 The integrated calendar — run it like month-end close

**April:** regime choice re-run. **June/Sept/Dec:** advance-tax checkpoints (liability > ₹10k). **January:** 80C/NPS gap-fill while choices are open (ELSS vs PPF per lock-in taste). **March:** TLH + LTCG-exemption harvest + final proof submissions. Household CFO bonus: keep one **tax log sheet** (sale date, cost, STT-paid notes for grandfathered units) — future-you, filing ITR-2 at 11pm, will send gratitude. Tax is a process with deadlines, and the deadline discipline from CF8 (month-end close) is the exact muscle.

## 🧪 LAB — The Menon Family Tax Pass (10 min)

**Setup:** Ananya Menon, 38 (30% slab), gross salary ₹42L, rent ₹35k/mo, HRA component ₹15L/yr, home loan (self-occupied flat she rents out — so not self-occupied for tax; interest ₹1.9L), parents 65 & 69 (health premium ₹48k), own family floater ₹18k. Investments: EPF ₹1.8L/yr (basic-linked), ELSS ₹50k, NPS ₹0, equity MF redemptions planned worth ₹9L LTCG over two years.

**Do this:**
1. Estimate deductions under OLD regime: 80C, 80D, HRA (basic ₹18L — least-of-three: HRA received ₹15L; rent − 10% basic = 4.2 − 1.8 = ₹2.4L; 50% basic = ₹9L), home interest (let-out: full ₹1.9L). Sum ≈ ?
2. Compare: her old-regime taxable base vs new regime (std ded ₹75k, no deductions). Quick verdict at ₹42L.
3. Fix the NPS gap and quantify the two-year benefit at 30% slab.
4. Design the LTCG landing: ₹9L gains over two years — how should redemptions be sequenced around the ₹1.25L exemption? Savings delivered?
5. Write her April-July tax calendar entries.

**Why this matters:** ₹42L earners are precisely where tax planning pays the most and is done the worst — the salary is big enough to hurt in the 30% band and usually disorganized. Ten minutes here saves more than a year of fund-picking alpha dreamwork; Ananya's lab total runs into lakhs per year of legal savings.

**🔑 Lab answers:**
1. 80C = min(1.8 EPF + 0.5 ELSS, 1.5) = **₹1.5L**; 80D = 18k + min(48k, 50k) = **₹66k**; HRA = min(15L, 2.4L, 9L) = **₹2.4L**; home interest (let-out) **₹1.9L**. Sum ≈ **₹6.56L** of deductions — an unusually deduction-rich profile.
2. OLD taxable ≈ 42 − 0.5(std old) − 6.56 ≈ **₹34.9L**; NEW taxable = 42 − 0.75 = **₹41.25L**. At ₹42L, her deduction stack (₹6.56L) far exceeds the ~₹3.5-4L break-even → **OLD regime wins here** (HRA + let-out interest being the heavy lifters). Lesson locked: the regime winner flips with the PROFILE, not the ideology — she recomputes every April.
3. NPS ₹50k (80CCD1B) → saves **₹15,600/yr** (30% + cess). Two years = ₹31,200; compounded at 12% for 22 years ≈ ₹1.7L+. It's the single most-skipped line on the menu among the 42-lakh crowd.
4. ₹9L LTCG plan: redeem ~₹1.25L-worth of gains by March 31 (tax-free), remaining ₹7.75L in April (new-year exemption covers ₹1.25L again; pay 12.5% on ₹6.5L ≈ **₹81k** vs ₹1.13L if dumped in one year). Savings ≈ **₹31k via one calendar maneuver** — plus TLH offsets if March delivers any losers.
5. April 15: regime recomputation file (both scenarios, one spreadsheet). April 30: NPS first ₹25k tranche. June 15: advance-tax estimate (rental income + redemptions push liability > ₹10k). July 31: ITR filed early, AIS cross-checked against her tax log sheet — filing early is the cheapest audit-repellent there is.

## 💪 Exercises

1. **Regime mirror.** Rishi: ₹18L gross, no HRA (own city), deductions only 80C. Regime choice with quick math (new: 18 − 0.75 = 17.25 taxable ≈ ₹1.62L tax approx; old: 18 − 0.5 − 1.5 = 16L ≈ ₹2.4L+ tax).
2. **Order ops.** List the savings at 30% slab for full utilization: 80C ₹1.5L, NPS ₹50k, 80D ₹25k+₹50k(parents senior). Total annual saving?
3. **TLH scenario.** Equity book: ₹80k unrealized gain (winner, 2y) and ₹45k unrealized loss (thesis intact). It's March 20. The desk play and the tax saved (LTCG 12.5%).
4. **Grandfather trap.** Units bought Jan-2018 at NAV ₹100 (grandfathered FMV ₹120), sold recently at ₹300. Cost basis for LTCG? Why does the log sheet matter?
5. **Advance-tax tripwire.** Freelancer, estimated year tax ₹1.1L — what must happen by which dates, and what's the penalty logic in one line?

### ✅ Selected answers

1. **New wins** even without HRA: ₹1.62L vs ~₹2.4L — because old-regime slabs (20% above ₹5L) bite mid-income hard; his ₹1.5L of 80C can't overcome the slab-structure gap until deductions cross ~₹4L+. Run both every April regardless.
2. 80C ₹46,800 + NPS ₹15,600 + 80D (₹25k → ₹7,800) + parents senior (₹50k → ₹15,600) = **₹85,800/yr** — the Act is subsidizing a full premium smartphone-class gift to your family every year. Claim hygiene: the parents' 80D needs *their* premium paid by YOU, in non-cash mode.
3. Realize the ₹45k loss against ₹80k gains → net taxable gain ₹35k, tax ₹4.4k vs ₹10k unharvested → **saved ~₹5.6k**, redeploy proceeds to keep the allocation (thesis intact → rebuy or substitute a similar fund). March-window + T+1 settlement: sell by the 27th-ish to settle within the FY.
4. Cost basis = max(actual cost, FMV 31-Jan-2018) = **₹120** → taxable gain ₹180/unit, not ₹200. The log sheet stores the purchase date + FMV evidence: one cell deciding ₹20 of taxable gain per unit — per lakh units, ₹20,000 of truth.
5. Advance tax in four tranches (June 15: 15%, Sept 15: 45%, Dec 15: 75%, March 15: 100%); penal interest 1%/month under 234B/234C for shortfalls — i.e., the government charges card-like rates for tardy citizens. The desk summary: estimate quarterly, pay on the 15ths, sleep properly.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** At ₹15L gross with modest deductions, the FY2025-26 arithmetic verdict is:
(a) Old regime — always better for everyone
(b) New regime: ₹97,500 vs ~₹1,95,000 old (₹2L deductions) — and old only competes when deductions cross ~₹3.5-4L of HRA + interest + 80C stacks
(c) Regime choice doesn't matter
(d) Pay the portfolio manager to decide

**Q2.** Full menu utilization at the 30% slab (80C ₹1.5L + NPS ₹50k) delivers per year:
(a) ₹10,000
(b) ₹62,400 — ₹46,800 + ₹15,600; ≈ ₹5,200/month free, forever, for reading two lines
(c) ₹2,00,000
(d) Nothing above inflation

**Q3.** The correct March habit with ₹80k gains + ₹45k losses is:
(a) Sell nothing — taxes are for July
(b) Realize losses against gains, redeploy per allocation, and separately bank the ₹1.25L LTCG-exemption harvest before the 31st, with T+1 settlement respected
(c) Sell everything to be safe
(d) Wait for profits to grow

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — the new regime's slab geometry + ₹75k standard deduction + rebate beats thin deduction stacks; fat HRA/interest profiles (Ananya's ₹6.56L) flip it back. It's arithmetic per April, not identity: spreadsheet, sign, move on.
2. **(b)** — 1.5L × 31.2% = 46,800; 0.5L × 31.2% = 15,600 (30% slab + 4% cess). Compounded at 12%/25y the habit approaches a crore; the Act is a product catalog for people willing to fill forms.
3. **(b)** — TLH converts a paper loss into ~₹5.6k of real tax saved *without* changing the portfolio's exposure, and the annual ₹1.25L exemption is use-it-or-lose-it — March 31 resets it, T+1 decides which year a trade lands in. Tax alpha is the only alpha that's contractual.
