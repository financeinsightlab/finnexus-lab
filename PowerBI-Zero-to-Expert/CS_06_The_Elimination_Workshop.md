# 🎯 CS6 · The Elimination Workshop — Where the Group Stops Lying to Itself

> This is the module the whole course exists for: the cleanup forge. Every deal between skins of the creature — parent selling goods to sub, sub lending cash to parent, sub paying parent dividends, skins trading machines at invented prices — is FAKE MOTION from the creature's seat: wealth never crossed the boundary between creature and world. The elimination workshop erases all of it: balances, transactions, dividends, and the sneakiest family — unrealized profits parked inside assets the buyer-skin hasn't yet consumed or sold to outsiders. Master four elimination families and the ₹6L lathe from DP2 finally faces justice: before the creature, moving it changed no wealth — the gain dies, the basis re-anchors, and only depreciation schedules remember the day the group tried to sell a machine to itself.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Erase intercompany balances first: receivable vs payable, loan vs borrowing — die in pairs
- Eliminate intercompany sales/purchases and the unrealized profit they plant in inventory
- Split the UPP burden by direction: downstream (parent 100%) vs upstream (NCI shares!)
- Repair fixed-asset transfers: gain erased, basis re-anchored, depreciation clawed back
- Extinguish intercompany dividends from the P&L family

## 📘 Concepts

### 6.1 Family one — balance pairs that die on sight

The creature cannot owe itself money. Parent-loan ₹25cr to subsidiary: parent's books show a receivable, child's a payable — combined lines would double-count a fictional asset-liability pair. Elimination: cancel 100% of every intra-group balance (trade rec/pay, loans, advances, accrued interest), any mismatches investigated like audit findings (timing, currency restatement differentials, honest recording gaps). Simple in concept, number-heavy in practice: the intercompany matrix is the workshop's master sheet, and unmatched pairs are where consolidation errors — and occasionally frauds — hide.

### 6.2 Family two — sales, purchases, and the UPP in inventory

Parent sells goods costing ₹80L to subsidiary at ₹1cr (a 25% markup on cost). Combined first-pass would show ₹1cr revenue + ₹1cr cost from the SAME exchange: cancel them fully (revenue −₹1cr, COGS −₹1cr). But if the subsidiary hasn't resold the goods by year-end, the creature's inventory sits at the intra-price ₹1cr — too rich by the intra margin: the creature's inventory must ride at original cost ₹80L. Unrealized profit = intra margin in the closing stock = ₹1cr × 25 ÷ 125 = **₹20L** (margin ÷ selling price, python-verified) — eliminated from group profit with inventory marked down. Next year, when stock actually sells to outsiders, this UPP releases (the creature finally earns it), the clean two-year rhythm exactly like IC1's ₹1-law self-correction.

### 6.3 Direction matters — downstream vs upstream, or who pays for the fake profit

- **Downstream** (parent → sub): the fake profit sat in the PARENT's own books → eliminate 100% against the parent's attributable share. NCI untouched.
- **Upstream** (sub → parent): the fake profit sat in the SUB's books → eliminate 100% of the amount, but the hit splits with the minority: sub's margins shrink by ₹10cr, so the NCI's 20% attribution absorbs its ₹2cr of the wound (python-verified) while owners absorb ₹8cr. Direction isn't bookkeeping trivia — it changes who inside equity feels the adjustment. Every working paper carries a direction column for precisely this reason.

### 6.4 Family three — asset transfers inside the creature (the lathe verdict)

DP2's lathe: sister sells to you at ₹26L against her ₹20L carrying; her books booked a ₹6L gain; your books now depreciate ₹26L (₹5.2L/yr at 5 years). Creature verdict, built in two moves (python-verified): (1) erase the ₹6L "gain" and re-anchor the lathe at its creature cost of ₹20L; (2) depreciation in the creature runs on ₹20L = ₹4L/yr, so claw back the ₹1.2L/yr overcharge the skin records (5.2 − 4.0) for the lathe's remaining life — eliminated annually until the machine exits, a standing remediation exactly as disciplined as any schedule in this course. Machinery, patents, whole business slices transferred between skins: all the same two-move justice.

### 6.5 Family four — intercompany dividends, interest, fees

The sub pays a ₹10cr dividend: parent's books record ₹8cr of dividend income, the NCI receives ₹2cr — yet INSIDE the creature, no money arrived; it moved pockets. Elimination: the ₹8cr dividend income dies (offset against S's distribution out of its profit attribution — watch that owner attribution isn't double-counted), and the NCI's ₹2cr leg simply REDUCES the NCI equity balance (an equity movement, never income). Same eraser for intercompany interest, royalties, management fees: income and expense cancel in pairs before the creature's P&L prints. Every one of these abides by the course soul-line: **a group cannot make a profit selling — or lending, or dispensing — to itself.**

### 6.6 The workshop discipline — matrix, tie-out, repeat

Workable consolidation shops run a three-tool discipline: (1) the **intercompany transaction matrix** every reporting date (all four families identified, valued, directioned); (2) the **tie-out** (every eliminated pair reconciles to both skins' ledgers: unmatched items are audit flags by construction); (3) the **standing file** (the lathe's 1.2-a-year clawback, past-year UPPs released this year, NCI direction table) so no elimination forfeits its memory. The workshop's rule of thumb: an elimination with no scheduled future is an elimination that will be wrong by year three.

## 💪 Exercises

1. Why does an upstream UPP hurt NCI while a downstream one does not? One doctrine paragraph.
2. The lathe: seller-skin already paid tax on its ₹6L "gain." What schedule does the workshop carry because of that? (Think DT!)
3. UPP release: last year's ₹20L-eliminated stock sells to outsiders this year. What happens in this year's workshop?
4. A 30%-associate sold the parent inventory with ₹5cr margin, all in stock. How much UPP vs the 100% rule? (CS9 preview.)

### ✅ Selected answers

1. Downstream, the fake profit was written by the parent — only owners' profit is inflated, so only owners absorb the correction; upstream, the fake profit sits in the SUBSIDIARY's books — erasing it shrinks the sub's earnings, and the minority's 20% attribution lawfully bears its ₹0.4cr-of-₹2cr portion of the shrink, exactly like it bears real losses. Direction decides whose column sweats.
2. A deferred tax schedule: the creature now carries the machine at ₹20L while the tax universe saw a real ₹26L transaction — a temporary difference on the transfer with DTA/DTL riding the clawback years (DT2 machinery at group seams).
3. The ₹20L releases back into group profit (the creature finally earns what the skins pre-booked), inventory line already neutralized; standing file tracks prior-year UPPs → current-year releases so nothing reverses twice or never.
4. Only the investor's SHARE eliminates against associates: 30% × ₹5cr = **₹1.5cr** adjusted through the equity-method carrying (CS9), not the 100% subsidiary-style elimination — associates lie outside the creature's body; only your share of the motion is internal.

**Q1.** Parent sold goods costing ₹80L to its sub at ₹1cr, all unsold at year-end:

**Q3.** Upstream UPP ₹2cr (sub sold to parent, margin still in parent's stock):
(a) eliminate ₹2cr against owners only
(b) eliminate 100% against the SUBSIDIARY's results because the fake profit is written on ITS books — attribution then spreads the wound: owners absorb ₹1.6cr and NCI absorbs its ₹0.4cr share, matching how the minority shares every real loss of the child

(d) carry to the cash flow only

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — pairs die, UPP sleeps in stock until outsiders wake it; downstream = owners' wound entirely.
2. **(b)** — gain erased, basis re-anchored, depreciation clawed ₹1.2L/yr on a standing schedule.
3. **(b)** — upstream corrections split by attribution: 1.6 owners, 0.4 NCI.
