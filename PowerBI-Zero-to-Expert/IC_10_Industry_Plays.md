## 📘 Concepts

### 10.1 FMCG — the expiry clock and the promo wall

Fast-moving goods aren't fast because they're moved; they're fast because they die young (shelf-life 6–24 months). Consequences:

- **FEFO logistics** (first-expired, first-out) govern PHYSICAL flow while accounting formulas sit on cost (WAC usually) — don't confuse the dispatch rule with the cost formula; FEFO is warehouses keeping science, not an Ind AS 2 formula.
- **Free stocks and promotions**: "buy 4 get 1" units are inventory gobbled by a SCHEME — their cost exits as COGS with the bundle (revenue side: an allocation question matched to the promotion's promises — RR doctrine), never as "marketing expense" shelves nonsense — the units are gone, and IC2's wall keeps promo logistics OUT of inventory cost.
- **Expiry NRV ladders**: stock near expiry reprices markdown-channel-wise (modern trade → discount chains → scrap/animal feed for some categories), so NRV testing runs on ageing tunnels per SKU: 6-months-left gets a different net backoff than 6-days-left. India's festive returns tide (distributors shipping unsold Diwali stock back in January in some channels) is the ladder's seasonal stress test.

### 10.2 Process industries — steel's tonnes teach the loss doctrine

Continuous processes price per tonne, and losses are the whole tuition:

**The ₹50,000/t canon (python-verified):** UrbanNest Steel charges 10,000t of input; total conversion + material cost **₹48cr**. Process physics: 400t normal loss (4% expected yield erosion — slag/scale at agreed norms; no value), 100t abnormal loss (a power-trip spoiled charge), 9,500t good output.

- Cost shares attach to ALL non-normal-loss units: 10,000 − 400 = 9,600t → **₹50,000 per tonne** (₹48cr ÷ 9,600)
- Good output carries 9,500 × 50,000 = **₹47.5cr → inventory**
- The abnormal 100t × 50,000 = **₹50L → straight to P&L this period**

That's IC5's doctrine in steel gloves: normal loss rides invisibly inside good tonnes (the 50,000 rate itself was formed by surviving the 400t erosion); abnormal loss exits loudly with a story. By-products (sellable slag, mill scale) clip at NRV first, exactly like dairy's whey.

### 10.3 Pharma — genealogies and funerals

- **Batch identity is law**: every batch carries its own cost record (validation, QC, stability runs) — effectively specific identification at batch grain, since one contaminated batch writes down alone (item-level NRV discipline, IC4, enforced by regulators rather than taste).
- **Expiry tunnels**: formulations die on scientific dates, not market moods — NRV ladders by remaining shelf-life per market rule (many channels refuse <60–75% of shelf-life remaining), and expired stock is ₹0-NRV with DESTRUCTION costs to boot (destruction is P&L, never a cost of the remaining pills).
- **Samples and trials**: physician samples and validation batches are not sale inventory — samples leave as marketing/CR expense; pre-validation batches and R&D inventories sit behind the fence (expensed per R&D rules unless a genuine production-quality salable stream emerges).
- **Generics price-crash NRV events**: patent cliffs and government price caps (NPPA ceilings, India flavor) crash NRV overnight — write-down cascades are a pharma season, item by item, SKU by SKU.

### 10.4 Jewellery — passports and weights

- **Specific identification** for the one-of-ones: certified solitaires, bespoke sets — each has its papal bull of cost and its own NRV story (IC3's fungibility fence, honored).
- **Weighted average** for fungible-by-weight merchandise: mass 22k chains of near-identical make — gold price flows through WAC smoothly (gold's wild daily price makes formula direction matter — falling gold punches WAC shelves; many jewellers hedge the PRICE side through metal loans/derivatives, but the INVENTORY measurement stays cost-with-brake; hedging changes risk, not the formula).
- **Making-charge accounting**: labour+overhead conversion per gram tracked separately from metal value (a two-layer cost per piece) — and gold loans funding the vault: the pledge disclosure (IC9) is jewellery's front page, since the inventory IS the borrowing base.

### 10.5 The pattern key — which dial turns where

| Industry | Hardest dial | Signature error |
|---|---|---|
| FMCG | NRV ageing tunnels / promo exit lanes | expired stock at full cost; promo units in "marketing inventory" limbo |
| Process (steel/cement/chem) | normal vs abnormal loss line; by-product NRV clips | abnormal costs riding good tonnes; slag mis-priced |
| Pharma | batch-level costs; expiry NRV | write-downs pooled across batches; expired stock discoverable in destruction logs before books |
| Jewellery | specific ID discipline; pledge/loan interplay | solitaire costs averaged; hedged metal double-counted in reconciliation |

2. FMCG: 10,000 promo units cost ₹141 each given free against a 40,000-unit scheme bundle. Where do ₹14.1L exit — and what must revenue-side allocation watch?
3. Pharma: batch PX-17 written to ₹32L cost; NPPA ceiling reprices NRV to ₹18L; batch PX-18 intact. Write-down and grouping verdict.
4. Jewellery: a ₹55L solitaire (certified) vs 4kg chain stock (WAC ₹5,800/g, cost ₹232L, NRV down 6%): treatment for each.
5. By-product clip: steel slag NRV ₹30L this year — where does it clip in the canon mechanics?

**Why this matters:** vertical fluency is the difference between knowing the standard and using it: auditors specialize by industry physics, analysts compare vertical-wise, and your interview answers get named examples instead of textbook vapour.

**🔑 Lab answers:**
1. Rate ₹48cr ÷ 9,600t = **₹50,000/t**; good output 9,500 × 50,000 = **₹47.5cr inventory**; abnormal 100 × 50,000 = **₹50L period expense** (foots: 47.5 + 0.5 = 48.0cr ✓).
2. The ₹14.1L exits through COGS with the scheme bundles' units — promo units are delivered goods, not "marketing inventory": basket allocation on the revenue side watches that the bundle's price splits fairly across its components (RR doctrine lives here too; the stock side just exits honestly).
3. Write down **₹14L** on PX-17 alone — item(batch)-level discipline: PX-18 can't cushion it, and the ceiling event is regulator-dated evidence. Notes carry the NPPA trigger narrative.
4. Solitaire: **specific ID** — its own cost, its own NRV test; WAC averaging with chains is forbidden for one-of-ones. Chains: WAC at ₹5,800/g with the lower-of-NRV brake: NRV −6% against ₹232L cost → write down **₹13.92L** unless price-traps (hedge values) alter the REALIZABLE evidence — hedges fix price risk, not measurement rules.
5. Slag clips at **₹30L NRV** against the joint/process pool before good-tonne allocation — by-products pay rent at NRV (dairy-whey doctrine in steel gloves): the ₹48cr pool nets the slag's contribution in the NEXT period's rate surgeries; never park slag at imagined sale premiums.

## 💪 Exercises

1. Why is FEFO a logistics rule and never an Ind AS 2 cost formula?
2. Walk the steel canon's footings: why must rate + good + abnormal foot exactly to ₹48cr?
3. A pharma CFO pools batch write-downs company-wide "for materiality". Demolish with two doctrines.
4. Gold price crashes 6% just after balance date. Adjusting event or not — and what happens to the chains' shelf?
5. Metal loans in jewellery: what does hedging change about inventory measurement — and what does it deliberately NOT change?

### ✅ Selected answers

1. Formulas choose which COST unit leaves; FEFO chooses which PHYSICAL unit leaves. The two live on different floors: expiry is a physical-remaining-life property, cost is an accounting history — a chain can ship its oldest-expiry milk while costing outflow at a WAC average of all vintages. Mixing the rules manufactures either fake freshness or fake costs; keeping them separate lets each do its honest job.
2. Because absorption is a PARTITION, not a profit source: total cost incurred (₹48cr) must emerge as either inventory (good units) or expense (abnormal) — with normal loss embedded in the rate. If 47.5 + 0.5 didn't foot to 48, the missing paisa is either absorbed-from-nowhere (fraud up) or expensed-twice (sandbag down) — footings are the machine's proof of work.
3. Doctrine one: item-level lower-of testing — pooling lets healthy batches net out dead ones (IC4's forbidden averaging). Doctrine two: estimate-fraud asymmetry — regulator-dated reprice events are item evidence, not ambience; materiality justifies presentation compression, never measurement-netting of factually distinct write-downs.
4. Adjusting event (Ind AS 10): gold prices at year-end were knowable — the crash crystallizes conditions existing at reporting date; the chains' shelf tests NRV at the post-crash evidence near signature, and write-downs land in the year just ended. Disclosure narrative: quantum, trigger, and any hedging offsets explained separately — hedge P&L flow belongs to its own lines.
5. Hedging changes RISK (the entity's economic exposure to gold swings now offsets via loan/derivative legs) — and changes NOTHING about the stock's measurement: inventory still rides cost-with-NRV-brake under its own formula. The deliberate non-change is the lesson: risk management lives in its own accounts; the shelf's discipline doesn't upvalue because derivatives exist. (Watch reconciliations: hedged-metal quantities are the double-count trap in physical-to-book tallies.)

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** Steel: 10,000t in, ₹48cr, 400t normal loss, 100t abnormal, 9,500t good:
(a) ₹48,000/t on all units
(b) ₹50,000/t across the 9,600 non-normal-loss tonnes → good output ₹47.5cr into inventory; abnormal charge ₹50L expelled to P&L — normal erosion hides inside the rate by design, accidents pay the period
(c) ₹50,526/t inventory only
(d) all ₹48cr to P&L (prudence)

**Q2.** A batch-level NPPA price-ceiling reprices batch PX-17's NRV to ₹18L vs ₹32L cost while PX-18 is fine:
(a) pool both batches before testing
(b) ₹14L write-down on PX-17 alone — batch-level specific costs and item-level NRV discipline mean healthy batches never cushion dying ones, and the ceiling's regulator date is hard evidence for the test
(c) reclassify PX-17 to samples
(d) amortize over shelf-life

**Q3.** Certified solitaire vs 4kg interchangeable chains in a 6% gold crash:
(a) both WAC; formula unity is mandatory
(b) solitaire under specific ID with its own NRV story; chains under WAC with the lower-of brake biting to the tune of ₹13.92L against a ₹232L cost — fungibility decides the formula, hedges change risk but never the shelf's measurement
(c) both at replacement cost
(d) solitaire WAC; chains expensed

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — the partition foots: ₹47.5cr shelved, ₹50L burned, rate formed through normal erosion.
2. **(b)** — item-level testing plus item-dated evidence: batch funerals are private affairs.
3. **(b)** — fungibility picks FIFO/WAC vs specific ID; derivatives manage risk, not the shelf.
