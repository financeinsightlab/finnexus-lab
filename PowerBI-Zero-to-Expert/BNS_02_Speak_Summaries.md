# BN2 · Speak the News: 30-Second Summaries & Sharp Opinions

> *Reading news privately makes you informed. Speaking news aloud makes you IMPRESSIVE. This module gives you the exact formulas professionals use — the 3-sentence summary, opinion frames, and the smooth-number trick — then makes you record yourself until it sounds natural. Bolna seekhna hai, padhna nahi.*

## 📘 2.1 The 30-second summary formula (your new superpower)

Anyone can ramble for 5 minutes about a story. Professionals compress it to **three sentences**:

```
SENTENCE 1 — FACT:       Who did what (the headline idea, past tense)
SENTENCE 2 — IMPACT:     Why it matters (the "so what?")
SENTENCE 3 — WHAT NEXT:  What happens now / your one-line view
```

Example, from your BN1 lab article:
1. *"ShopKart's profit tripled in the September quarter on strong festive demand."*
2. *"It matters because it shows quick-commerce growth is now coming from tier-2 cities, not just metros."*
3. *"Next, they're opening 100 dark stores — I think margins will be the number to watch."*

That's it. 25 seconds. You sounded like a consultant. 💼
Practice template (fill blanks aloud): *"[Company] ____ [what happened]. It matters because ____. Next, ____."*

## 📘 2.2 Signposts — the glue of professional speech

Signposts tell the listener where you are. Sprinkle 2–3 per summary:

| Purpose | Say this |
|---|---|
| Starting a summary | "Basically,…" / "In short,…" / "Long story short,…" |
| Highlighting | "The key point is…" / "What really matters is…" |
| Adding | "On top of that,…" / "Another thing is…" |
| Contrasting | "That said,…" / "On the other hand,…" |
| Closing | "The bottom line is…" / "So overall,…" |

⚠️ **Indian-English trap:** "Basically" at the START of every sentence = filler abuse. Rule: ONE signpost per sentence, maximum.

## 📘 2.3 Reporting verbs & attribution — borrowed credibility

When you repeat news, say WHERE it's from. That's not weakness — it's how anchors + analysts sound trustworthy:

- **Neutral:** *"The company announced…"* / *"ShopKart reported a 21% rise…"* / *"RBI said…"*
- **Attribution:** *"According to the Economic Times,…"* / *"As per analysts,…"*
- **Careful distance:** *"The CEO claimed margins will improve."* (you're not vouching!) / *"Reportedly,…"*

Verb intensity ladder (strong → soft): **confirmed → announced → said → indicated → suggested → hinted**. Pick per your confidence.

## 📘 2.4 Opinions: the frame ladder + polite hedging

News talk isn't a quiz — people want YOUR take. Climb this ladder by situation:

| Strength | Frame | Use with |
|---|---|---|
| Soft/safe | "I'd say…" / "It seems like…" | bosses, new groups |
| Neutral | "I think…" / "In my view…" | everyone |
| Strong | "I'm convinced…" / "Clearly,…" | when you have data! |

**Hedging = professional softness.** Absolute statements ("This will DEFINITELY crash") make you sound naive. Pros hedge: *"Margins could tighten,"* / *"It's likely that hiring slows,"* / *"I might be wrong, but…"*. Hedge + reason = the analyst's voice.

Formula for a bulletproof opinion: **FRAME + VIEW + BECAUSE + EXAMPLE**
*"In my view, quick commerce will hurt kirana stores **because** customers now expect 10-minute delivery — **for example**, my own colony's kirana lost weekend orders to Zepto."*

## 📘 2.5 Numbers into smooth speech — the anchor's trick

Anchors never say "seventy-one point seven eight lakh". They ROUND and PAINT:

| Exact | Smooth spoken version |
|---|---|
| ₹71.8 lakh | "around ₹72 lakh" / "roughly ₹70-odd lakh" |
| ₹2.8 crore | "close to ₹3 crore" / "about ₹2.8 crore" |
| +21% | "up about a fifth" / "up 21%" |
| +252% | "more than tripled" / "up over two-and-a-half times" |
| 2.0% → 2.4% margin | "up 0.4 percentage points" |
| ₹3,84,622 | pause after the crore/lakh chunk: "three point eight lakh" |

Golden rules: **round to 2 digits max** · attach a **direction word** (rose/fell to/about/nearly) · **pause before and after** the number — the pause is what makes it land.

## 📘 2.6 Pronunciation clinic — business word gym 🏋️

Stress the CAPITALS, drill 10× aloud:
- **REV**enue · **PROF**it · **PROF**itable (⚠️ stress JUMPS: proFIT? no — **PROF**itable)
- e**CON**omy vs eco**NOM**ic (stress moves with the suffix!) · **FI**nance (noun, UK-style) ·
  **ANA**lyst vs a**NAL**ysis vs ana**LY**tical
- **CROre** · **LA**kh · **SE**ptember · **QUAR**ter (kwar-ter)

**Teen vs TY (the money confusion):** 15 "fif-TEEN" (stress end) vs 50 "FIF-ty" (stress start) — drill pairs: 13/30, 14/40, 15/50, 16/60, 17/70, 18/80, 19/90. In India, saying "₹15 crore" when you mean "₹50 crore" is a career-limiting syllable!

## 📘 2.7 The record → review → retry loop (where growth lives)

1. 📱 **Record** your summary on your phone (voice recorder = free coach).
2. 👂 **Review with 3 passes:** pass 1 only for CONTENT (formula followed?) · pass 2 only for PACE (too fast? pauses around numbers?) · pass 3 only for CLARITY (stress, teen/ty).
3. 🔁 **Retry** once, fixing ONE thing. Two recordings a day > twenty recordings in your head.

Target pace: **~130 words in 60 seconds.** Faster = nervous; slower = boring. Anchors live here.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Compress to 3 formula sentences: "RBI holds repo rate at 6.5%; experts say inflation is cooling; rate cut possible in October."
2. Upgrade with attribution + hedge: "Zomato will buy Paytm's ticketing business." → soft, sourced, careful version.
3. Convert to smooth speech: ₹14.72 crore · +38.4% · ₹9,847 employees · margin 11.3% → 9.8%.
4. Write 4 opinions on "Work from home is ending in India" using 4 different frames (soft → strong).
5. Spot the error: "According to me, the stock will surely double, basically." — fix all 3 problems.

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 1: *"The RBI kept the repo rate unchanged at 6.5%. It matters because cooling inflation gives the central bank room to cut rates. Next, watch the October policy meeting — a cut looks possible."*
- Ex 2: *"Reportedly, Zomato is planning to acquire Paytm's ticketing arm — though the deal hasn't been officially confirmed yet."*
- Ex 3: "close to fifteen crore" · "up nearly forty percent" (or "up over a third") · "close to ten thousand employees" · "margin slipped one and a half percentage points".
- Ex 5: (a) "According to me" ❌ — attribution is for SOURCES; for yourself say "In my view". (b) "surely double" — hedge it ("could double"). (c) trailing "basically" — delete. Fixed: *"In my view, the stock could double."*

## ❓ Quiz
1. Why exactly THREE sentences (FACT–IMPACT–NEXT)?
2. "The CEO claimed profits will rise" vs "The CEO confirmed profits rose" — what's the difference and why does it matter?
3. Why is hedging ("could", "might", "it seems") a sign of professionalism, not weakness?
4. Why round numbers instead of saying exact figures aloud?

### ✅ Answers
1. It forces compression: one fact, one meaning, one forward look — everything a listener can hold. Anything longer is a monologue; anything shorter is gossip.
2. *Claimed* signals the speaker isn't vouching for it; *confirmed* says it's verified. Mixing them up can make you repeat rumors as facts — a trust-killer in offices.
3. Business is probability, not certainty. Hedged + reasoned predictions ("margins could tighten because costs are rising") survive being wrong; absolute ones get quoted against you.
4. Listeners remember direction + size ("up about a fifth"), not decimals. Rounding makes you fluent and keeps the pace; exactness lives on paper.
