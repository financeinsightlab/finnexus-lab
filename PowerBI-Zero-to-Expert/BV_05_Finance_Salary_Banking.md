## 📘 5.1 Salary slip English — decode your own! 💼

The offer letter says **CTC ₹9,00,000** — but the bank gets ~₹60,000/month. Where did the rest go? Every rupee has a NAME:

| Word | Meaning | Say it like |
|---|---|---|
| **CTC** (Cost to Company) | EVERYTHING the company spends on you: salary + PF + benefits | "My CTC is nine lakh per annum." |
| **gross pay** | salary before deductions | "Gross this month is ₹75,000." |
| **net / in-hand / take-home** | what actually reaches your bank | "In-hand is about sixty thousand." |
| **basic** | the core pay (40–50% of CTC) — other parts calculate from it | "Basic is 40% of CTC." |
| **HRA** (House Rent Allowance) | rent component, tax-friendly | "HRA is ₹15,000." |
| **allowances** | extra named payments (travel, special, medical) | "My allowances make up the rest." |
| **deductions** | money taken OUT before paying you | "Deductions: PF + TDS + professional tax." |
| **PF / EPF** (Provident Fund) | retirement saving: 12% of basic, company matches | "PF goes straight to my EPF account." |
| **TDS** (Tax Deducted at Source) | income tax cut by employer monthly | "TDS eats a chunk every month." |
| **Form 16** | yearly TDS certificate for filing returns | "I download Form 16 from the HR portal." |
| **payslip** | the monthly salary document | "Your payslip tells the CTC story." |
| **gratuity** | loyalty bonus (after 5 years) | "Gratuity accrues in your CTC." |
| **variable pay / bonus** | performance-linked part | "₹1 lakh of my CTC is variable." |
| **arrears** | back-payment (appraisal applied late) | "Arrears credited this month — party!" |

🗣️ **The interview sentence:** *"My current CTC is nine lakh, with an in-hand of around sixty-two thousand; my last appraisal gave me a twelve-percent increment."*

## 📘 5.2 Banking words — your bank app, in English 🏧

- **savings account** (personal, earns interest) vs **current account** (business, no interest, big limits)
- **deposit** (put money in) · **withdrawal/withdraw** (take out) · **balance** (what's in there now) · **account statement** (transaction history) · **passbook**
- **cheque** · **demand draft (DD)** · **pay order** · **NEFT / RTGS / IMPS / UPI** (payment rails: NEFT = batch, RTGS = big instant, IMPS = instant 24×7, UPI = phone scan-pay) · **beneficiary** (who you're paying) · **limit** (transaction/daily)
- **KYC** (Know Your Customer — identity proof: "KYC pending ho gaya" = account frozen! 😱) · **nominee** (person who receives money if you die — set one TODAY)
- **FD** (Fixed Deposit: lock money, higher interest) · **RD** (Recurring Deposit: monthly small deposits) · **maturity** (when the FD unlocks)
- **overdraft (OD)** (spend more than balance — allowed limit) · **minimum balance** · **charges/penalty**
- Simple vs compound **interest**: FDs compound ("interest on interest") — the magic word of all investing.

## 📘 5.3 Loans & credit words — the EMI files 📄

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **principal** | the amount borrowed (before interest) |
| **interest rate** | the % the loan costs per year |
| **EMI** (Equated Monthly Instalment) | fixed monthly payment = principal + interest parts |
| **tenure** | loan length ("a 20-year home loan") |
| **sanction** | bank approves the loan ("loan sanctioned!") |
| **disbursement** | bank releases the money |
| **prepayment / foreclosure** | paying early / closing the whole loan early |
| **credit score / CIBIL score** | your borrowing trust report card (300–900; 750+ = good loans get approved) |
| **credit card vs debit card** | borrow-then-pay vs spend-your-own |
| **collateral** | asset pledged against a loan (house, gold) |
| **mortgage / home loan** | loan against property |
| **default** | failing to repay · **NPA** (non-performing asset — what a bank calls a defaulted loan) |
| **guarantor** | person who pays if you don't |

🗣️ Say the loan story: *"I took a **home loan** of forty lakh at eight-point-five percent for a **twenty-year tenure** — the **EMI** comes to about thirty-five thousand, and I plan a small **prepayment** every year from my bonus."*

## 📘 5.4 Business-finance words — straight from ShopKart's GL 📚

You already SAW these in your Finance GL dataset (P5/S5/M10 modules) — now they enter your SPEECH:

- **invoice** (the "please pay" document: "Raise an invoice to the client") · **bill** (whoever receives it calls it a bill) · **receipt** (proof AFTER payment)
- **vendor / supplier** (who you buy from) · **purchase order (PO)** (formal "we order X")
- **accounts receivable (AR)** (money customers OWE you) · **accounts payable (AP)** (money YOU owe vendors) — heartbeats of **working capital**
- **ledger** (the master book of accounts) · **journal entry** (one accounting record: debit + credit) · **trial balance** (all account totals, checking debits=credits)
- **assets** (what you own) · **liabilities** (what you owe) · **equity** (owner's share: assets − liabilities)
- **depreciation** (assets losing value over time — "₹2 lakh annual depreciation on delivery bikes")
- **audit** (official checking of the books: statutory/internal audit) · **auditor**
- **GST** (Goods & Services Tax — India's sales tax; shown on every business invoice) · **input tax credit** (GST you paid on purchases, set off against GST collected) · **HSN code** (product classification on invoices)
- **P&L**, **balance sheet**, **cash flow statement** — the holy trinity of financial reports (you built them in Power BI! 📊)

## 📘 5.5 Investment words — grow-paisa kit 📈

- **mutual fund (MF)** (many people's money, professionally invested) · **fund manager** · **AMC** (the fund company)
- **SIP** (Systematic Investment Plan — fixed auto-investment monthly: "I run a ₹5,000 SIP") · **lumpsum** (one big investment)
- **NAV** (Net Asset Value — a fund unit's daily price)
- **returns** (what you earn) · **XIRR/CAGR** (annualized growth % — you computed these in the Python course! 🐍) · **inflation-adjusted / real returns**
- **stocks/equity vs bonds/debt** (ownership vs lending) · **portfolio** (your full set of investments) · **diversification** (don't keep all eggs in one basket — spread across types) · **asset allocation**
- **risk profile** (how much fall you can stomach) · **volatility** (price swings)
- **insurance** words: **policy** · **premium** (what you pay) · **sum assured** (what they pay) · **term insurance** (pure protection, recommended) · **claim** · **nominee** · **maturity benefit**
- **PPF / NPS / ELSS** (India's tax-saving trio)

## 📘 5.6 Register: money talk do's & don'ts 🤐

- Bragging exact salary loudly = junior. Pros speak in ranges and structures: "a CTC in the mid-teens" / "an eight-figure package one day, inshallah" 😄.
- Public money questions that are polite CURIOSITY, not nosiness: "how do SIPs work for you?" not "kitna kamate ho?"
- Business invoices: always say **"as per invoice #…"**, **"payment terms: net 30"** (pay within 30 days), **"due date"**, **"overdue"**.

## 💪 Exercises
1. CTC ₹12 LPA, in-hand ₹85,000 — where does the difference go? Name 4 deduction/benefit words.
2. Match: collateral / nominee / beneficiary / guarantor ↔ (a) receives if you die (b) pays if you default (c) receives a bank transfer (d) asset pledged for a loan.
3. Why does a 15-minute "break-even vs cash flow" chat confuse freshers? Explain both words like to a friend.
4. Translate to bank-English: "maine fd karvai hai aur har mahine rd mein paise daalta hoon, upi se."
5. An investment gives "12% CAGR". Decode and say it in a client-ready sentence.

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 1: employer PF contribution, gratuity accrual, insurance premium, TDS, professional tax, employee PF — all the named money between CTC and in-hand.
- Ex 2: collateral=(d), nominee=(a), beneficiary=(c), guarantor=(b).
- Ex 3: Break-even = the point where revenue covers costs (no loss, no profit); cash flow = the TIMING of money moving in/out — a business can be past break-even and still die because customers pay late (receivables pile up!).
- Ex 4: "I've booked a fixed deposit, I invest in a recurring deposit every month, and I pay through UPI."
- Ex 5: "The investment has grown at a compounded annual rate of about twelve percent — comfortably above inflation."

## ❓ Quiz
1. CTC ₹10 LPA but in-hand ₹68,000/month — explain the gap to a fresher in one breath.
2. Why do banks love prepayment but sometimes charge for foreclosure?
3. What's the difference between an invoice and a receipt?
4. Why is a nominee + a healthy CIBIL score the two most underrated money decisions?

### ✅ Answers
1. CTC is the company's total cost — it includes employer PF, gratuity, insurance and bonuses; monthly deductions (employee PF, tax/TDS, professional tax) leave the in-hand. Same rupee, different wrappers.
2. Prepayment reduces principal early (saves interest — bank accepts politely, sometimes with small charges); foreclosure closes the loan entirely — banks lose future interest so some charge a foreclosure fee. Read the loan terms!
3. Invoice = the demand for payment, issued BEFORE; receipt = the proof of payment, issued AFTER. Business 101: never "pay a receipt."
4. Nominee = your money finds your family without court battles; CIBIL score 750+ = cheaper loans for life (home-loan rates can be 0.5–1% lower — lakhs saved). Boring words, massive money.
