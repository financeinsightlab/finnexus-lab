# BE1 · English Foundations: Letters, Sounds & First Words

> *Every expert was once a beginner. This first Basic English course assumes NOTHING — not even confidence. By the end of this module you'll know all 26 letters by sound, count to 100, greet people politely, and have your first 50 survival words. Chalo, shuru karte hain!*

## 🎯 Objectives

## 📘 1.1 The alphabet — names vs sounds (the big secret!)

Every letter has TWO identities: its NAME ("ay", "bee", "see") and its SOUND (/æ/, /b/, /k/). We say letter names when SPELLING ("My name is Ravi — R-A-V-I") and letter sounds when READING words.

| Letters | Name | Sound example |
|---|---|---|
| A a | "ay" | **a**pple, c**a**t |
| E e | "ee" | **e**lephant, p**e**n |
| I i | "eye" | **i**ndia, s**i**t |
| O o | "oh" | **o**range, d**o**g |
| U u | "yoo" | **u**mbrella, c**u**p |

⚠️ **Indian trap #1**: letter **C** is pronounced "see" (सी), never "ch" — 'cat' uses the /k/ sound of C. Letter **V** is "vee" with the TOP TEETH on the lower lip — different from W ("double-u", both lips rounded). Indians mix v/w constantly; we fix it fully in BE6, for now just note it.

## 📘 1.2 Vowels & consonants — the engine of every word

5 vowels: **A E I O U**. 21 consonants: everything else. Every English word needs at least one vowel sound — vowels carry the voice (say "psst" — no vowel, feels broken, right?).

Rules you'll use forever:
- **a** before consonant sounds: a boy, a cat, a university (sounds "yoo-" = consonant!)
- **an** before vowel SOUNDS: an apple, an hour (h is silent!)
- Short words glue: one-syllable words live on consonants around ONE vowel: cat, bed, pit, hot, run.

## 📘 1.3 Capital letters — when English shouts

Use CAPITALS for: 1) first word of a sentence, 2) names of people/places (Ravi, Delhi, India, ShopKart), 3) the word **I** (always capital, always!), 4) days and months (Monday, January), 5) titles before names (Mr Sharma, Dr Khan). Everything else stays lowercase.

✏️ Fix these: "i am from india." → "**I** am from **I**ndia." / "my friend suresh lives in mumbai" → "My friend **Suresh** lives in **Mumbai**."

## 📘 1.4 Numbers, dates & time — everyday survival

- **1–20**: one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, seventeen, eighteen, nineteen, twenty.
- **Tens**: 20 twenty, 30 thirty (⚠️ 13-30 confusion! "thirTEEN" vs "THIRty" — stress moves!), 40 forty (no "u"!), 50 fifty, 60, 70, 80, 90, 100 hundred.
- Big numbers: 250 = "two hundred (and) fifty" · ₹1,250 = "twelve fifty" or "one thousand two hundred fifty".
- **Dates**: 23 July 2026 = "the twenty-third of July, twenty twenty-six" (Indian format DD/MM). ✍️ Write "23rd July" or "23-07-2026". Beware American format 07/23 = July 23!
- **Time**: 6:00 "six o'clock" · 6:15 "quarter past six" · 6:30 "half past six" · 6:45 "quarter to seven".

## 📘 1.5 Greetings & the magic words — your first conversations 🪄

| Situation | Say |
|---|---|
| Meeting anyone (any time) | Hello! / Hi! |
| Morning | Good morning! |
| Afternoon / after lunch | Good afternoon! |
| Evening | Good evening! (NOT "good night" — that's for goodbye/sleep!) |
| Leaving | Goodbye! / Bye! / See you! |
| You want something | **Please**… ("One tea, please.") |
| Someone helps you | **Thank you**! / Thanks a lot! |
| Get attention / small push | **Excuse me** |
| You made a mistake | **Sorry**! / I'm sorry. |
| Reply to sorry | It's okay. / No problem. |

Mini-dialogue (read aloud 3×, smile each time 😊):
> A: Good morning! How are you?
> B: I'm fine, thank you. And you?
> A: I'm great, thanks!

## 📘 1.6 Your first 50 survival words — daily drill

Verbs (actions): be, have, go, come, do, make, get, take, give, eat, drink, see, speak, work, live, want, need, like, know, buy
Nouns (things): water, food, tea, milk, rice, money, house, shop, bus, train, road, name, day, time, book, phone, friend, family, work, school
Adjectives (describing): good, bad, big, small, new, old, happy, hot, cold, easy

Method: 10 words/day → say the word → make ONE tiny sentence ("I like tea.") → tomorrow revise yesterday's first. In 5 days you have 50 words earning interest.

## 📘 1.7 The 15-minute daily habit (non-negotiable!)

```
5 min — revise yesterday (words/letters)
5 min — new lesson part
5 min — SPEAK out loud (mirror practice: say 5 sentences about your day)
```

English is a SKILL like cricket, not a SUBJECT like history: you don't "know" cricket, you PLAY it. Same here — speak daily, even alone, even badly. Quantity first, quality follows.

## 🧪 LAB BE1 — Foundations boot camp (30–45 min)

1. Letter run: write the alphabet small + capital (aA, bB…) reading each NAME aloud. Time yourself — twice today, once tomorrow. Record your speed in your notes 📝 sidebar.
2. Spell-by-name: spell your full name, your city, and "SHOPKART" aloud with letter names. Have a family member copy what you say — spelling success = being understood!
3. Numbers sprint: write figures as English words: 7, 13, 30, 42, 100, 250, ₹1,999. Then read 5 random years aloud (1995, 2008, 2024…).
4. Magic words drill: write the polite word for each: (a) you bump into someone in the metro, (b) the shopkeeper gives change, (c) you want mom's attention while she's busy, (d) teacher asks a question you can't answer, (e) you ask for one samosa.
5. Day-1 diary sentence: write + SAY: "Today I started English. My name is ___. I live in ___. I like ___." — tomorrow you'll write a better one. That's progress made visible.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Vowel hunt: in "elephant", "umbrella", "alphabet" — circle vowels, count consonants around them.
2. a/an correct: ___ apple, ___ boy, ___ hour, ___ university, ___ egg, ___ MBA.
3. Fix capitals: "i live in delhi. my friend amit works in an office."
4. Say the times aloud: 3:00, 4:15, 7:30, 9:45, and write what you'd SAY if someone asks the time now.
5. Mini-dialogue: write A/B for a morning meeting at a tea stall (greeting + how are you + bye). Speak both parts!

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 2: an apple, a boy, an hour (h silent!), a university ("yoo-niversity"), an egg, an MBA ("em-bee-ay" starts with vowel SOUND).
- Ex 3: "I live in Delhi. My friend Amit works in an office."

## ❓ Quiz
1. A letter's NAME vs its SOUND — what's the difference?
2. When MUST the word "I" be capital?
3. "An hour," not "a hour" — why?
4. What's the right reply to "How are you?" in a normal greeting?

### ✅ Answers
1. The name is what we SAY when spelling (R-A-V-I); the sound is the noise the letter MAKES inside words ("r-uh"). Both needed.
2. Always — "I" is always a capital letter in English, everywhere in the sentence.
3. "Hour" begins with a VOWEL SOUND (h is silent: "our") — articles follow sound, not spelling.
4. "I'm fine, thank you. And you?" — answer + thanks + return the question. That's the polite loop.
