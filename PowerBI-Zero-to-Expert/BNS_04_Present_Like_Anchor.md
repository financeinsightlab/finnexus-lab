# BN4 · Present the News Like a Pro: Briefings, Anchors & Q&A

> *From talking WITH people to speaking AT the front — team huddles, college presentations, client updates, "market news" segments in interviews. This module gives you the anchor's briefing structure, delivery skills that make people look up from their phones, and calm Q&A handling. Mic on, bro! 🎙️*

## 🎯 Objectives
The 4-beat news briefing (HEADLINE → CONTEXT → IMPACT → WHAT NEXT) · anchor delivery: pace, pause, emphasis · presenting numbers & charts in words · the 2-minute, 3-story bulletin with transitions · Q&A under pressure · beating stage nerves.

---

## 📘 4.1 The 4-beat briefing — every update, one skeleton

A BN2 summary is 3 sentences. A **briefing** is its bigger, structured cousin — 60–90 seconds, one story, four beats:

```
BEAT 1 — HEADLINE:   the fact + hook (one line, past tense)
BEAT 2 — CONTEXT:    why this story exists (background, 2-3 lines)
BEAT 3 — IMPACT:     who gains, who loses, numbers (the meat)
BEAT 4 — WHAT NEXT:  outlook + your closing line (memorable!)
```

Full example (notice the beats):
> **B1:** "ShopKart's profits tripled last quarter — the best quarter in the company's history."
> **B2:** "Two years ago ShopKart was a small metro player losing money on every delivery. Then it bet everything on tier-2 cities and 10-minute delivery."
> **B3:** "This quarter, revenue rose 21% to ₹71.8 lakh and net profit hit ₹3.85 lakh. The winners: tier-2 customers who now get metros-level service. The worry: delivery costs are still 40% of revenue."
> **B4:** "Next, 100 dark stores by Diwali. My view? Growth looks real, but margins decide whether this is a unicorn story or a bubble. Thank you — questions welcome."

💡 That closing "my view" line is what separates a *news reader* from an *analyst*.

## 📘 4.2 Anchor delivery — the 4 knobs 🎛️

| Knob | Target | Drill |
|---|---|---|
| **Pace** | 130 words/min; slow DOWN on numbers | read one lead para with a stopwatch |
| **Pause** | 1-sec pause after the headline and before each number | read aloud, clap at every pause mark |
| **Emphasis** | one stressed word per sentence | mark it in CAPS in your script first |
| **Body** | still feet, open chest, eyes left→centre→right | present to 3 chairs pretending they're people |

⚠️ The two rookie speed problems: rushing numbers (→ pause before!) and ending fast (→ slow the LAST line; last words get remembered).

**Line that buys instant authority:** start with time + source. *"Good morning — it's 10 o'clock, and here's what moved business overnight."*

## 📘 4.3 Presenting numbers & charts in words (your analytics superpower 📊)

As a future analyst you'll PRESENT data, not just read it. Describe any chart in 3 moves:
1. **Say the SHAPE first:** "The line climbs steadily from January…" / "Sales dip every February — see the V-shape…"
2. **Land ONE number** (rounded, paused): "…reaching about ₹72 lakh in September."
3. **Translate to meaning:** "…which basically means festive demand now lasts the whole quarter."

**Trend word-bank, say them until automatic:**
climbing steadily · inching up · sharp spike · gradual decline · plunged · bottomed out · flattened · plateaued · seesawing · rebounded · hit a peak / a low.

⚠️ Rule: the room should remember ONE number, not eleven. Choose your hero number and sacrifice the rest.

## 📘 4.4 The 2-minute, 3-story bulletin 🗞️

Anchors chain stories with **transitions** — memorize this shelf:
- "In other news,…" · "Meanwhile,…" · "Turning to the markets,…" · "And finally, in some good news,…" · "Staying with tech,…"

Bulletin blueprint (say section times aloud as you practice):

```
0:00  Greeting + "top story" line        (B1-B4, 45 sec — gets the most time)
0:45  Transition → market/policy story   (B1-B3, 40 sec)
1:25  Transition → lighter/global story  (B1-B2 + closer, 30 sec)
1:55  Sign-off: "That's your business update. Back to you."
```

Sign-offs that stick: "...and that's the number to watch this week." · "Holiday-shortened week ahead — stay sharp."

## 📘 4.5 Q&A under pressure — the P.A.R. method 🛡️

Every briefing ends with questions. Handle them like a CEO:

1. **PAUSE** (2 seconds — silence reads as confidence, never rush)
2. **ACKNOWLEDGE** — "Good question." / "Fair point."
3. **RESPOND** — one point + one number or example. Stop talking. (Rambling = fear.)

If you DON'T know: never bluff. The professional hat-trick:
*"I don't have that number with me — I don't want to guess on live data. I'll confirm it after this and share it by EOD."* → then actually do it. That's how juniors become trusted.

If the question is hostile: **bridge** — acknowledge, then steer: *"That's a concern for sure. What I can tell you today is that revenue grew 21%, and margins are the metric we're watching…"*

## 📘 4.6 Stage nerves — the pre-flight checklist ✈️

- **4-4-6 breath:** in-4, hold-4, out-6, three times — heart rate drops measurably.
- **Memorize ONLY your first line** cold; after 10 seconds, adrenaline becomes energy.
- **Practice standing, phone recording, once in the actual room/spot** if possible.
- **Reframe:** nerves = your body preparing to perform. Say "I'm excited" (same chemistry as fear).
- **The 10-second rule for mistakes:** no one remembers a stumble after 10 seconds UNLESS you apologize and magnify it. Pause, correct, continue.

## ❓ Quiz
1. What's the difference between a BN2 summary and a BN4 briefing?
2. Why should your LAST line be said slowly?
3. Why describe a chart's SHAPE before its numbers?
4. Someone asks something you don't know — walk through the correct handling.

### ✅ Answers
1. Summary = 3 sentences, conversational, one listener. Briefing = 4 beats, 60–90 sec, addressed to a room, with opening/closing view and performance delivery.
2. Terminal weight: audiences remember endings most; a rushed ending throws away your whole briefing's punchline.
3. The brain processes pictures faster than digits; shape gives meaning, then ONE number anchors it. Numbers-first loses the room in 5 seconds.
4. Pause → acknowledge → respond honestly: no bluffing, offer "I'll verify and share by EOD" — then follow through. Honesty under pressure is a promotion signal.
