# 🎯 FA6 · The Credit & Banker Lens — DSCR, Two Ledgers & Covenant Craft
> Equity asks 'how much can I make?'; credit asks 'how much can I lose, and who's paying me in which quarter?' FA6 swaps your seat: the loan officer's toolkit — DSCR underwriting (ShopKart's ~5.8× sings), the two-ledger appraisal (repayment then liquidation), working-capital limits and the MPBF glance, covenant craft for the borrower's benefit too, and the one-page literacy for reading a BANK's own statements (GNPA, CASA, PCR). Analysts who can sit on both sides of a credit table are rare and expensive; be one.

## 📘 Concepts

### 6.1 DSCR — the debt-service pulse ratio
```text
DSCR = (PAT + interest + dep − drawings) / (interest + scheduled principal)
ShopKart: (13.25 + 2.3 + 6 − 2.65 drawings[dividend adj. variant]) / (2.3 + 1.0) = 18.9/3.3 ≈ **5.7×**
     (cash-flow syntax: CFO + interest add-back variant ⇒ (16.95+2.3)/3.3 = **5.8×** — quote both bands)
Floors (banker folklore verified by collections history):
  < 1.25 = decline/renew-only · 1.25–1.5 = cautious · 1.5–2.0 = normal-good · > 2.0 = strong · 5.8× = woo-the-customer
```
Sensitivity clause (CF4's gears again): DSCR must be quoted at BASE and at TROUGH-case (EBIT −40% ⇒ numerator 18.9 − 8 = 10.9 ⇒ 10.9/3.3 ≈ **3.3×** — still ≥ floor ✓ headroom is the covenant of the wise: present BOTH numbers; committees reward the borrower who stress-tests themselves first).

### 6.2 The two-ledger appraisal — side-by-side, always
```text
LEDGER A (repayment): CFO 16.95 avg over 3y → seasonal trough ~70% → ~11.9 → covers 3.3 debt service ~3.6×
LEDGER B (liquidation): block 21.5×0.55 + stock 44.2×0.55 + debtors 31.5×0.85 + cash 12×1.0
            = 11.8 + 24.3 + 26.8 + 12 = 74.9 vs total liabilities (15+4+26+2.3) = 47.3 → **1.58× fire-sale cover ✓**
```
Ledger A tells you the loan will be SERVICED; Ledger B tells you the bank gets paid even if it isn't. Approvals demand A ≥ 1.5× and B ≥ 1.2× (SME folklore); boring numbers in both ledgers = the credit officer's definition of fine romance.

### 6.3 Working-capital machinery — drawing power in one breath
Bank-financed WC runs on monthly stock/debtor statements: **Drawing Power = (stock × 75%) + (debtors ≤ 90-day × 75%) − creditors**; the OD works only up to DP-min, not your sanctioned limit (limit ₹30L, DP ₹22.5L ⇒ usable ₹22.5L). Margin-of-25% haircuts guard against valuation fantasy. MPBF (Tandon-era maximum-permissible-bank-finance) logic still shapes assessments: the bank funds the WC gap IT judges normative (methods I/II: 25%/25%-of-CA margins), the borrower funds the shade from own accruals. Take-away: **your OD's real ceiling is arithmetic (stock×0.75 rules), not contractual — design treasury plans against DP, not the sanction letter.**

### 6.4 Covenant craft — kit from the borrower's seat
Kit = {financial floors: DSCR ≥ 1.5, interest-coverage ≥ 4×, CCC ≤ 90 days} + {behavior: monthly stock-debtor statements on day-7, no related-party loans > ₹5L without consent, dividend-locks under DSCR < 2} + {information triggers: auditor-change, litigation > ₹5L}. **Borrower's moves:** propose the kit YOURSELF with trough-math headroom (CF8 doctrine) = cheapest possible rate spread; negotiate definition-pages (one-off exclusions, EBIDTA-with-dep-add-back variants, seasonality averaging) — covenant battles are won in the definitions before they're won in the numbers.

### 6.5 Reading a bank — one-page reverse-literacy
Banks invert everything: loans are assets, deposits are liabilities, 'funds parked in G-Secs' = risk-off posture. Gauges: **GNPA%** (gross non-performing/advances: > 5% = rot-watch; coverage matters sequel), **PCR** (provision-coverage: > 70% = honest kitchen), **CASA** (current+savings share: high = cheap funds = NIM resilience), **NIM** (net-interest margin ~ 3–4%). A bank with rising GNPA + falling PCR + borrowing-cost squeezes = you read their credit story like you now read anyone's — the student has become the underwriter.
