## 📘 Concepts

### 6.1 The goals stack — collision management

The Sharma file (TV7) pays simultaneously for house (6y), college (9-14y), retirement (26-28y) — the art is *collision management*: goals share one salary, so each carries a rank: **(1) protection always first (WM5), (2) retirement second — the only goal with NO borrowing route** (education has scholarships/loans, houses have mortgages, retirement at 60 has only its own corpus), **(3) dated goals next, (4) upgrades last.** When the month runs short, cut from the bottom, never tier-2 (the exponent law: pausing retirement at 31 costs 2.8× dose at 40, TV6 canon). By 60 the stack resolves: college paid, house owned, and the retirement bucket — if tiers held — has become the family's entire financial reality.

### 6.2 Safe withdrawal rate — India edition

US 4% rule: 4% initial + inflation raises, 30y horizon, US data. India-adjust: 6% inflation + higher volatility + 60/40 blend ~10.7% nominal (WM2) → real ≈ 4.4%, then off ~0.7-0.9pp of volatility drag (PM2's AM-GM law) → **3-3.5% SWR** (PM7/WM1 canon: real blend ≈ 3.77%). Practical: **₹3Cr corpus supports ₹9-10L/yr (=₹75-83k/mo) safely at 3-3.5%**; Ramesh's ₹12L/yr worked because 7%-assumption conservative + LTCG frictions minimal: stress-test every client at (SWR + 0.5pp) and (returns − 2pp): if the plan only survives the brochure scenario, it's marketing, not a plan.

### 6.3 The 3-bucket defense — sequence risk's antidote

Sequence risk (PM7): average returns lie when withdrawals are ordered — retire into −30% year-1 and ₹3Cr can die inside a decade at fixed withdrawals. The **bucket architecture** (₹3Cr, ₹1L/mo canon): **B1 Cash ₹36L** (3y of withdrawals, liquid fund) — neversells equity in a crash; **B2 Debt ₹84L** (y4-10, short-duration/ladder ~8%) — recharges B1 annually; **B3 Equity ₹180L** (y11+, index/flexi) — the growth engine, reviewed annually: good years skim gains INTO B2 (top-up), flat years refill only B1, crash years **freeze B3 sales entirely** (buckets 1&2 carry 9+ years of spending — longer than any Indian bear market in history). The defense turns volatility from a killer into a subscription.

### 6.4 SWP mechanics — the salary you pay yourself

Systematic Withdrawal Plan = redeeming units monthly; tax-elegant vs annuities (each SWP installment splits principal + gains: only LTCG 12.5% >₹1.25L/yr on the *gains portion* — early years mostly principal → near-zero tax: **SWP beats annuity income (fully slab-taxed) in year-1-8 dramatically**). NPS forced-annuity tail (WM3): treat its 6% slab-taxed pension as the *floor income*; SWP is the variable layer; rental/pension benefits stack as layer 3. Raise SWP with inflation annually (6% step-up), sourced from the B3 skim rule — the corpus must out-grow the raise by the SWR margin (~7% return needed in steady state for a 3.5% SWR + 6% raise arithmetic to hold long-run).

### 6.5 The estate layer — so the machine survives you politely

India's inheritance reality: nominations in banks/MFs make the *nominee a custodian, not the owner* (legal heirs own per law/will) — so nomination ≠ estate plan. The checklist: **(1) nominations updated everywhere** (MFs, FDs, EPF, PPF, NPS, lockers, insurance, Demat); **(2) joint accounts/holdings** ("either or survivor" for operating accounts); **(3) registered will** naming executors + guardians for minors (unregistered wills work but probate fights love ambiguity); **(4) letter of instruction** — passwords, policies, property papers, advisors' contacts — the Sharma household CFO dashboard (WM1) doubles as this letter; **(5) asset register updated annually** (Diwali review). Estate planning is not morbidity; it's the final WM gear — PROTECT, extended past your own existence.

## 🧪 LAB — Pilot the Iyer Retirement (10 min)

**Setup:** Suresh Iyer 60, retiring this month: corpus ₹2.8Cr (equity 60% = 1.68Cr, debt/EPF ₹1.12Cr), NPS tail gives ₹14k/mo annuity from 62, no other income. Monthly need ₹75k (₹9L/yr). Wife 57, house owned, kids independent. Family history: long-lived (85+).

**Do this:**
1. Test sustainability: SWR = 9/280 = 3.21% — pass? Stress at inflation jumps to 7%.
2. Build the 3 buckets with amounts, instruments, and exact refill rules.
3. Set the SWP: which fund redeems first, expected tax in year 1 (gains portion small — explain why), and the annual raise policy.
4. Two-estate defects: nominations say "father" (deceased 2015) on the Demat from his 30s; no will. Fix list + the conversation nobody wants to have.
5. **The offer:** a bank RM pitches "guaranteed 9.25%/yr immediate annuity for the full ₹2.8Cr." Evaluate with the annuity-vs-SWP lens (tax, inflation, inheritance). Decision and script.

**Why this matters:** Retirement is where every prior module either pays off or collects — FI math, allocation, protection, tax, TVM. The Iyer case is deliberately *normal* (₹2.8Cr is achievable for the Sharma-file discipline): plans that work for normal cases are the profession. The lab also drills the single most-sold retirement product in India (immediate annuities) so you can defend parents properly at the dinner table.

**🔑 Lab answers:**
1. 3.21% SWR — **pass** at the 3-3.5% band, aided by the ₹14k NPS floor (covers 19% of need: effective equity-side SWR ≈ 2.5%). At 7% inflation the raise treadmill tightens: first-decade corpus outflow at real − returns spread narrows; safety valve = the bucket refill rules + willingness to flat-line the raise in any −20% equity year. Verdict: sustainable with discipline, not with autopilot.
2. **B1 ₹27L** (3y × ₹9L) liquid fund/FD ladder; **B2 ₹56L** short-duration debt + 5y RBI bonds class (~7-8%); **B3 ≈ ₹1.97Cr** equity (he can hold ~60-65% equity — the NPS floor + long genes argue for a *higher* B3 than textbook 50%; the buckets exist precisely to permit that). Refill rules: annual Diwali review; B3 skim only when yearly return > +8%; B1 always topped to 3y; B2 refilled from B3 skims; **any year equity < 0%: zero B3 sales, B1+B2 absorb (9y of runway).**
3. SWP ₹75k/mo from the equity flexi (or B1 drawdown with B2 refills per the rules). Year-1 tax: redeeming ₹9L of a ₹1.68Cr equity base — gains portion ≈ (growth share, say 35-40%) ≈ ₹3.2-3.6L; LTCG applies > 12m holdings: 12.5% × (gains − 1.25L exempt) ≈ **₹24-29k total** — vs the ₹9L annuity alternative taxed at slab ≈ **₹1.9L** — SWP's tax edge in early years is an order of magnitude (principal comes home untaxed; only gains pay).
4. Fix list: (1) Update every nomination (Demat → wife; MFs, insurance, EPF rechecks: bank FDs too) — nominee = custodian until the will speaks; (2) **Registered will**: 50/50 kids after spouse, executor named, guardianship moot (kids grown), witnesses & probate-city logic; (3) letter of instruction + the WM1 dashboard shared with wife + one kid; (4) joint "either or survivor" conversion on operating accounts. The conversation: sit the family down ONCE — estate secrecy is how ₹2.8Cr becomes a Netflix-style court saga over ₹500 stamps.
5. The 9.25% immediate annuity: ₹2.8Cr → ₹25.9L/yr for life, taxed at slab (~30% band → ~₹18L net), **flat forever** — no inflation step: ₹25.9L this year equals ~₹13L purchasing power by year 12 at 6% (and ~₹9L by 20y) — quietly hiring inflation as your expense-raiser while the insurer keeps the corpus; nothing to heirs on death. Verdict: **decline for the whole corpus** — it's the Double-Plan in senior clothing. If the sleep-at-night urge persists, annuitize a CAP slice (≤ 20%, ₹56L → ~₹5.2L/yr floor + NPS ₹14k/mo + SWP rest: floor-layered, corpus largely intact, heirs protected). Script for the RM: *"Show me the inflation-indexed option and the death-benefit schedule; I'll compare against a 3.2% inflation-stepped SWP at 24% tax advantage. If yours still wins on paper in year-10 purchasing power, we talk."* (It won't.)

## 💪 Exercises

1. **SWR compute.** Corpus ₹4.5Cr, need ₹1.6L/mo, no floors. SWR? Pass/stress verdict; the corpus or the lifestyle must move — quantify both fixes.
2. **Bucket design.** ₹1.5Cr, need ₹45k/mo, very low willingness. Buckets + the equity cap logic.
3. **Sequence horror math.** Two retirees, same 8% average return: one starts with +25%,+15%,−20%; other with −20%,+15%,+25%. Same ₹1L/mo SWP on ₹3Cr. Explain in two sentences why one dies early (use PM7's drawdown law, no table needed).
4. **SWP tax.** Redeeming ₹12L/yr from a corpus where NAV grew 2× (cost half): gains portion, LTCG after exemption at 12.5%? Compare to fixed deposit interest ₹8L at 30% slab.
5. **Estate audit.** List every defect: single-name Demat no nomination; FD nominees "mother" deceased; property purchased 2009, no will, two sons abroad; oral promise of flat to daughter. Fix in priority order.

### ✅ Selected answers

1. SWR = 19.2/450 = **4.27%** — **fail** (>3.5% band). Fixes: corpus → 19.2/0.035 = **₹5.49Cr** (₹1Cr gap = ~4-6 more working years at Sharma-level SIPs accumulating ~₹20-25L/yr plus growth), or lifestyle → 450 × 3.5% = **₹15.75L/yr (₹1.31L/mo)** — an ₹29k/mo lifestyle trim, or a hybrid (part-time income ₹5L/yr restores the bands instantly). The SWR is a budget: corpus, lifestyle, or dates — someone pays.
2. **B1 ₹16.2L** (3y), **B2 ₹37.8L** (y4-10), **B3 ₹96L** — 64% equity is too hot for very low willingness: cap B3 skims aggressively (skim > +6% years) and consider **35-40% equity cap** with the rest to B2 extension (y11-15 debt ladder 7%): survival of the PLAN beats maximization of the corpus — a 40% equity portfolio funding 3.6% SWR with an 8% blended return still outlives 30y comfortably; re-run WM2's tie-break before copying the Iyer numbers.
3. At withdrawal, **early losses are worth ~3× late losses** of equal percentage: the −20% first hits ₹3Cr while ₹12L/yr keeps leaving (drawdown law: −20% needs +25% to heal *on the reduced base*, and the withdrawals deny the healing). Both sequences average 8% — same brochure — but the second retiree's corpus dips toward ₹2.3Cr and compounds on a gutted base, dying ~12-15 years earlier.
4. NAV 2× ⇒ each ₹1 redeemed = ₹0.50 principal + ₹0.50 gain → gains portion ₹6L; LTCG = 12.5% × (6 − 1.25) = **₹59.4k**. FD interest ₹8L × 30% = **₹2.4L**. The SWP delivers 50% more cash for 75% less tax — equity withdrawal elegance in one subtraction.
5. (1) **Will today** (all four defects flow from its absence; registered, executor + guardian-if-minors clause, witnessed); (2) nominations everywhere: Demat, FDs (re-nominate surviving spouse/kids — nominee dead ⇒ class-I heirs + paperwork war); (3) Demat → add joint holder + transmission-ready records; (4) the oral flat promise is a family landmine — put it (or its correction) IN the will in writing: courts enforce documents, not dinner-table sentences; sons abroad ⇒ also notarize + keep one original with the executor and scan to the letter-of-instruction vault.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** The India-honest safe withdrawal band is:
(a) 6-7% — FD rates say so
(b) 3-3.5% — 10.7% blend minus 6% inflation minus volatility drag; ₹3Cr quietly supports ~₹9-10L/yr
(c) 2% — never touch principal
(d) 10% if markets are good

**Q2.** The 3-bucket defense defeats sequence risk because:
(a) buckets earn different returns
(b) buckets 1-2 carry 9+ years of withdrawals, so the equity engine (B3) is never *forced* to sell into a crash — volatility becomes refillable noise, not a death spiral
(c) it eliminates market risk entirely
(d) SEBI mandates buckets at 60

**Q3.** The ₹2.8Cr-for-9.25%-annuity pitch should be declined chiefly because:
(a) annuities are illegal past 65
(b) the payout is slab-taxed, flat forever (real value halves roughly every 12 years at 6% inflation), and the corpus (plus everything for heirs) belongs to the insurer on death
(c) 9.25% is below FD rates
(d) SWPs are commission-free always

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — the 4% US rule assumed US inflation-realities; India's 6% treadmill plus deeper drawdown culture puts the honest band at 3-3.5% with bucket discipline. Above the band, plans only survive brochure markets — and brochures don't raise children.
2. **(b)** — sequence risk is *forced* selling at bad prices; the buckets remove the gun from volatility's hand for 9+ years — longer than any Indian bear market on record. The equity sleeve goes from payroll to pension fund: it refills, on the plan's schedule, from strength.
3. **(b)** — triple underperformance: tax at slab vs SWP's gains-only-12.5%, zero inflation step vs the 6% raise rule, zero estate vs the corpus-plus-heirs intact. Annuities fit as a slim floor-layer (≤20%), never as the whole retirement — the RM's 9.25% is five years of comfort exchanged for twenty years of erosion witnessed visibly.
