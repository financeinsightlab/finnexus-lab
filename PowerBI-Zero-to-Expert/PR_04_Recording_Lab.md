## 📘 Concepts

### 4.1 The zero-budget studio

Quality speech recording needs only physics:

| Setup element | The rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Distance** | One fist (12–15 cm) from phone mic | Closer = mouth noise + pops; farther = room echo |
| **Angle** | Slightly **off-axis** (45°), at cheek level pointing at mouth | Direct breath blasts cause "pop" distortion on B/P sounds |
| **Room** | Smallest furnished room; curtains/bed/books = free acoustic treatment | Bare walls tile-bounce = bathroom sound |
| **Machine silence** | Fan/AC off, phone on **airplane mode** (no notification pings), door sign "recording" | Hums and buzzes destroy otherwise-good takes |
| **Stability** | Phone propped on books (never held — hand noise travels) | Consistency between takes |

**The 30-second room-tone test:** record silence in your setup. Play it back: any hum/fan/door = fix before real takes. Pros call this *recording the room*; we call it *saving the afternoon*.

### 4.2 File discipline (60 seconds that save hours)

- **Naming:** `YYYYMMDD_what_takeN` → `20260723_ic5_greadout_take2`. Searchable forever.
- **Format:** whatever the phone recorder gives (m4a/mp3/wav) is fine for practice — quality differences matter at broadcast level, not coaching level. Don't burn willpower converting files.
- **Backup ritual:** weekly, dump the month's takes to one folder/drive. Your *month-old* take is the progress proof you'll want in mock-interview season (your own before/after ↗ motivation).
- **Length budget:** 30–120 seconds per take. Longer = impossible to review honestly.

### 4.3 The 3-take method (the pro loop)

| Take | Mode | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Take 1 — Cold** | No prep; read/speak as-is | Captures your *baseline habits* (fossils visible!) |
| **Take 2 — Marked** | Thought groups + stress marks visible (PR3); slow slightly | Applies technique consciously |
| **Take 3 — Performed** | Marks away except one keyword card; treat it as real audience | Technique + energy at performance level |

⚠️ **Never go past take 3 in one sitting.** You're training a habit, not chasing a perfect file — take 12 obsession = diminishing returns + voice fatigue. Tomorrow gets a fresh take 1. (Rule from IC4: improvement cadence, not perfection chase.)

### 4.4 The 5-axis self-review rubric

Score each take /5 per axis (total /25); journal one line per axis:

| Axis | 1 = | 5 = |
|---|---|---|
| **Clarity** | words lost, endings swallowed | every word lands, -ed endings audible |
| **Stress** | robotic equal beats | content words punched, function shrunk |
| **Rhythm & pauses** | run-on marathon | clean thought groups, golden pause on key terms |
| **Tone** | rising endings, monotone | falling answers, smile audible, lists rise-rise-fall |
| **Fluency** | filler storms (umm, actually) | <6 fillers per minute, smooth linking |

**The error bank (your SoundBank extension — one note):** format `date · take · fossil · fix`. E.g. *23 Jul · take1 · rising end on result line · land it, pause*. Review bank weekly: if the same fossil appears 3× → that's next week's ONE fix (one at a time, GD4's daily-fix rule).

### 4.5 Mic manners for interviews & calls

- **Devices:** wired-earphone mic or laptop mic both fine — but **test 5 minutes before**, and keep the phone on table (in hand = wobble noise).
- **Placement:** earphone mic at chin, not rubbing collar; laptop mic: don't lean toward the fan vent.
- **Call-opening script:** *"Good morning — can you hear me clearly?"* — one line = professional; mid-call garble script: *"I think we lost 5 seconds — repeating from [landmark]."* Calm rewinds beat panicked re-runs.
- **Never eat, sip loudly, or type unmuted.** On panels, mute between your turns (IC3) — and announce you're switching camera/device if tech changes: *"One moment, switching to earphones."* — narrating tech = composed; silently fighting tech = chaos visible.
- **Your recordings double-role:** The clips you make in this course are your library. Share your best 60-second intro with friends for feedback — real ears catch what self-review misses.

## 💪 Exercises

1. Your recording sounds "bathroom-ish" and pops on every B/P. Name the two setup fixes.
2. Write a 5-axis review of a fictional awful take (1s everywhere) — then describe what its Take-2 fixes would be, axis by axis.
3. Draft your error-bank entry format (one exact line of text).
4. Virtual interview, 2 minutes to start, and your earphones crackle. Script the next 120 seconds.

### ✅ Selected answers

1. Bathroom-ness = bare-room echo → move to a furnished room (or add cushions/curtains); pops = breath on-axis → slide mic to 45° off-axis at fist distance.
2. Sample: Clarity 1 (endings gone) → slow to enunciate; Stress 1 (equal beats) → mark content words, punch them; Rhythm 1 (no pauses) → slash thought groups; Tone 1 (everything rises) → practice falls; Fluency 1 (filler storm) → PR3 pause replaces "umm".
3. Model: `2026-07-23 · ic5_greadout_take1 · rising tone on the 252% line + endings swallowed · land the number falling, exaggerate -t endings`.
4. 0:00 notice crackle → 0:10 switch to phone speaker/laptop mic → 0:30 test aloud with 3 words → 0:45 announce: *"Testing one-two — switched microphones, can you hear me now?"* → 1:30 positive, proceed calm. Total drama: zero; total professionalism: high.

## ❓ Quiz

1. The 3-take method forbids take 12 in one sitting because…
2. In the self-review rubric, the Fluency axis's fix for fillers is…
3. "Can you hear me clearly?" at call start works because…

### ✅ Answers

1. **We train habits, not perfect files** — diminishing returns + voice fatigue; tomorrow's fresh take 1 is the real training. (Same logic as one-fix-per-day in GD4's journal.)
2. **The pause.** Replace "umm/actually" with a 200 ms silence — silences sound confident; fillers sound evasive. Count them per minute (<6 target).
3. It **invites one calibrating correction at the cheapest moment** — discovering garbled audio 12 minutes in costs the whole interview; checking line 1 costs a second and builds trust.
