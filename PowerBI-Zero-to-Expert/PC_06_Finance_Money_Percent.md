## 📘 Concepts

### 6.1 Interest = percentages with a calendar (SI/CI as multipliers)

**Simple Interest** = Principal × Rate × Time ÷ 100 — a % machine with a year-count: ₹1,00,000 at 8% for 3 years → SI = 1,00,000 × 8 × 3/100 = **₹24,000** (FD-style \"8% of principal per year, paid flat\").

**Compound Interest** = the PC3 growth chain: ×(1+r)^n. Same ₹1,00,000 at 8% CI, 3 years: ×1.08³ = 1.2597 → **₹25,971 interest** — ₹1,971 MORE than SI. The gap = \"interest on interest\" = PC3's ab/100 compounding at scale.
Quick CI table (memorize for speed): 1.1²=1.21 · 1.1³=1.331 · 1.05²=1.1025 · 1.08²=1.1664 · 1.08³=1.2597.
**The \"difference\" classic:** CI − SI for 2 years = P×(r/100)² (the compounding term alone): ₹1,00,000@8% → 640. For 3 years the formula lengthens — ladder it (1.2597… use the table).

### 6.2 Recovery ladder in real money talk (+ ad hygiene)

Your equity fund falls −25%: needed recovery **+33.3%** (PC2 ladder). A \"−50% then +50% year pair\" ad claims \"flat performance\" — truth: ×0.5×1.5 = **0.75 → still −25% down** (AV7's CAGR trap, PC-cousin). **Analyst reflex:** convert any return stream to ONE multiplier and compound from there; arithmetic means of returns are marketing, geometric are money.

### 6.3 Discount economics (where margins bleed)

**Successive ≠ additive:** 20% + 10% off = survivor 0.8×0.9 = 0.72 → **28% net** (PC3). ShopKart's Diwali \"20%+10%\" tag legally ≠ \"30% off\" — and customers FEEL it's bigger. Both facts matter: marketing gets punch, finance keeps truth.

**Margin compression (the module's core insight):** ShopKart sells an item at ₹100 with COGS ₹71 (gross margin 29%, FY25 exact). A 10% store discount:
```
New price ₹90 · COGS still ₹71 → gross profit ₹29 → ₹19
Margin: 29% → 21.1% · Gross ₹ per unit DOWN (29−19)/29 = 34.5%!!
```
A 10% price cut eats **over a third of gross profit** on a 29%-margin store. On the 2.4% NET margin, it's existential: ₹6.7L FY25 net ÷ 280L — each 1% blanket discount ≈ ₹2.8L = **42% of yearly net**. Small percentages of price, giant percentages of profit. This asymmetry IS retail finance.

### 6.4 GST mechanics (forward + reverse + chained)

- Forward: ₹47,250 + 18% = ₹55,755 (FF-221 — fourth time you've computed it; 2 seconds now).
- Reverse (inclusive bill): ₹59,000 ÷ 1.18 = **₹50,000** base (never \"−18% = 48,380\" — PC5 trap 5).
- Chained: \"10% discount, THEN 18% GST on the discounted value\": MRP 10,000 → 9,000 → ×1.18 = **₹10,620** final. Order matters; GST always applies to the transaction value (discounted), which is why invoice math chains discount-then-tax.

### 6.5 Inflation & real returns (money's silent haircut)

Nominal return 7% FD with 6% inflation → **real ≈ 1%** (quick: 7−6; precise: 1.07/1.06 − 1 = 0.94%). Money illusion demo: salary +8% in 6% inflation = \"richer\" by only ~1.9% really. ShopKart context: if grocery inflation runs 5% and ShopKart holds prices flat, its REAL revenue falls 5% on identical volumes — flat nominal ≠ flat real. Exams ask \"real growth\"; life asks louder.

### 6.6 CAPSTONE — The ShopKart Price-War Audit 🏆

**Situation (Q2-FY26):** A quick-commerce app drops grocery prices 15% across ShopKart's catchments. Q2 revenue ₹71.8L, blended gross margin 29%, net margin ~2.4% (3.85L net would annualize ~15L run-rate on ~72L×4... the family wants options, numbers, and a decision brief).

**Three modeled responses (annualized store impact per ₹100L revenue):**

| Option | Price action | New GM% | Units needed to hold gross ₹ | Verdict driver |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HOLD | none | 29% | 100% (units at risk to rival) | Brand/service moat |
| CUT 10% | −10% price | (29−10)/90 = **21.1%** | 29/19 = **+52.6% units** | Must nearly 1.5× volume! |
| SMART BUNDLE | −5% on 60% of lines | ≈ (29−3)/97 ≈ 26.8% | 29/26 ≈ **+11.5% units** | Targeted defense |

**Break-even volume formula (the star):** required unit-lift = old GP / new GP = 29/(29 − discount-in-points-adjusted). The audit's answer: **a blanket 10% cut needs +52.6% unit growth to keep the same gross ₹ — a fantasy in 3 months; the SMART BUNDLE at +11.5% is the only survivable counter** (and pair it with the FF-221-style Net-30 vendor discipline to claw 1-pt COGS = ₹2.8L — from the DI7 finding!).

**Deliverables checklist:**
1. The 3-option table rebuilt from scratch (all multiplier lines shown).
2. Margin-compression one-liner (\"10% off = 34.5% of gross profit gone\").
3. Net-impact estimate: blanket cut ≈ −₹10L/yr on 100L-scale → net → NEGATIVE (2.4% net can't host a 21.1%-gross world… the audit must SAY so).
4. Vendor-claw addendum: renegotiate FF to −1% COGS = +₹2.8L ≈ softens the bundle cost.
5. **60-second board brief** (recorded): recommendation = SMART BUNDLE + vendor claw + loyalty push; ONE caveat (rival may re-cut; revisit quarterly).

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** ₹1,00,000 @ 8% for 2 years: CI − SI =
- (a) ₹0
- (b) **₹640 — the 'interest on interest' term P(r/100)²; SI pays 8% flat ×2 (₹16,000), CI's ×1.08² pays ₹16,640**
- (c) ₹1,600
- (d) ₹320

**Q2.** A ₹100 item with ₹71 COGS gets a 10% discount. The damage is:
- (a) 10% off profit
- (b) **Gross profit falls ₹29→₹19 = −34.5% (margin 29%→21.1%): small price-% eats giant profit-% — the retail asymmetry every analyst must quote cold**
- (c) Margin falls exactly 10 points
- (d) COGS rises 10%

**Q3.** A quick-commerce rival cuts 15%; your blanket 10% counter needs what unit-growth to hold gross ₹?
- (a) 10%
- (b) **+52.6% — old-GP/new-GP = 29/19; near-1.5× volume in a quarter is fantasy, so the audit backs the targeted bundle (+11.5% need) + a 1-pt COGS claw (₹2.8L) from vendor renegotiation**
- (c) 15%
- (d) 5%

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — P(r/100)² for 2 years, ladder for longer. This single difference-line answers the most-asked CI exam classic in 5 seconds.
2. **(b)** — price-% ≠ profit-%. 10% of price = 34.5% of gross = ~42% of net-per-point. Quote this in the audit and the room goes quiet (the good kind).
3. **(b)** — the break-even volume formula. Blanket cuts on 29%-gross/2.4%-net retailers are theater; survival = targeted bundle + vendor claw + discipline. YOU just did real management consulting with PC3 multipliers.
