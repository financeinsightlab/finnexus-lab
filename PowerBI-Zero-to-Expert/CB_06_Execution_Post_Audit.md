## 📘 Concepts

### 6.1 The sanction memo — the contract with the future

One page, signed, filed: (1) strategic rationale (one line, honest); (2) incremental flows with the CB2 blocks; (3) hurdle with receipt (WACC 12.14% target weights, divisional where relevant); (4) NPV/IRR/MIRR/payback + PI if rationed; (5) **the swing driver + evidence plan** (CB4 decree: "savings ≥ ₹2.24L/yr measured by Q2 meter"); (6) **options booked**: abandon at ₹X salvage trigger, expand at gate Y, delay rights; (7) named owner + milestones + **audit date at sanction, not at convenience**. The memo weaponizes institutional memory: six months later, when scope creep asks for "+30% more," the memoboard reads its own handwriting.

### 6.2 Overrun physics — why 2x is the base rate

Reference-class evidence (Flyvbjerg's infrastructure canon, adapted to corporate capex): large projects overrun cost **~1.5-2x** and schedule ~1.6x on average — driven by optimism bias (sponsors underprice their own projects), scope creep mid-build, and strategic misrepresentation (lowball to clear the hurdle, escalate after). Counters: **(1) reference-class pricing** — budget from the OUTSIDE view ("what did our last 5 store builds cost per sq ft?") before trusting the inside-engineering view; **(2) contingency as a priced line** (10-20% by project novelty — dark-store #1: 20%; machine #8: 8%), drawn only via change-board, not casually; **(3) staged tranches** — sanction ₹X now, milestone-gated rest (options from CB4 hardwired into governance); **(4) fixed-price contracting** for the components where vendors can bear risk better than you.

### 6.3 The incentive machine — optimism bias vs sandbagging

Two equal-and-opposite viruses: **optimism bias** (aggressive projections to win sanction — straight-line IRR 12.1% at hurdle 12%, CB3's game #1) and **sandbagging** (managers padding budgets to guarantee "under-promise/over-deliver" glory). Both corrupt the same input: forecast integrity. The cure is the **post-audit loop with memory**: every sanctioned project's variance report cites its sponsor; the committee tracks sponsor accuracy as a metric (3-year hit-rates; CFO's office keeps the ledger). Incentives follow: accuracy rewarded in bonuses, fairy tales priced into future reviews of that sponsor's decks. FA/BF lesson retold in org-language: people do what their incentives observe, so make accuracy observable.

### 6.4 The ShopKart month-12 audit — actuals vs model

The ₹22.5L program, one year live: capex came in at **₹24.8L (+10.2%, contingency partially drawn via change-board: one chiller replacement + civil extras)**; sales lift vs old-store trend: **+₹58L annualized vs the +₹84L deck plan (69% attainment)**; measured spoilage savings running **₹2.3L/yr (≥ the ₹2.24L breakeven ✓ — the CB4 decree held)**; WC build ₹19L vs ₹17.0L modeled (CCC drifted to 80d on festive overstock — RT3 monitor flags). **Verdict: amber-on-track.** The stores ramp 6 months slower than the deck promised (the classic base-rate), savings discipline outperformed, WC discipline slipped (+₹3.3L trapped vs model — corrective: replenishment tune per RT3's 70-day DIO target). The audit's outputs: (a) re-forecast NPV (+0.6L → −0.4L honest mid-model → actionable, not fatal: the put stays sheathed), (b) two correction tickets with owners, (c) one calibration note for the NEXT deck: ShopKart ramp curves minus 6 months, permanently.

### 6.5 Kill and scale triggers — the governance of good exits

The CB4 options only exist if governance executes them: **abandon trigger** = swing driver below breakeven for 2 consecutive quarters WITH salvage > continue-PV (execute the put, report as *plan working*, not failure — the ₹7.5L dark-store put is a success story the day it's exercised); **scale trigger** = demand gates hit ± evidence bar (release the expansion tranche). Also the kill criteria for the *proposal stage*: NPV < 0 under the honest-model (no rescue by optimism), swing evidence impossible to produce, or a misapplied divisional hurdle (CB3 game #2 rerun). Capital discipline's deepest cultural marker: **a company that can kill its own beautiful projects on schedule.**

## 🧪 LAB — Write the Audit Report Yourself (10 min)

**Setup:** You are ShopKart's capex controller. Month-12 actuals (vs deck): capex ₹24.8L vs ₹22.5L; sales lift +₹58L vs +₹84L; spoilage savings ₹2.3L vs ≥₹2.24L decree; WC build ₹19L vs ₹17.0L; CCC 80d vs 74d; sponsor remains bullish, requests +₹6L more for "marketing push" at the same stores.

**Do this:**
1. Score each item — green/amber/red — with one line each.
2. Recompute the rough remaining NPV picture: is the put (sell fixtures ~₹9L today, salvaging the store at abandonment) in or out of the money vs a continue-PV of ~₹14L at trend?
3. Rule on the +₹6L marketing request under the governance lens (which tranche-staging / evidence rules apply).
4. Write the two correction tickets (owner + metric + date).
5. Write the calibration note for the next expansion deck in one sentence.

**Why this matters:** This lab is the job: controllers don't build most models — they keep builders honest. The month-12 audit is where capital budgeting stops being spreadsheet sport and becomes company memory. Write this report well and the next ₹22.5L program arrives already 10% more truthful than the last.

**🔑 Lab answers:**
1. **Capex +10.2%: AMBER** — contingency drawn via proper change-board for real items (chiller failure + civil), inside the 10-20% band; next time price civil at reference-class not blueprint. **Sales lift 69%: AMBER-RED** — the classic 6-month ramp optimism; the deck's own ramp calibration was issued and ignored. **Savings ₹2.3L: GREEN** — decree held with evidence (metering works; procurement wins a point). **WC +₹3.3L/CCC 80d: RED** — festive overstock habit; the growth tax grew fangs.
2. Salvage ₹9L vs continue-PV ₹14L at trend → **put is OUT of the money by ₹5L → continue** (and the audit says so explicitly: governance documents the put's price at each review — that's what "binding the option in the memo" means).
3. **DEFER pending evidence:** +₹6L is a fresh capital tranche, not opex noise — require (a) measurable sales-response pilot (₹1L in ONE store for 45 days, matched against a control store), (b) staged release (₹2L only if pilot lift ≥ 1.5× the flowed breakeven), (c) sponsor co-signature on the variance line. The +₹6L "push" as proposed is the optimism-bias sequel — greenlit frameworks beat greenlit money.
4. **Ticket 1 (Owner: Replenishment Lead):** cut DIO from 78 to ≤70 days by Q2 via festive-overstock caps + vendor-signal integration (RT3 target: ₹198.8L COGS × 8d/365 ≈ **₹4.4L cash release** replenishing the WC hole). **Ticket 2 (Owner: Expansion PM):** publish the ramp-curve calibration (actual −6 months vs deck) as the mandatory reference class for all 2027 store decks; sponsor sign-off filed with the controller.
5. *"Calibrate every future ShopKart expansion deck with: ramp = announced ramp minus six months, contingency 15% for first-of-kind builds, WC at the 80-day observed CCC until replenishment evidence proves 70 — or don't bring the deck."*

## 💪 Exercises

1. **Memo completeness.** The sanction memo for the +₹6L marketing push (had it been approved) needs which six elements, minimum?
2. **Reference-class drill.** Your last 4 store builds cost ₹4.1L, ₹4.6L, ₹4.0L, ₹4.8L per unit. The new deck claims ₹3.2L/unit thanks to "new vendor relationships." The outside-view budget and challenge line?
3. **Virus ID.** (a) Sponsor strips 15% contingency "to make IRR clear the hurdle"; (b) manager pads OCF estimates down 20% "to be safe"; (c) committee approves anyway as custom. Virus and counter for each.
4. **Kill discipline.** A store's swing driver (daily footfalls 620 vs breakeven 780) has missed 2 straight quarters; continue-PV ₹11L vs salvage ₹14.5L. The governance action and the sentence for the town hall.
5. **Sponsor ledger.** After 3 years, sponsor hit-rates: A 2.9/3.0 (inflated decks later corrected), B 1.1/1.0, C 0.7/1.0. How does the committee price future decks from each sponsor, in one line each?

### ✅ Selected answers

1. (1) strategic rationale + referenced deck number; (2) incremental flows (the ₹6L and the response curve, control-store logic inside); (3) hurdle receipt + NPV/IRR/payback recomputed; (4) swing driver (response lift ≥ breakeven 1.5×) + evidence/measurement plan with dates; (5) options: abandon-at-pilot-fail + scale-at-gate; (6) owner + milestones + **audit date committed at sanction**.
2. Outside-view budget ≈ median ₹4.35L/unit + 10% novelty = **~₹4.8L/unit carried into the deck.** Challenge line: *"Vendor-relationship savings appear in our model AFTER two invoiced builds prove them at scale; until then the reference class prices the project at ₹4.8L — the vendor can earn the deck's trust with invoices, not the other way around."*
3. (a) **optimism-bias-by-contingency-theft** → counter: contingency priced by risk-class table, fenced behind change-board; decks can't delete it, only reclassify it. (b) **sandbagging** → counter: variance ledger tracks the padding (a manager "beating" estimates by 20% every time is forecasting 20% wrong, annually, on purpose) plus sponsor hit-rate pricing in future reviews. (c) **committee rubber-stamping** → counter: accountability inversion — the committee's own approval hit-rate gets reviewed annually by the audit committee; nobody in the loop escapes the loop.
4. Action: **exercise the abandon put** (salvage 14.5 > continue 11 → +₹3.5L of realized option value over persistence). Town-hall sentence: *"We killed a store the way our memo said we would, recovering ₹14.5L of the ₹18L spent — the put clause was cheaper than hope, and the same discipline is why your next project gets sanctioned at all."* (CB4's ₹7.5L counter-example taught: companies that never kill anything kill their capital slowly instead.)
5. A: *"Decks from Sponsor A carry a standing 25-35% optimism haircut before modeling — or triple evidence requirements."* B: *"Sponsor B's decks flow through standard review; hit-rate 1.1 earns the fast lane."* C: *"Sponsor C's decks require a red-team and reference-class budget attached on arrival; the 0.7 rate is not attitude, it's arithmetic with a signature."*

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** The ShopKart month-12 audit red-flag was:
(a) capex +10.2%
(b) WC build ₹19L vs ₹17.0L modeled with CCC slipping to 80d — festive overstock habits compounding the growth tax; the savings decree held green
(c) sales at 69% of plan, which was expected
(d) the chiller replacement

**Q2.** With continue-PV ₹14L vs salvage ₹9L, the correct governance call on the dark-store put is:
(a) exercise it — salvage is cash
(b) stay — the put is ₹5L out of the money; document its price at every review so exercising is arithmetic the day it flips, not a mood the day it storms
(c) hold a vote of store managers
(d) exercise only if sales fall 50%

**Q3.** The best governance response to a +₹6L "marketing push" at the same stores is:
(a) approve — momentum matters
(b) defer under tranche-staging: pilot ₹1L/45d with a control store, staged ₹2L releases on evidence, sponsor co-signature on variance
(c) reject forever and scold publicly
(d) fund fully from contingency

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — the ramp miss (69%) was calibration-typical amber, capex overruns inside band via change-board stay amber; the WC/CCC slip is the red because working-capital sins compound silently (RT3 growth tax was modeled, observed, and then exceeded anyway). The audit exists to name exactly this.
2. **(b)** — options are exercised on prices, not vibes: salvage 9 < continue 14 means persistence wins today, and the memo-bound review cadence guarantees the flip gets caught if footfalls decay further. Executive discipline = the put's strike printed in every review pack.
3. **(b)** — the pilot structure converts an opinion ("push works") into evidence at 1/6th the price, with tranche gates protecting the rest. Governance isn't smaller ambition; it's ambition with receipts — defer-pending-evidence IS the approval path, not the block.
