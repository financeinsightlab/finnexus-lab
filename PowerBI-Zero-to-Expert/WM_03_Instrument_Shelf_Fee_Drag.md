# 🎯 WM3 · The Instrument Shelf — and the Fee That Eats a Fifth of Your Retirement

> Six shelves hold 95% of what an Indian family ever needs: index funds, EPF/PPF, NPS, gold, real estate, and an FD parking slot. Everything else is either decoration or a trap wearing a brochure. This module prices each shelf honestly — and names the quiet assassin: the 1% annual fee that compounds into 20% of your corpus.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Map the six shelves: what each instrument is FOR (horizon, tax, liquidity)
- Prove the fee-drag canon: 1%/yr ≈ 19.8% of a 30-year corpus
- Rank equity access: index (core) → flexi/active (satellite) → direct stocks (opinion)
- Price the government shelf: EPF ~8.25%, PPF 7.1%, NPS 60/40 with annuity tail
- Honest real-estate & gold math: rental yield ~2-3% vs EMI, SGB vs ETF vs jewellery

## 📘 Concepts

### 3.1 The shelf map — instruments are jobs, not brands

| Shelf | Job | Horizon | Recent rates* | Wealth-manager note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Index equity funds | Growth engine (core) | 7y+ | equity long-run 11-13% nominal | 0.1-0.2% TER; own the whole market, skip the heroics |
| EPF / PPF | Tax-free debt anchor | 15-40y | ~8.25% / 7.1% govt-set | EEE tax status; the only "free lunch" New Delhi hands salaried India |
| NPS | Retirement wrapper | to 60 | market-linked | Extra ₹50k deduction (80CCD1B); 60% lump at 60, 40% forced annuity (the tail to plan around) |
| Gold (SGB > ETF > coin) | 5-7% insurance/asset, not return engine | 5y+ | SGB 2.5% coupon + price | SGB: tax-free maturity, fresh issues paused in recent budgets (secondary market); jewellery = 6-30% making-charge loss, an adornment, not an investment |
| Real estate | Shelter first, investment second | 10y+ | rental yield ~2-3% metros | ₹2Cr flat renting at ₹35k/mo = 2.1% yield vs home loan 9%: leverage against a 2.1% yield NEEDS price growth to win (TV3's honest ledger) |
| FD / liquid funds | Parking & emergency | 0-3y | 6.5-7.5% | Post-tax real −1.37% canon (TV5): a waiting room, never a destination |

*rates drift quarterly; the STRUCTURE is the lesson, not the decimal.

### 3.2 The fee-drag canon — the worm in the fruit

Same ₹10k SIP, 30 years: at 12% gross → factor 3,495; at 11% (a 1% fee skimmed) → factor 2,804. **Difference: 19.8% of the corpus — a full fifth of retirement, donated one basis point at a time.** It scales: a 1.5% drag gap ≈ 27% of terminal wealth. Now armed, review the battlefield: regular-plan mutual funds carry ~1% distribution commission annually FOREVER (direct plans don't); ULIPs and endowments hide 3-6% first-year commissions plus ongoing charges; PMS/AIF fees + profit shares (16/52-week carnivals, BF4). The counter-move is unglamorous: **direct plans, index cores, and reading the TER line like a credit officer.** Nobody sends you a bill for the drag — that's precisely its business model.

### 3.3 Equity access ladder — match the rung to the skill

**Rung 1 — index funds (core):** Nifty 50 / broad flexi; 20-year SPIVA-style verdict: most active funds underperform after fees (PM5's tribunal: Sharpe 0.44 winners are rare and rarely persistent). **Rung 2 — active funds (satellite):** only with a written thesis for WHY this manager persists (process > past returns; CE Ramos' analysis desk over WhatsApp tips, BF/IN2 canon). **Rung 3 — direct stocks:** a legitimate hobby inside the ≤30% satellite cap — it now requires the full RT/CF8 kit (DuPont, forensics, valuation) you already own; without them it's astrology with a Demat login. The ladder's one rule: **climb only as far as your kit and temperament certify — the core carries the plan regardless.**

### 3.4 Gold & real estate — the family darlings, adult supervision

**Gold:** 5-7% strategic sleeve; SGB (where available) pays 2.5% coupon + price upside + tax-free maturity — strictly dominant over coins (3% GST + making charges + locker rent) and roughly tied with ETFs (cheaper storage, LTCG applies). **Real estate:** the honest ledger — self-occupied home = consumption + forced saving + stability (own it on those grounds, proudly); *investment* property must clear: rental yield + expected appreciation − costs − loan drag ≥ equity blend with LESS effort (a 2-3% yield needs ~7-8%/yr appreciation to tie a 10.7% blend — possible in pockets, impossible as a default). Add liquidity risk (6-18 months to exit) and the ₹2Cr-flat-at-₹35k-rent exhibit ends most debates.

### 3.5 Parking slots — liquidity as a feature

Emergency fund (₹7.5L Verma canon) lives in **liquid funds / FD ladder** — never equity, never locked instruments: the parking slot's job is *presence*, not performance (RT4's ALM lesson retold at home). Short-goal money (house down payment 1-3y out): short-duration debt funds / FDs — TV7's address discipline applied to product codes. The complete shelf, assembled: equity for far goals, government wrappers for taxed debt, SGB/ETF for the hedge sleeve, property for living in, FD/liquid for waiting — and an invoice demanded of every fee.

## 🧪 LAB — Stock the Verma Shelf (10 min)

**Setup:** Vermas (WM2): 60% equity ceiling, ₹13.5k/mo, goals: college 14y ₹33.8L, retirement 26y ₹3.76Cr. He qualifies for EPF (₹5k/mo already running, ~8.25%). Existing junk: a ULIP (₹3L paid over 3y, surrender value ₹2.55L), 250g gold jewellery "as investment", FD ₹6L earning 6.9%.

**Do this:**
1. Allocate the ₹13.5k across the shelf (index / EPF top-up or PPF / NPS / SGB-ETF / satellite) with amounts and one-line job descriptions.
2. Fee audit: the ULIP vs a direct index plan — estimate the 30-year drag of a ~3% all-in cost gap (factors: 12% → 3,495; 9% monthly → (1.0075^360 − 1)/0.0075 = 1,829). Verdict on surrendering at a ₹45k loss?
3. Gold triage: keep the jewellery as investment, or re-route future gold allocation to SGB/ETF? Quantify the making-charge haircut (assume 12%).
4. Real-estate question: their landlord offers the flat for ₹1.6Cr (rent ₹30k/mo). Compute the rental yield and the buy-vs-rent break-even appreciation vs the 10.65% blend (tax ignored for simplicity).
5. Emergency redesign: the ₹6L FD at 6.9% — keep, ladder, or liquid-fund it? Their slab: 30%.

**Why this matters:** WM's daily grind is exactly these five micro-decisions — wrapper audits, drag math, darling-asset reality checks. Each looks small; compounded across 26 years they're the difference between ₹3.76Cr arriving on time and a family wondering where it went.

**🔑 Lab answers:**
1. ₹6.5k **Nifty 50 index** (core growth, 0.15% TER); ₹2k **flexi-index** (second core sleeve); ₹1.5k **NPS Tier-1** in his name (tax + retirement lock — forces good behavior); ₹1.5k **SGB/ETF gold** (5-7% hedge sleeve); ₹2k **satellite active mid** (written thesis + exit); continue the ₹5k EPF (debt anchor) + ₹7.5L emergency already parking in liquid fund. Debt grows automatically via EPF/PPF as salaries rise; equity ratio stays near the 60% ceiling.
2. 9% monthly factor = (1.0075³⁶⁰ − 1)/0.0075 = (14.72 − 1)/0.0075 = **1,829**. Drag vs 3,495: 1 − 1,829/3,495 = **a ~3% cost gap erases ~48% of the 30-year corpus** — ULIP economics in one subtraction. Verdict: **surrender**: losing ₹45k today stops a leak worth lakhs; sunk cost is not an asset class (CB1's first rule arrives early).
3. Future gold → SGB/ETF. The jewellery's 250g × 12% making-charge = **~30g-equivalent haircut (₹1.5-1.8L at ~₹6k/g class prices) already vaporized** — sunk, so don't sell it in anger; just stop *adding* to the haircut. Jewellery gets reclassified: adornment column, not investment column.
4. Yield = 3.6L/160L = **2.25%**. To tie the 10.65% blend, the flat must appreciate ~8.4%/yr — versus a realistic 4-6% metro drift → **rent-and-invest-the-difference wins** at these numbers; buy homes for living, not for 2.25% yields (WM3's adult-supervision rule).
5. At 30% slab the FD nets 4.83% → after 6% inflation ≈ **−1.1% real** — a paid waiting room at best. Plan: keep ₹4L as the yield-bearing tranche of the emergency fund (ladder 3 FDs at 4/8/12-month maturities), move ₹2L into the 14y college SIP start — parking is a job with a term, and 14-year money parked is theft from the kid.

1. **Drag compute.** ₹20k/mo, 25 years, direct index (12%) vs regular active (10.5%): terminal corpus each and the donated gap (factors @25y: 12%→1,893; 10.5%→1,432 approx-verified).

3. **NPS tail.** ₹1Cr NPS at 60: what arrives, what's forced, and one planning adjustment for the 40% annuity tail (annuity ~6%).
4. **Real-estate counter.** A broker's pitch: "rent is money down the drain — EMI builds your own house!" Write the two-line professional reply using the ₹1.6Cr/₹30k exhibit.
5. **Shelf mismatch ER.** Each belongs to which shelf — and which are misused? (a) entire retirement in FDs; (b) kid's admission-fee money in midcap fund, admission next year; (c) emergency fund locked in 5y PPF; (d) 40% of net worth in employer's ESOP.

### ✅ Selected answers

1. Direct: 20k × 1,893 = **₹3.79Cr**; regular: 20k × 1,432 = **₹2.86Cr**; **donated gap ≈ ₹92L ≈ 24% of the corpus** — the "free" advice inside the regular plan costs 92 lakhs over a working life. Fees compound exactly like returns, with the opposite sign.
2. **(a) VPF first** — 8.25% EEE equals an ~12% pre-tax debt return at 30% slab; unbeatable risk-free math. (b) taxable debt funds now at slab rate post-2023 (indexation gone) → meh, for parking only. (c) dividends taxed at slab + ₹5k TDS → poor for accumulators; growth option instead. (d) last: −1.1% real canon.
3. Arrives: **₹60L tax-free lump**; forced: **₹40L into an annuity** paying ~6% (₹2.4L/yr, taxable). Planning adjustment: model retirement income as SWP(60L+bucket-3 corpus) **plus** a 6% annuity floor stream — or size NPS contributions so the forced-annuity slice equals the *intended* pension floor anyway (turn the tail into the plan, not the other way).
4. *"The ₹1.6Cr flat yields 2.25% while its loan costs 9% — the EMI builds the bank's house for years 1-5 (front-loaded interest, TV3 canon). Rent the ₹30k flat, SIP the ₹1L/mo difference, and revisit when yield + honest appreciation can see 10.65% without squinting."*
5. (a) **misused** — 26-year money at −1.1% real (grow shelf empty); (b) **misused** — dated money in volatile shelf; glide to liquid NOW (WM2 college canon); (c) **misused** — emergency money must be liquid, not locked (PPF's 15y+partial rules are for retirement debt, not emergencies); (d) **misused** — concentration: employer = salary + portfolio single point of failure (SNL/ENRON lesson family) — cap employer stock ~10%, sell systematically on vesting.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** A 1% annual fee-vs-direct gap over 30 years of ₹10k SIP costs about:
(a) 1% of the final corpus
(b) 19.8% of the corpus — factors 3,495 vs 2,804: the drag compounds like a fifth columnist
(c) ₹3,000 per year, flat
(d) Nothing if markets rise

**Q2.** The correct household shelf-map is:
(a) everything in equity for maximum growth
(b) equity = far goals, government wrappers = taxed debt anchor, SGB/ETF = gold sleeve, property = living first, FD/liquid = parking only
(c) ULIPs for discipline
(d) FDs for retirement, equity for emergencies

**Q3.** The landlord's ₹1.6Cr flat renting at ₹30k/mo shows the honest buy-vs-rent gap because:
(a) rent rises 5% yearly, always fixing the math
(b) a 2.25% rental yield needs ~8.4%/yr appreciation to tie a 10.65% blended portfolio — possible in pockets, not as a default
(c) EMIs are cheaper than rent by RBI rule
(d) flats never appreciate

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — 1 − 2,804/3,495 = 19.8%: the fee doesn't take 1% of your money; it takes 1% of your *compounding base every year*, and the base is where the exponent lives. Ask any product for its all-in cost the way a credit officer asks for collateral.
2. **(b)** — jobs, not brands: far goals need the growth engine, dated goals their parking, retirement debt its EEE wrapper, gold its insurance sleeve, housing its stability logic — and every rupee's job description named before its product. (c) dies to the drag canon; (d) inverts both addresses.
3. **(b)** — 3.6L/160L = 2.25%; the spread to the blend must come from appreciation, and betting the family corpus on 8.4% property growth for 20 years is a thesis (write it, falsify it) — not a default. Rent + SIP wins until the math changes.
