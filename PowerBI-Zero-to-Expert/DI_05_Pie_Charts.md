# 🥧 DI5 · Pie Charts: The 360° Playground

> Pie charts compress a whole into slices — and exams compress three conversions into every question: **degrees ↔ percent ↔ value**. Master the one tri-conversion formula and the fraction table from DI1, and pie sets become the fastest marks in DI: no slopes to judge, no gridlines to interpolate — just one circle, one total, and your anchors. Today we bake two ShopKart pies: the FY25 store-mix pie and the FY25 EXPENSE pie (where the legendary 2.4% net margin becomes a sliver you can finally *see*).

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Own the **tri-conversion**: 100% = 360°, so 1% = 3.6°, and value = share × total.
- Convert in all six directions without hesitation (deg→%, %→deg, deg→₹, ₹→deg, %→₹, ₹→%).
- Handle **multi-pie sets**: same-total pies vs different-total pies (different rules!).
- Crunch **donut charts** (= pies) and **pie + bar combo sets**.
- Answer \"difference between slices\" and combined-slice questions in one step.

---

## 📘 Concepts

### 5.1 The one formula that rules the circle

```
100% ≡ 360° ≡ Total        →        1% = 3.6°  ·  1° = 0.2778%
slice value = (degrees ÷ 360) × Total = (% ÷ 100) × Total
```

The DI1 fraction table pays off huge: 15% = 54° (because 15 × 3.6), 25% = 90°, 12.5% = 45°, 33.33% = 120°. Shout them until reflex.

|---|---|---|---|

### 5.3 Pie #2 — ShopKart FY25 expense pie (of ₹280L revenue)

Where every ₹100 went in FY25 (ties to the GL story — net ₹6.7L, margin 2.4%):

| Head | % of revenue | ₹L | Degrees |
|---|---|---|---|
| COGS (goods bought) | 71.0% | 198.8 | 255.6° |
| Rent (5 stores) | 11.0% | 30.8 | 39.6° |
| Salaries (23 staff) | 10.0% | 28.0 | 36.0° |
| Marketing | 3.5% | 9.8 | 12.6° |
| Other/admin | 2.1% | 5.88 | 7.6° |
| **NET PROFIT** | **2.4%** | **6.72** | **8.6°** |

That tiny 8.6° sliver IS ShopKart's whole FY25 profit — the number your finance modules quote (₹6.7 lakh net). When the GD course says \"retail is a 2–3% margin knife-fight\", now you've SEEN the knife.

### 5.4 Multi-pie sets — the rules change by total

- **Same-total pies (both ₹280L):** slice sizes directly comparable across pies.
- **Different-total pies (FY24 = ₹236L vs FY25 = ₹280L):** compare ONLY shares within each pie — or convert: FY24 Online share 21% × 236 = 49.6L vs FY25 Online 23.57% × 280 = 66L → **Online grew +16.4L (+33%)** even though its share grew ONLY ~2.6 points. **Share-up + total-up double-win** — and the reverse question (\"share fell, so value fell?\") is the trap: 20% of a bigger total can beat 25% of a smaller one.
- **Rule:** write each pie's TOTAL under it before question 1 (totals-first, pie edition).

### 5.5 Donuts, combos & \"combined slices\"

- Donut = pie with the center punched out. All rules identical.
- **Pie + bar combo:** pie gives the mix of the total; bars give the total per period. \"KB's Q1 value?\" Total Q1 58L × KB-share… if the pie is FY-level, combos demand cross-referencing — locate ONE number per chart (DI1 locate rule).
- **Combined slices:** \"stores BEYOND the top-2 share?\" = 100 − (19.29 + 23.57) = 57.14% → 57.14% × 280 = 160L (verify: 42+38+34+46 = 160 ✔ — subtraction trail often cheaper than addition).

## 💪 Exercises

1. 12.5% equals how many degrees? (1/8 anchor!)
2. A slice = 90°. Its share of 280L?
3. Two pies, same total, show marketing at 3% vs 3.5%. \"By how much did marketing ₹ change?\" — the minimal data needed?
4. Money slices: name the TWO slices of the expense pie a CFO attacks first to grow the 2.4% — and justify in one sentence each.
5. True/False: \"If Online's share drops from 23.57% to 20% next year, Online's ₹ must fall.\"

### ✅ Selected answers

1. **45°** (1/8 of 360 — fraction table doing free work again).
2. 90° = 25% = **₹70L** (quarter circle, quarter value).
3. ONLY the pie TOTAL is needed: (3.5 − 3)% × 280 = 0.5% × 280 = **+₹1.4L**. Same-total pies let share differences convert straight to ₹.
4. **COGS (255.6°):** a 1-pt COGS cut on ₹280L = ₹2.8L ≈ HALF the year's profit → vendor terms (Fresh Farms Net-30 discipline!) matter more than any ad. **Rent (39.6°):** one renegotiated Lajpat lease = visible sliver growth. Small bases, big levers.
5. **FALSE (as stated).** If FY26 total jumps to ₹350L, 20% × 350 = 70L > 66L — share down, value UP. Different totals break visual comparison; convert before concluding (5.4 rule).

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** A pie slice measures 54°. Its share is:
- (a) 54%
- (b) **15%** — because 1% = 3.6°, so 54 ÷ 3.6 = 15; on ₹280L that's ₹42L (Rohini's slice!) 
- (c) 5.4%
- (d) 150°

**Q2.** Comparing slices ACROSS two pies is legal only when:
- (a) The pies look the same size
- (b) **The pie TOTALS are equal — else compare SHARES only, or convert share × total for each pie first** (20% of 350L > 25% of 236L!)
- (c) You're quick; totals don't matter
- (d) Colors match

**Q3.** The expense pie shows COGS at 255.6° and Net Profit at 8.6°. The analyst line for the Sharma review is:
- (a) 'Profit looks small, panic'
- (b) **\"Every ₹100 earned keeps ₹2.4 — margin lives/dies in the 255.6° COGS slice; a 1-pt saving there adds ~₹2.8L, half of last year's entire profit\"** — tiny sliver, giant lever
- (c) 'Rent is the problem'
- (d) 'Pies can't show profit'

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — tri-conversion: ÷3.6 for degrees→%. Then share × total for ₹. One formula, three costumes, all marks.
2. **(b)** — totals rule multi-pies. Equal totals → direct compare; unequal → shares-only or convert. The \"share down, value up\" flip is the exam's favorite pie bait.
3. **(b)** — the 8.6° sliver is the STORY: retail margins are knife-thin, so the fat COGS slice is where analysts hunt. FY25 says it exactly: 71% COGS → ₹6.7L net on ₹2.8 cr.
