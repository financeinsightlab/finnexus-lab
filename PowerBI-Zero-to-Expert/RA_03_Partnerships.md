# 🤝 RA3 · Partnerships: Capital × Time = The Fair Share

> Rakesh puts ₹40,000 for 6 months; Arjun puts ₹50,000 for the full year. Fifty-fifty? The ledger says NO: money in business isn't measured in rupees, it's measured in **rupee-months**. The partnership law is one line — **profit splits in the ratio of (capital × time)** — and the moment you internalize it, joining-mid-year puzzles, sleeping-vs-working partner splits, and salary carve-outs all become arithmetic. This module is where the ratio course signs its first contract, and where ShopKart's Gurugram expansion gets priced fairly.

## 📘 Concepts

### 3.1 The product law (rupee-months)

```
Share ratio = (Capital1 x Months1) : (Capital2 x Months2) : ...
Rakesh: 40,000 x 6 = 2,40,000     Arjun: 50,000 x 12 = 6,00,000
Ratio = 2,40,000 : 6,00,000 = 2:5
Profit Rs 1,40,000 -> 2/7 = 40,000 (R), 5/7 = 1,00,000 (A)
TILE: 40,000 + 1,00,000 = 1,40,000 ✔
```

Logic: a rupee invested for 12 months does twice the work of one parked for 6. Capital without time is half the story — exams exploit exactly that half.

### 3.2 Mid-year joining (time is the only dial)

A starts with ₹30,000; B joins after 3 months with ₹45,000. Year-end profit ₹1,02,000 split:

```
A: 30,000 x 12 = 3,60,000    B: 45,000 x 9 = 4,05,000
Ratio 3,60,000 : 4,05,000 = 8:9
A gets 8/17 x 1,02,000 = 48,000     B gets 54,000   TILE: 1,02,000 ✔
```

(Bigger capital, shorter season — B still takes the larger share. Intuition checked by product law, not by who arrived first.)

### 3.3 The compensation ladder (order of operations!)

Real partnership deeds pay in LAYERS before the residual split:

```
PROFIT
  - (1) Interest on capital (e.g., 10% p.a. on each partner's capital)
  - (2) Salary/commission to working partners (fixed or % of profit)
  - (3) RESIDUAL split in the agreed ratio (often the capital ratio)
```

**Worked:** Capitals A ₹5L, B ₹3L; profit ₹2,00,000; interest 10%, salary ₹24,000 to A (working partner); residual split 3:2.

```
Interest: 50,000 (A) + 30,000 (B) = 80,000
Salary A: 24,000
Residual: 2,00,000 - 1,04,000 = 96,000  ->  3:2 = 57,600 : 38,400
TOTALS: A = 50,000 + 24,000 + 57,600 = 1,31,600
        B = 30,000 + 38,400          =    68,400     TILE: 2,00,000 ✔
```

The ladder order is sacred: interest and salary are EXPENSES of the firm, the ratio eats only whatever is left.

### 3.4 Working-partner commission (off the top)

Deed clause: "A, the managing partner, takes 25% of profit for management; the rest splits in capital ratio 3:2." Profit ₹1,00,000:

```
Commission A: 25,000
Remaining 75,000 -> 3:2 = 45,000 : 30,000
TOTALS: A = 70,000    B = 30,000   TILE: 1,00,000 ✔
```

Two pay-streams for A (commission + ratio share of remainder) — compute them in separate rows, sum at the end, tile-check always.

## 💪 Exercises

1. Kamla invests ₹72,000 for 5 months; Dev ₹54,000 for 10 months. Profit ratio and the split of ₹63,000.
2. ShopKart's cloud kitchen pilot: ShopKart invests ₹6L all year; a partner chef invests ₹4L but only from month 7 (6 months). Profit ₹90,000 — split?
3. Full deed: capitals ₹8L & ₹6L, interest 8% p.a., working partner #1 salary ₹36,000, residual in capital ratio, profit ₹2,78,000. Totals + tile.
4. Two ways to pay a salesman: flat 15% of ₹1,20,000 profit, OR ₹10,000 salary + 5% of profit. Which pays him more, and by how much?
5. Compose the one-line law that explains why "half the year, double the money" splits exactly 1:1.

### ✅ Selected answers

1. 72k×5 = 360k; 54k×10 = 540k → **2:3** → ₹25,200 : ₹37,800 ✔ (sums 63,000).
2. 6L×12 = 72L vs 4L×6 = 24L → **3:1** → **₹67,500 : ₹22,500** ✔.
3. Interest 64,000 + 48,000 = 1,12,000; salary 36,000; residual 1,30,000 split 8:6 (4:3) → **74,285.71 : 55,714.29** — not round but the audit passes (sums exactly to 1,30,000 ✔; real deeds pay paise, exams prefer clean splits). Totals: **#1 = 1,74,285.71** (64,000 + 36,000 + 74,285.71), **#2 = 1,03,714.29** (48,000 + 55,714.29); grand tile = 2,78,000 ✔.
4. Flat 15% pays ₹18,000; hybrid pays 10,000 + 5% × 1,20,000 = ₹16,000 → **flat wins by ₹2,000 at this profit level**. Cross-over line: 10,000 + 0.05P = 0.15P → P = **₹1,00,000** — below that profit the hybrid's salary base wins, above it the flat-15% slope outruns. Verify at ₹1,00,000: flat 15,000 = hybrid 10,000 + 5,000 ✔, at ₹1,20,000 flat leads ✔.
5. Capital × TIME is the product: halve one factor, double the other, product unchanged → 1:1 ✔.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** Rakesh ₹40,000 × 6 months vs Arjun ₹50,000 × 12 months — the split ratio:
- (a) 4:5
- (b) **2:5 — capital × time: 2,40,000 vs 6,00,000; rupee-months, not rupees, tell the partnership story, and the tile-check seals ₹1,40,000 as ₹40,000 : ₹1,00,000**
- (c) 1:1
- (d) 5:2

**Q2.** A joins with ₹30,000 all year; B pours ₹45,000 but only from month 4. Year-end profit ₹1,02,000 pays A:
- (a) ₹45,000
- (b) **₹48,000 — products 3,60,000 : 4,05,000 = 8:9; A takes 8/17 × 1,02,000; bigger-then-late capital still led, but time trimmed it to a 9-part share**
- (c) ₹51,000
- (d) ₹54,000

**Q3.** In a deed with interest-on-capital AND working-partner salary, the residual ratio-split applies to:
- (a) the full profit
- (b) **whatever REMAINS after interest and salary are deducted — ladder order is sacred: firm-expenses first (interest + salary), ratio eats only the leftovers (₹96,000 in our canon deed, split 3:2)**
- (c) only the salary portion
- (d) the interest portion

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — the product law; capital without months is half the ledger.
2. **(b)** — time-factor is the only dial a mid-year joiner turns.
3. **(b)** — expenses before splits; the deed reads top-down like a P&L, not bottom-up like a battle.
