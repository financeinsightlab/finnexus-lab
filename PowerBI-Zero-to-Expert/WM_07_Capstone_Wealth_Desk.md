## 📘 Concepts

### 7.1 The 8-step protocol

1. **DISCOVER** — facts + fears + the willingness battery (BF canon: past behavior only)
2. **DASHBOARD** — net worth + cash-flow map + surplus rate (WM1)
3. **PROTECT** — emergency, term, health, PA/CI + estate-first-aids (WM5/WM6)
4. **ALLOCATE** — capacity/willingness/required → tie-break → AAP (WM2)
5. **SHELF** — instrument map + fee-drag audit (WM3)
6. **TAX** — regime + order-of-operations + TLH calendar (WM4)
7. **GOALS & RETIREMENT** — doses, buckets, estate layer (TV7/WM6)
8. **COVENANT & REVIEW** — signed IPS, Diwali date, escalation rules

### 7.2 The client file — Mehta family (full picture)

Ages 36/32; incomes ₹32L + ₹8L (take-homes ₹2.1L + ₹65k/mo); kids 5 & 1; parents 61/64, dependent, no cover; home loan ₹55L @ 8.8% (EMI ₹48k, 18y left); assets ₹85L (SIP 55, EPF 20, FD 10); endowment ₹1L/yr + ULIP ₹60k/yr running; surplus after leak-hunt ₹65k/mo identified but unallocated; employer health ₹5L; his 2020 behavior: did NOT sell (good), but bought random names on tips (₹6L, now −40%, "long-term investor" by accident).

### 7.3 The deliverable skeleton (what lands on the desk)

Net-worth statement · protection stack with premiums · AAP one-pager (60-65% equity ceiling per measured willingness — he held 2020, good, but bought junk: willingness for *volatility* is fine, willingness for *process* is broken) · shelf + drag audit (endowment paid-up path, ULIP surrender math) · tax sheet (regime + ₹62,400 menu) · goals/doses (college ×2, retirement) · estate checklist · covenant + review calendar. Nine pages, zero jargon, a signature line.

### 7.4 What the desk is really testing

Not product knowledge — **triage under constraint**. The Mehtas cannot fund everything this year: the desk must sequence (protection NOW, college doses NOW, retirement stepped-up, endowment exit scheduled, estate in 30 days) while the family's agent-uncle pitches "guaranteed 9% pension plans" at the dinner table. The advisor's product is the *process*: anyone can allocate ₹65k; keeping it allocated for 14 years through two crashes and one job loss is the actual job.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** The Mehta file's weakest gear requiring week-1 action is:
(a) GROW — equity too low
(b) PROTECT — ₹20L of endowment cover against ₹3.5Cr need, employer-locked health, no nominations hygiene or will
(c) EARN — need side hustles
(d) SHELF — wrong index fund

**Q2.** Funding all three goals today (factors 310 / 573 / 1,713 for ₹40L / ₹50L / ₹4.5Cr) needs monthly doses of about:
(a) ₹1L+ — impossible, so drop retirement
(b) ≈ ₹32.6k total (₹12.9k + ₹8.7k + ₹11k stepped-retirement) against a ₹65k surplus — funded WITH headroom
(c) ₹5k — goals are cheap
(d) ₹48k — exactly the EMI, so cancel the EMI

**Q3.** "Term insurance is money wasted — nothing comes back" is answered by:
(a) Agreeing and buying an endowment plan
(b) The Separation Law: protection is rented income-stream (₹2,500/mo for ₹3.5Cr), not an investment; products that 'return money' return less of everything
(c) Buying ULIPs instead
(d) Skipping insurance entirely

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — investments ₹85L exist *before* the floor does: classic inverted stack. Week-1 sequence: term applications (medicals take 2-4 weeks — start the clock), parent health stack, nomination fixes. Growth problems are optimizable; protection gaps are existential.
2. **(b)** — 12.9 + 8.7 + 11(stepped) = 32.6k vs 65k surplus: the family funds everything with ₹32k headroom remaining for prepay tokens and buffer. The myth "we can't afford goals" was a premium-confusion artifact — the paid-up endowment and surrendered ULIP even refilled the reserves. Address discipline, not income, was the missing crore.
3. **(b)** — "nothing comes back" IS the product working: you bought a ₹3.5Cr promise for the price of two streaming subscriptions, and the premium isn't lost — it's the rental cost of your family's resilience. Money-back plans return your own money at 3.3%, minus cover adequacy, minus two decades. Family-friendly and arithmetically fatal.
