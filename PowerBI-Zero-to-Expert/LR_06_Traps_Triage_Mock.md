## 📘 Concepts

### 6.1 The five LR traps (names are vaccines — you know the drill)

1. **The Facing Flip** — mixed-facing row or outward circle person; left/right counted wrong (LR2/LR3). *Vaccine: `(flipped)` tag at frame-time; center-facing \"L = clk\" note.*
2. **Between-Count Slip** — \"two between\" solved as difference-2 (LR1). *Vaccine: |diff| − 1, said aloud every time.*
3. **Possibility Upgrading** — treating a \"maybe-position\" as locked after 2 clues, poisoning the chain (LR1). *Vaccine: OPEN slots carry tiny-letters; only proven facts go in boxes; two-case branching when forks persist.*
4. **The Arrow Reverse** — if-then and \"either-or\" read backwards (LR5). *Vaccine: convert to A → B / A ⊕ B BEFORE placing anyone; check every proposed team against every law.*
5. **The Overstay** — sinking 6+ minutes into one puzzle (usually a multi-rail grid) while cheaper sets starve. *Vaccine: the triage + skip-rule below; marks are equal, minutes are not.*

### 6.2 The 30-second triage (read every puzzle's WRAPPER first)

```
BANK:   single-rail, one hierarchy (row/floor/schedule), ≤ 7 clues, unique-ish
GAMBLE: two rails (seating + attribute), 8+ clues, some conditionals — pocket if
        banking sets are done
DUMP:   3-attribute grids with multiple \"either-or\"s, circular+attribute giants,
        anything you'd need double-branching for — LAST, or marks-per-minute dies
Rule: bank first, gamble second, dump last. 3 minutes max per BANK puzzle; a
puzzle that resists after 4 minutes gets parked — return only with surplus time.
```

**Question-weight rule:** puzzles pay 3–5 Qs PER SET — finishing one whole set beats half-solving two. Abandoning a set at 60% costs the 2 questions its last locks anchored — only bail EARLY (triage) or finish. No mid-chain exits.

### 6.3 Pacing architecture (banking/SSC reasoning section)

- Puzzle sets first? NO — LR mixes puzzles + single Qs (coding, syllogisms, series). **Single-Qs first (fast points), then BANK puzzles, then GAMBLE.**
- Rough-sheet header (actually write): `L=clk · |diff|−1 · flipped? · A→B before placing · 4-min park`.
- The PARK mindset: parking is a decision WIN, not a defeat — parked puzzles are exactly where 60-second late-kills come from.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** 'Possibility Upgrading' means:
- (a) Improving your puzzle options
- (b) **Treating a MAYBE-position as LOCKED — every downstream lock built on a guess collapses silently; keep tiny-letters under open slots and branch at hard forks**
- (c) Placing conditional clues late
- (d) Using two cases

**Q2.** The 30-second triage classifies a single-rail, 6-clue floor puzzle as:
- (a) DUMP — floors are slow
- (b) **BANK — single hierarchy, bounded clues, near-unique: solve it first; puzzles pay 3-5 questions each, so finished sets out-score half-solved pairs**
- (c) GAMBLE
- (d) SKIP

**Q3.** The correct bail-out discipline for a resisting puzzle is:
- (a) Exit mid-chain whenever frustrated
- (b) **Bail EARLY at triage or FINISH the set — a 60% puzzle abandons the 2-3 questions its last locks anchor; the 4-minute park is the only mid-chain mercy**
- (c) Never bail
- (d) Randomly guess its questions

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — LOCKED vs OPEN; never the twain. One assumed placement quietly re-arranges six people behind your back.
2. **(b)** — triage is marks-per-minute math, not fear. Bank → Gamble → Dump; weights favor finished sets.
3. **(b)** — early or never. The park-rule keeps mercy surgical; frustration-based exits are the Overstay trap's evil twin (the Under-harvest).
