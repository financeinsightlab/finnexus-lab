# 🎯 SY1 · Statements, AEIO & the Golden Law
> Four letters run the whole subject: **A**ll, none (**E**), some (**I**), some-not (**O**). Learn to read a statement by its *logical content alone* — with ShopKart's policy folder as the training set — and recite the Golden Law that kills half of all wrong answers: **your knowledge of the real world is not evidence.**

## 🎯 Objectives
- Define a syllogism: two (or more) **statements** taken as 100% true, then conclusions that **must** follow — or don't.
- Identify the four proposition types **A / E / I / O** on sight, with symbols and set language.
- Rewrite scrambled sentences into standard logical form ("All S are P").
- Apply the **Golden Law**: only the statement's words bind you — no real-world facts, no likelihood, no vibes.
- Separate **definite** conclusions from **possible** ones (SY4 takes this further).

## 📘 Concepts

### 1.1 What a syllogism actually is
Two statements, one question: what MUST be true if both are true?

```text
Statements:  All auditors are readers of fine print.
             Riya is an auditor.
Conclusion:  Riya reads fine print.        ← MUST follow ✓
```

Notice what the game did NOT ask: whether auditors really do read fine print. Inside the arena, statements are **gospel**. The syllogism is a machine: feed it premises, it grinds out certainties or refuses to.

### 1.2 AEIO — the four forms of everything
Every syllogism statement is one of four shapes (vowels from Latin *affIrmo / nEgO*):

| Code | Form | Symbol | Set language | ShopKart flavor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | All S are P | S → P | S ⊆ P | "All invoices above ₹50,000 carry GST." |
| **E** | No S is P | S ✗ P | S ∩ P = ∅ | "No cash sale exceeds ₹2,00,000." |
| **I** | Some S are P | S ∿ P | S ∩ P ≠ ∅ | "Some refunds are instant." |
| **O** | Some S are not P | S ↛ P | S ⊄ P | "Some suppliers are not GST-registered." |

Crush the vocabulary once: **"some" in logic means AT LEAST ONE — and possibly ALL.** Some refunds are instant does *not* imply some are not. The logic "some" is a door left open, not a headcount. And **"some-not" (O) tells you nothing about the rest** — the untouched part of S may or may not be P.

### 1.3 Standard form — the 10-second rewrite
Exam sentences arrive dressed like conversation. Strip them to AEIO:

- "Every manager carries a scanner" → **All managers are scanner-carriers** (A).
- "Only GST-registered vendors get purchase orders" → flip it: **All PO-holders are GST-registered** (A). ("Only X are Y" = All Y are X — the classic flip; only-X owns the door.)
- "A few stores are franchises" → **Some stores are franchises** (I).
- "None of the interns signs cheques" → **No intern is a cheque-signer** (E).
- "Not every discount is approved" → **Some discounts are not approved** (O). ("Not every" = some-not — never "none"!)

### 1.4 The Golden Law — the statement is the whole world
Inside a syllogism, the ONLY universe is what the statements say. Three corollaries that print marks:

1. **Reality leaks are illegal.** "All cats are dogs. Tom is a cat." ⇒ Tom is a dog. Absurd in Delhi, mandatory in syllogism-land. If the conclusion follows from the statements, it's valid even if the premise is biological nonsense.
2. **Likelihood is not logic.** "Some traders are rich" does NOT support "some traders are poor" — even though both feel true at a market. Inside the game you know *nothing* beyond what was stated.
3. **Big claims need big proof.** An "All …" conclusion demands an "All …" chain behind it; "Some" in the premises can never earn an "All" in the conclusion (SY3 formalizes this).

### 1.5 Definite vs possible — the two verdicts
Conclusions come in two strengths. A **definite** conclusion is forced: in *every* world where the statements hold, it holds. A **possibility** conclusion is merely *not blocked*: some world consistent with the statements contains it. Both are legitimate answer types with different tests (SY4 sharpens possibilities). For now, stamp every conclusion you meet: **MUST it be true, or MIGHT it be?** Confusing the two stamps is the #1 beginner wipeout.

### 1.6 Why finance people worship AEIO
Policies, audit assertions, KYC rules, credit memos — all are AEIO propositions ("All wire transfers above ₹10L need dual authorization"). Audits are living syllogisms: given the rule (A) and a sampled transaction (member of S), the duty *must* follow. SY6 turns your compliance folder into a logic gym.

## 🧪 LAB — The reading room (10 min)
Type each as A / E / I / O, then rewrite in standard form:
1. "All stores open at 10 AM."
2. "No delivery agent enters the server room."
3. "Some weekend shoppers are bulk buyers."
4. "Some drivers are not on the night roster."
5. "Only supervisors can approve a return above ₹5,000." (Flip!)
6. Verdict drill: From "Some discounts are festival discounts," can we conclude "Some discounts are NOT festival discounts"? Definite, possible, or garbage?

**Why this matters:** typing speed here is everything — the whole course is pattern-recognition at 8 seconds per statement.

**🔑 Lab answers:** (1) **A** — All stores are 10-AM openers (2) **E** — No delivery agent is a server-room enterer (3) **I** — Some weekend shoppers are bulk buyers (4) **O** — Some drivers are not night-roster members (5) **A, flipped** — All approvers of ₹5,000+ returns are supervisors (6) **garbage** — an I statement never forces an O; "some" might be all of them.

## 💪 Exercises
Type A/E/I/O + standard form for each:
1. "Every cashier carries a float."
2. "Not all SKUs are barcoded."
3. "A few vendors deliver on Sundays."
4. "No refund is processed without a receipt."
5. "Only auditors may open the GL locked period."
6. Verdict: from "All pickers are badge-holders," can we conclude "Some badge-holders are pickers"? (Peek SY2's conversion table if you must — then explain WHY it works.)
7. Golden-Law drill: "All bankers are vegetarian. Some tall people are bankers." Conclusion offered: "Some bankers are not tall" — valid, invalid, or possible? Explain in one line about what the statements *say*.

### ✅ Selected answers
1. **A** — All cashiers are float-carriers.
2. **O** — Some SKUs are not barcoded (watch: "not all" = some-not, NOT "none").
3. **I** — Some vendors are Sunday deliverers.
4. **E** — No refund is a receipt-less processed refund.
5. **A, flipped** — All locked-period openers are auditors.
6. **Yes — definite.** "All pickers are badge-holders" forces the overlap to exist (there exist pickers, and they're inside badges), so *some* badge-holders are pickers. Conversion with a diet: All → Some, safely.
7. **Invalid.** The statements never separate bankers from tall people — maybe every banker is tall. An I conclusion ("some bankers are tall") DOES follow from the chain; the offered O does not. Statement-words only: no height facts, no diet facts.

## ❓ Quiz
1. "Only supervisors can approve a return above ₹5,000," in standard logical form, is:
   - (a) All supervisors are return-approvers
   - (b) All return-approvers (₹5,000+) are supervisors — "only X are Y" flips to All Y are X; the only-class owns the door
   - (c) Some supervisors are return-approvers
2. "Some discounts are festival discounts" logically guarantees:
   - (a) some discounts are NOT festival discounts
   - (b) at least one discount is a festival discount — possibly all of them; logic's "some" is a door left open, never a headcount
   - (c) most discounts are festival discounts
3. A syllogism states "All cats are dogs. Tom is a cat." The conclusion "Tom is a dog" is:
   - (a) invalid — cats are not dogs in reality
   - (b) valid — conclusions are judged inside the statement-world; reality leaks are illegal under the Golden Law
   - (c) possible but not definite

### ✅ Answers
1. **(b)** — option (a) reverses the containment: the rule grants approver-status ONLY to supervisors, so every approver must be one.
2. **(b)** — I means existence, not majority (c is statistics), and never the negative (a).
3. **(b)** — inside the machine, the premises are gospel. The Golden Law: statements bind, world knowledge doesn't.
