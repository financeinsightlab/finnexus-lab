## 📘 Concepts

### 11.1 SaaS — the drip business

The platform subscription is one series-PO (RR3) transferring over time: **₹1.2L annual = ₹10,000/month** straight-line — time is the faithful pattern when access is continuous. The recurring fights:
- **Implementation/onboarding**: distinct only if the customer could run it with another vendor's platform or a third party could do it — deep-config implementations are **welded** and thus drip with the subscription; light, transferable setups might separate at SSP.
- **Usage/overage fees**: variable consideration tied to the stream — recognized as usage occurs (typically via the as-invoiced expedient when bills meter value faithfully), never front-loaded on a contract estimate.
- **Upfront non-refundable platform fees** (setup/access): RR9 gym-canon logic — drip with the service life.
- **Renewals & auto-renew outs**: month-to-month outs re-define the enforceable period (RR2) — the contract often IS the current term; renewal-only discounts look like material rights.
- Balance-sheet trail: multi-year prepaid deals → contract liability; enterprise true-up clauses paid in arrears → contract asset; commission-heavy sales motions (5-8% on 3-yr deals) → obtain-cost assets amortized over expected life including justifiable renewals.

### 11.2 Telecom — the bundle machine, production version

The canon math becomes an industry: ₹24,000 total consideration → **₹12,000 handset at activation + ₹500/month × 24 service** (RR5), with the contract asset tick-tocking down (RR7). The production extras: dealer commissions (₹2,500/port-in) are obtain-cost assets amortized over the expected plan-plus-justifiable-renewal life; handset subsidies dressed as "instant cashback" are consideration-payable-to-the-customer netting (RR4's blade); early-termination balances trigger remaining-consideration neurosurgery (service liability released, handset asset often not clawed back — hence customer-lifetime-value models inside telcos). Porting/free-plan promos: zero-price POs consume SSP allocation like anyone else ("free" is a price, remember).

### 11.3 IT services — T&M vs fixed-bid, bifurcated lives

**Time-and-materials**: Indians' favorite contract form rides the right-to-invoice expedient (₹3,800/hr rate card, hours logged ≈ value transferred → as-invoiced revenue). **Fixed-bid projects**: full POC — over-time via criterion 3 (bespoke code for client systems + payment-for-work clauses if drafted right) or PIT at delivery otherwise; cost-to-cost measures with inefficiency surgery (RR6), milestone acceptance gates as delivery evidence. The discipline wedge: fixed-bid margin hinges on estimate-at-completion honesty — the same margin-at-completion curve as construction, with a Jira board instead of a site.

### 11.4 Indian real estate — the RERA landing

Globally, developers ask: can I POC flat sales? The answer turns on criterion 3 — bespoke unit + enforceable right to payment for performance-to-date. In India, buyer agreements overwhelmingly let the developer cancel on buyer default, retain a slice, and RESELL the unit — the unit stays generic enough to have alternative use, and payments for work are not enforceable as the contract stands. RERA then super-structures cash: **70% of customer collections into a separate escrow**, withdrawals tied to construction progress certified by engineer/architect/CA certificates — cash extraction tied to progress, but revenue recognition still runs on its own test. India's consensus landing: most residential sales are **point-in-time at handover/registration-style control-transfer**, with POC pockets where contract design genuinely creates alternative-uselessness + payment rights (some BOT/EPC structures, bespoke industrial builds). The analyst's fog-light: recognize-at-completion developers carry years of sales as contract liabilities + inventory — their "sudden" profit at handover is backlog aging, not miracle sales.

### 11.5 The bridge note — disaggregation in practice

An Ind-AS-115 revenue note disaggregates by product/service line, geography, timing-of-transfer (over-time vs PIT), and contract type. Your professional drill: for one company, take two reporting periods and reconcile the revenue bridge: opening contract liability + new bookings − recognized − FX/adjustments = closing contract liability; then tie recognized revenue to disaggregated slabs and backlog (remaining performance obligations) disclosures with expected-conversion years. The numbers stay honest when the bridge ties; restatements breed in unexplained churn between the buckets.

## 🧪 LAB — Four Countertops (10 min)

1. **CloudDukaan SaaS**: ₹2.4L 2-year platform + ₹60k deep-config onboarding (welded). Monthly revenue and the day-1 receipt of ₹1.5L advance?
2. **SIMWala**: activates the ₹24,000 canon plan, pays dealer ₹3,000; customer churns month 8 (plan enforceable 24 mo? assume no). Contract asset status and commission treatment at churn?
3. **CodeCraft fixed-bid**: ₹90L payment-work-guaranteed bespoke build, 8 months; month 5 costs ₹38L of ₹60L expected total. Progress, revenue to date, margin-at-completion pulse?
4. **GruhNirman homes**: sold 400 flats ₹1.2cr avg over 3 years, 55% collected, construction 70% certified; handovers start next quarter. Revenue so far, the two big balances, and the one-line RERA comment?

**Why this matters:** these are the four balance-sheet shapes you'll audit most in India — drip, bundle, POC, escrowed-completion — in one sitting.

**🔑 Lab answers:**
1. Welded onboarding → single PO over 24 months: **(2.4L + 0.6L)/24 = ₹12,500/month**; the ₹1.5L advance is a contract liability bleeding down, no day-1 revenue.
2. Contract asset at churn ≈ 12,000 − 8 × 500 = **₹8,000** — the conditional slice remaining when the plan dies flips: remaining service liability releases, but the handset-side asset recovery depends on contractual clawbacks; most telcos write down the stranded slice (impairment through ECL if collectability fades). Commission asset already amortized 8 months (₹3,000 over ~24-36 months) — remainder amortizes faster or impair-writes-off on churn, per policy consistent with expected-life assumptions.
3. POC: progress = 38/60 = **63.3%**; revenue to date = 90L × 63.3% = **₹57.0L**; margin-at-completion = 90 − 60 = **+₹30L (33%)** — green, healthy but dependent on estimate-discipline for the remaining 22L.
4. **Revenue ≈ ₹0 to date** (PIT landing — handovers start next quarter); the balances: contract liability ≈ 400 × 1.2cr × 55% = **₹264cr** collected, and inventory WIP carrying construction costs; RERA comment: 70% of those collections routed through escrow and drawn against certified progress — cash exists, is ring-fenced, and isn't revenue yet.

## 💪 Exercises

1. Why is straight-line usually right for SaaS but sometimes wrong for usage-priced API platforms?
2. SIMWala's CFO proposes booking gross GMV of phone+plan as activation-day revenue "since the customer owns the phone." Verdict with canon numbers.
3. Fixed-bid vs T&M — which one needs an estimate-at-completion committee, and why does T&M mostly escape it?
4. A developer claims POC because "flat 1204 is unique to the buyer." Defeat or defend with criterion 3's two halves.
5. Write the note-fragment tying contract liability ₹264cr to backlog ₹216cr of unsold-linked obligations and next-year handover plans.

### ✅ Selected answers

1. Straight-line mirrors continuous access where value flows evenly. Usage-priced platforms transfer value per call/compute — the right-to-invoice/usage pattern beats time; forcing straight-line there books revenue in months when customers consumed nothing.
2. Rejected: separation already priced the pieces — handset ₹12,000 (control transfers at activation) + service ₹500/mo going forward; gross day-1 booking mixes over-time service into PIT product and overstates revenue by ₹12,000 on this plan alone.
3. Fixed-bid needs EAC honesty because its margin is the plug between fixed price and estimated costs — cost-to-cost progress literally reports estimates. T&M escapes: hours at rate cards ARE the economics (expedient), so no estimate drives the topline — only staffing/realization margins drive the P&L.
4. Defeat: "unique unit" supplies half of criterion 3 only. Payment-rights half fails where terms let the developer cancel, retain ~10%, and resell — no enforceable pay-for-performance-to-date. Unit uniqueness with weak payment rights = PIT at handover.
5. "Contract liability ₹264cr = collections on sold units awaiting handover-driven recognition; remaining performance obligations ₹216cr = the unsold/old bookings' SSP not yet under sales agreement (executory) plus un-delivered slices of signed agreements; expected conversion: ~70% of the ₹264cr liability recognized on FY27 handovers per delivery schedule disclosed."

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** CloudDukaan's welded ₹2.4L platform + ₹60k onboarding over 24 months recognizes monthly revenue of:
(a) ₹10,000 with onboarding deducted
(b) ₹12,500 — (240,000 + 60,000) ÷ 24: welded implementation rides the subscription stream as one over-time PO
(c) ₹60,000 day one + ₹10,000 after
(d) ₹12,000 with the fee as marketing expense

**Q2.** On SIMWala's ₹24,000 canon plan, activation-day revenue is:
(a) ₹24,000 — full consideration
(b) ₹12,000 — handset SSP-share at control transfer, plus the first ₹500 service slice as the month passes; the rest drips at ₹500/month
(c) ₹0 — plans start monthly
(d) ₹12,500 — dealer commission added

**Q3.** GruhNirman's ₹480cr of sold flats (55% collected, 70% built, handovers next quarter) most likely shows:
(a) ₹336cr revenue now (70% POC)
(b) ₹0-recognition to date with ₹264cr contract liability: Indian buyer agreements generally fail enforceable-payment-rights, landing residential sales point-in-time at handover
(c) ₹264cr revenue released proportionately
(d) ₹480cr revenue under bill-and-hold

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — the weld pools the fee; time-elapsed spreads the pool.
2. **(b)** — activation books the phone's relative-SSP slice; the service keeps dripping monthly.
3. **(b)** — RERA-era reality: control lands at handover, so sold units ride as contract liability and inventory until then.
