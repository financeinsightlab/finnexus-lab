# RP2 · Everyday & Workplace Reading: Notices, Emails, Forms & Product Pages

> *The reading that actually pays you: office notices with hidden deadlines, HR circulars, boss emails, offer letters, product pages, bills. Each document type has a SHAPE — once you know the shape, you read 5× faster and miss nothing. Silent risk lives in the fine print; this module makes you the person who CATCHES it before it catches you.*

## 🎯 Objectives
The document-shape map (every doc = predictable structure) · office notices & HR circulars · email reading for action (the 20-second triage) · forms & fine print · product pages & bills · reading tone/urgency between lines · 3 real-shape practice documents.

---

## 📘 2.1 The document-shape map 🗺️

Every professional document wears the same uniform. Know the uniform → jump straight to the pockets:

| Document | Where the GOLD lives | Where the TRAPS live |
|---|---|---|
| Notice/circular | subject line + last bullet (the deadline!) | "effective immediately" hidden at bottom |
| Email (boss/client) | subject + first 2 lines + any numbered list | the casual PS ("PS: needed by Fri") |
| Offer letter | CTC table + notice period clause + bond clause | the definitions page at the end changes everything! |
| Invoice/bill | total + due date + tax lines | extra charges in small rows (convenience fee!!) |
| Product page | price + ratings count + return policy | "only 2 left" urgency theater |
| Policy/bank doc | bolded numbers, tables | exclusions list ("not applicable if…") |

Rule: **read structure first, words second.** Scan a document's headings/bold/numbers for 10 seconds before any linear reading — you'll know where to deep-read.

## 📘 2.2 Office notices & circulars — read the ACTION 📢

Every notice answers 5 questions. Scan for them in this order:

```
1. WHAT changed? (the subject)
2. WHO does it affect? (all staff? one department?)
3. FROM WHEN? (effective date)
4. WHAT must I DO? (action + deadline — usually the LAST lines!)
5. WHO do I ask? (contact)
```

📄 **PRACTICE NOTICE** (read in 20 seconds, answer the 5 questions):

> **CIRCULAR — Diwali Working Arrangements**
> *To: All Departments · From: HR Desk · Date: 20-Oct-2026*
>
> 1. Office will remain closed on **21–22 October** on account of Diwali.
> 2. **23 October (Friday)** will be a working day for the Finance and Operations teams only, due to the month-end close.
> 3. Employees working on 23 October are eligible for a **comp-off** to be availed within 60 days.
> 4. The **Festive Bonus** will be credited along with the October salary, after TDS as applicable.
> 5. For travel reimbursements during the festive week, submit bills to **accounts@shopkart.in by 31 October**.

Q: Which department works 23 Oct? · Till when can the comp-off be used? · Bonus taxed or tax-free? · The LAST action date in the circular? (Answers in exercises — but try first!)

## 📘 2.3 Email triage — the 20-second read 📧

You already WRITE emails (EM course!) — now read them like triage nurses:

```
LINE 1: subject → category: ACTION / FYI / DECISION?
LINE 2: first 2 sentences → the ask + the deadline
SKIP  → middle paragraphs (context; you'll return only if needed)
HUNT  → numbered lists & bold (the payloads)
CHECK → am I in To (must act) or CC (must know)? — decide your job
```

⚠️ The PS trap: busiest people hide the real ask in **PS** ("PS: Can you send this by Friday?"). Proceed rule: after reading any email, answer aloud — "What's the ONE action, and by when?" Can't answer? That email gets a reply: "Quick check — do you need X by [date]?" (Never guess deadlines silently!)

## 📘 2.4 Forms & applications — zero-error mode 📋

Reading a FORM is active work, not passive:
1. **Full skim first** (2 min) — never start filling before seeing every field; later fields change earlier answers!
2. **Field traps to circle:** "as per PAN" (EXACT match demanded), "DD/MM/YYYY" date format (India trap!), "in words" amount lines, tick-boxes meaning OPPOSITES ("Tick if you DON'T want…"), mandatory asterisks (*).
3. **Declaration section** at the end = legal words. Read it — you're signing it. "I hereby declare that the above information is true" = they can act on false info.

## 📘 2.5 Product pages & bills — the fine-print hunt 🛒

📄 **PRACTICE PRODUCT PAGE** (a phone listing — find the 3 traps):

> **TechNova T9 Pro · ₹14,999** ~~₹21,999~~ (32% off)
> ★★★★☆ 3.9 (47,210 ratings)
> **Bank offer:** Extra ₹1,500 off with TNB Bank credit cards (EMI only, on orders above ₹15,000)
> **Delivery:** FREE by tomorrow
> **Warranty:** 1 year manufacturer warranty. Physical damage not covered.
> **Seller:** NovaRetail (4.2) · 7-day return for defects only
> *Special price valid for today only! Only 3 left in stock — order soon.*
> *Exchange offer available. Packaging charges of ₹49 apply.*

Trap hunt: (a) Does the ₹1,500 bank discount apply at ₹14,999? (NO — "above ₹15,000"!!) (b) Is "1 year warranty" full cover? (Nope — physical damage excluded.) (c) Will the total be ₹14,999? (No — ₹49 packaging charge, and "today only" + "3 left" = urgency theater to stop you thinking!)

📄 **PRACTICE BILL LINE:** "*Total: ₹1,180 · Convenience Fee: ₹59 · Delivery: ₹40 · GST (5%): ₹63.95 · To Pay: ₹1,342.95*" — learn the pyramid: headline price → fees → taxes → **To Pay** = the only real number.

## 📘 2.6 Reading between the lines: tone radar 📡

Same words, different urgency. Radar training:

| Signal phrase | Translator mode |
|---|---|
| "Please ensure…" | polite but firm — compliance expected |
| "Kindly note…" | information-only; no action usually |
| "As discussed / Per our last call…" | creating a paper record — this email is evidence! |
| "It has come to our notice that…" | a complaint/warning is starting 🚩 |
| "We regret to inform…" | bad news landing (rejection, delay, fee) |
| "We are pleased to…" | good news (offer, approval, credit!) |

Tone isn't decoration — it's your early-warning system. 🚩 = read SLOWLY.

## 🧪 LAB RP2 — The document gym (45 min)

1. **Notice analysis (10 min):** re-read the Diwali circular in 2.2, answer its 4 questions in writing, then try inventing a 6th: "If I need travel money after 31 Oct — what happens?" (Reading consequence = senior reading!)
2. **Email triage relay (10 min):** take 3 REAL emails from any inbox (work/college/promotional — blur private info!), apply the 5-line triage to each in 60 seconds, write your one-line "action + when" per mail.
3. **Form dry-run (10 min):** any real form (bank KYC / college / railway concession): skim ALL fields first, circle 3 trap-fields ("as per PAN", date format, declaration), write how each would bite a careless person.
4. **Offer-letter drill (10 min):** find a sample offer letter online (or your own!). Locate: CTC table, notice period, bond/service agreement, variable-pay condition. Write each as: "What it says in simple words: ___"
5. **Tone radar (5 min):** classify 6 signal phrases from 2.6 as green/yellow/red and say why. Then find one such phrase in a real message today.

## 💪 Exercises
1. The circular answer set: which teams work 23 Oct · comp-off validity · bonus tax status · last action date?
2. Your manager's email has 4 paragraphs and a PS. Where is the action most likely — and why does PS-danger exist?
3. An offer letter says "Variable pay: 20% of CTC, subject to individual and company performance." Translate to risk-language: how much money is NOT guaranteed?
4. Product-page math: item ₹9,990, 40% off, delivery ₹99, "bank offer ₹500 off on orders above ₹10,000". What's the trap, and what's the real final amount WITHOUT the bank offer?
5. Write the 3 questions you'd ask after reading THIS bank line: "Interest at 10.5% p.a. reducing balance, with processing fee of 2% of the sanctioned amount."

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 1: Finance + Operations · within 60 days · after TDS (taxed!) · 31 October (travel reimbursements).
- Ex 2: PS — people who skim paragraph-bodies still read PS lines, so busy/hurried writers park the ask there; readers must ALWAYS check PS.
- Ex 3: The 20% variable of CTC is conditional — if CTC is ₹10L, ₹2L is NOT guaranteed money; plan EMIs only on the fixed 80%.
- Ex 4: Trap: ₹9,990 < ₹10,000, so the ₹500 bank offer does NOT apply (10-rupee cliff!). Real: ₹9,990 − 40%?? (careful — "40% off" was already the displayed price? Assume price after discount = ₹9,990) + ₹99 delivery = **₹10,089**. Read every "offer" twice before celebrating! 🎯
- Ex 5: (a) reducing balance = interest on outstanding principal only? (b) 2% processing fee = upfront cost on full sanctioned amount — add it to loan cost comparison; (c) is the rate fixed or floating?

## ❓ Quiz
1. Why scan a document's structure BEFORE reading words?
2. Name the 5-question scan for office notices — and which question is usually in the LAST lines?
3. "It has come to our notice that…" — what does your tone radar say, and how should your reading speed change?
4. What makes "To Pay" on a bill different from the headline price?

### ✅ Answers
1. Documents have predictable shapes; structural scanning tells you WHERE the facts and traps live, so deep-reading targets 20% of the text with 100% of the value.
2. What changed? · Who's affected? · From when? · What must I DO (action+deadline)? · Who to ask? — Question 4 (your action and its deadline!) hides in the last bullets.
3. 🚩 Red: a complaint/warning/precedent-setting record begins. Slow down to full deep-read speed and note exactly WHAT is claimed and WHAT response window you have.
4. Headline price excludes fees and taxes; "To Pay" = price + convenience/delivery fees + GST — the only number that leaves your pocket.
