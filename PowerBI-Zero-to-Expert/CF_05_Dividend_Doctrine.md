# 🎯 CF5 · Dividend Doctrine — Payouts, Signals & Cosmetic Fireworks
> ShopKart paid ₹2.65L of its ₹13.25L PAT as dividends — 20% out, 80% compounding. Was that wisdom, stinginess, or signal? Dividend policy is corporate communication written in cash: markets re-read every rupee of it. CF5 gives you the theories, the ex-date physics, India's post-2020 tax map, and the cosmetic fireworks (bonuses, splits) that change nothing and excite everyone.

## 🎯 Objectives
- Run the irrelevance→relevance arc: MM's homemade-dividend pizza vs bird-in-hand, signaling, clientele.
- Compute **ex-dividend price physics** and explain why the drop is arithmetic, not punishment.
- Compare dividend vs **buyback** as cash-exit machines (tax, signal, EPS optics — extending AC9).
- Map Indian tax: dividends taxed at slab (post-2020), TDS 10% beyond ₹5,000; buyback tax at company level.
- Demolish bonus shares and splits: par-value cosmetics with zero value creation.

## 📘 Concepts

### 5.1 The irrelevance opening — MM again
Perfect world: whether a firm pays ₹1 dividend or retains it, total shareholder wealth is identical — investors *manufacture* their own dividends by selling a slice (homemade payout). The firm's only real lever: **project quality**, i.e., invest while returns > WACC, return the rest. Payout policy becomes the *residual* of investment policy — memorize that hierarchy before the theories start fighting.

### 5.2 The relevance counterattack (three armies)
1. **Bird-in-hand:** retained earnings are riskier than cash in hand ⇒ some investors pay a premium for payouts (behavioral cousin: Course 3's mental accounts — salary-like dividend discipline).
2. **Signaling:** dividends are *sticky* — boards raise them only when durable confidence exists; a cut is a five-alarm confession (prices savage cuts ~2× harder than they cheer raises). ShopKart's steady 20% payout reads: *growth funded, nerves calm.*
3. **Clientele:** pensioners want yield, founders want compounding — policies attract their audiences; consistency builds the clientele, surprise purges it.

### 5.3 Ex-date physics — the arithmetic everyone misreads
Cum-dividend price ₹30 with a ₹1 dividend becomes ≈ **₹29 ex-dividend**: the cash left the company and entered your pocket — the stock is the same factory minus ₹1 in its drawer. **FAILURE MODE:** retail seeing '−3.3% at open' as a crash. Professional read: identity, not sentiment. (Same physics powers record-date arbitrage-illusions and the 'dividend capture' daydream that taxes then murder.)

### 5.4 Dividend vs buyback — the exit door comparison (post-AC9 upgrade)
| Lens | Dividend (₹2.65L, 20% payout) | Buyback (₹15L at ₹30, 50,000 shares) |
|---|---|---|
| Cash reaches | all holders pro-rata | only sellers |
| EPS arithmetic | unchanged | +11% illusion (PAT over fewer shares) |
| Signal | durable-confidence telegraph | 'undervalued + cash rich' claim |
| Indian tax | slab-rate to shareholder, TDS 10% > ₹5k | company pays buyback tax; shareholder mostly clean |
| Honesty audit | sticky commitment | one-off — check insiders aren't the sellers! |

### 5.5 Cosmetics: bonuses & splits
**Split (₹10 par → ₹2):** one share becomes five, price divides by five, everything else untouched — cutting a pizza slice, not baking pizza. **Bonus (1:1):** reserves convert to capital, shares double, net worth constant, price halves — accounting re-labeling with confetti. Why they exist anyway: liquidity/lot-size psychology and *anchoring* (₹600 feels buyable, ₹3,000 doesn't) — Course 3's price-tag brain bugs doing marketing for the CFO. Professional verdict: cosmetics are legal mood-lighting; never value.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Residual policy with numbers: PAT ₹40L, capex pipeline ₹25L at spreads +4pp, next year pipeline ₹35L equally good. Size this year's dividend and defend against a yield-hungry activist.
2. Signal audit: a company RAISES dividend 3× in the year CFO/PAT slid to 0.4. Compose the forensic sentence a credit analyst writes home.
3. Ex-date chain: announcement +20 days, record date, ex-date — order the physics (who must own WHEN to get paid) and kill the myth 'buy on ex-date to collect free money'.
4. Buyback-or-dividend board memo for ShopKart: RE 13.4L, cash 12L, projects ₹40L dark-store at +4pp spread, WACC 12.6%. Recommend, with the two-line tax-footnote for a 30%-slab founder.
5. Bonus fog: promoter headlines '1:1 bonus = shareholders' Diwali gift!' — draft the 3-line respectful takedown (math only, no emotion).
6. Clientele shock: a yield-stock cuts payout to fund a cloud pivot. Predict the two-way holder migration and the short-horizon price pressure logic (clientele effect in motion).
7. Sticky-dividend design: propose ShopKart's formal policy line (payout band + conditions) that a future CFO can defend in a bad year without 'signaling' a funeral.

### ✅ Selected answers
1. Dividend = 40 − 25 = **₹15L** this year; defense: 'each retained rupee earns +4pp over your outside options — we return the rest, on schedule, in writing.' Doctrine = retention-with-proof, not hoarding.
2. 'Payout raised while cash-conversion collapsed — either earnings quality is doubtful (cash isn't arriving) or governance is buying applause; check insider selling + the funding source of the dividend itself (borrowed?).'
3. Buy **before ex-date** (cum) to earn the dividend; on/after ex-date the price already dropped by the dividend — 'free money on ex-date' pays you cash with one hand and de-prices the share with the other; taxes finish the illusion.
4. Recommend **residual**: fund the ₹40L store (spread +4pp over 12.6%), THEN pay residual out of what remains; if payout politics demand a token, prefer a small *buyback* (one-off, company-taxed) over raising a sticky dividend that FY26's capex will starve — footnote: at 30% slab the founder keeps ₹0.70 per dividend rupee pre-surcharge, so buybacks preserve optionality.
5. '1) Shares double, price halves, value per holder identical — nothing baked. 2) Reserves merely re-labeled as capital on the same ₹63.4L net worth. 3) If you want to gift shareholders, raise ROCE; confetti is free and worth exactly that.'
6. Yield-seekers exit (their clientele contract broken), growth mandates enter gradually — transition gap = transient price pressure BEYOND fundamentals; professional play: pre-read the clientele flip, size position for the migration, not the quarter.
7. 'Payout band 15–30% of PAT, paid only while (a) coverage ≥ 4×, (b) CCC ≤ 80 days, (c) pipeline projects below hurdle — any breach pauses payout with a written plan.' A *conditional* policy converts shocks into procedure — procedure doesn't signal panic; silence does.

## ❓ Quiz
1. On ex-date, a ₹30 share paying a ₹1 dividend opens near ₹29 because:
   - (a) the market hates dividends
   - (b) cash left the company's drawer and entered yours — price drop identity, not punishment; the factory is unchanged minus ₹1 in the till; reading it as a crash is the classic retail tell
   - (c) HFTs front-ran it
2. A firm with a ₹55L queue of >WACC projects and ₹50L PAT should, by doctrine, pay:
   - (a) 50% payout
   - (b) roughly zero — payout is the residual of investment policy: retain while spreads are positive, return explicitly what isn't needed — retention is a claim that must keep proving itself each year
   - (c) as much as peers
3. A 1:1 bonus issue makes shareholders:
   - (a) twice as rich
   - (b) exactly as rich — shares double, price halves, reserves re-label as capital with net worth untouched; it is par-value cosmetics plus liquidity psychology, never value creation — audit anyone who headlines it as a gift
   - (c) twice as poor

### ✅ Answers
1. **(b)** — ex-date physics is an identity; sentiment readings of it are comedy.
2. **(b)** — residual doctrine; (c) is clientele cosplay, (a) is a costume number.
3. **(b)** — cosmetics legal, mood-lit, and value-free; value comes only from spreads × capital.
