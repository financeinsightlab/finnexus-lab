## 📘 5.1 Finance-result verbs — conjugated for the panel 📊

Finance interviews constantly report results. Own the verb families and their grammar:

| Verb | Past | V3 | Grammar rule in use |
|---|---|---|---|
| grow/raise | grew / raised | grown / raised | "Revenue **grew** 21%." "We **have raised** ₹80 crore." |
| rise/rose/risen vs raise/raised/raised | rose | risen | **rise = no object** ("rates rose"); **raise = takes object** ("we raised funds")! ⚠️ classic pair |
| fall/fell/fallen | fell | fallen | "Margins **fell** last quarter." (fall ≠ "down-fall") |
| beat estimates | beat | beat (same!) | "Profit has **beaten/matched** estimates? NO — profit HAS **beat** estimates!" (beat = beat = beat) |
| miss | missed | missed | "The company **missed** guidance by 5%." |
| improve / decline | improved/declined | improved/declined | "Margins **have improved** 40 bps YoY." |
| double / triple / halve | doubled | doubled | verb-forms are gold: "Profit **tripled** to ₹3.85 lakh." |

**Object rule drill — rise vs raise:** The sun ROSE. Prices ROSE. RBI RAISED rates (object: rates!). "We RAISED the forecast." Never "margin raised 2%" — say "margin **rose** 2%" or "we **raised** the margin target".

## 📘 5.2 Number sentences — grammar around the figures 🔢

```
PATTERN 1 — change by:        VERB + BY + amount/%
   "Revenue rose by 21%."  "Costs fell by ₹2 lakh."
PATTERN 2 — change to:        VERB + TO + new value
   "Revenue grew to ₹71.8 lakh."  "Margin improved to 2.4%."
PATTERN 3 — from–to:          from X to Y
   "Profit jumped from ₹1.09 lakh to ₹3.85 lakh."
PATTERN 4 — percentage POINTS (bps):   for rates/ratios
   "Margin expanded by 40 basis points — from 2.0% to 2.4%."
PATTERN 5 — multiples:        X-times / doubled / tripled
   "Profit more than tripled."  "Revenue is 1.2 times last year's."
```

⚠️ BY vs TO is the panel's favourite trap: "rose **to** 21%" = the new level is 21%; "rose **by** 21%" = the GROWTH is 21%. Mixing when the stakes are percentages = instant credibility loss.

Also: "an increase **of** 21%" (noun form!) · "a 21-percent **rise** in revenue" · "roughly **a fifth higher** YoY". Noun vs verb forms both armed!

## 📘 5.3 HR-round grammar set — the money conversation 💼

The exact sentence vault for the final round (bold = the fossil risks):

**Current compensation:**
- "I'm currently **drawing** ₹9 LPA, with an in-hand of about ₹62,000 a month." (draw = formal receive; "I am getting salary" = fossil!)
- "My package **comprises** a fixed component plus a performance bonus." (NOT "comprises of"!)

**Expectations:**
- "Based on my current compensation and the role's scope, I'**d** expect around ₹11 lakh fixed."
- "I'm **flexible** if there's a clear growth path or a 6-month review **built in**."
- ⚠️ Never open with "whatever you offer" (desperation) — anchor + flexibility (your EM5 skill in speech form!).

**Notice period & joining:**
- "I **have** a 60-day notice period." / "I can **explore a buyout** for part of it." / "Realistically, I can **join** in about five to six weeks."
- Relocation: "Yes, I'm **open to relocating**." / "I'd need two weeks **to settle in**."

**Logistics:**
- Offers: "I **have** one offer in hand; I'm **at the final round** with two others — but this role is my first choice because…"
- Gaps: "I **took a six-month break to upskill** — I've **since completed** SQL and Power BI certifications."

## 📘 5.4 Explaining finance to non-finance — definition grammar 🗣️

Cross-functional panels test: can you make a COMPLEX thing simple? The grammar frame:

```
DEFINE:      "An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both
              principal and interest."
ANALOGY:     "Think of working capital as the month's cash breathing room —
              money stuck between paying suppliers and collecting from customers."
EXAMPLE:     "For instance, if margins are 2.4%, every ₹100 of sales leaves
              ₹2.40 after costs."
CHECK:       "Does that make sense? / Happy to go deeper into any part."
```

**The 3-language rule:** DROP jargon or translate instantly — "EBITDA — that's profit before interest, tax and write-downs". Owning a translation makes you look senior; jargon-dumping makes you look junior.

Grammar kit: "X **is** a [type] that [verb]" · "It **means** …" · "**In simple terms**, …" · "**Think of it as** …" · "**For instance**…".

## 📘 5.5 Case-question conditionals — the CFO seat 👑

Finance case questions = G4 conditionals with money stakes:

```
"If I were the CFO, my first priority would be margin protection —
 because revenue is already growing 21%."
"If costs rose another 10%, I would renegotiate the top-3 vendor contracts
 before touching headcount."
"If we had hedged the FX exposure earlier, we would have saved
 roughly ₹4 lakh last year."
```

Answer skeleton: **IF-setup → priority verb → because-reason → first action.** Panels score STRUCTURE + confidence of delivery, not a "right" answer.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Rise or raise: "Last year RBI ___ the repo rate by 50 bps, and loan EMIs ___ sharply." · "We ___ the sales forecast after margins ___."
2. Pattern-5 it: "Q2 profit: ₹3.85 lakh; street estimate: ₹3.2 lakh" — say it with 'beat' (careful with V3!) and with 'more than'.
3. Fix the HR fossils: "I am getting the salary of 8 LPA and my notice period is of 60 days. Whatever you offer I am okay."
4. Define for a non-finance manager: EBITDA. Use the 4-step frame (define/analogy/example/check).
5. Conditional case: "What would you do if your biggest vendor raised prices 25% overnight?"

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 1: RBI **raised** the repo rate, and EMIs **rose**. We **raised** the forecast after margins **rose** (object = raise; no-object = rise!).
- Ex 2: "Profit has **beat** estimates — ₹3.85 lakh against the street's ₹3.2." / "Profit was **more than** the street expected, by about 20%." (beat-beat-beat!)
- Ex 3: "I'm currently drawing ₹8 LPA, and my notice period is 60 days. Based on the scope, I'd expect around ₹9.5 fixed — though I'm flexible if the growth path is strong."
- Ex 5: "If my biggest vendor raised prices 25% overnight, my first move would be data, not drama: quantify the margin impact, then negotiate — volume commitments for a phased increase — while onboarding one backup vendor. If prices still didn't move, I'd execute the backup switch."

## ❓ Quiz
1. "Profit has beaten estimates" — acceptable or fossil? Rule the V3.
2. Why does HR grammar insist on "I'm currently drawing" over "my salary is"?
3. The by/to trap: company announced "margin rose to 21%" vs "rose by 21%" — restate both meanings correctly.
4. In definition grammar, why is the analogy step (not the definition step) the panel's actual test?

### ✅ Answers
1. Fossil! beat is the strange triplet beat–beat–beat; V3 is also "beat": "Profit has BEAT estimates." Beaten exists as adjective/passive flavor ("a beaten stock") — but the clean V3 is beat.
2. "Drawing" frames compensation professionally (total CTC + components); "my salary is" invites the in-hand/CTC confusion and sounds pre-corporate. Also pairs with package-comprise grammar.
3. To 21% = final LEVEL is 21% (from maybe 15%); by 21% = the JUMP equals 21% (to maybe 24.2%). In rates-of-rates world, panels listen for exactly this.
4. Definitions can be memorized from Google; analogies prove you've digested the concept ("working capital = the month's cash breathing room"). Understanding-first speakers get hired; reciters get filtered.
