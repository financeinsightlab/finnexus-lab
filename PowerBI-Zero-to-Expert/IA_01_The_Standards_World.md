## 📘 Concepts

### 1.1 Why standards exist — the trust engine

A set of financial statements is a **promise made in a language**. If every company invents its own grammar, comparison dies and capital gets expensive: investors price the risk of *not understanding* on top of the risk of the business itself. Standards exist to make ₹1 of profit mean the same thing in Mumbai, London, and (almost) New York. The 2008 crisis, Satyam (2009), and every post-mortem since repeat the same lesson: markets don't crash on bad numbers — they crash on numbers they **cannot trust**. Standards are the trust infrastructure; auditors are its inspectors; your job as a finance professional is to read the infrastructure fluently.

### 1.2 The rule-writing machines

| Level | Body | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Global | **IFRS Foundation** (trustees, London) | Governance + funding of standard-setting; publishes IFRS Foundation Constitution |
| Global | **IASB** (International Accounting Standards Board) | Writes **IFRS Standards** (new) inheriting **IAS** (older, pre-2001); issues IFRIC interpretations |
| India | **ICAI** (Institute of Chartered Accountants) | Drafts the Indian-converged versions with carve-outs |
| India | **NFRA** (National Financial Reporting Authority, 2018) | Recommends standards to government; oversees auditors of listed/large cos |
| India | **MCA** (Ministry of Corporate Affairs) | **Notifies** Ind AS into law under the Companies Act — only notification makes a standard enforceable |

Key mental model: the IASB writes IFRS; nobody in India follows "IFRS" directly. India follows **Ind AS** — IFRS converged with deliberate Indian deviations.

### 1.3 Convergence, not adoption — carve-outs and carve-ins

India chose **convergence** (Ind AS ≈ IFRS with Indian modifications) instead of blind adoption, so the standards fit Indian law and market reality:

- **Carve-outs** (Ind AS differs from IFRS): Ind AS 115 allows excise duty-style presentation choices historically; Ind AS 101 adds exemptions specific to Indian transition; Ind AS 24 (related parties) aligns with Companies Act definitions; the famous one — IFRS 9's effective-interest and ECL mechanics arrived in Ind AS 109 with Indian clarifications.
- **Carve-ins** (Ind AS requires MORE than IFRS): Ind AS 1 demands presentation aligned to **Schedule III of the Companies Act 2013** (balance-sheet formats); extra disclosures the law demands (CSR, managerial remuneration) ride along with the standards.
- Practical read: an "IFRS vs Ind AS" differences list is exam and interview gold — the professional habit is to **never assume** an IFRS answer travels to India unchecked.

### 1.4 Who must follow Ind AS — the roadmap (canon thresholds)

The Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015 phased it in by net worth (as at specified dates):

| Phase | From FY | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Voluntary | 2015-16 | Any company could opt in early |
| **Phase I** | **2016-17** | Listed (or listing) companies with **net worth ≥ ₹500 crore** (+ their holding/subsidiary/JV/associate chain) |
| **Phase II** | **2017-18** | **All listed companies**, plus unlisted companies with **net worth ≥ ₹250 crore** (+ group chain) |
| NBFCs Phase I | 2018-19 | NBFCs with net worth ≥ ₹500 crore |
| NBFCs Phase II | 2019-20 | NBFCs (listed/unlisted) with net worth ≥ ₹250 crore |
| Banks & insurance | — | **Deferred** by RBI/IRDAI — a separate notification is still awaited |

Two professional drills: (1) once Ind AS applies, it applies **forever** — you cannot go back even if net worth later falls below the threshold; (2) the group chain rule pulls in subsidiaries and associates automatically so groups report in one language.

### 1.5 Principles vs rules — and the IFRS/IAS naming

- **IFRS/Ind AS is principles-based**: it tells you the objective ("recognize revenue when control transfers") and trusts preparers to apply judgment; **US GAAP is rules-based** (bright-line tests, extensive industry guidance). Principles travel across industries; rules travel across court cases.
- Naming: pre-2001 standards are **IAS** (IAS 2 Inventories, IAS 16 PPE, IAS 12 Income Taxes), post-2001 are **IFRS** (IFRS 9, 15, 16, 17). Ind AS mirrors both families: the low numbers carry the IAS heritage (Ind AS 2 ≈ IAS 2, Ind AS 16 ≈ IAS 16, Ind AS 33 ≈ IAS 33), while the 100-series carries the IFRS generation — **Ind AS 101 ≈ IFRS 1** (first-time adoption), **Ind AS 109 ≈ IFRS 9** (financial instruments), **Ind AS 115 ≈ IFRS 15** (revenue), **Ind AS 116 ≈ IFRS 16** (leases). Memorize the translation pairs as we hit each one this course.
- The famous exhibit: **LIFO is permitted under US GAAP and banned under IFRS/Ind AS** — one line that explains "principles vs the inventory debate" forever.

## 🧪 LAB — Is Ind AS Even Your Problem? (10 min)

Meridian Paints Pvt Ltd facts: unlisted; net worth ₹260cr (latest audited BS); revenue ₹900cr; has one wholly-owned subsidiary (net worth ₹40cr). Its bank asks: "Will next year's covenants be tested on Ind AS numbers?"

1. Is Meridian inside mandatory Ind AS, and from which FY/phase?
2. What about the ₹40cr subsidiary?
3. Meridian's net worth drops to ₹180cr two years later. Can it exit Ind AS?
4. Meridian's sister concern is an NBFC with net worth ₹300cr. When did SHE enter Ind AS?

**Why this matters:** lenders, boards, and investors ask "which GAAP?" before they ask "what's the number?" — answering in seconds with the phase table is instant credibility.

**🔑 Lab answers:**
1. Yes — **Phase II (FY 2017-18)**: unlisted companies with net worth ≥ ₹250cr are mandatory (the all-listed rule doesn't matter here; the NW test catches Meridian at ₹260cr).
2. The subsidiary is pulled in **through the chain rule** — holding/subsidiary/JV/associate of a covered company follows Ind AS regardless of its own ₹40cr net worth.
3. **No exit.** Once Ind AS applies, it applies forever — comparability would die if companies bounced between frameworks as net worth moved.
4. NBFC with ₹300cr NW: **NBFC Phase II, FY 2019-20** (≥ ₹250cr threshold).

## 💪 Exercises

1. Your CFO says "we follow IFRS, not Ind AS — simpler for the foreign parent." Correct her in two sentences.
2. Name the three Indian bodies in the standard pipeline in order, and the single act that makes a standard law.
3. Give one carve-out style reason and one carve-in style reason India kept deviations from IFRS.
4. A US investor compares your company's inventory to Coke's. What single policy difference could distort gross margins, and which system allows it?
5. Explain to a founder, in plain words, why his ₹260cr-NW private company "suddenly" became an Ind AS company while his ₹240cr friend did not.

### ✅ Selected answers

1. "No Indian company follows IFRS directly; MCA notifies Ind AS, which is the legally enforceable, IFRS-converged text with Indian carve-outs. If we are covered, Ind AS is the law — IFRS-group reporting to the parent is a separate consolidation mapping exercise."
2. ICAI drafts → NFRA recommends → **MCA notifies** (notification = enforceability).
3. Carve-out logic: fit Indian law/market structure (e.g., Schedule III presentation formats, Companies Act definitions in Ind AS 24). Carve-in logic: standards & law travel together in India — CSR, managerial remuneration, audit-trail style disclosures ride with the books.
4. **LIFO** — permitted under US GAAP, banned under IFRS/Ind AS. In a rising-price world LIFO pushes recent, expensive units into COGS → lower US gross margins on identical purchases.
5. The 2015 Rules drew hard net-worth lines and phased mandatorily: ₹250cr unlisted NW from FY 2017-18. Your friend sits under the line (for now — growth will cross it), and once you cross, the language is permanent.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** In India, an accounting standard becomes legally enforceable when:
(a) ICAI issues the exposure draft
(b) MCA notifies it under the Companies Act — drafting by ICAI and recommendation by NFRA are steps, not enforcement
(c) NFRA publishes a guidance note
(d) SEBI circularizes it to listed companies

**Q2.** Meridian (unlisted) crosses ₹250cr net worth this year and drops below it three years later. Its Ind AS status:
(a) applies only while net worth stays above ₹250cr
(b) mandatory from the Phase II trigger and permanent — there is no exit once Ind AS applies
(c) optional at board discretion each year
(d) lapses automatically below the threshold

**Q3.** The cleanest one-line difference between US GAAP and IFRS/Ind AS for inventory:
(a) US GAAP bans LIFO; IFRS allows it
(b) US GAAP permits LIFO; IFRS/Ind AS ban it — a living fossil of the principles-vs-rules debate
(c) both mandate weighted average
(d) IFRS mandates FIFO; US GAAP is free choice

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — ICAI drafts and NFRA recommends; only MCA notification turns text into law.
2. **(b)** — permanence protects comparability; the threshold decides entry, never exit.
3. **(b)** — LIFO's US survival is the standard exhibit for why 'identical' inventory can price differently across systems.
