## 📘 Concepts

### 5.1 The Five Traps (memorize the names — names are vaccines)

**TRAP 1 · The Gender-Name Bait — \"Suman is the sister of…\" no wait.**
Names like **Suman, Kiran, Ashu, Jyoti?** (Jyoti is fairly female) — the exam LOVES ambiguous names: *Suman, Kiran, Kamal(?), Neeraj is male but \"Neeta alias…\"*. Rule: **names never gender-lock; only WORDS do** (son, brother, wife, nephew, father). \"Suman is Ramesh's child\" → Suman stays ◇. The question \"How many females?\" then punishes guessers. *Vaccine: gender comes from relation-words, never from names.*

**TRAP 2 · The Self-Reference \"only son of my father\" trick.**
\"He is the only son of MY FATHER's only son\" (said by a man about himself) → his own son. \"My mother's husband's only son\" → ME. *Vaccine: wherever 'my' appears, walk ME-anchored: my parent → parent's \"only X\" → is that me or my sibling? The word ONLY is the lock that points back at the speaker (or exactly one sibling).*

**TRAP 3 · The Cannot-Be-Determined Phobia.**
Students refuse the option \"cannot be determined\" out of pride — exams count on it. CBD is correct whenever a **gender, a link, or a bridge** is missing even after a complete honest tree. *Vaccine: if the tree has a ◇ on the questioned person or a missing edge between branches, CBD is the ANSWER, not a failure. Say it boldly — it scores the same mark.*

**TRAP 4 · Cross-Cousin vs Sibling long-range mix-up.**
\"My mother's brother's son\" = cousin (mama's son) — students in a hurry answer 'nephew'. One-stair counting error: mother's brother is +1; HIS son is back to 0 = MY LEVEL = **cousin**, never nephew (nephew needs a −1 endpoint). *Vaccine: trace aloud with stairs: up 1, same-level 0, down to… 0 → cousin. Nephew/niece END at −1.*

**TRAP 5 · The Generation Skip.**
\"My father's mother's only grandson\" — grandmother +2, her GRANDSON must be at 0… grandsons live at HER −2 = my level 0 → the person is **me or my brother** (only-grandson → just me, if speaker is male). Students who \"count by feel\" land on nephew/uncle. *Vaccine: grand-words = two stairs, always. grand-father: +2. grand-son: −2. No exceptions.*

### 5.2 The 20-second elimination kit (when the tree stalls)

1. **Generation filter:** options crossing impossible stairs die first (a \"grandmother\" option can't sit at my level).
2. **Gender filter:** options needing a gender that the data never locked → die (unless CBD is an option — then it may live, see Trap 3).
3. **Edge-count parity:** \"cousin\" = 2 edges minimum through junction; \"sibling\" = shares a parent node. Compare required vs actual edges.
4. **Back-plug the MIDDLE option** (copy of AV6 superpower): assume it's true, redraw 10-second tree, clash? → eliminate, collapse search space by half.
5. **Last resort:** write your tree's raw trace (\"up 1, across 0, down 1\") and match that FINGERPRINT to the option names. Fingerprints don't lie.

## 🏟️ THE MOCK ARENA — Paper BR-01 (10 Qs · 8 minutes · negative self-marking −0.25)

*Set your timer BEFORE reading Q1. Rough sheet top line: \"Chain → Tree → Trace. CBD is legal. Names ≠ genders.\"*

**Q1.** Pointing to a photograph, a man said, \"I have no brothers or sisters. That man's father is my father's son.\" Who is the man in the photograph?
(a) His son (b) Himself (c) His father (d) His nephew

**Q2.** If `A + B` means A is the brother of B; `A − B` means A is the mother of B; `A × B` means A is the daughter of B: in `P − Q + R`, how is Q related to P?
(a) Son (b) Daughter (c) Son or daughter (d) Mother

**Q3.** Deepa is the sister of Rahul. Rahul is the son of Kamla. Mohan is the father of Kamla. How is Deepa related to Mohan?
(a) Granddaughter (b) Daughter (c) Niece (d) Cousin

**Q4.** In a family of 6 — P, Q, R, S, T, U: P is the wife of Q. R is the son of S. S is the wife of U. T is the daughter of U. Q is the brother of S. How many FEMALES are certainly in the family?
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 5

**Q5.** \"Pointing to a woman, Raj said, 'She is the only daughter of the only sister of my father.'\" The woman is Raj's:
(a) Sister (b) Cousin (c) Aunt (d) Niece

**Q6.** Introducing a boy, a girl said, \"He is the son of the daughter of the father of my uncle.\" How is the boy related to the girl?
(a) Brother (b) Uncle (c) Nephew (d) Cousin

**Q7.** A family has: a father, a mother, their 3 children, and the father's father. Minimum members, and how many generations?
(a) 6 / 3 (b) 7 / 3 (c) 6 / 2 (d) 5 / 3

**Q8.** DATA SUFFICIENCY — Is T the daughter of Q? I: Q is the father of S and T. II: S is the son of Q.
(a) I alone (b) II alone (c) Both together sufficient (d) Neither sufficient

**Q9.** Statements: X is brother of Y. Y is wife of Z. Kiran is child of Z. How is Kiran related to X if Kiran is X's niece?
(a) It confirms Kiran is female — but wait, 'niece' itself locks it (b) Kiran must be Z's daughter and X knows it (c) If Kiran is the 'niece of X', Kiran is certainly female AND certainly Z's child — both locks from the question's own wording (d) Cannot be determined even with the niece wording

**Q10.** \"A is the sister of B. B is the mother of C. C is the sister of D. D is the son of E.\" Which is TRUE?
(a) E is certainly male (b) A is certainly C's aunt (c) D is certainly female (d) C has exactly one sibling

### ✅ Mock Arena Answer Key & Analysis

1. **(a)** — \"my father's son\" with NO SIBLINGS = ME; \"that man's father is me\" → photo is **his son**. (Trap 2 self-reference.)
2. **(b)** — `P − Q` = P mother of Q; `Q + R` = Q is BROTHER of R → **Q ♂ = P's son.** Operator 2 genders Q ✔. (Coded + gender gift.)
3. **(a)** — Deepa ─ Rahul siblings; both children of Kamla; Mohan is Kamla's father → Deepa is Mohan's **granddaughter**. (+2 endpoints, Trap 5 safe.)
4. **(b)** — Couple P♀═Q♂; couple S═U… S's gender: \"R is SON of S\" doesn't gender S! But \"S is the WIFE of U\" → **S ♀**, U ♂; T ♀ (daughter of U); Q's sibling S ✔. Females locked: P, S, T = **3**. R ◇ excluded. (Trap 1 + honest count.)
5. **(b)** — father's only SISTER = my **bua (paternal aunt)**; her only daughter = my **cousin**. (Trap 4 defended: same level 0.)
6. **(d)** — \"father of my uncle\" = my **grandfather** (uncle's father); \"daughter of grandfather\" = my **aunt** (father's sister, since dad's other daughters are my aunts — or my own MOTHER… \"the daughter of my grandfather\" could be my mother herself!); \"son of [that daughter]\" = **cousin or brother** — MCQ offers cousin as the standard intended answer (daughter read as *another* daughter). **(d) Cousin** ✔ — classic intended-answer reading; note the ambiguity in your journal!
7. **(a)** — F, M, 3 kids, GF = **6** members (GF is extra, no stacking possible — GF isn't 'the father' because the father is stated distinctly; children-gen = 1). Generations: GF › couple › kids = **3**.
8. **(c)** — I alone: S, T both children; T's gender open → insufficient. II alone: nothing about T → insufficient. Together: 2 named children, one is SON S → T is the other child… \"daughter\"? NO! T could be another SON. II genders S, leaves T ◇ → **NEITHER sufficient → (d)!!** — Re-audit: is there any lock on T? None. **Answer (d) Neither.** (This is DS's legendary sting — re-run the triple audit if you marked (c).)
9. **(c)** — \"niece\" is a gendered word from the QUESTION STEM (not a guess) → Kiran ♀ locked ✔ AND niece-of-X means child of X's sibling → Z is X's sibling's spouse… Kiran is child of Z, Z married to Y, Y ─ X siblings → Kiran is child of Y-and-Z = Y's child = X's sibling's child ✔ **niece, female, certain** → (c). (Trap 3 inversely: certainty CAN come from the stem's own words.)
10. **(b)** — A is B's SISTER (A♀); B is C's mother → A is C's **aunt, certain** ✔. E's gender: \"D is son of E\" doesn't gender E (E = D's father or mother — C's sibling D … B mothers C; is E B's spouse? never stated — E is parent of D, D is C's sister-sibling's child-edge… C is SISTER of D → they share parents; B mothers C and thus D; E must be the other parent ♂ or ♀ — **ungendered** → (a) false. D ♂ (son) ✔ so (c) false. \"Exactly one sibling\" — only D shown, never 'only' → (d) overstated.

**Scoring journal:** /10 raw?, −0.25 per wrong, top-band = 8+ net in 8:00. Under 6? Re-run BR2 (chains) + BR4 (DS) checklists, then attempt Arena again in 48 h.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** \"Suman is the child of Ramesh\" — and the question asks \"how many females in the family?\" — Suman must be:
- (a) Counted as female (Suman is a girl's name)
- (b) **Left GENDER-NEUTRAL (◇)** — names never lock gender; only relation-words do; Suman is excluded from BOTH counts until a word like 'son/daughter' locks it
- (c) Counted as male (more common)
- (d) Removed from the tree

**Q2.** A man says, \"He is the only son of my father's wife.\" (No adoptions in play.) 'He' is:
- (a) The man's brother
- (b) **The man HIMSELF** — father's wife = his mother; mother's ONLY SON = him; the word 'only' points back at the speaker
- (c) The man's nephew
- (d) Cannot be determined

**Q3.** \"Cannot be determined\" as an MCQ option should be chosen when:
- (a) You couldn't finish in time
- (b) **A complete, honest tree still leaves the questioned person's gender OR a linking edge open** — CBD is a legitimate scoring answer, not an admission of weakness
- (c) The question feels hard
- (d) Two options both look close

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — Trap 1, the Gender-Name Bait. Suman/Kiran/Kamal stay diamonds until a relation-WORD locks them. Counting them into females hands the setter your mark, gift-wrapped.
2. **(b)** — Trap 2, the self-reference lock. Chain: my father's wife → my mother → mother's only son → ME. Every 'my' is a walk starting from the speaker's own node; 'only' is the padlock at the end.
3. **(b)** — Trap 3 phobia cured. CBD is CORRECT precisely when the locked tree honestly can't answer: diamond on the target, or two branches with no bridge. Choosing it boldly, with the tree as proof, is the professional play.
