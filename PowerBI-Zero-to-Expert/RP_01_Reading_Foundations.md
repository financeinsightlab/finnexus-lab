## 📘 1.1 Purpose before speed 🎯

Readers fail BEFORE line one — by not knowing WHY they're reading. Three purposes, three speeds:

| Purpose | Speed | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Hunt a fact** (price, date, name) | FAST scan, skip everything else | "When is the meeting?" → find only the time |
| **Get the idea** (gist) | MEDIUM skim, headline + first/last lines | "What is this article about?" |
| **Master it** (study mode) | SLOW deep read, notes | Your course modules! |

✏️ **The one-question habit:** before ANY text, say aloud: "After reading this, I want to know ___." Reading with a question = reading with a magnet.

## 📘 1.2 Chunking — the #1 speed technique 🧱

Beginners read: "The / manager / approved / the / new / budget / yesterday."
Readers chunk: **[The manager] / [approved] / [the new budget] / [yesterday.]**

Your eyes should take **3–5 word meaning-groups** per glance. Grammar itself tells you where chunks end — noun-group, verb, preposition-group:

```
[ShopKart India] [has opened] [100 new dark stores] [across tier-2 cities]
[in just one year] — [the company's fastest expansion] [to date.]
```

Drill: take any sentence, draw "/" between meaning groups, then read gliding chunk-to-chunk. Day 3 of this drill feels like your eyes got an upgrade. ⚠️ Speed killer #1 is NOT vocabulary — it's word-by-word eye movement + silently saying every word (subvocalization — Module RP4 has the cure).

## 📘 1.3 Sentence surgery — find the engine 🔧

Long sentence = traffic jam? Cut to the engine (subject + verb), add parts back one by one:

*"The quarterly report, which was prepared by the finance team after two weeks of analysis and several meetings with vendors, shows a 21% increase in revenue."*

1. Engine: **The report … shows … a 21% increase.** (SUBJECT + VERB + OBJECT — done, gist captured!)
2. Add chunks: *(which was prepared by the finance team)* — who made it · *(in revenue)* — where the increase is.
3. Skip safe zones: between dashes, inside commas — decoration, never the engine.

Rule: commas and dashes hide "extra" information; the main road is SUBJECT → VERB. Practice on EVERY confusing sentence you meet this week.

## 📘 1.4 Unknown words — the 3 rescue moves 🛟

**Move 1 — SKIP:** if the sentence still makes sense without it, drop it silently. "The CEO's *perspicacious* comments impressed investors" → "The CEO's ___ comments impressed investors" — you still get 95%!

**Move 2 — GUESS from context (4 context types):**
- **Definition clue:** "A unicorn, that is, a $1-billion startup,…" (the sentence DEFINES it!)
- **Example clue:** "Tech giants — Infosys, TCS, Wipro — hired freshers." (giants = big companies)
- **Contrast clue:** "Unlike the bullish traders, the bears expected prices to fall." (bulls ↔ bears = optimists vs pessimists)
- **Logic clue:** "She was *famished*, so she ordered two thalis." (famished = very hungry, obviously!)

**Move 3 — RESCUE (dictionary):** only for (a) the word repeats everywhere, (b) it's central to the meaning, (c) it's a word you want to OWN for speaking. Write THOSE in your vocabulary notes 📝. Rule: max 2 dictionary stops per page — reading flow is sacred.

## 📘 1.5 The 3-gear method (course refresher, upgraded) ⚙️

- **GEAR 1 — Skim (30 sec):** title + first line of each paragraph → gist sentence: "this is about ___"
- **GEAR 2 — Scan (as needed):** hunt facts with your question-magnet
- **GEAR 3 — Deep read:** full read with chunking + surgery + rescue moves
- **GEAR 4 (NEW!) — React:** 10 seconds — "What do I think? What will I remember?" Reading without reaction evaporates by dinner. Active reading = gear 4 always on.

## 📘 1.6 GRADED TEXT A (level: easy) — read with chunking 🎧

🎧 **[Read-along: listen + read together — model voice, slow speed (Indian English)](english_audio/rp1_readalong_graded_text.mp3)** — play it, follow the text with your eyes, then read alone. Eyes + ears = double learning!

> **Ravi's First Week**
>
> Ravi started his new job at ShopKart on Monday. The office in Delhi was bigger than his old one, with bright lights and a wall full of colorful charts.
>
> His manager, Priya, gave him three tasks for the first week. First, he had to learn the company dashboard. Second, he had to meet everyone on the finance team. Third — and most important — he had to send one report by Friday 5 PM, without fail.
>
> On Wednesday, Ravi got stuck. A number in the sales file did not match the dashboard. His old habit said, "Stay quiet and figure it out alone." But Priya had told him, "Questions are free here."
>
> So he asked. Ten minutes later, the error was found — not in his file, but in the source system. On Friday at 3 PM, Ravi sent his first report. On it, Priya wrote two words: "Keep going."

**Comprehension check (answer after reading ONCE — no re-reading!):**
1. How many tasks did Ravi receive? What were they (in 5 words each)?
2. What went wrong on Wednesday?
3. Where was the real error — Ravi's file or the source system?
4. What habit did Ravi change because of Priya's advice?

## 📘 1.7 GRADED TEXT B (level: easy+) — hunt the numbers first 🔢

> **The Weekend Market**
>
> Meera reaches the Sabzi Mandi at 8 AM every Saturday. Her list never changes: 2 kg tomatoes, 1 kg onions, 250 grams ginger, and a bunch of coriander.
>
> Last Saturday, tomatoes had jumped from ₹40 to ₹60 per kg. "Festival season," the seller explained, "everything doubles in October." Meera smiled, bought just 1 kg, and took 2 kg of cabbage at ₹30 instead.
>
> Her mother tasted the cabbage sabzi at lunch and said, "Tomato would have been better." Meera replied, "Mummy, ₹60 tomatoes have to earn their place on my plate. Next week, when prices fall, tomatoes return — cabbage is fired!"
>
> That is how Meera runs her kitchen: like a small company — a fixed budget, flexible plans, and absolutely no emotional shopping.

**Comprehension check:**
1. List Meera's usual 4-item shopping list.
2. Why did tomato prices rise?
3. What exactly did Meera buy last Saturday (quantities!)?
4. In one line: what is Meera's "company rule" for shopping?

## 🧪 LAB RP1 — The active-reading boot camp (45 min)

1. **Chunk-marking (10 min):** from Text A, copy any 3 sentences into your notes 📝, draw "/" between meaning-groups, then read aloud gliding chunk-to-chunk twice.
2. **Sentence surgery (10 min):** operate on this one — "The festive quarter, during which ShopKart added forty thousand new customers from smaller cities and towns, pushed yearly revenue up by almost one-fifth." → write the ENGINE only, then full meaning.
3. **Gear-4 practice (10 min):** re-read Text B's last paragraph. React aloud in one sentence: "I think Meera is ___ because ___." (Reaction = memory glue!)
4. **Unknown-word workout (10 min):** in both graded texts, find 2 words you don't fully own (e.g., "mandi", "earn their place"). Do NOT open a dictionary — guess each from context type (definition/example/contrast/logic), write your guess, THEN verify.
5. 🎧 **Read-along (5 min):** play the model audio twice — first eyes-following, then read WITHOUT audio. Notice how the second solo read feels smoother. That's the read-along effect.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Which reading purpose and why: (a) finding a train number in a ticket SMS, (b) understanding a new company's offer letter, (c) checking who won the match.
2. Chunk this correctly with /: "Revenue grew by eighteen percent while costs stayed flat during the festive quarter."
3. Sentence surgery: find the engine — "Our analysts, supported by a new data-cleaning pipeline and years of experience, delivered the quarterly variance report ahead of schedule."
4. Guess without dictionary: "The kirana shop, unlike the supermarket, offers *udhaar* — the shopkeeper writes your purchase in a notebook, and you pay at month-end." What is udhaar? Which context clue type worked for you?
5. Your one-question habit: write the magnet-question you'd set before reading (a) today's weather, (b) a company's About page before an interview.

### ✅ Selected answers
- Text A check: 1) Three tasks: learn dashboard, meet finance team, send Friday report. 2) Sales number didn't match dashboard. 3) Source system. 4) Asking questions instead of quietly struggling.
- Text B check: 1) 2 kg tomatoes, 1 kg onions, 250 g ginger, coriander. 2) Festival season (October). 3) 1 kg tomatoes + 2 kg cabbage (NO ginger? she kept the list minus one tomato kg!). 4) Fixed budget, flexible plans, no emotional shopping.
- Ex 2: [Revenue / grew / by eighteen percent] / [while costs / stayed flat] / [during the festive quarter.]
- Ex 3: Engine: "Our analysts delivered the variance report." (supported-by-pipeline = decoration!)
- Ex 4: Udhaar = buy now, pay later / informal credit. Clue type: definition (the sentence literally explains it after the dash!).

## ❓ Quiz
1. Why does a reader set a question BEFORE reading — what does it change in the brain?
2. Chunking says eyes should stop every ___ words — and WHY does grammar help?
3. "The report, compiled over three months by the credit team, warns of rising customer defaults." — what's the engine, and what's the safe-to-skip decoration?
4. When is the dictionary (rescue move) actually correct?

### ✅ Answers
1. A question activates the reticular filter — your brain starts flagging relevant lines while skimming. Reading with purpose filters 80% noise automatically.
2. 3–5 words per glance, and grammar is the map: noun-groups, verbs and preposition-groups are natural chunk borders — you're reading the sentence's own architecture.
3. Engine: "The report … warns of rising customer defaults." Skip zone: "compiled over three months by the credit team" (comma-bracketed decoration).
4. When the word (a) repeats throughout the text, (b) is central to meaning, or (c) is one you want to use in your own speech. Otherwise: skip or guess and keep flowing.
