## 📘 Concepts

### 7.1 The five named traps

**TRAP 1 · WHOLE-PART FLIP** — "staff ratio 2:5 → 40% men". Wrong: whole = 7 parts → 2/7 ≈ 28.6%.
*Antidote:* every ratio sets up two numbers — term-over-term (2:3 cousin) and term-over-SUM (2/7). Decide which the question asks before computing.

**TRAP 2 · UNIT SNEAK** — "ratio of ₹3 to 60 paise = 3:60?" Ratios need same units: ₹3 = 300 paise → **5:1**.
*Antidote:* unit-convert BEFORE the colon, never after.

**TRAP 3 · MIDDLE-TERM MISMATCH** — chaining A:B = 3:4 with B:C = 5:6 by writing 3:4:6 straight. B has two faces; LCM them first (→ 15:20:24).
*Antidote:* stare at the middle term's two values; different → normalize, always.

**TRAP 4 · TIME DILUTION** — partnership split on capital alone, ignoring months (₹45k-for-9-months beats ₹30k-for-12 in total product — 8:9, remember?).
*Antidote:* partnership = rupee-MONTHS; months column first.

**TRAP 5 · ADD-SPLIT CONFUSION** — "adjust 3:5 by adding 4 to both — cleaner ratio?" 7:9 is NOT an operation, it's a new ratio (ABCD law: bends toward 1).
*Antidote:* multiplication/division preserves; addition/subtraction transforms. If the question adds k to both terms — compute the NEW ratio fresh, never assume "same".

### 7.2 The 5-gate radar

```
GATE 1: TERM-VS-WHOLE?   (fraction of WHAT: term, sum, or other?)
GATE 2: SAME UNITS?      (Rs vs paise, cm vs m, L vs ml)
GATE 3: MIDDLE COMMON?   (chains: LCM the shared term)
GATE 4: TIME ON THE LINE?(partnership: months docket open)
GATE 5: LEGAL OPERATION? (only x or / preserves ratios)
```

### 7.3 Triage + guess-EV (refresher)

BANK: splits, HCF-simplify, fourth proportional (<25 s). GAMBLE: income-savings skeletons, two-vessel mixtures (60–90 s). DUMP: replacement formula + weighted vessels + a disguised unit-sneak under 60 s — the −0.25 bleed is real; blind guess is +0.0625, one elimination raises it to +0.167.

## 💪 Exercises

1. Name the trap: "ratio of ₹2.5 to 75 paise is 2.5:75 = 1:30."
2. Name the trap: "A:B = 3:4, B:C = 7:8, so A:B:C = 3:4:8."
3. Radar the classic: "A reads 2/7 of his salary as 28.6%, but his colleague says the ratio of his rent:savings is 2:5 so he spends 40% on rent." Who's making which error?
4. A partner wants "fairer shares": keep 3:4 capitals but "add ₹10,000 to each share at payout". Legal operation? Predict the family's true ratio-shift direction.
5. Speed-gates: run all 5 gates on "₹3 tiles vs 75-paise parts in 4:1 — tiles per part?"

### ✅ Selected answers

1. **UNIT SNEAK** — convert first: ₹2.5 = 250 paise → 250:75 = **10:3**, never 1:30.
2. **MIDDLE-TERM MISMATCH** — B speaks 4 and 8: LCM 8 → A:B = 6:8, B:C = 7:8 → **6:7:8** is the only legal chain.
3. Both flip term-vs-whole: 2/7 OF THE WHOLE is indeed 28.6% ✔ (his split was 2:5 → parts 7); the colleague reads term 2 over part 5 for the same 2:5 split and lands 40% — **TRAP 1**, one ratio, two readings, only one matches the question asked.
4. Illegal: +₹10,000 to each share CHANGES the ratio (toward 1 — the smaller share gains relatively more); a "fairness tweak" is a new deal, not the same deal — recompute fresh and disclose.
5. Units first (₹3 = 300 paise) → 4:1 with same-kind parts → 300:75 = **4 tiles per paise-part** ✔ gates passed: whole (N/A — direct ratio), units ✔, middle (single ratio), time (N/A), operation legal ✔.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** Arena re-run: ₹66,000 in 7:4 pays out:
- (a) ₹36,000 and ₹30,000
- (b) **₹42,000 and ₹24,000 — 11 parts of ₹6,000 taken 7 and 4; gate 1 names the whole, the tile-check (66,000) signs the receipt**
- (c) ₹38,000 and ₹28,000
- (d) ₹44,000 and ₹22,000

**Q2.** "Adjust 3:5 by adding 4 to each term for a cleaner split" — the verdict:
- (a) legal, same ratio
- (b) **illegal — 7:9 is a NEW ratio (37.5% → 43.75%): the ABCD law; only multiplication and division preserve ratios, addition bends them toward 1**
- (c) legal if k is small
- (d) illegal only if decimals appear

**Q3.** The 5-gate radar's FIRST gate asks:
- (a) is the HCF extracted?
- (b) **TERM-VS-WHOLE — does this question want term-over-term or term-over-sum? Naming the denominator (2:5 vs 2/7) before arithmetic is half the subject**
- (c) calculate the cross product
- (d) draw the vessels

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — equal-parts plus tile-check; the oldest move in the course never loses a mark.
2. **(b)** — additive tweaks are new ratios with good PR; compute fresh, disclose the bend.
3. **(b)** — then units, middles, time, and legality — five gates, five seconds.
