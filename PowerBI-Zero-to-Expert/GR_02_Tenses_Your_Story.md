## 📘 2.1 The interview tense map — only 5 matter 🗺️

| Tense | Interview job | Signal words | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Present simple** | facts, habits, duties now | daily, usually, every week | "I build Power BI dashboards." |
| **Present continuous** | temporary/current projects | currently, these days, right now | "I'm currently automating the month-end pack." |
| **Past simple** | finished events, old jobs | in 2022, last year, ago | "I studied commerce at DU." |
| **Present perfect** | experience (time NOT specified) | ever, never, already, since, for, so far | "I have worked with SQL for 2 years." |
| **Future (will/going to)** | goals, promises, plans | next year, soon, plan to | "I plan to complete PL-300 this quarter." |

Rule that solves 70% of tensing: **finished TIME = past simple. Open/experience time = present perfect.**

## 📘 2.2 Present perfect — the experience tense ⭐

This is THE interview tense — it connects past work to today's value:

```
Formula: I/we/they + HAVE + V3   |   he/she/it + HAS + V3
"I have worked on 4 client dashboards."
"ShopKart has grown 21% this year."
I HAVE → I've · she HAS → she's
```

Uses in your story:
- **Experience, counting achievements:** "I have automated 6 monthly reports." "I have never missed a closing deadline."
- **Change that matters today:** "The market has shifted toward quick commerce."
- **Since/for ongoing:** "I have been leading this project since January." (more below!)

⚠️ The V3 list (drill): work–worked–worked · build–built–**built** · learn–learned–learned · take–took–**taken** · make–made–made · see–saw–**seen** · go–went–**gone** · do–did–**done** · write–wrote–**written** · speak–spoke–**spoken** · choose–chose–**chosen** · grow–grew–**grown**.

Trap: ❌ "I have went" / "I have did" → V3 only!

## 📘 2.3 Past vs present perfect — the panel's acid test 🧪

```
"I worked at Infosys for 2 years."        ← FINISHED (you left) → past
"I have worked at ShopKart for 2 years."  ← STILL THERE → present perfect
"I studied SQL in 2024."                  ← exact finished time → past
"I have studied SQL."                     ← anytime experience → perfect
```

**The law: present perfect HATES exact past time.** ❌ "I have joined in 2023" → ✅ "I joined in 2023" OR "I have been here since 2023" (moves the point into open time!).

Pronounce the timeline while speaking: "In 2023, I **joined** (past, time-boxed) ShopKart, and since then I **have built** (perfect, continuing) the finance reporting suite."

## 📘 2.4 Since vs For — 10 seconds to never forget ⏳

- **SINCE + POINT** (when it started): since 2023 · since Monday · since January · since I joined.
- **FOR + DURATION** (how long): for 2 years · for 6 months · for a decade.

✏️ Drill aloud: "I've worked here **for** 2 years — **since** 2024." · "ShopKart has focused on tier-2 **since** 2024 / **for** about 2 years."

Bonus (India extraoil): "5 years **back**" ❌ → "5 years **ago**" ✅ ("I joined 2 years ago", "back" alone confused global panels).

## 📘 2.5 Future forms — goals that sound real 🚀

- **will** — promises & decisions made NOW: "I **will** finish the migration by Friday." / spontaneous: "I think I'**ll** specialize in finance analytics."
- **going to** — plans with evidence/intention: "I'm **going to** take PL-300 this quarter." (already registered!)
- **present continuous as future** — fixed arrangements: "I'm **joining** the Mumbai office in September."

Interview magic line: *"Currently I'm **working** as an analyst; this quarter I'm **going to** certify in PL-300; and in 2 years I **will** be ready to lead a small team."* — all three futures in one breath = grammatical sorcery. 🪄

## 📘 2.6 The timeline method — rebuild ANY answer ⏱️

```
PAST (finished)      → past simple: "I studied X. I worked at Y."
BRIDGE               → "Then/After that/In 2024, I moved to..."
PRESENT (open)       → present perfect: "Since then, I have built/automated/led..."
RIGHT NOW            → present continuous/simple: "Currently I'm working on... I handle..."
FUTURE               → going to / will: "Next, I'm going to... I will..."
```

## 🧪 LAB G2 — Your story on rails (50 min)

1. **Timeline your life (10 min):** draw a line in 📝: 3 PAST milestones (education, first job, project), your PRESENT post, 1 FUTURE plan. Under each, write the signal words (in 2022, since, currently, next…).
2. **V3 drill (5 min):** speak all 12 verbs from 2.2 in perfect form: "I have build…" wait — "I have BUILT!" — catch yourself, fix, continue. Repeat until zero trip-ups.
3. **Since/for speed test (5 min):** 6 rapid-fire: ___ 2023 · ___ 8 months · ___ Monday · ___ my graduation · ___ a long time · ___ I was 22.
4. **Write your 90-second story (15 min):** use the 2.6 rail with the model transcript as scaffolding — your real details, ALL 5 tenses present, every signal word underlined.
5. 📱 **Record & tense-audit (15 min):** record, listen content-blind, write down only VERBS. Mark their tense against the map. Every "I am working since" → rewrite "I have been working since". Re-record until 3 clean takes.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Fix the timeline: "I am working at ShopKart since 2024. Before I have completed my B.Com in 2023."
2. Choose: "I (worked / have worked) at Infosys for 2 years, then I (joined / have joined) ShopKart last year."
3. Write it with ALL 3 futures: your next month, next quarter, next 2-years.
4. Since or for: "I've known Python ___ 18 months" · "She's led the team ___ the merger happened."
5. Speak the achievement in perfect form 3 ways: "automate the invoice tracker" (I / we → have + V3), once with 'already', once with 'never' (negative), once as a question.

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 1: "I **have been working** at ShopKart since 2024. Before that, I **completed** my B.Com in 2023." (since = open time → perfect; 2023 = closed → past)
- Ex 2: worked (finished job), joined (exact time 'last year' → past, not perfect!).
- Ex 4: **for** 18 months (duration) · **since** the merger (point).
- Ex 5: "I **have automated** the invoice tracker." / "I have **already** automated it." / "I have **never** missed a tracker deadline." / "**Have** you automated any tracker yet?"

## ❓ Quiz
1. "I have joined the company in 2023" — diagnose the exact disease and give both medicines.
2. Why is present perfect called "the interview tense"?
3. "I work here for 2 years" (present simple) vs "I've worked here for 2 years" — what does each actually MEAN to a panelist?
4. When does present continuous become a FUTURE form?

### ✅ Answers
1. Disease: exact past time (2023) attached to present perfect — the closed/open-time clash. Medicine A: "I **joined** in 2023" (past + closed time). Medicine B: "I have been here **since** 2023" (perfect + open depuis-point).
2. Because interviews are experience audits — "what have you DONE?" — and perfect form carries achievements to the present without tripping on exact dates: "I have automated 6 reports" (impact now, whenever finished).
3. "I work here for 2 years" sounds broken to native ears (habit + floating duration → goes to the Error Log). "I've worked here for 2 years" = cleanly still-here. Tiny letter, giant signal.
4. For fixed arrangements with a date/person already decided: "I'm meeting the CFO on Friday" — the arrangement EXISTS today, so the -ing form carries it.
