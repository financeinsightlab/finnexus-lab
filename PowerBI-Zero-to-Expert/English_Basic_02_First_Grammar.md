# BE2 · Your First Grammar: Nouns, Pronouns, Articles & "to be"

> *Grammar is not a monster — it's Lego. Small blocks, infinite sentences. Today: the two most important blocks (nouns + the verb "to be"), how to point at things (this/that), and the famous Indian-learner errors — killed one by one, forever.*

## 🎯 Objectives
Nouns: singular & plural · pronouns (I→me, he→him…) · articles a/an/the/zero · am/is/are in every form · this/that/these/those · S→V→O sentence machine · the top-8 Indian English errors, fixed.

## 📘 2.1 Nouns — one thing, many things

A noun is a naming word (person/place/thing/idea): boy, Delhi, tea, honesty.
- **Plural**: add **-s** (book→books), **-es** after s/sh/ch/x (bus→buses, watch→watches), y→**-ies** (city→cities, baby→babies).
- Irregular core 6: man→men, woman→women, child→children, tooth→teeth, foot→feet, person→people.
- **Uncountable** (NO plural, NO "a"): water, milk, rice, money, information, news, work, furniture. Say "some water / two glasses of water / a piece of information".

⚠️ Indian trap: ❌ "informations", "furnitures", "advices", "works are" — English says information/furniture/advice/work, always singular.

## 📘 2.2 Pronouns — words that replace names

| Subject (actor) | Object (receiver) | Possessive |
|---|---|---|
| I | me | my |
| you | you | your |
| he / she / it | him / her / it | his / her / its |
| we | us | our |
| they | them | their |

- Ravi is tall. **He** works in a shop. I gave the money to **him**.
- "Me and Ravi went" ❌ → "**Ravi and I** went" ✓ (subject position = I; politeness says the friend goes first).
- Whose pen? → **Whose** pen is this? (not "Who's" — that's "who is").

## 📘 2.3 Articles a / an / the / (nothing) — Hindi has none, so respect them!

- **a/an** = one, any, not special: "I saw **a** dog." (first time we mention it)
- **the** = this exact one, listener knows which: "**The** dog was big." (the same dog from before!)
- **no article** for: general plural ("Dogs are friendly"), names (India, Ravi), meals (I had lunch), sports/languages (I like cricket; English is fun).

Watch the classic: "I go to **the** school" vs "I go to school" — as a student = no article; as a visitor to the building = the. Meaning changes! Same with hospital/jail/college.

## 📘 2.4 The king verb: TO BE (am / is / are)

| Person | + | Question | − |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | am | Am I…? | am not |
| he/she/it | is | Is he…? | isn't (= is not) |
| you/we/they | are | Are you…? | aren't |

Uses (90% of beginner sentences!):
- Name/identity: I **am** Ravi. She **is** a doctor.
- Feeling/state: We **are** happy. The tea **is** hot.
- Place: They **are** at home. Where **are** you?
- Age (no "have years"!): I **am** 22 years old. ❌ "I have 22 years."

Short answers (the polite pattern): "Are you tired?" → "Yes, I am." / "No, I'm not." — never just "yes" alone in real conversation (sounds robotic/rude-ish).

## 📘 2.5 The sentence machine: S → V → O

English order = **Subject + Verb + Object**. Hindi flips the end: "मैं चाय पीता हूँ" (I tea drink) but English: "**I drink tea.**" (I drink tea.)

```
[Subject]   [Verb]   [Rest]
I           like     tea.
She         is       a teacher.
They        live     in Delhi.
We          want     two tickets.
```

Adjectives sit BEFORE nouns: a **big** house, an **old** man — ❌ "a house big". With 'to be', adjective after is fine: The house **is** big.

## 📘 2.6 This / That / These / Those — the pointing fingers

- **this** (+singular) = near, in hand: This pen is mine.
- **that** (+singular) = far / just mentioned: That shop is closed.
- **these** (+plural) = near: These shoes are new.
- **those** (+plural) = far: Those children are playing.

In phone calls: "Who is this?" → "**This is** Ravi speaking" (NOT "I am Ravi" over the phone!). Before self-intro on stage though: "I am Ravi." Both correct in their place.

## 📘 2.7 The top-8 Indian-learner errors — kill list 🔪

1. ❌ I am having a car. → ✓ I **have** a car. ("have" for ownership, not "having")
2. ❌ He go to school daily. → ✓ He **goes** (he/she/it takes -s in present!)
3. ❌ I am agree with you. → ✓ I **agree** (agree is a verb by itself)
4. ❌ She is more taller. → ✓ She is **taller** (never "more" + "-er" together)
5. ❌ We are five members. → ✓ **There are** five members in my family.
6. ❌ Myself Ravi. → ✓ **My name is** Ravi. / **I am** Ravi. ("Myself" for emphasis: "I did it myself")
7. ❌ He is senior than me. → ✓ He is senior **to** me. (senior/junior/superior → to)
8. ❌ What is your good name? → ✓ What's your name? ("good name" is a literal translation — skip it)

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 2: I gave **it** to **him**.
- Ex 5: "I am 25 years old. My name is Meena and I agree with my friend." (3 fixes: have→am, Myself→My name is, am agree→agree)

## ❓ Quiz
1. Hindi says "मैं चाय पीता हूँ" — chair-pivot: what ORDER does English demand?
2. "He's tall" vs "He tall is" — which word class must never be dropped in English?
3. "an information" / "some information" — which is right and WHY?
4. On the phone, how do you introduce yourself correctly?

### ✅ Answers
1. S→V→O: "I drink tea." — verb stays right after the subject; Hindi's final-verb flip is THE learner habit to break.
2. The VERB — English sentences need am/is/are or another verb; adjectives can't hold a sentence alone.
3. "**some information**": information is UNCOUNTABLE — no 'an', no plural; use some/a piece of.
4. "This is Ravi speaking." — phone convention uses This, not "I am".
