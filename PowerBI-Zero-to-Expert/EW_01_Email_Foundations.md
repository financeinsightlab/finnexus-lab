# EM1 · Email Foundations: Anatomy of Emails That Get Read

> *Every professional writes 20–100 emails a week. Recruiters judge you by your first email; clients feel your whole company through a follow-up line. The good news: email is the most formula-based writing on earth — 80% is reusable structure. This module teaches the anatomy, the subject-line science, and the formatting that makes busy people actually READ and REPLY. Zero experience needed — BV3 phrases are helpful but not required.*

## 🎯 Objectives
Why email is a career skill (not "just typing") · purpose-first writing · the 7 parts of an email · subject-line formulas · greetings & sign-offs library · scan-friendly formatting (bullets, one-idea paragraphs) · when NOT to email.

---

## 📘 1.1 Email ≠ typing — email = your professional voice

An email travels where you can't: into a CEO's phone, a client's office, a recruiter's shortlist. It has three jobs:

1. **Get opened** → the subject line's job
2. **Get understood in 15 seconds** → structure's job
3. **Get actioned** → the ask + deadline's job

Fresher emails fail job #2 (walls of text, no clear ask). By module EM3 you'll write mails a vice-president can process while walking to a lift. 💼

**✏️ The purpose-first question (ask BEFORE writing):** "After reading this, what should the person KNOW, FEEL or DO?" If you can't answer in one line — you don't have an email yet, you have a diary entry.

## 📘 1.2 The 7 parts of an email — the skeleton 🦴

```
1. SUBJECT LINE    → the headline: say the topic + the action
2. SALUTATION      → Dear Mr Rao / Hi Priya / Hello Team
3. OPENING LINE    → one line of context/warmth (not your life story)
4. BODY            → the substance: short paragraphs or bullets
5. THE ASK (CTA)   → exactly what you want + by when
6. SIGN-OFF        → Regards / Best regards / Thanks
7. SIGNATURE       → Name · Role · Company · Phone · Email
```

✨ Watch them work as one machine:

```
Subject: Approval needed: ₹45,000 Fresh Farms invoice by Thursday

Hi Sneha,

Sharing this month's vendor invoice for your approval.

Invoice #FF-221 from Fresh Farms is due Friday:
- Amount: ₹45,000 + 5% GST = ₹47,250
- Terms: Net 30 (due 08-Aug-2026)
- PO matched: yes (PO-118, attached)

Could you please approve by Thursday EOD so Finance can release
payment on time? The invoice is attached.

Thanks,
Amit

Amit Sharma · Accounts Payable · ShopKart India
+91 98XXX XXXXX · amit@shopkart.in
```

Notice: numbered/skimmable facts, ONE clear ask, deadline, attachments named. This email is a machine — steal its shape forever.

## 📘 1.3 Subject lines — the science of being opened 🎯

The subject is a promise of the content. Pros write it LAST, after the email.

**Formula:** `[Action/tag] + Topic + (deadline)`
- `Approval needed: Fresh Farms invoice by Thursday`
- `Meeting request: Q3 forecast review — propose 2 slots`
- `Status: dark-store rollout on track (July update)`
- `Question: GST treatment on invoice #FF-219`
- `Leave application: 21–22 July — Amit Sharma`

❌ The junk drawer: "Hi", "Question", "Important", "URGENT!!!", "(blank)", "Regarding". These say nothing and age terribly in search.
✅ Search-test: could YOU find this email in 6 months by searching 2 words from the subject? If yes, ship it.

**Prefix tags offices love:** `Action needed:` · `FYI:` · `Decision:` · `Request:` · `Status:`

## 📘 1.4 Greetings & sign-offs — the wardrobe 👔

| Level | Greeting | When |
|---|---|---|
| Formal | Dear Mr Sharma, / Dear Ms Iyer, | clients, seniors, official mail |
| Neutral (default) | Hi Ravi, / Hello Team, | daily office, most emails |
| Friendly | Hey Priya, | close teammates only |
| Unknown person | Dear Hiring Manager, / Dear Team, | no known name — better than "Dear Sir/Madam" (dated) |

| Level | Sign-off | Vibe |
|---|---|---|
| Formal | Yours sincerely, / Respectfully, | very official (job apps, ministries) |
| Neutral | Best regards, / Kind regards, / Regards, | universal safe ✅ |
| Warm | Thanks, / Best, / Thanks again, | teammates, after help received |

⚠️ **Rookie traps:** "Reagrds" (the #1 typo in India!), "Thx", "Your's", mixing "Dear" + "Cheers" in one mail. Pick ONE level per email.

## 📘 1.5 Scan-friendly formatting — write for a walker 🚶

Your reader is walking, between meetings, on a phone. Design for them:

1. **One idea per paragraph.** Topic shift = new paragraph. Max 3–4 lines per paragraph.
2. **Bullets for lists, numbers for sequences.** Any list of 3+ items/blocked details → bullets.
3. **Bold the hero.** The amount, the date, the ONE number — bold it once. (Don't bold half the email!)
4. **White space is oxygen.** Two short paragraphs > one fat block.
5. **The upside-down rule:** most important info FIRST (full method = BLUF, module EM3).
6. **Attachments:** name files professionally — `ShopKart_Q2_Report_2025.pdf`, never `final_final2.pdf` 😅 — and REFERENCE them in the body ("report attached").

## 📘 1.6 When NOT to email 📵

- **2 minutes of talking > 20 emails** — for debate, emotional topics, or a 5-reply thread: call or walk over, then email a one-line recap ("As discussed…").
- NEVER email in anger. Draft it, wait 30 minutes, delete half. (Module EM3 covers the full difficult-email toolkit.)
- Privacy: salary, passwords, ID proofs — prefer secure channels or phone.
- After hours: use **schedule-send**, or expect "reply later" energy from the reader.

## 🧪 LAB EM1 — Build your first machine (45 min)

1. **Anatomy marking (10 min):** take the model email in 1.2 and label all 7 parts (subject → signature) in your notes 📝. Now spot: the ONE ask, the deadline, the bullets, the bold-able hero.
2. **Subject-line clinic (10 min):** these emails all exist in your future — write their subject lines: (a) you need 21–22 July off for a family function, (b) you're chasing the client for a pending ₹1.2 lakh payment 10 days overdue, (c) weekly status: project on track, one risk (IT server delay), (d) you found a data error in the Q2 report and want a correction call.
3. **Rebuild the wall (15 min):** here's a real-style fresher email — rebuild it with the 7 parts, bullets and ONE ask:
> *"Hi sir, I am writing this email to inform you that I need 2 days leave as there is a family function at my hometown which I have to attend and it is very important for me as my cousin is getting married and I also need to book my train tickets so kindly grant me leave. Sorry for the inconvenience."*
4. **Signature card (10 min):** build your signature in 4 lines (name · role · phone · email). Save it; every module from here uses it.
5. **Read-aloud check:** read your rebuilt email aloud in 15 seconds. If it takes longer — cut more. Emails should speak like a brisk walk. 🚶

## 💪 Exercises
1. Choose the best subject line & say why the others lose: (a) "Payment" (b) "Action needed: invoice #FF-221 (₹47,250) — due today" (c) "PLEASE READ: money matter" (d) "Hello".
2. Match greeting → situation: "Dear Hiring Manager," · "Hey Priya," · "Hi Team," · "Dear Mr Sharma," ↔ (a) entire project group (b) job application (c) close teammate (d) formal client mail.
3. Fix this sign-off cluster: "Dear Sir, …[body]… Thx, your's truly, Amit".
4. Apply the one-idea rule: split into TWO emails — a mail that asks (1) approval for the ₹47,250 invoice AND (2) feedback on your new PPT deck AND (3) their availability for a Goa offsite.
5. Write your 3-line "purpose-first" answers (know/feel/do) for an email to IT about your slow laptop.

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 1: (b) — action + reference + amount + deadline, all searchable. (a) vague, (c) spam-flag energy, (d) invisible in search.
- Ex 3: "Dear Mr Sharma," … "Kind regards, / Amit" — fix "Thx" (too casual for the level), "your's" (grammatically cursed ❌), match levels.
- Ex 5: Know = laptop model + symptoms; Feel = it's slowing billable work; Do = request a fix/replacement slot this week.

## ❓ Quiz
1. What are the 7 parts of an email, in order?
2. Why write the subject line LAST?
3. What's wrong with a one-paragraph email containing three requests?
4. When is a phone call better than an email — and what do you send after?

### ✅ Answers
1. Subject → salutation → opening line → body → the ask (CTA) → sign-off → signature. Each part has exactly one job.
2. Because the subject is a PROMISE of the content — you can't promise accurately until the content exists. Write mail, then label it.
3. The reader processes at the speed of the WEAKEST point: they answer #1, skim past #2 and #3. One email = one purpose (or at most, clearly numbered items of one topic).
4. Sensitive, emotional or back-and-forth topics. Then send a short "As discussed..." email so there's a written record + action items.
