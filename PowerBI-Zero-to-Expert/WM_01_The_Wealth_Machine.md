# 🎯 WM1 · The Wealth Machine — Income → Surplus → Corpus

## 📘 Concepts

### 1.1 The machine — a flow, not a pile

Wealth is a **pipeline**, not a vault: salary flows in → expenses leak out → the **surplus** drips into compounding assets → assets throw off income → income enlarges the flow. Sharma canon (TV7): ₹1.5L/mo salary, ₹51.3k SIPs + ₹16.6k EMI = 45% of income doing future-work. The machine's three dials, in order of leverage: **(1) surplus rate** (the % of income converted — moving 25% → 32% changes retirement dates by more than any fund pick), **(2) time** (the exponent — TV1's fuel law), **(3) return** (the least controllable dial that everyone obsesses over). Beginners polish dial 3; professionals tighten dial 1 and respect dial 2.

### 1.2 The household CFO dashboard — two statements, one page

**Net worth statement** (the household balance sheet): assets (bank + investments + EPF/PPF + property − avoid counting self-use house as investment *fully*) minus liabilities (loans + card dues). Ramesh-and-Sharma example: assets ₹42L (SIPs 18L + EPF 12L + FD 6L + gold 3L + cash 3L), liabilities ₹26.6L home loan → **net worth ₹15.4L** — the honest starting coordinate. **Cash-flow map** (the household P&L): income lines vs expense families; the leak hunt finds the 8-12% most families can't name (subscriptions, food-delivery creep, "small" EMIs). Review **quarterly**, not annually — statements are navigation instruments, not obituaries. CF8 taught CFO discipline for companies; the household is the smallest business you'll ever run and the only one whose bankruptcy hurts personally.

### 1.3 The four gears — in transmission order

1. **EARN** — the biggest asset early is *you* (human capital: MBA NPV +₹22.85L, TV4 canon). Career ROI dwarfs portfolio ROI before corpus > ~5× salary.
2. **KEEP** — surplus rate, tax order (WM4), fee drag discipline (WM3's 1% = 20% of retirement), and the behavioral brakes from BF (lifestyle inflation is the silent gear-slipper).
3. **GROW** — asset allocation per risk + horizon (WM2-3): equity for long goals, debt for dated ones, always real-return aware (TV5's −1.37% FD warning).
4. **PROTECT** — emergency fund (6 months' expenses, IN2-style), term insurance (WM5), health cover, wills/nominations (WM6). Protection *precedes* investing: an uninsured SIP is a donation to the next hospital.

The law of the gears: **shift in order — never floor GROW while KEEP leaks and PROTECT is missing.**

### 1.4 The FI number — your family's "replacement value"

**FI corpus = 25 × annual expense** (the 4% rule, US-calibrated) — India-adjust with 6% inflation to **28–30×** (PM7's Ramesh: ₹3Cr supporting ₹12L/yr = exactly 25×, sustained by the 7%-conservative assumption; a blended 10% portfolio at 6% inflation supports ~3.77% real withdrawal → ~27× for true safety). Sharma file: annual expense ₹9L (non-SIP household spend) → FI ≈ **₹2.2–2.7Cr** — which is why the TV7 desk targeted a ₹4.27Cr family corpus across ALL goals and an inflation-buffered lifestyle. The FI number converts the vague dream of "enough" into a spreadsheet cell — and once it's a cell, it's a plan.

### 1.5 The baseline budget — 50/30/20, Indian-tuned

50% needs / 30% wants / 20% savings is the *training-wheels* discipline. Indian salaried reality-check: metros push needs toward 55-60% (rent), so the pro move is **ceiling the wants first**, then attacking needs (house selection is the single biggest lever — a ₹10k smaller rent ≈ a ₹10k larger SIP; even a modest ₹1,200/mo redirected compounds to ₹1,200 × 989.26 = **₹11.9L in 20 years at 12%**). Automation is the governor: salary hits bank on the 1st, SIPs fire on the 2nd — *saving what remains after spending never works; spending what remains after saving always does* (PM7's pay-yourself-first law).

## 🧪 LAB — Build Your Household CFO Dashboard (10 min)

**Setup — the Verma household:** Gross salary ₹1.8L/mo (take-home ₹1.32L); expenses: rent ₹28k, groceries/food ₹22k, transport ₹8k, utilities/phone ₹6k, kid school ₹10k, "lifestyle" ₹26k, subscriptions ₹3k, misc ₹9k; existing: FD ₹6L, EPF ₹9L, one gold chain ₹2L, car loan EMI ₹14k (outstanding ₹5L @ 10.5%), no insurance, no SIPs yet.

**Do this:**
1. Build the net-worth statement (assets, liabilities, net worth).
2. Compute the surplus rate: income vs total outflow (expenses + EMI). Is the machine's dial 1 healthy?
3. Hunt the leaks: name ₹6-8k/mo of painless cuts. If routed to SIP at 12% for 20 years (factor 989.26), what corpus do yesterday's leaks become?
4. FI number: annualize current expenses, apply 28×. What corpus does the Verma family actually need?
5. Sequence the four gears for them: what happens FIRST this month, before any investment decision?

**Why this matters:** This is deliverable #1 of every wealth manager on earth — the dashboard before the advice. Ninety percent of "investment problems" are actually surplus-rate or protection problems wearing a portfolio costume; the dashboard separates them in ten minutes flat.

**🔑 Lab answers:**
1. Assets = 6 + 9 + 2 = **₹17L**; liabilities = **₹5L** (car loan); **net worth ₹12L**. (EPF counts as retirement asset; the car itself is a depreciating asset and typically excluded from the investment column.)
2. Outflow = expenses 28+22+8+6+10+26+3+9 = **₹112k** + EMI ₹14k = **₹126k vs ₹132k take-home → surplus ₹6k/mo ≈ 4.5%** — dial 1 is nearly stalled; *this*, not fund selection, is the Verma problem.
3. Leaks: "lifestyle" −₹4k (delivery/dining cap), subscriptions −₹2k, misc firm-up −₹1.5k → **₹7.5k**. Corpus = 7.5k × 989.26 = **₹74.2L in 20y** — the family's first ₹74 lakh sits inside its subscriptions tab.
4. Annual expense (excl. aggressive saving) ≈ 112k × 12 ≈ **₹13.4L → FI ≈ 13.4 × 28 ≈ ₹3.76Cr**. (With GEARS fixed, the SIP dose math mirrors TV2: ₹15.2k/mo at 12%/20y builds ₹1.5Cr; the full ₹3.76Cr needs step-ups + the car-loan EMI redirected after closure.)
5. **PROTECT first:** term cover (WM5: ~₹1Cr for ~₹1k/mo) + health floater + 6-month emergency fund (₹126k × 6 ≈ **₹7.5L** parked in liquid fund) — *before* the SIP starts. Then KEEP (tax order, WM4), then GROW. An uninsured SIP is a donation to the next emergency.

## 💪 Exercises

1. **Machine dials.** Rank these by impact on a 25-year-old's corpus: (a) finding a fund beating the index by 1.5%, (b) raising surplus rate from 15% to 25%, (c) starting 3 years earlier. Defend the ranking.
2. **Dashboard.** Verma's friend: assets ₹95L (flat ₹70L self-occupied, MF ₹20L, cash ₹5L), liabilities ₹40L home loan. Net worth? Two dashboard cautions you would attach.
3. **Gear diagnosis.** A client has ₹30L in equity MFs, no health insurance, salary ₹2L/mo, surplus 8%. Which gear is broken, and what's the one-line conversation?
4. **FI compute.** Your own household today: annual expense ₹8.4L, age 28. FI number at 28×, and the SIP dose at 12% for 20 years (factor 989.26) — then the honest comment.
5. **Lifestyle inflation.** Salary +12%/yr but surplus stuck at ₹40k/mo for 3 years. Diagnose the gear-slip and prescribe two mechanical fixes.

### ✅ Selected answers

1. **(c) > (b) > (a).** Time compounds the whole stack (fuel law: the 3 extra years multiply every subsequent contribution); surplus rate scales the base; alpha is the smallest, most fragile dial (most active funds *lose* the 1.5%, PM5 tribunal data). Beginners rank a > b > c; professionals run c → b → a.
2. Net worth = 95 − 40 = **₹55L**. Cautions: (1) ₹70L of it is the roof over their head — it pays no rent-yield income and isn't spendable without downsizing; quote "investable net worth ₹25L" alongside; (2) the home loan's interest must appear in the cash-flow map as an expense family, not vanish into "asset building".
3. **PROTECT is broken** (and KEEP is second). Conversation: *"One week in an ICU can invoice ₹8-15L — half your portfolio — while you're unconscious. Before another SIP: ₹10L floater health cover (~₹15-20k/yr), then we talk funds."* Insurance is not pessimism; it's the floor the compounding stands on.
4. FI = 8.4 × 28 = **₹2.35Cr**; flat dose = ₹235L / 989.26 = **₹23,755/mo**. Honest comment: at 28, use the **step-up escalator** (10%/yr → starting dose ≈ ₹9,900 with the 2.405× TV6 canon): early-career salaries can't flat-dose FI, and they don't need to — the escalator exists for exactly this.
5. **Gear-slip: KEEP.** The 12% raise is being spent at exactly 12% — classic lifestyle inflation, invisible because it's incremental. Mechanical fixes: (1) **auto-escalate the SIP 8-10%/yr** (step-up) so the raise intercepts before lifestyle does; (2) route *next* increment 50% to SIP on appraisal day itself — the raise you never see is the raise you never miss.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** The wealth machine's biggest early-career dial is:
(a) Picking the best-performing fund
(b) The surplus rate — the % of income converted to assets; moving 4.5% toward 25% outperforms any fund pick
(c) Churning portfolios annually
(d) Waiting for a market crash

**Q2.** Verma FI arithmetic: annual expense ₹13.4L, India-adjusted multiple 28× means the FI corpus is about:
(a) ₹1.34Cr
(b) ₹3.76Cr — 28 × the annual expense, inflation-adjusted for a 6%-inflation economy
(c) ₹67L
(d) 25× monthly income

**Q3.** The gear to fix FIRST in every household plan is:
(a) GROW — max equity
(b) PROTECT — emergency fund + term + health cover, because an uninsured SIP is a donation to the next emergency
(c) EARN — side hustles
(d) KEEP — cut all expenses to zero

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — dial 1: the corpus is contributions × compounding; you control contributions fully, returns approximately, and time only by starting. Verma's real problem wasn't funds — it was a 4.5% conversion rate on a ₹1.32L machine.
2. **(b)** — 13.4 × 28 = 3.755 ≈ ₹3.76Cr. The US 4%-rule's 25× assumes ~3% inflation; at India's ~6%, real sustainable withdrawal ≈ 3.5-3.77% (TV5/PM7 canon) → 27-28× is the honest multiple. Vague dreams don't retire people; spreadsheet cells do.
3. **(b)** — sequence: PROTECT → KEEP → GROW → (EARN runs in parallel always). Invert the order and compounding builds on sand: one medical bill or one breadwinning death converts the SIP book into a settlement cheque. The gears work as a transmission, not a menu.
