# 🎯 SY4 · Possibility & Either-Or
> Two special answer types with their own courts. A **possibility** conclusion is innocent until the statements prove guilt — one legal drawing with it alive is enough. An **either-or** pair wins together when two weak conclusions share the same subject and predicate, are **complementary**, and **neither** is definite alone. Three gates, one verdict.

## 🎯 Objectives
- Run the one-question possibility court: **"Do the statements explicitly BLOCK this?"** If not → possible.
- Distinguish unblocked vs supported: *possible* needs no evidence; *definite* needs no escape.
- Apply the **three either-or gates**: same S–P pair · complementary (contradictory) forms · neither definite alone.
- Know the two classic complementary pairs: **All vs Some-not** (A↔O) and **No vs Some** (E↔I).
- Dodge the forgeries: some+all aren't complements (both can be true together); different terms = different cases.

## 📘 Concepts

### 4.1 The possibility court — one question, one exhibit
```text
Statements: All engineers are readers. Some readers are musicians.
Claim: "Some engineers being musicians is a possibility."
Court: Do the statements FORBID engineer-musicians? No — draw the musicians-circle
       crossing BOTH engineers and readers freely. Legal. ⇒ POSSIBILITY ✓
```

The rule is asymmetric and worth memorizing cold:
- **Definite** fails on ONE counter-drawing (false-friend method, SY2–3).
- **Possible** succeeds on ONE friendly drawing — UNLESS the statements explicitly block it.

The classic BLOCK scenario: statements pin down *exact* membership — "All and only…", E-separations, or a chain that logically forces the contradictory. If nothing forces the contradictory, the possibility breathes. **Possibility is the default state of an unconstrained world.**

### 4.2 The standard possibility plays (all with "being X is a possibility" phrasing)
- All A are B. Some B are C. ⇒ "All A being C" — possible? Draw C swallowing A entirely ✓ (unless blocked) — yes.
- No engineer is a musician. ⇒ "Some engineers being musicians" — **BLOCKED** by the E-wall ✗ — possibility dies.
- All A are B. ⇒ "Some B not being A" — possible? ✓ (ring outside A may be inhabited) — and "All B being A" equally possible ✓. Possibilities are cheap; that's their nature.
- Trap flavor: a **definite** conclusion is automatically also *possible* — but papers test the distinction: if it's definite, the "possibility" phrasing is still accepted ONLY when the paper separately lists it… convention in Indian bank exams: **if the conclusion is definite, answer definite; possibility answers apply to cases that are true but not forced.** When both columns exist, definite wins.

### 4.3 The complementary pairs — opposites that exhaust the world
For a fixed subject S and predicate P, the two statements that **exactly partition** all worlds:

| Pair | Forms | Why complementary |
|---|---|---|
| **All S are P** ↔ **Some S are not P** | A ↔ O | either every S is inside P, or at least one is outside — no third world |
| **No S is P** ↔ **Some S are P** | E ↔ I | either zero overlap, or at least one element — no third world |

Memorize the switching law: **negation flips the gate AND the quantifier** — All⇄Some-not, No⇄Some. ("Some are" ↔ "Some are not" is NOT complementary — both can hold simultaneously; that's the forgery we'll jail below.)

### 4.4 The either-or machine — three gates, then the pair answers
```text
Statements: All managers are graduates.  (nothing about "leaders"… wait, a clean set:)
All officers are employees. No officer is a clerk.
Conclusions: (I)  No employee is a clerk.
             (II) Some employees are clerks.
Gate 1 — same S–P?  employees ↔ clerks in both ✓
Gate 2 — complementary? No (E) vs Some (I) ✓ — exhausts all worlds
Gate 3 — neither definite? Employees ≠ officers: draw clerks inside employees-but-not-officers ⇒ (I) dead ✓;
         draw clerks fully outside employees ⇒ (II) dead ✓. Neither forced ✓
VERDICT: either (I) or (II) follows — TOGETHER guaranteed: in every world, one of them is true.
```

That's the machine: neither conclusion alone is owed, but **the statements force at least one of the pair to hold**, because the pair covers every world. Answer format: "either 1 or 2 follows."

### 4.5 Forgeries that fail the gates (each on a different gate)
1. **Wrong pair:** (I) All employees are clerks (II) Some employees are not clerks — same pair, complementary ✓, but wait — is (II)'s partner really (I)? A↔O yes complements ✓… but check Gate 3: if statements FORCE All officers are employees AND No officer is clerk, does (I) become… employees aren't clerks isn't forced and ALL employees clerks isn't forced either — this pair can actually pass! The true forgeries:
2. **Some + Some:** "Some employees are clerks" / "Some employees are not clerks" — **not complementary** (both true together is legal); gate 2 kills it.
3. **Different terms:** (I) No officer is a clerk (II) Some employees are clerks — different subjects (officers ≠ employees) — gate 1 kills it.
4. **One already definite:** if the statements DO force "No employee is a clerk," then conclusion (I) follows **definitely** — the pair dissolves into a single definite answer; never answer either-or when one already stands. Gate 3 kills it.

### 4.6 Protocol card
1. Possibility claim? → ask "explicitly blocked?" (E-walls and full-pin-downs block; ring-space doesn't).
2. Two weak conclusions, same S–P? → check complementarity (A↔O, E↔I).
3. Both weak? → attempt a false-friend draw on EACH.
4. All three gates green → "either … or … follows."
5. Any gate red → judge each conclusion on its own merit.

## 🧪 LAB — The two courts (10 min)
1. Statements: All agents are trained. Some trained people are certified. Claim: "All agents being certified is a possibility." Verdict + one drawing line.
2. Statements: No intern is a signatory. Claim: "Some interns being signatories is a possibility." Verdict?
3. Either-or audit: All marketers are employees. No marketer is an accountant. Pair: (I) No employee is an accountant (II) Some employees are accountants. Run all three gates and verdict.
4. Forgery ID: (I) Some invoices are GST-carrying (II) Some invoices are not GST-carrying — either-or? Which gate fails?
5. Statements: All planners are analysts. Pair: (I) Some analysts are planners (II) Some analysts are not planners. Verdict — CAREFUL with gate 3!

**Why this matters:** possibility + either-or questions pay better per second than any other reasoning type once the gates are drilled.

**🔑 Lab answers:** (1) **possible ✓** — certified-circle drawn swallowing agents entirely; nothing blocks it (2) **dead ✗** — the E-wall explicitly blocks the overlap (3) gates: same S–P ✓; E↔I complementary ✓; (I) dead (accountants inside employees-outside-marketers drawing ✓), (II) dead (accountants fully outside employees ✓) ⇒ **either-or ✓** (4) gate 2 — Some/Some-not are NOT complementary (both can hold), so NO either-or; each judged alone (5) **(I) follows definitely** (All ⇒ Some downhill) — gate 3 fails ⇒ no either-or; answer the single definite conclusion.

## 💪 Exercises
1. All auditors are employees. Claim: "All employees being auditors is a possibility." Verdict?
2. Statements: Some riders are insured. Claim: "All riders being insured is a possibility." Verdict?
3. All CAs are graduates. No graduate is a dropout. Claim: "Some CAs being dropouts is a possibility." Verdict + the blocker.
4. Either-or run: All associates are analysts. No associate is an intern. (I) No analyst is an intern (II) Some analysts are interns. Verdict with gate lines.
5. Forgery ID: statements "All managers are employees." Pair: (I) All employees are managers (II) Some employees are not managers. Legit either-or or not? (Gate 3 deep-check!)
6. Pair audit: (I) Some stores are profitable (II) No store is profitable. Same S–P? Complementary? And if statements are silent about stores and profit entirely — verdict?
7. Statements: All invoices carry a number. Some numbers are sequential. Claims: (a) "All invoices being sequential is a possibility" (b) "No invoice being sequential is a possibility" (c) "Some invoices are sequential" (definite). Verdicts?

### ✅ Selected answers
1. ✓ possible — auditors-circle drawn coinciding EXACTLY with employees-circle; nothing forbids the coincidence. (Also the ring being empty is equally possible — possibilities travel in packs.)
2. ✓ possible — insured-circle swollen to contain ALL riders; the stated "some" stays true, the all-version unfalsified.
3. ✗ BLOCKED — CA ⊆ graduates and graduates-wall against dropouts forces CA ∩ dropouts = ∅; the contradictory is forced, so the possibility is dead on arrival.
4. G1 employees… interns ↔ interns ✓; G2 E (No) ↔ I (Some) complementary ✓; G3: (I) dead — draw interns inside analysts-outside-associates; (II) dead — draw interns fully outside analysts ⇒ **either-or follows** ✓.
5. **Legit either-or** ✓ — G1 same pair; G2 A↔O complementary; G3: (I) dead (ring outside managers inhabited — one non-manager employee drawing suffices… wait, is (I) FORCED false? No — employees COULD coincide with managers: (I) alive in that world, so not definite ✓); (II) dead? (II) says ring inhabited — draw coincidence-world ⇒ (II) dead in that world ✓ neither definite ⇒ either-or stands ✓.
6. Same S–P ✓ (stores ↔ profitable); complementary ✓ (I-pair: Some ↔ No = I↔E ✓!). Statements silent ⇒ neither definite ⇒ **either-or follows** — yes, with ZERO premises the pair still answers; every world either has a profitable store or doesn't.
7. (a) ✓ possible (b) ✓ possible (both live in different friendly worlds — cheap, remember?) (c) ✗ NOT definite — sequential-dots may all sit outside invoices. Answer format: possibilities acknowledged, definite rejected.

## ❓ Quiz
1. Statements: "All engineers are readers. Some readers are musicians." The claim "Some engineers being musicians is a possibility":
   - (a) fails — no chain forces it
   - (b) holds — possibility needs only ONE friendly drawing (musicians-circle crossing engineers), and nothing in the statements blocks the engineer–musician overlap
   - (c) holds, and is also definite
2. The complementary partner of "All employees are clerks" is:
   - (a) No employee is a clerk
   - (b) Some employees are not clerks — negation flips the quantifier AND the gate: All↔Some-not exhausts every world (that A–O pair is the classic either-or fuel)
   - (c) Some employees are clerks
3. Statements force "No officer is a clerk" and "All officers are employees." Offered: (I) No employee is a clerk; (II) Some employees are clerks. Verdict:
   - (a) (I) follows; (II) dies
   - (b) either (I) or (II) follows — same S–P, complementary E↔I, and each dies in a legal drawing (clerks inside/outside the employee space), so the pair is jointly guaranteed
   - (c) neither follows

### ✅ Answers
1. **(b)** — possibility court asks only "explicitly blocked?" No E-wall -> alive. (c) fails: unblocked ≠ forced.
2. **(b)** — A↔O across the aisle: total containment vs at-least-one-escapee. (a) pairs All↔No — NOT complementary (both false together when the world is mixed).
3. **(b)** — three gates green; (a) dies because employees ≠ officers — one clerk-employee outside the officer-circle is a legal world.
