## 📘 Concepts

### 10.1 The POC cycle, properly operated

Over-time qualification for towers usually comes via criterion 2 (building on the customer's site) or criterion 3 (no-alternative-use + payment rights — the developer-sales wrinkle lands in RR11). Then the cycle: progress = costs-to-date ÷ current-best-estimate total costs (with the RR6 exclusions: uninstalled materials, inefficiencies); revenue-to-date = price × progress; period revenue = to-date minus previously recognized. Estimate refreshes are CONTINUOUS, not annual — steel price spikes, monsoon delays, labor-rate hikes all re-cut total-cost estimates, and catch-ups flow through the current period. **Canon (python-verified):** ₹10cr contract; year 1: costs ₹3.0cr, expected total ₹7.5cr → progress 40% → revenue **₹4.0cr**, margin **₹1.0cr**. Year 2: cumulative costs ₹7.0cr, revised total ₹10.5cr → progress 66.67% → cumulative revenue ₹6.667cr → **period revenue ₹2.667cr** — and the loss law fires (10.4 below).

### 10.2 Claims & variations — the unsigned-is-zero law

Variations (change orders) travel as modifications: approved = Door-3 cumulative catch-up or Door-2 prospective (RR2 map); **unapproved claims live at ₹0** — the enforceability knife from RR2 slices hardest in construction, where "we'll settle later" cultures run deep. Price-adjustment clauses (escalation formulas with indices) are DIFFERENT — they're enforceable by formula: variable consideration estimated via expected value and slapped with the constraint until the index outcome is probable-stable. Bonus/liquidated-damages contingencies: binary most-likely estimates, constraint-clamped by track record. The discipline sentence for site reviews: **no unsigned paper in progress, no unenforceable dreams in price.**

### 10.3 Advances, retentions, milestones — the balances on site

- **Mobilization advances** (10-20% upfront): contract liability — bleed into progress as work runs; consider SFC lenses when the duration exceeds a year and the advance is significant (usually the 12-month expedient or the fact performance burns it quickly shelters most site cases).
- **Retention money** (5-10% withheld until defect-liability period ends): contract asset until the gating condition (defect-free certification) lapses — then it flips to receivable. When retention carries multi-year tails, question SFC on it (India practice: often shielded by convention/expedients, disclosed).
- **Milestone invoices**: a billing schedule is a collection dance, not a progress measure (RR6's output-faithfulness rule): the 30/50/20 invoice plan is only honest progress if the customer genuinely takes control along those slices. Invoice-vs-revenue gaps present as contract assets/liabilities — the two lines in the site dashboard that tell you whether the job is billing ahead (liability — cash-rich, revenue-poor) or running on faith (asset — revenue-rich, cash-poor).

### 10.4 Onerous contracts — the immediate-loss law ⚠️

Ind AS 37 rides on Ind AS 115 here: when total expected costs exceed total transaction price, the contract is onerous, and the **FULL expected loss is recognized immediately** — not stretched over future progress. Mechanics on the canon: year 2 of the ₹10cr tower: progress 66.67%, cumulative revenue ₹6.667cr, cumulative costs ₹7.0cr → booked margin = **−₹0.333cr**. Total expected loss = ₹10 − ₹10.5 = **−₹0.5cr**. Additional provision now = 0.5 − 0.333 = **₹0.167cr** (python-verified), recognized through Ind AS 37 — the remaining-loss provision sits against the unavoidable cost of completing (net of any termination penalties avoided), and it can't hide in future-progress dilution. The law exists because stretched losses are the construction industry's oldest whisper-game — recognize the hole the day you see its depth. Post-provision, subsequent periods run revenue against costs with the provision drawn down as costs land.

### 10.5 The site dashboard — control-freak edition

Every month-end: progress % (with exclusions audited), total-estimate variance (steel/index/labor bridges), claim pipeline split unsigned/enforceable (₹0 vs EV+constraint), contract asset aging (retention tails), liability→cash coverage, margin-at-completion trend line (its slope is the company's honesty curve), backlog with expected conversion years. The two numbers boards actually watch: **margin-at-completion** (does this job end green?) and **order-book-to-CFO conversion** (does recognized revenue become cash?). A margin-at-completion drifting down 6 months straight is the earnings-quality alarm long before the loss year prints.

## 🧪 LAB — The Tower Turns (10 min)

Sharma Infra's ₹12cr wing: year 2 status — cumulative costs ₹7.8cr, total estimate ₹11.7cr, price fixed ₹12cr (+₹0.6cr escalation claim unsigned, +₹0.8cr approved variation settled and included in a revised price of ₹12.8cr — assume included). Cumulative revenue booked through year 1: ₹5.0cr. Retention ₹0.77cr held. Review board tonight:

1. Progress %, cumulative revenue, year-2 period revenue.
2. The unsigned ₹0.6cr escalation: position your one sentence for the board.
3. Margin-at-completion — green or red, by how much?
4. If steel adds +₹0.9cr to remaining costs (new total ₹12.6cr vs price ₹12.8cr — still green): recompute and describe the year-2 P&L story.
5. Now the killer: contractor-side delays push total to ₹13.4cr. Full onerous math: booked margin to date, expected total loss, and the extra provision tonight.

**Why this matters:** this is the actual monthly review cycle of Indian EPC companies — progress, claims discipline, and the exact moment the loss law fires.

**🔑 Lab answers:**
1. Progress = 7.8/11.7 = **66.67%**; cumulative revenue = 12.8 × 66.67% = **₹8.53cr**; year-2 period revenue = 8.53 − 5.0 = **₹3.53cr**.
2. "The ₹0.6cr stays at ₹0 — unsigned claims are enforceability-open Step-1 items, not estimates; bank it in the pipeline, collect the signature, then let Step-3 EV-plus-constraint do its work."
3. Margin-at-completion = 12.8 − 11.7 = **+₹1.1cr (8.6% margin)** — green but thin on escalation exposure.
4. New totals: still green by 12.8 − 12.6 = +₹0.2cr; progress = 7.8/12.6 = **61.9%**; cumulative revenue = 12.8 × 61.9% = ₹7.92cr; year-2 revenue = 7.92 − 5.0 = **₹2.92cr**. P&L story: LESS revenue than answer 1 despite same costs — the steel shock re-cuts both the denominator and the margin, and the board sees it today.
5. Total loss = 12.8 − 13.4 = **−₹0.6cr**. With the total estimate at ₹13.4cr, progress = 7.8/13.4 = 58.2%, cumulative revenue = 12.8 × 58.2% = **₹7.45cr**, booked margin to date = 7.45 − 7.8 = **−₹0.35cr**; against the full expected loss of ₹0.6cr → **extra provision tonight = 0.60 − 0.35 = ₹0.25cr**, booked straight through P&L tonight — the whole point of the law.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** An unsigned escalation claim of ₹0.6cr on a running contract is accounted as:
(a) expected value ₹0.3cr in progress
(b) ₹0 — no unsigned paper in progress: Step-1 enforceability gates every claim; only then does Step-3 estimation start
(c) included at full value per management intent
(d) booked as an asset with full provision

**Q2.** On the ₹10cr tower (Y2: ₹7.0cr costs, total ₹10.5cr), the immediate-loss law forces tonight:
(a) nothing until project completion
(b) an extra provision of ₹0.167cr now — booked margin −₹0.333cr against the full expected −₹0.5cr loss; Ind AS 37 makes the hole visible the day it forms
(c) spread ₹0.5cr over remaining progress
(d) reversal of year-1's ₹1.0cr margin

**Q3.** Retention money withheld until defect-liability expiry sits as:
(a) a receivable from day one
(b) a contract asset — performed work whose billing is conditioned on the defect-free certification; it flips to receivable only when that condition lapses
(c) revenue deferred entirely
(d) inventory until certified

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — enforceability first, estimation second; the claim pipeline is not the transaction price.
2. **(b)** — the law books today what completion would only confirm: 0.5 − 0.333 = ₹0.167cr tonight.
3. **(b)** — work done ≠ billing rights until the defect window closes; the conditionality knife parks it as a contract asset.
