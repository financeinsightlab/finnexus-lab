# 🎯 TSD1 · The Speed Engine — D = S × T and the Unit Wars
> One triangle runs the whole course: **Distance = Speed × Time**. But exams don't fight you with the formula — they fight you with **units**: km/h vs m/s, minutes hiding inside hours. We install the ×5/18 bridge, the proportionality flips (faster ⇒ less time, not "more speed"), and ShopKart's delivery radius on the promise clock.

## 🎯 Objectives
- Drive D = S × T in all three gears (find D, find S, find T) with locked units.
- Convert **km/h ↔ m/s** reflexively: ×5/18 and ×18/5 (54 km/h = 15 m/s on command).
- Use **inverse proportionality**: speed ×3/4 ⇒ time ×4/3 — and harvest the classic late-by-20-minutes problem.
- Set up **two-leg/journey problems** with a uniform unit before touching numbers.
- Apply the engine to delivery zones, promise times and catch-up meetings.

## 📘 Concepts

### 1.1 The triangle and its one law
```text
        D
      ─────        D = S × T   ·   S = D / T   ·   T = D / S
     S  ×  T
```
The law beneath the formula: **units must agree**. Speed in km/h demands time in hours, distance in km. Speed in m/s demands seconds and meters. Every TSD wound in history is a unit disagreement — which is why half this module is the bridge.

### 1.2 The 5/18 bridge — km/h and m/s are cousins
1 km = 1000 m, 1 h = 3600 s ⇒ multiply km/h by **1000/3600 = 5/18** to get m/s; multiply m/s by **18/5** to go back. Chant the anchors:

| km/h | 18 | 36 | **54** | 72 | 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| m/s | 5 | 10 | **15** | 20 | 25 |

**54 km/h = 15 m/s** is the exam's favorite gate: a 150 m train at 54 km/h crosses a pole in 150/15 = **10 s** (TSD3 builds the whole train chapter on this one conversion). The trick family's logic also serves meetings: if you double-check with m/s and km/h separately and get different answers, the bridge—not the algebra—is guilty.

### 1.3 Inverse proportionality — the late-man portait
For FIXED distance: **speed and time are inversely proportional** — time ratio = inverse of speed ratio. The masterpiece problem:

```text
Walking at 3/4 of usual speed, a man is 20 minutes late.
Time now = 4/3 × usual → EXTRA time = 1/3 × usual = 20 min
⇒ usual time = 60 minutes.  ✓
```

One flip, one fraction, done. Same machine everywhere: 2× speed ⇒ half the time; 5/6 speed ⇒ 6/5 time ⇒ 20% MORE time (note: a ⅙ slower speed is a 20% longer journey — ratio flips never mirror in percentages symmetrically!). Delivery translation: a rider stuck at 3/4 pace from rain turns a 30-min promise into 40 — the promise engine must read the flip, not the speed.

### 1.4 The ShopKart promise radius
Quick-commerce promise: delivery in 10 minutes, rider cruising **24 km/h**. How wide is the service zone?

```text
Radius = 24 km/h × (10/60) h = 4 km   →   8 km round trip, 20 min round flight
```

This single multiplication prices a dark store: fewer km radius ⇒ more stores needed per city. (TSD7 turns radius into ₹ per order — the costing capstone.) Bonus drill in the same frame: System's average speed including signals: rider covers the 4 km in 12 min actually ⇒ actual = 4/(12/60) = **20 km/h**; promise engines run on actuals, not speedometers.

### 1.5 Meetings and catch-ups — relative speed preview
Two people walk toward each other from 30 km apart at 6 and 4 km/h: gap closes at 6+4 = 10 km/h ⇒ meet in **3 hours** (12 km and 18 km marks). Chaser at 8 behind quarry at 5 with 12 km lead: closes 3 km/h ⇒ catches in **4 hours**. Full treatment (trains, boats, circles) lands in TSD3–TSD5; today just lock the instinct: **toward = add, chase = subtract** (same direction subtracts; opposite adds).

## 🧪 LAB — The pit lane (10 min)
1. Convert: 90 km/h = ___ m/s; 12.5 m/s = ___ km/h.
2. A rider takes 24 min for 12 km. Speed in km/h? In m/s?
3. At 2/3 of usual speed, Shubham is 25 min late. Usual time?
4. Zone planning: promise 15 min, rider average 16 km/h in traffic. Radius?
5. Two stores 45 km apart; vans leave toward each other at 40 and 50 km/h. When and where do they meet?

**Why this matters:** units + one inverse flip cover ~60% of all TSD marks. Speed here is not a luxury; it's the table stakes for TSD2's harmonic surprises.

**🔑 Lab answers:** (1) **25 m/s**; 12.5 × 18/5 = **45 km/h** (2) S = 12/(24/60) = **30 km/h** = 30×5/18 = **8.33 m/s** (3) time now = 3/2 usual ⇒ extra = 1/2 usual = 25 ⇒ usual = **50 min** (4) 16 × 0.25 = **4 km** (5) closing 90 km/h ⇒ **0.5 h**; meeting point 40×0.5 = **20 km** from the 40-kmph store.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Convert express: 126 km/h → m/s; 30 m/s → km/h.
2. A bus does 240 km in 5 h with uniform speed. Speed? Time for 336 km?
3. Usual speed S; today's speed 4/5 S; late by 12 min. Usual time? (Set the flip, then answer.)
4. A promise of 8 minutes from a ghost kitchen, rider average 15 km/h — what's the max delivery radius? If the actual average with traffic is 12 km/h, what promise is honest for a 2.4 km customer?
5. A cop at 9 m/s chases a biker at 7 m/s with a 300 m gap. Time to catch? Distance run by the cop?
6. Unit autopsy: a student computed T = 240 km ÷ 40 km/h = 6 and wrote "6 minutes." State the false reflex, the right units, and the 5-second sanity smell that would have caught it.
7. Ratio chain: if speed rises 25%, time falls ___% for a fixed distance. (Careful — 25 ≠ 20; do the flip.)

### ✅ Selected answers
1. 126×5/18 = **35 m/s**; 30×18/5 = **108 km/h**.
2. S = 240/5 = **48 km/h**; T = 336/48 = **7 h**.
3. Time = 5/4 × usual ⇒ extra = 1/4 usual = 12 ⇒ usual = **48 min**.
4. Radius = 15 × 8/60 = **2 km**; honest promise for 2.4 km at 12 km/h = 2.4/12 h = **12 min**.
5. Closing 2 m/s ⇒ **150 s**; cop runs 9×150 = **1,350 m** (biker covered 1,050 + 300 lead ✓).
6. 240/40 = 6 **hours**, not minutes — the numbers carried hour-units all along; sanity smell: "240 km in 6 minutes = 2,400 km/h — rockets don't do highways." Lock units onto every quotient the moment it lands: T = 6 h.
7. Speed ×1.25 ⇒ time ×1/1.25 = ×0.8 ⇒ **20% less** time. (The asymmetry club: +25% speed is −20% time.)

## ❓ Quiz
1. 54 km/h expressed in m/s:
   - (a) 12 m/s
   - (b) 15 m/s — 54 × 5/18; the train chapter's master key (a 150 m train crosses a pole in exactly 10 s at this speed)
   - (c) 18 m/s
2. Walking at 3/4 of his usual speed, a man is 20 minutes late. His usual time:
   - (a) 80 min
   - (b) 60 min — speed ratio 3:4 flips the time ratio to 4:3; the extra 1/3 = 20 min means the usual 3/3 = 60
   - (c) 45 min
3. ShopKart's rider averages 24 km/h with a 10-minute promise. The delivery radius:
   - (a) 2.4 km
   - (b) 4 km — 24 × 10/60; one multiplication that prices an entire dark-store network
   - (c) 6 km

### ✅ Answers
1. **(b)** — ×5/18 always; 12 (a) confused the direction of the fraction.
2. **(b)** — inverse proportionality: time ratio is the speed ratio turned upside-down.
3. **(b)** — hours in the speed ⇒ hours in the time: 10/60, not 10.
