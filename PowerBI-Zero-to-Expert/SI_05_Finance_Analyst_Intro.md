## 📘 Concepts

### 5.1 Why finance rooms are different (again)

Every earlier finance module said it: numbers are the trust language. In intros this means:

| Generic-room intro | Finance-room intro |
|---|---|
| Energy + warmth wins | Energy + **calibration** wins |
| "Passionate about data" | "I traced a GL for two years of revenue" |
| Adjectives tolerated | Adjectives politely ignored |
| Vague outcomes OK | Number expected — *with the base named* |

At ShopKart's analyst hiring, the hiring manager's notes literally contain a column: *"used own numbers?"* / *"caveats unprompted?"*. That's who's writing your future review — write for that column.

### 5.2 The money-number trick (one metric + the caveat reflex)

Embed exactly one metric in your intro, delivered with its honest size:

> "…last semester I analyzed two years of a retail company's general ledger — I found that **extending vendor payment terms could free roughly 30 days of working capital**, though the company's margin sits at just **2.4%**, so it's a real lever, not theory."

Why this works double:
1. **The number** = proof of craft (nobody fakes 30 days of WC analysis convincingly).
2. **The caveat** = calibration signal — *the margin context volunteered* says "I don't blow smoke" (RP5's audit-before-celebrate rule, IC5's unprompted caveat).

⚠️ Guardrails: ① ONE metric not five ② the caveat rides inside the same breath, not sheepishly later ③ never inflate ("I saved the company crores" when it's a classroom model — *"modeled"*, "estimated", "found the lever" are honest verbs).

### 5.3 The analyst intro formula (the 60-second version)

| Beat | Content | Model line |
|---|---|---|
| **Headline** | profession + superpower | "I'm Aarav — an analytics graduate who turns messy ledgers into decisions." |
| **Project** | ONE flagship, real-named | "My capstone: a full profitability analysis of ShopKart India's real general ledger." |
| **2 numbers** | scale + outcome | "Revenue grew 18.5% to ₹2.8 crore; my analysis found vendor terms could free ~30 days of working capital." |
| **Caveat** | base/one-off honesty | "— proper small base, margins at 2.4%, so I focused on underlying trends." |
| **Ask/close** | why this room | "I'm here because this analyst role sits exactly at data-meets-finance, where I do my best work." |

~60 seconds, exactly three numbers, one caveat, zero adjectives. This IS the IC5 GL star story compressed — the academy converges on one voice: yours.

### 5.4 Finance networking lines (small room, big doors)

| Setting | The line |
|---|---|
| **Chai-line** (meetups, corridors) | "I'm the friend who reads P&Ls for fun — currently obsessed with why strong revenue growth can still hide fragile profits. Ask me about base effects." 😄 |
| **Conference 15s** | "Aarav — fresher analyst, retail finance. My current question: how far can vendor terms stretch before suppliers push back. What are you working on?" |
| **LinkedIn DM read-aloud** (practice for calls) | "Your post on working capital is why I'm writing. I just modeled Net-30 extensions on a retail GL — if you have 10 minutes, I'd love to sanity-check my assumptions with you." |
| **HR screen 30s** | Headline + the capstone sentence + "available in [notice period]" logistical close (PR rules: stand, smile, land falling). |

**Finance-room etiquette bonus:** know ONE current market fact before any finance event (this week's repo stance, festive-sales headline — BNS1's 5-minute habit). One fresh fact = "this person watches business" signal.

### 5.5 🏆 CAPSTONE — The Intro Suite (your permanent asset)

Record all four variants **in one sitting**, 3-take for the two you'll actually use soon (interview + networking):

| Variant | Length | Must contain |
|---|---|---|
| **A · Networking** | 15 s | Headline + ONE number + question back |
| **B · Team/college** | 30 s | O·R·P·H-lite + fun fact card |
| **C · Interview** | 60–75 s | Full analyst formula (headline → project → 2 no's → caveat → ask) |
| **D · Story version** | 120 s | C + one story with turn (canteen/GL discovery) for panels/presentations |

**Delivery protocol:** SI3 full — launch energy, name-drop pause, pace ladder (120 on numbers), falling lands, hand-off + smile-silence. PR4 setup (fist-distance, quiet room), naming discipline (`20260723_siC_interview_take3`).

**Scoring:** SI4 rubric (take 3s, target ≥20/25 each) + finance add-on: ① ≤3 numbers per variant ② caveat inside C/D unprompted ③ every window named (FY25, YoY) ④ honest verbs (modeled/found, never "saved crores").

**Peer vote:** friends pick their favorite variant per room and say *why* — blind votes beat compliments.

## ❓ Quiz

1. Finance intros win on energy + …
2. The caveat rides INSIDE the money-number breath because…
3. "I saved the company crores" in a classroom-model world must become…

### ✅ Answers

1. **Calibration** — number with context. Anyone can boom energy; only analysts volunteer the margin context around their own metric.
2. **Separate = sheepish; inside = calibrated.** Volunteering the base size in the same breath sounds like honesty worn lightly — later, it sounds like a correction.
3. **Honest verbs**: "modeled", "found", "analyzed", "estimated" — the claim survives intact; only the inflated verb dies. Interviewers reward truthful framings with follow-up questions, not challenges.
