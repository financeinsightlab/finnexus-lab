# 🤝 GD3 · Speak & Play Well With Others

> You have points (GD2). Now the hard part: **getting the floor without starting a war**. This module gives you the exact spoken lines — word by word — for entering, interrupting, disagreeing, and leading a room you're not in charge of. Read them aloud until they feel like your own voice, then shadow the 🎧 model audio.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Enter any discussion mid-flow using **window hunting** and trigger phrases.
- Deploy the **5-rung interruption ladder** — from polite to assertive — without losing points.
- Use **agree-and-add**, **disagree-with-grace** and **build-on** moves by name.
- Perform the 4 **leadership-without-title** moves observers score highest.
- Recover from being attacked, cut off, or steamrolled — with composure intact.

---

## 📘 Concepts

### 3.1 Window hunting: how the floor actually changes hands

The floor changes during **micro-gaps** — 1–3 second pauses after someone lands a point, takes a breath, or finishes an example. Amateurs wait for a "real" silence that never comes; performers enter **on the exhale**.

**Entry triggers (learn as one chunk):**
- *"If I can add one thing to Riya's point—"*
- *"Right — and that raises the cost question."*
- *"Quick thought before we move on—"*

Two rules: keep your **first sentence under six words** (long wind-ups get re-interrupted), and **start speaking on someone else's last word**, not after a full stop of silence.

### 3.2 The interruption ladder (5 rungs)

Climbing down destroys you; climbing *up slowly* is the skill:

| Rung | Move | Line |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Visible signal** | Lean in, hand slightly raised, start nodding — often earns you the next gap |
| 2 | **Polite phrase** | "If I may come in here—" / "One small addition—" |
| 3 | **Bridge (their words → yours)** | "What Sana said about cost connects directly to my point—" |
| 4 | **Polite volume-up** | Same words, 20% louder, slower, zero anger — survives mild chaos |
| 5 | **Name-check entry** | "Kabir, hold that thought — one thing—" (direct, respectful, final tool) |

⚠️ **Never use rung 4–5 twice in a row.** If you fail twice, the room isn't ready — wait 30 seconds and enter through an **agree-and-add** instead.

### 3.3 The three friend moves (use name + reason + so)

- **Agree-and-add:** *"I agree with Dev on convenience — and there's a second benefit nobody's touched: the digital record it creates."* (Agrees = social deposit; adds = content.)
- **Disagree-with-grace:** *"I see the logic, Riya — but the data cuts the other way on small towns…"* (Acknowledge → "and/but" → your reasoning. Never "You're wrong", never disagreement aimed at the *person*.)
- **Build-on (the highest-scoring move in GDs):** *"Building on Aman's point about delivery jobs — the same riders serve three apps, so job quality matters more than job count."* You took their point and extended it — the room now associates both of you with progress; the observer writes "team player" twice.

### 3.4 Leadership without a title — the 4 big moves

| Move | Spoken line | Why observers love it |
|---|---|---|
| **Summarise the room** | "So far we've agreed delivery is fast but costly; the open question is who pays." | Proves listening + thinking, resets chaos |
| **Park the rabbit hole** | "The rider-uniform debate is a detail — can we park it and return to margins?" | Keeps the group on-mission |
| **Bring in the quiet** | "Sana, you've been observing — what's your read on the cost side?" | Generosity + inclusion, in one line |
| **Clock-check convergence** | "Three minutes left — shall we try to close on a view?" | Closure is a scored criterion; someone must trigger it |

⚠️ Golden caution: **earned, not announced.** Nobody gets to say "As the leader here…". Leadership is *what the lines above are*, never a title you claim.

### 3.5 When it gets hostile: recovery moves

- **The 3-breath rule:** someone attacks your point — inhale once before replying. The pause reads as confidence to the observer, not slowness.
- **Thank-and-pivot:** *"Fair pushback, Dev — the exception you raised is real; my claim covers the median case."* You absorbed the hit publicly and moved on.
- **Being cut off mid-sentence:** don't chase the lost floor with rising volume. Note your point, re-enter 20 seconds later: *"Finishing the half-sentence from earlier — data supports it."* That line alone gets ticked "persistent, polite".
- **Never do this:** sarcasm, eye-rolls, name-and-shame ("Kabir's idea makes no sense"), speaking over a woman in the group with "Let me explain" energy. One visible instance and the observer stops writing about you.

### 3.6 Body and voice — the silent 40%

- **Eye triangle:** look mostly at the person you reference, sweep to the two people on either side. Never stare at the table, the observer, or your notes.
- **Posture:** lean slightly forward when you hold the floor; settle back *visibly* when you yield it. That settlement is "listening", and observers watch for it.
- **Hands:** open, above the table, moderate motion. Pointing = aggression; fidgeting = nerves.
- **Voice:** ~140–160 words a minute, drop the pitch at the end of claims (falling tone = certainty), and **pause after your key line** — the pause is what makes it quotable.

## 🧪 LAB — Phrase-card drills (20 minutes, two rounds)

**Round 1 — recognise (5 min):** Read the 6-turn transcript from GD1's LAB and mark every move in the margin: *entry trigger / build-on / summarise / agree-add / ladder rung*.

**Round 2 — produce (15 min):** Record yourself on the topic **"Social media does more harm than good"**, and in this exact order, speak once each —

1. an entry trigger,
2. a rung-2 polite phrase,
3. a rung-3 bridge from an imagined speaker="Riya on mental health",
4. an agree-and-add,
5. a disagree-with-grace aimed at "Aman on breaking news",
6. a summarise-the-room,
7. a bring-in-the-quiet aimed at "Kabir",
8. a convergence trigger ("two minutes left…").

Play the recording. **Scoring:** every move that came out *without an umm-uhh-never-mind* = 1 point. 7–8 = placement-ready. Below 5 = do Round 2 again tomorrow — the phrases must be reflexes, not memories.

## 💪 Exercises

1. Rewrite these raw interruptions into polite ladder rungs: (a) "No, wait wait — let me speak." (b) "That's totally wrong yaar." (c) "You're just repeating yourself."
2. Write the full sentence chain for: disagree-with-grace aimed at *"Kabir: quick-commerce will kill all kirana stores in three years."*
3. From the GD1 LAB transcript, quote the exact line Riya uses as a **summarise/converge** move and rewrite it for a discussion on **"Online vs offline classes"**.
4. Your point got attacked, slightly unfairly, in front of everyone. Script your reply using the thank-and-pivot (attack: "Your UPI point ignores rural India completely").

### ✅ Selected answers

1. (a) "If I may come in here—" (b) "I see it a little differently — here's my reasoning…" (c) "Let me build a distinction on what you said…" — the content survives, the hostility disappears.
2. Model: *"Kabir, I see the logic — delivery speed is genuinely pulling customers. But 'all kirana stores in three years' assumes no adaptation; the ones already adding WhatsApp ordering suggest the floor is upgrading, not vanishing."* (acknowledge → distinction → evidence → re-claim)
3. Riya's line: *"We seem to have two threads: affordability and convenience. With two minutes left, can we agree both models survive, but in different income segments?"* → Online classes version: *"We've been debating two threads: flexibility and classroom bonding. With two minutes left, can we converge on: online wins for reach, offline wins for formation — the best system blends both?"*
4. Model: *"Fair pushback — rural coverage is exactly where UPI's limits show, so let me sharpen the claim: I'm talking about the payments layer, which travels wherever a smartphone does; rural logistics problems sit in the delivery layer, and I agree those are unsolved."*

## ❓ Quiz

1. You want the floor while someone is mid-flow. The correct first step is…
2. The highest-scoring single move in a GD is usually…
3. Someone says, mid-chaos, "You're wrong, that makes no sense." The observer-scored response is…

### ✅ Answers

1. **Rung 1 — visible signal** (lean in, hand slightly up, nodding) and enter in the next micro-gap with a short trigger phrase. Yelling for the floor spends your politeness budget instantly.
2. **Build-on:** "Building on X's point…" — it scores content, listening AND group behaviour in one sentence.
3. **3-breath pause, then thank-and-pivot** ("Fair point — let me narrow my claim…"). Composure under attack is free marks; most candidates donate them by getting hot.
