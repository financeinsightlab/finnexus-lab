## 📘 Concepts

### 5.1 Why finance rounds are a different animal

| Normal interview | Finance-flavored interview |
|---|---|
| Story clarity | Story clarity **+ numbers accuracy** |
| Vague quantities tolerated ("we grew a lot") | Vague = red flag ("grew how much from what?") |
| Tools discussion | Tools + **the logic of money** (revenue vs profit vs cash) |
| Ethics rarely asked | **Ethics asked directly** — they hand you the ledger |

ShopKart's finance-adjacent rounds run it deliberately: interviewer SLIDES a mini P&L across the table mid-talk. Your reaction time with *their* numbers is the test.

### 5.2 Money fluency drills (say these until boring)

The core sentences, graded from easy to trap-grade:

1. "Revenue grew **by** 18.5% **to** ₹2.8 crore in FY25." *(by-to pair)*
2. "Net margin improved from 2.0% to 2.4% — up 0.4 **percentage points**." *(points vs percent)*
3. "Festive quarter: revenue ₹71.8 lakh against ₹59.3 lakh last year — **up 21% year-on-year**." *(comparison window named)*
4. "Net profit rose 252% — but **that's off a small base** of ₹1.09 lakh; the absolute gain is about ₹2.8 lakh." *(base-effect honesty)*
5. "After **exceptional items**, underlying profit was ₹3.45 lakh — so the underlying growth is about 217%, not 252%." *(one-off separation — RP5 skill, spoken)*
6. "Vendor terms of **Net-30** free up roughly 30 days of working capital per cycle." *(cash vs profit distinction)*

**Trap rehearsal:** interviewer: "So is the company very profitable?" — answer with calibration: *"Growing and improving — but ₹6.7 lakh net on ₹2.8 crore revenue means a 2.4% cushion; one bad quarter can erase it, which is exactly why the cost and vendor-term work matters."* Enthusiasm + calibration = analyst.

### 5.3 The 6 finance-round classics

| # | Question | Winning skeleton |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Walk me through a project with numbers." | The **GL star story** below — 60–75 seconds |
| 2 | "Revenue is rising but profit is falling. What do you check?" | Segments (which region/product) → cost lines (COGS vs opex) → mix shift (low-margin growth) → one-offs → **data quality last-but-checked** |
| 3 | "Explain working capital to a non-finance manager." | Fridge analogy → Net-30 reality: "money already earned but resting as inventory and unpaid supplier invoices — we can free it without selling a single extra unit" |
| 4 | "Which ratio do you trust most for a retailer?" | Net margin + inventory days together (one is profitability, one is cash speed) — and *why* each can lie alone |
| 5 | "How do you check data quality before presenting?" | The 4-step ritual: nulls/dupes → document checks (invoices, e-invoice trails) → period consistency → reconciliation to the GL total |
| 6 | **Ethics:** "Your manager asks you to 'smooth' a bad quarter's numbers before a review." | No smoothing. Facts + options: transparent presentation, honest drivers, offer to build a recovery plan — escalate only if pushed. Never fabricate: one fabrication ends careers permanently |

**The GL star story (memorize, adapt):**
> "In my capstone I analyzed ShopKart India's real general ledger across fiscal years. Revenue grew by 18.5% to about ₹2.8 crore, but the net margin sits at 2.4% — so I dug into drivers: festive quarter revenue rose 21% to ₹71.8 lakh, and profit jumped to ₹3.85 lakh from ₹1.09 lakh — a 252% jump that I *deflated* — because that's a small-base effect, and exceptionals sit in both years. My recommendation: renegotiate vendor terms — extending the Fresh Farms Net-30 model across 20 suppliers — to unlock working capital before funding new dark stores. The deck went to the class as the term analysis." **(= project + numbers + reasoning + recommendation + honest caveats. 65 seconds.)**

### 5.4 Negotiation & offer choreography

- **CTC vs in-hand fluency:** they say "₹9 LPA"; think *in-hand* — fixed + variable + contributions. The clean question: *"Could you break down fixed vs variable and the in-hand estimate?"* — asking this marks you as an adult. (GR5 grammar: "the in-hand component", "the variable part".)
- **Counter politely:** anchor with range + evidence, never with need. ✅ *"Based on the role scope and my SQL + BI + finance capstone depth, ₹10–11 works better for me — is there room on the fixed component?"* ❌ *"I need more because rent is high."*
- **Deadline games:** "We need your answer by Friday" — ✅ *"Understood — I'll confirm by Thursday evening. Quick honesty: I'm also at the final stage elsewhere; this role stays my first choice."* Calm transparency > games.
- **Declining gracefully:** the industry is small; decline in writing, grateful, one line: *"Thank you — genuinely. I've accepted another offer closer to my specialization; I hope our paths cross again."* Never ghost an offer.

### 5.5 🏆 CAPSTONE — the full 10-question mock

**Format:** 30 minutes, camera on, all protocols live (intro → species) — friend reads the questions below *verbatim*, mixing in one stress move (interruption or "are you sure?"), then scores 3-pass (IC4 rubric) + the finance add-on below.

1. Tell me about yourself. *(60 s, one number inside)*
2. Walk me through a project with numbers. *(GL star story)*
3. Why business analytics — and why a finance-flavored role?
4. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate. *(STAR #3 + disagree-with-grace)*
5. ShopKart's revenue rose 21% last quarter. Your manager says "we're flying — no need to look deeper." Respond. *(segmentation + base effects + exceptionals)*
6. Estimate: how many dark stores does ShopKart need to double revenue in 18 months? *(4-gear aloud)*
7. How would you check data quality before a board deck? *(4-step ritual)*
8. What's your expected CTC? *(range + rationale + flexibility)*
9. Do you have questions for us? *(the smart three)*
10. Anything to add? *(closing statement below)*

**The 30-second closing statement template:**
> "Thank you — genuinely enjoyable conversation. If I can leave one line: I turn messy ledgers into decisions managers trust; I did it on a real P&L with the ShopKart capstone, and I'd love to do it on yours. Whatever the outcome, thank you for the rigor of the process." *(Calm, specific, no begging. Last impression = the one that lingers.)*

**Finance add-on self-score (4 = analyst grade):** ① every % has a by/to or window ② margin moves said in *points* ③ one caveat (base or exceptionals) delivered unprompted ④ at least one question answered with a live number from the table.

## ❓ Quiz

1. "Grew a lot last quarter" in a finance round is a red flag because…
2. The ethics classic (#6) has exactly one permanently wrong answer —
3. In the capstone, the finance add-on demands an *unprompted*…

### ✅ Answers

1. **Numbers fluency is the whole test**: no by/to, no window, no driver = no analyst signal; calibration *is* competency here.
2. **Fabricating/smoothing the numbers.** Every other framing can be recovered; that one is career-ending. The scored answer: transparent facts + honest drivers + a recovery plan.
3. **Caveat** — small-base or exceptionals honesty without being asked. That's what separates a number-reader from an analyst.
