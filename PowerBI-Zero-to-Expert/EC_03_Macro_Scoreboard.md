# 🎯 EC3 · The Macro Scoreboard — GDP, Real vs Nominal & the Inflation Thermometers
> "GDP grew 7%" is the most repeated and least parseable sentence in business news. Which GDP — nominal or real? Output, income, or expenditure? And grew against which deflator? EC3 teaches you to read the macro scoreboard like a professional: GDP's three faces that MUST agree, the real-vs-nominal surgery, CPI vs WPI thermometers (and why your grocery bill disagrees with the headline), unemployment's three disguises, and the India-context numbers that anchor every macro conversation to 2025 reality (~₹330 lakh crore economy, 6.5% growth lane, 4%±2% RBI target zone).

## 🎯 Objectives
- Construct GDP three ways (production, income, expenditure) and prove they agree — identity, not coincidence.
- Perform real/nominal surgery: nominal 9% ÷ deflator ~2.3% = real ~6.5%, and know which number markets care about.
- Compare CPI vs WPI baskets (food 46% vs 24%) and explain divergence seasons — and "headline vs core".
- Decode unemployment: LFPR vs unemployment rate vs disguised unemployment — the three sentences never said together.
- Anchor India's macro vitals: GDP ~₹330L Cr, growth ~6.5%, CPI 4-5%, per-capita ~$2,700 — and what each means for a portfolio.

## 📘 Concepts

### 3.1 GDP — one economy, three cameras
**GDP = market value of all FINAL goods & services produced within a territory in a period.** Three measurement cameras that must reconcile: (1) **Production (GVA + taxes − subsidies)** — sum of value added at each stage (flour's value minus wheat's, not double-counted); (2) **Expenditure: C + I + G + (X − M)** — consumption, investment, government, net exports — India runs C ~55-60%, I ~30%, G ~10%, and X−M negative (we import more, EC6); (3) **Income** — every rupee spent is earned: wages + profits + rents + interest + taxes − subsidies. The identity is a double-entry system for a whole country (AC course instincts apply): if the cameras disagree, the data is lying, not the identity.

### 3.2 Real vs nominal — surgery with the deflator
Nominal GDP grows when prices rise even if nothing more is made. Surgery: **real growth ≈ nominal growth − deflator inflation** (exact: ratio). Canon FY25-style: nominal ~9%, deflator ~2.3% → **real ~6.5%** (exact ratio: 1.09/1.065 = 1.0235 → the deflator rose 2.35%). Markets price REAL growth for volumes + NOMINAL for revenues/GDP-linked ratios. Two scoreboard traps: (a) deflation seasons (weak pricing power) can make real growth look fine while corporate revenue (nominal lives!) stalls — profits care about the NOMINAL line; (b) the deflator (all-GDP prices) ≠ CPI (consumer basket), so "GDP inflation" and "family-budget inflation" legitimately differ.

### 3.3 CPI vs WPI — two thermometers, different patients
**CPI** measures what households BUY (food & beverages ~46% weight — monsoon and tomato prices steer it), drives RBI's **4% ±2% mandate**, and decides your real wage (nominal wage +8% with CPI 5.5% → real ≈ (1.08/1.055 − 1) = **+2.4%**). **WPI** measures wholesale/producer prices — heavier in manufactured goods (64%) and fuel, lighter on food (24%), NO services. Divergence seasons: when food spikes, CPI>WPI (consumer pain, farmer gain); when commodity cycles cool, WPI can go NEGATIVE while CPI sits at 5% — both correct, measuring different bodies. **Core inflation** strips food & fuel from CPI — RBI watches it for the sticky, policy-relevant trend. Rule: quote inflation always with its thermometer attached.

### 3.4 Unemployment — three numbers that argue politely
- **Unemployment rate:** share of the LABOR FORCE seeking work but not finding it (India ~7-8% recent years; urban higher).
- **LFPR:** labor force participation — what share of the working-age population even seeks work (India's ~50-55% vs world 60%+ — women ~30-35%, the economy's biggest untapped lever).
- **Disguised unemployment:** farms where 5 people do 3 persons' work — employed on paper, zero marginal product in reality; India's classic under-employment story that headline unemployment hides.
Read jobs data as a TRIANGLE: a falling unemployment rate with falling LFPR can mean discouragement, not health; headline + participation + quality (formal vs informal) must move together for a real recovery story.

### 3.5 India's vitals card — memorize the rows
~**₹330 lakh crore** nominal GDP (FY25, ~$3.9-4.2T — world's 5th, walking to 3rd) · real growth lane **6.5% ± 0.5** (fast lane among large economies) · CPI mandate **4% ±2%** (RBI's constitution) · per-capita income ~**$2,700** (the growth-runway argument in one number) · structure: services ~55% of GVA, industry ~28%, agriculture ~17% but employing ~45% (the productivity gap that explains migration, politics, and half the macro debate) · young median age ~28-29 (the demographic dividend has a working-life, not a guarantee). For portfolios: each row is a long-horizon assumption wearing a date — check them yearly, because strategies built on vitals die slowly when vitals drift.

## 🧪 LAB — Scoreboard desk (10 min)
1. Identity proof: an economy spends C 190, I 95, G 65, X 55, M 85 (₹k Cr). GDP by expenditure? Then state the production and income sides in one line each.
2. Surgery: nominal GDP grew 9.2%, deflator rose 2.6%. Real growth — subtraction shortcut and exact ratio, both.
3. Thermometer tribunal: month sees tomatoes +40%, crude −10%, services steady. Call CPI vs WPI directions and the core-CPI verdict — with one sentence for RBI's reaction odds.
4. Wage honesty: Priya's appraisal gave +8%; CPI printed 5.5%; her rent (in CPI basket ~10% weight) rose 12%. Her real wage — and the legitimate personal-inflation rebuttal she can bring to a negotiation?
5. Triangle audit: unemployment fell 8.2% → 7.0% while LFPR fell 55% → 53% and NREGA demand rose. Healthy jobs recovery or discouraged exits? Write the 3-line verdict.

**Why this matters:** item 2 is the split markets reprice on data days; item 5 is the difference between reading the economy and reading the press release.

**🔑 Lab answers:** (1) 190 + 95 + 65 + (55 − 85) = **₹320k Cr**; production side: sum of value-addeds across farms/factories/services must total the same 320 (+ tax-subsidy bridge); income side: wages + profits + rent + interest earned by everyone who made that output = 320 — one economy, three cameras, identity holds (2) shortcut: 9.2 − 2.6 = **6.6%**; exact: 1.092/1.026 − 1 = **6.43%** — markets quote the shortcut in headlines and price the exact in models (3) CPI UP (food 46% weight — tomatoes steer), WPI DOWN-to-flat (fuel-heavy, no services), core CPI steady → RBI's reaction function keys on core: probability of a knee-jerk hike LOW; food spikes are supply events — rates can't grow tomatoes (4) real wage ≈ (1.08/1.055 − 1) = **+2.4%**; her rebuttal: personal inflation = rent-driven, basket reweighted toward her spending: (0.1×12 + rest-share×~4) ≈ 4.9-5% similarly — honest point is the DISTRIBUTION: renters' effective inflation exceeds owners', and metro baskets exceed national; negotiate on her city's numbers, documented (5) **discouragement-signature verdict**: falling unemployment with FALLING participation means seekers exited the labor force (numerator shrank, not jobs grew); NREGA demand rising corroborates weak rural work availability; triangle broken — the print is a mirage until LFPR and formal-job quality recover together.

## 💪 Exercises
1. Three-camera build: mini-economy — farmer sells wheat 100, miller flour 180, baker bread 300 (no taxes): compute GDP by production vs final-goods vs income-logic in 6 lines, naming the double-count trap avoided.
2. Deflator detective: FY shows real 7% and CPI 4.5%, yet nominal grew 11%: back out the deflator, then explain WHICH sectors likely drove the wedge (export prices? investment goods? government?).
3. Basket engineering: design a "Metro-Renter CPI" with weights (rent 25%, food 30%, transport 12%, services 20%, rest 13%): show how this index can run HOTTER than headline CPI during housing booms — with a one-period numeric example.
4. Dividend arithmetic: working-age population grows 2%/yr, labor productivity grows 5%/yr, and the 15-59 age share rises from 64% to 66%: decompose the GDP-growth sources and name the expiry condition of the dividend.
5. Per-capita translation: GDP $4T, population 1.43B: per-capita in $; then redecompose into the path to $10,000 (growth rate × years compound) — the sobering or energizing math, pick your framing.
6. Core-reading: headline CPI 6.8%, core 3.9%, food 11%: write the 4-line note a bank economist sends the RBI-watcher desk — including the supply-vs-demand diagnosis and the policy patience argument.
7. Interview forge: "India grows 7%, but markets fell 5% in the same quarter" — reconcile in 5 numbered lines (nominal vs real, profit share of GDP, flows/positioning, global discount rate, expectations vs prints).

## ❓ Quiz
1. Nominal growth 9%, deflator ~2.3%. Markets price the real number near:
   (a) 11.3%
   (b) ≈6.5% (exact ratio 1.09/1.0235) to 6.7% by the subtraction shortcut — the surgery is routine, and quoting nominal as real is how press releases manufacture miracles in inflation years
   (c) 4.2%
2. CPI spikes on tomatoes while crude falls and core sits quiet. RBI's mature reaction:
   (a) emergency hike
   (b) look through the food spike — rates can't grow tomatoes; watch core and expectations, tighten only if wage-chasing second-round effects appear; supply shocks tax patience, not repo
   (c) cut rates fast
3. Unemployment falls while LFPR falls faster. The correct reading:
   (a) recovery
   (b) discouragement signature — seekers exited the force, shrinking the numerator not the job gap; the triangle (rate, participation, quality) is broken until all three recover together
   (c) measurement error

### ✅ Answers
1. **(b)** — scoreboards matter because models, not headlines, are built from them.
2. **(b)** — core and expectations are the policy thermometers; tomato novels end on their own.
3. **(b)** — participation is the confessor the headline hides.
