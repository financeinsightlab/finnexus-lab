# 🎯 EC4 · Money, Banking & Inflation — The Creation Machine and the Silent Tax
> If the government "prints money", why does your bank account matter more than the mint? Because 90%+ of money isn't printed — it's LENT into existence by commercial banks, one loan at a time, multiplied against a sliver of reserves. EC4 opens the creation machine: fractional reserve, the CRR-gated multiplier, M0 vs M3, RBI's toolkit — then the disease it can catch: inflation, its three engines (demand-pull, cost-push, imported), and the compounding silent tax that halves your money's purchasing power in about 18 years at 4%. After this module, "money" and "prices" are never fuzzy words again.

## 🎯 Objectives
- Explain money creation by lending: deposit → loan → new deposit, bounded by reserve ratios (CRR 4.5%/SLR era, canon multiplier at CRR 5% = 20×).
- Separate money definitions: M0 (base) vs M1/M3 (broad) — and why broad money growth matters more than printing presses.
- Run RBI's toolkit: repo, CRR, SLR, OMO — and trace one 50bp move through banks to EMIs to demand (FI4's chain, monetary edition).
- Diagnose inflation's engines: demand-pull vs cost-push vs imported (rupee 83.5+ depreciation) — each needing a different medicine.
- Compute the silent tax: real value decay (4% → half-life ~17.7 years), real rates, and the inflation-linked logic of FI/IN2 asset choices.

## 📘 Concepts

### 4.1 Money is mostly loans — the machine
Deposit ₹1L: the bank parks a slice at RBI (CRR, say 5%) and lends ₹95k — which lands as a NEW deposit at another bank, which lends ₹90,250... The chain: **money multiplier ≈ 1/reserve ratio = 1/0.05 = 20×** theoretical max from one deposit's seed (real world lower: cash leakages, idle reserves, credit demand). M0 (base: currency + bank reserves) vs **M3 (broad: all deposits)** — India's M3 ≈ 5-6× M0, and it's M3 growth (~10-12%/yr healthy) that tracks nominal spending. Revolution in mindset: banks don't lend deposits — loans CREATE deposits; the constraint is capital, creditworthiness, RBI ratios, and demand — not a physical money pile. Bank runs = the machine's one design flaw: all deposits are promises on the same underlying slices; DICGC ₹5L and RBI liquidity exist exactly there.

### 4.2 RBI's toolkit — steering the machine
- **Repo rate:** the anchor price of overnight money → prime lending/EML rates → loans/EMIs → demand → prices (the FI4 transmission, hours-to-quarters).
- **CRR:** cash slice frozen at RBI — raising it burns lending capacity directly (multiplier denominator).
- **SLR:** bond-holding slice — the captive demand floor for G-Secs (FI5: your yields have a constitutional buyer).
- **OMO (open market ops):** RBI buys/sells G-Secs to inject/absorb liquidity — the day-to-day fine-tuning dial.
- **Standing facilities:** the corridor (MSF/SDF) framing overnight rates around repo.
One number governs the driver's instinct: **real policy rate = repo − expected inflation** — negative real rates stimulate, positive restrain. When CPI runs 6% and repo sits 6.5%, policy is barely tapping the brake (real ≈ 0.5%); the same 6.5% repo at 4% CPI is a firm foot (real 2.5%).

### 4.3 Inflation's three engines — prescribe per engine
- **Demand-pull:** too much spending chasing goods (stimulus seasons, credit booms) → medicine: tighten money (repo up, liquidity down); classical, RBI-shaped.
- **Cost-push:** input shocks — crude, fertilizer, wage spirals → rate hikes CAN'T pump oil: medicine is supply response + targeted fiscal relief; central banks hold and watch second-round expectations.
- **Imported:** rupee slides (83.5 → 86 per $), everything dollar-priced inflates at customs → mixed medicines: FX intervention from reserves (EC6), hawkish rates to defend the currency's return, and time.
Diagnosis before prescription: hiking into a cost-push shock starves the economy without cooling oil; holding during demand-pull lets the spiral learn to compound. Inflation is also a DISTRIBUTION event — savers on fixed nominal income are taxed, borrowers on fixed EMIs are quietly subsidized (your home loan's real installments melt 4-5%/yr).

### 4.4 The silent tax — purchasing-power decay, canon
Rule of 72: at **4% inflation, money halves in ~17.7 years** (72/4 = 18 aim); at 6%, ~12 years; at 8% (rough 90s print), ~9 years. ₹1L under a mattress at 6% inflation buys ₹54k worth in a decade — safe-box is a slow furnace. The tax is INVISIBLE because it's levied on NOMINALISM: statements show +7% interest while purchasing power logs +2%. Real return = (1+nominal)/(1+inflation) − 1: FD 7% at CPI 5.5% → real ≈ **1.4%** — not 1.5 (subtraction lies slightly; exact ratios don't). Every real plan in PM7 is denominated in tomorrow's purchasing power — this module is why.

### 4.5 Hyperinflation & deflation — the machine's two crashes
Hyperinflation (Zimbabwe 2008, Weimar 1923): when money creation funds governments directly, trust flees, velocity explodes, and prices double in days — the cure is credibility (currency boards, independent central banks) not arithmetic. Deflation (Japan's decades, 1930s America): prices fall, so buyers hold off, so prices fall — debts GROW in real terms, the machine stalls, and policy fights back with zero rates and QE. Modern central banking's whole shape — inflation TARGETING (RBI 4±2%) — is engineering to park the system in the narrow corridor between those two cliffs: enough inflation to grease the wheels, never enough to torch the savings.
