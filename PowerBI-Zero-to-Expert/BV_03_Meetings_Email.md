# BV3 · Meetings, Email & Desk Talk: The Communication Word Kit

> *Half of office life is meetings and email — and they run on a small, fixed set of words. Miss "CC" vs "BCC" once and the whole office notices 😅. This module gives you every word for inviting, attending, minuting, following up — plus a special section on Indian-English phrases to keep (like *prepone*) and fix (like *revert* and *do the needful*).*

## 🎯 Objectives
Meeting vocabulary (agenda → minutes → action items) · meeting verbs & meeting phrases in use · email vocabulary end-to-end (subject → signature) · polite email frames · desk-talk phrases (availability, handover, bandwidth) · Indian-English watchlist: revert, prepone, do the needful, co-brother.

---

## 📘 3.1 Meeting nouns — the furniture of every meeting 🪑

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **agenda** | the list of topics ("What's on the agenda?") |
| **minutes / MoM** | written record of decisions ("Priya will take minutes") |
| **action item** | a task assigned in the meeting ("My action item: send the report by Friday") |
| **attendees / participants** | who's there |
| **chair / facilitator** | who runs the meeting |
| **quorum** | minimum people needed to make it official |
| **invite** | the calendar request ("Send me an invite") |
| **recap** | quick summary at start/end |
| **MOM** | minutes of meeting (same as minutes) |

**Meeting types:** **stand-up** (15-min daily status, often literally standing) · **huddle** (quick team sync) · **one-on-one / 1:1** (you + manager) · **review meeting** (check progress) · **brainstorm** (idea-generating, no judging) · **town hall** (all-company meeting with leadership) · **board meeting** (directors) · **kickoff** (project's first meeting) · **retrospective / retro** (what went well/badly after a project).

## 📘 3.2 Meeting verbs & live phrases 🗣️

- **schedule** a meeting → **postpone** (move later) / **advance / prepone** (move earlier — prepone is Indian English, see 3.6) → **cancel** → **reschedule**
- **convene / call** a meeting (formal "call a meeting") · **adjourn** (formally end)
- In-meeting gold phrases:
  - "Let's **set the agenda** first." · "Can we **stay on track**?" · "Let's **park that** for now."
  - "I'll **take it offline** with IT." (discuss later/privately — NOT the internet!)
  - "Let's **circle back** to budget at the end." · "Time to **wrap up** — action items?"
  - "Sorry to **jump in**, but—" (polite interruption) · "**You're on mute**!" (2020s classic 😄)

**"Table it" WARNING ⚠️:** in the US, *table a topic* = postpone it; in the UK/India = put it ON the table for discussion. Opposites! Safer: "Let's park it" / "Let's discuss it now".

## 📘 3.3 Email vocabulary end-to-end 📧

**Parts:** **subject line** · **salutation** (Dear Mr Rao / Hi Priya) · **body** · **closing/sign-off** (Regards, Best regards, Thanks) · **signature block** · **attachment** · **thread** (the reply chain).

**The holy buttons:**
- **To:** main receiver (must act) · **CC** (carbon copy = keep informed, "FYI people") · **BCC** (blind carbon copy — recipients CAN'T see who else got it; use for privacy, rarely)
- **Reply** vs **Reply All** (career hazard: always check!) · **Forward (FWD/Fw)** · **Draft** · **Out of office (OOO)** auto-reply

**Abbreviation kit:** **FYI** (for your information) · **ETA** (expected time of arrival/completion) · **EOD/COB** (end of day / close of business) · **ASAP** (as soon as possible — rude if overused!) · **PFA** (please find attached) · **TBD/TBC** (to be decided/confirmed) · **RSVP** (please respond) · **N/A** (not applicable) · **w.r.t.** (with regard to) · **POC** (point of contact).

**Polite frames that pros use:**
- Opener: "I hope this email finds you well." (skip it in India-internal mail — too stiff; just say "Hi Ravi,")
- Gentle push: "**Gentle reminder** on the invoice below." / "Just **checking in** — any update?"
- CC a senior: "**Looping in** my manager for visibility."
- Delay: "Apologies for the delayed response."
- Request with date: "Could you share the file **by EOD Thursday**?"

## 📘 3.4 Desk talk — the 20 hallway phrases 🚶

- **Availability:** "Are you free at 4?" / "What does your calendar look like?" / "I'm **tied up** till 5." / "I'm **swamped** today." / "My **bandwidth** is full this week."
- **Requests:** "Could you **take a look at** this?" / "Can I **borrow you for two minutes**?" / "What's the **ETA** on that file?"
- **Handover:** "I'll **hand over** my tasks before leave." · "Please **share the handover doc**."
- **Syncing:** "Let's **catch up** tomorrow." / "Let's **touch base** on Friday." / "Quick **sync** at 3?"
- **Status:** "It's **in progress**." / "It's **on track**." / "It's **blocked** — waiting on Legal." / "It's **done and dusted**."

## 📘 3.5 Indian-English watchlist 🇮🇳 (keep some, fix some)

| Phrase | Global reaction | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **prepone** (opposite of postpone) | understood + admired | ✅ KEEP — it's efficient Indian English |
| **revert** (meaning "reply") | confusing (means "return to old state") | ⚠️ FIX → "I'll **reply** by evening" |
| **do the needful** | vague, slightly dated | ⚠️ FIX → state the exact action: "Please **approve the invoice**." |
| **kindly** (everywhere) | sounds overly formal/HR-ish | ⚠️ use "please" |
| **good name** (for "your name?") | puzzled look | ⚠️ → "May I have your name?" |
| **cousin brother/sister** | confusing | ⚠️ → just "cousin" |
| **out of station** | understood in India only | ⚠️ → "travelling / out of town" |
| **co-brother** | unknown | ❌ → "my wife's brother" / "brother-in-law" |
| **passed out** (graduated) | means fainted! 😅 | ❌ → "graduated in 2023" |

Rule of thumb: with Indian colleagues anything goes; with global clients/interviews, keep the safe column.

## 📘 3.6 Tone ladder — same message, three weights 🎚️

1. **Casual (teammate):** "Hey, send the file na?"
2. **Professional (office default):** "Could you share the file by EOD?"
3. **Formal (client/senior):** "I would appreciate it if you could share the file by end of day Thursday."

Write everything at level 2 first; soften up or down after. Level 4 exists ("Per my last email…") — but that's politely-aggressive; use only when truly chasing. 😄

## 🧪 LAB BV3 — The ShopKart meeting & email workout (50 min)

1. **Fix this disaster email (15 min):** rewrite with level-2 tone, fixing ALL seven word problems:

> *Dear Sir,*
> *Kindly do the needful. My cousin brother and I will revert back to you with the informations, once I am back from out of station.*
> *PFA the reports.*
> *Reagrds, Amit*

2. **Meeting invite (10 min):** ShopKart's monthly **review meeting**: write a 5-line invite — subject line, attendees (To/CC selection!), 3-point agenda, and mention who takes minutes.
3. **Action items email (10 min):** after that meeting, write the MoM email: "Thanks all — quick recap + action items:" with 3 action items, each with owner + deadline (use the words: owner, ETA, blocked).
4. **Say it aloud (10 min):** practice the 8 in-meeting phrases from 3.2 in full sentences about a real topic ("Let's park the pricing question…"). Then the tone-ladder drill: say "send the report" at all three levels.
5. **File your phrase bank 📝:** copy your best 15 phrases into one note called "Desk Talk Cheat Sheet" — this is interview gold.

## 💪 Exercises
1. CC or BCC? (a) keeping your manager informed on a client thread (b) sending a newsletter to 500 customers, (c) asking IT to fix your laptop.
2. Match word → meaning: agenda / minutes / quorum / kickoff / retro.
3. Your teammate replies "I'm swamped — can we reschedule?" Write a 2-line gracious reply with a new proposal.
4. Fix Indian-English: "I passed out from Delhi University in 2023. Please revert the needful by EOD."
5. Rank by politeness: "Send the file." / "Could you share the file by 5?" / "File. Now." / "Gentle reminder on the file below."

### ✅ Selected answers
- Ex 1: (a) CC — you want it visible; (b) BCC — protect 500 people's privacy; (c) just To — direct request.
- Ex 2: agenda = topic list; minutes = written record; quorum = minimum headcount for official decisions; kickoff = project's first meeting; retro = lessons-learned meeting after a project.
- Ex 4: "I **graduated** from Delhi University in 2023. Please **send the approval** by EOD." (passed out = fainted!; "revert the needful" mixes two traps.)
- Ex 5: rude→polite: "File. Now." < "Send the file." < "Could you share the file by 5?" < "Gentle reminder on the file below."

## ❓ Quiz
1. When do you choose CC vs BCC — and what's the embarrassment risk of each?
2. "Let's take this offline" — what does it mean and when do you say it?
3. Why is "do the needful" a weak request even though it's perfectly polite?
4. What's the difference between minutes and an agenda?

### ✅ Answers
1. CC = visible FYI copy (risk: "reply all" storms); BCC = hidden copy (risk: if someone replies, secrecy breaks / looks sneaky if discovered). Rule: CC for openness inside teams, BCC for privacy with many external recipients.
2. "Discuss it later/privately, not in this meeting" — used to stop a side-topic from eating everyone's time. Nothing to do with the internet!
3. It transfers thinking to the reader — they must GUESS the action. Strong emails name the action: "Please approve invoice #221 by Thursday."
4. Agenda = planned topics, written BEFORE the meeting; minutes = what actually happened (decisions + action items), written DURING/AFTER.
