# 🎓 PL5 · The Exam Classics: Four Trains That Never Stop Running

> Some P&L questions are so loved by exam-setters that they compile them annually like greatest-hits albums. "If the selling price were doubled, the profit would TRIPLE — find the profit percent." "Selling at ₹450 gains y%, selling at ₹300 loses y% — find CP." "By selling 33 meters of cloth, a man gains the selling price of 11 meters." Each classic has a ONE-LINE solution once you know the move — and a 10-line meltdown if you don't. Today we archive the four legends with proofs, so the exam hall feels like playback, not premiere.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Solve **ratio-shift classics** (double SP → triple profit) with one subtraction.
- Apply the **midpoint law**: equal gain% and loss% at two prices ⇒ CP = average of the two prices.
- Chain **multi-party trades** (A→B→C) with multiplier discipline.
- Crack **article-length riddles** (cloth, pens, toffees) via the "gain = SP of k units" map.
- Recognize each classic's COUSINS so recognition stays fast under disguise.

## 📘 Concepts

### 5.1 CLASSIC 1 — "SP doubled, profit tripled"

```
Let profit = P = SP - CP. Given: 2SP - CP = 3P.
Subtract the first equation from the second: SP = 2P  ->  CP = SP - P = P
So profit% = P/CP = P/P = 100%
DEMO: CP 100, SP 200 (P = 100 = 100%); doubling SP -> 400 - 100 = 300 = 3P ✔
```

**The move:** write the two situations as equations, subtract, watch CP and SP surrender. Generalizes: "SP ×a triples/etc." — always subtract the baseline equation.

### 5.2 CLASSIC 2 — the midpoint law

```
Sells at Rs 450 -> GAINS y%;    sells at Rs 300 -> LOSES y%.
Equations: 450 = CP(1+y),  300 = CP(1-y)
ADD them: 750 = 2CP  ->  CP = (450+300)/2 = Rs 375
LAW: equal gain/loss rates at two prices -> CP = mean of the two prices.
(Bonus: y = (450-300)/(450+300) = 20% — the spread over the sum.)
```

The symmetry that lied in PL2 tells the truth HERE — because the RATES are symmetric around CP, the prices sit symmetric around CP. VerifyShopKart-style: shelf test priced the air-fryer at ₹5,600 (+12% → CP 5,000) and ₹4,400 (−12% → same CP 5,000). Midpoint: 5,000 ✔.

### 5.3 CLASSIC 3 — chain trades (A→B→C)

```
A's CP = Rs 100. A sells to B at +10%: B pays 110 (B's CP).
B sells to C at a 20% loss on HIS CP: C pays 110 x 0.8 = Rs 88.
Net vs A's original: 88 vs 100 -> 12% below.
```

Two multipliers, three owners. The classic trap: adding +10 − 20 = "−10%" — the real chain says 0.88 (−12%), because the 20% bite lands on 110, not on 100 (successive-changes law, PC3's a+b+ab/100: +10 −20 − 2 = −12 ✔). Every middleman question, agency commission question, and C&F-channel pricing question is this train with different passengers.

### 5.4 CLASSIC 4 — the cloth riddle ("gain = SP of k units")

"By selling 33 m of cloth, a man gains the SELLING price of 11 m. Gain%?"

```
Let SP per meter = s. Profit on 33 m = 11s.
CP of 33 m = SP of 33 m - profit = 33s - 11s = 22s
Gain% = 11s / 22s = 50%
MAP: "gain = SP of k of the n sold" -> gain% = k / (n - k)
Check: k=11, n=33 -> 11/22 = 50% ✔
```

Same machine works with pens ("gain = SP of 3 pens when 12 sold" → 3/9 = 33.33%) and toffees. Careful the flip: "gain = CP of k units" has its own map (k/(n) — different base, different answer; the question's exact noun decides).

## 🧪 LAB — Classic reps (12 min)

Equations first. Subtract, add, or map — recognize within 10 seconds.

1. If SP were trebled, profit would quintuple. Find P%.
2. Speaker: gains y% at ₹2,700; loses y% at ₹1,800. CP and y?
3. Chain: A (CP ₹200) → B at +25%; B → C at −10%. C pays what, and net vs original?
4. Pens: selling 16 gains the SP of 4 pens. Gain%?
5. Spot-the-cousin: "loss equals the CP of 2 fans when 10 fans are sold at ₹1,200 each" — rush it.

**Solutions (hide till done):** 1) P = SP − CP; 3SP − CP = 5P → subtract: 2SP = 4P → SP = 2P → CP = P → **100%** ✔ · 2) CP = (2,700+1,800)/2 = **₹2,250**; y = (2,700−1,800)/(2,700+1,800) = 900/4,500 = **20%** ✔ · 3) B pays 250; C pays 250 × 0.9 = **₹225 → +12.5% vs original ₹200** (chain: 1.25 × 0.9 = 1.125) · 4) 4/(16−4) = 4/12 = **33.33%** · 5) loss = CP of 2 of the 10 → loss map k/n: CP(fan) × 2 = 10 × 1,200 − 10 CP → solve: 10 SP − 10 CP = 2 CP → 10×1,200 = 12 CP → CP = **₹1,000**, loss% = 1,200−1,000 over 1,000... = **20% loss** ✔ the map running both directions.

## 💪 Exercises

1. "SP quadrupled, profit becomes 7×." Find P%.
2. Rice-cooker: +y% at ₹3,100, −y% at ₹2,100. CP and y — full midpoint law.
3. Chain audit: manufacturer (CP ₹1,000) → distributor +15% → retailer −8% on his cost → customer price and net% vs ₹1,000?
4. Toffees: selling 20 gains the SP of 5 toffees. Gain%? And the cousin: gain = CP of 5 toffees — now what?
5. Invent a ShopKart-flavored classic of EACH type (four one-liners with answers hidden).

### ✅ Selected answers

1. 4SP − CP = 7P and SP − CP = P → 3SP = 6P → SP = 2P → CP = P → **100%** again (the family all lands here — recognition over re-derivation).
2. CP = (3,100+2,100)/2 = **₹2,600**; y = 1,000/5,200 = **19.23%** ✔ (verify: 2,600 × 1.1923 ≈ 3,100 ✔).
3. 1,000 × 1.15 × 0.92 = **₹1,058 → +5.8%** vs original — the retailer's 8% giveback eats more than half the distributor's 15%.
4. 5/(20−5) = **33.33%**; cousin: gain = CP of 5 while selling 20 → 5×CP = 20(SP−CP) → 25 CP = 20 SP → SP = 1.25 CP → **25%** — the noun (SP vs CP) picks the map, always.
5. Samples: (a) doubled-SP-tripled-profit on headphones ⇒ 100%; (b) mixer +y% at ₹3,800 / −y% at ₹2,600 ⇒ CP ₹3,200, y = 18.75%; (c) FF paneer lot +12% to ShopKart, ShopKart −5% to hotel vs original: 1.12 × 0.95 = 1.064 ⇒ +6.4%; (d) khakhra packs: selling 25 gains SP of 5 ⇒ 5/20 = 25%.

## ❓ Quiz

**Q1.** "If the SP were doubled, the profit would triple." The profit% is:
- (a) 50%
- (b) **100% — SP − CP = P with 2SP − CP = 3P force SP = 2P, so CP = P; the whole train family terminates at profit = cost, and one subtraction drives it there**
- (c) 75%
- (d) 200%

**Q2.** A table at ₹450 gains y%; at ₹300 it loses y%. The cost price:
- (a) ₹350
- (b) **₹375 — the midpoint law: symmetric rates put CP dead-center between the two prices: (450+300)/2; bonus y = 150/750 = 20% comes free**
- (c) ₹400
- (d) ₹325

**Q3.** Selling 33 meters of cloth earns the SP of 11 meters as profit. Gain%:
- (a) 33.33%
- (b) **50% — profit equals SP of 11 of the 33 sold, so the cost of all 33 equals SP of 22: 11/22; the k/(n−k) map, one line deep**
- (c) 25%
- (d) 11%

### ✅ Answers

1. **(b)** — write the two equations, subtract the baseline, collect your mark.
2. **(b)** — symmetric rates ⇒ CP = average of prices; the exam plants ±y% precisely so the midpoint trick exists.
3. **(b)** — gain-as-SP-of-k → k/(n−k); read the noun (SP vs CP) before choosing the map.
