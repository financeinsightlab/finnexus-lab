# 🎬 IC4 · The Mock Interview Lab: Weapons-Grade Practice

> Reading about interviews is like reading about batting — the net session is where runs are born. This module is your net: the 24-question bank with model answers, a script your friend/family can play interviewer with, a video-review rubric to see yourself as the panel sees you, and the follow-up game after the real interview ends.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Command the **24-question bank** — the classic questions with one-line model answers.
- Ask the **3 smart questions** you owe at the end (never "no questions").
- Run a **full mock** with the friend-as-interviewer script + recording.
- Review recording with the **3-pass rubric** (audio → video-muted → full).
- Execute **follow-up protocol**: thank-you mail in 24h, waiting game, second-round prep.

## 📘 Concepts

### 4.1 The 24-question bank

**Warm 12 (every interview):**

| # | Question | Model-answer seed (expand with YOUR facts) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tell me about yourself | 60-sec Headline→Hand-off (IC2) |
| 2 | Walk me through your resume | Chronology-lite: 3 stops, each with one outcome |
| 3 | Why this role/company? | Specifics + self (2 facts × 1 fit) |
| 4 | Greatest strength | Claim + proof + humility coat |
| 5 | Weakness | Real → limiter → fix → evidence |
| 6 | Why should we hire you | 3-match against the JD |
| 7 | Project you're proud of | STAR #1 with the biggest honest number |
| 8 | Tell me about a failure | STAR #5 + Learning line (mandatory) |
| 9 | Conflict with teammate | STAR #3; show the resolution, not the fight |
| 10 | Worked under pressure? | STAR #4; tactics, not drama |
| 11 | Where in 5 years? | Depth first, scope later |
| 12 | Expected CTC / logistics | Range + rationale + flexibility; direct = short |

**Deep 12 (analyst-flavored — ShopKart rounds ask these):**

| # | Question | What it tests | Seed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | Walk me through a project end-to-end | Ownership, sequence | Goal → data → messy part → method → number → learning |
| 14 | How do you handle messy data? | Method | The 4-step validation ritual from your capstone |
| 15 | Explain a complex analysis to a non-tech manager | Translation | One-decision-first structure; analogy insurance |
| 16 | SQL test debrief | Honesty on skill | Walk one query: intent → joins → edge cases |
| 17 | A metric is falling — what do you check first? | Diagnostic frame | Segments → time → mix → data quality, in that order |
| 18 | Dashboard lots of charts — your rule for design? | Judgment | One decision per page; compare-to-period, not raw numbers |
| 19 | Tell me a time you disagreed with data/stakeholder | Spine + grace | Disagree-with-grace + evidence (GD3 in a suit) |
| 20 | Estimate something on the spot | Structure (IC3) | 4-gear guesstimate aloud |
| 21 | Why analytics and not finance/sales/other? | Self-knowledge | Genuine thread: "I like decisions backed by ledgers" |
| 22 | Difficult stakeholder who wanted wrong analysis | Ethics | Facts + escalation pattern; never fabricate |
| 23 | How fast can you pick up our stack? | Learning velocity | Proof of past pick-up speed (10-day Power Query) |
| 24 | Anything to add? | Closing | The closing statement (IC5 capstone) |

### 4.2 Your questions for them — the smart three

When they ask "any questions for us?", you owe 2–3. Recipe: one about the work, one about success, one about growth:

1. *"What would a great first 90 days look like for this analyst?"* (signals ownership)
2. *"How does the analytics team interact with finance/operations today?"* (signals business sense)
3. *"What's the growth path from analyst to senior here?"* (signals stay)

**Banned:** salary/benefits/questions answerable on their site in round 1; "many questions in a row" energy; nothing at all ("no questions" = low curiosity).

### 4.3 The mock protocol: friend-as-interviewer script

Get anyone (friend, sibling, cousin) + their phone camera + 30 minutes.

**Give the interviewer this script:**
> Start: "Hi, I'm [name], I lead analytics here at our mock company. Intro: tell me about yourself." → then pick 6 from the bank (mix: 2 warm, 2 deep, 1 behavioral, 1 their choice of curveball) → halfway, one stress move (interruption OR "are you sure?") → end: "any questions for us?" → close politely. **Do not help the candidate. Silence is part of the test.**

**Recording:** camera at their eye level framing YOU. **Scoring:** they score ① start impression ② any time you went >75 s ③ number of fillers (umm/actually/basically) ④ whether you answered the question asked ⑤ their closing gut-feel (hire / maybe / no) — gut-feel is a real metric; interviewers use it too.

### 4.4 The 3-pass video review rubric (see yourself like they see you)

| Pass | Watch/listen | Score 1–5 on |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Audio only** (eyes closed) | verbal | pace (140–150?) · filler count · pauses after key lines · clear endings |
| **2. Video muted** | body | posture · hands visible · nodding while they speak · eye splits |
| **3. Full playback** | content | species-matched answers (IC1) · STAR ratio · numbers present · honest-pivot quality |

Journal one line per pass: *worst moment + replacement line*. Next mock must fix it. Improvement cadence: **1 mock per week minimum** during interview season; mocks beat revision 3→1.

### 4.5 The follow-up game (after the real interview)

- **Within 24 hours:** thank-you email (EW course skills live here!). Template skeleton: Subject *"Thank you — Business Analyst interview today"* → thanks + one specific thing you enjoyed discussing + one-line reinforcement of fit + sign. ≤80 words, no demands.
- **Waiting game:** follow up politely after the stated timeline (+1 working day): one line + still interested + offer anything else needed. Never more than twice.
- **Second-round prep:** same bank, but upgrade: deeper project dives, panel gaze (IC3), research update (their latest news/quarterly result).
- **The interview journal:** after every real interview, 5 lines: questions asked / answers delivered / worst moment / better line / what they reacted to. Real rooms teach things labs can't — capture them.

## 🧪 LAB — your first full mock (plan 45 minutes)

1. **Setup:** book the friend; send them the §4.3 script; charge the phone; dress like the real thing (yes, it changes your voice).
2. **Run it:** intro + 6 questions + one stress move + your 3 questions. No resets, no stopping mid-mock.
3. **3-pass review** with the rubric; write the journal line; compute fillers per 10 minutes (target: <6).
4. **Second pass through the worst question only:** re-record that single answer with the replacement line.
5. **Log it:** date, mock #1 scores, and the ONE fix for mock #2.

Repeat weekly. Mock #3 is usually where students report the weird sensation of *enjoying* being interviewed. That's the signal you're ready.

### ✅ Selected answers

1. Typical gaps: #8 failure (pick a small honest one + big learning), #19 disagreement (prep an evidence story), #21 why-analytics (find your genuine thread — usually "I like proof over opinion").
2. Model: "Subject: Thank you — BA interview today. Dear Ms. Rao, thank you for the conversation this morning. I genuinely enjoyed walking through the margin analysis — your point about vendor terms was new to me and sharpened my thinking. It reinforced that this role's analytics+finance mix is exactly where I want to grow. Happy to share anything else useful. Best regards, [name]." (75 words — does the job.)
3. **Time-boxing + landing lines.** Answers over 75 s = answer got no structure: apply the species map — direct = 1 line + 1; behavioral = STAR ratio; deep-dive = layered with check-in pauses. Practice each answer type aloud once with a timer.
4. Wrong: "…maybe 2%? or 2.5%? I'm not sure suddenly." Right: re-check mentally, then: "I'm fairly confident — ₹6.7 lakh on ₹2.8 crore ≈ 2.4%. If there's a different treatment (like excluding exceptionals) you'd want applied, I can run it on the spot." Same facts, confident spine, moving forward.

## ❓ Quiz

1. The gut-feel score in the mock protocol exists because…
2. In the 3-pass review, why do Pass 1 audio-only and Pass 2 video-muted separately?
3. "Any questions for us?" — the best close is…

### ✅ Answers

1. **Real interviewers run on it too.** The rubric mirrors the room: technical scores and a final "hire/maybe/no" gut vote — your mock should measure both.
2. Splitting channels isolates causes: fillers/pace are invisible on video-muted; posture/eye-split are invisible on audio. One combined pass always hides which channel is leaking.
3. **2–3 smart questions** on work, success-in-90-days and growth path. "No questions" broadcasts low curiosity — the one free impression you'd rather keep.
