# 🎯 IN2 2 · SEBI & the Rulebook — The Referee's Origin Story
> Every honest market earns its referee the hard way. India’s was forged in the 1992 Harshad Mehta securities scam — a scandal that moved bank money into stocks through a loophole big enough to crash a market and burn a generation’s trust. Parliament answered with the SEBI Act, 1992: one referee with power to license, inspect, fine, ban, and (with government) help write the rules. IN2 2 is the rulebook module: what SEBI actually does, insider trading and UPSI, the enforcement chain (order → SAT → Supreme Court), your SCORES complaint button, and the modern F&O guardrails — so you can tell protection from friction and use both.

## 🎯 Objectives

## 📘 Concepts

### 2.1 1992 — the scam that built the referee
Harshad Mehta’s machine: fake Bank Receipts + ready-forward deals sucked idle bank money into equities; prices levitated on borrowed crores; the bubble’s burst vaporized public savings and exposed that the market had rules but no single empowered referee. Alongside 1991’s liberalization, Parliament passed the **SEBI Act, 1992** — statutory teeth replacing a 1988 non-statutory body. SEBI’s three mandates: **protect investors, develop & regulate the securities market.** The pattern to remember: Indian regulation arrives AS a response — Ketan Parekh (2001) brought T+1 settlement discipline seeds and banned badla-style carry-forward; Satyam (2009) hardened audit & governance rules; every scandal enrolled a new guardrail you trade under today.

### 2.2 Insider trading — the UPSI doctrine
**UPSI = Unpublished Price-Sensitive Information:** results, mergers, dividends, big orders — anything a reasonable investor would price, not yet public. The 2015 PIT Regulations make trading while in possession (a rebuttable presumption!) of UPSI illegal for **connected persons** (directors, employees, bankers, families receiving tips) and tippers alike. Defences exist: genuine trading plans (pre-filed, cooled-off), due-diligence access, and structured board processes. Practical dossier for professionals: trading WINDOWS close before results; designated persons pre-clear trades and disclose; contra-trade restrictions apply. The prosecution’s burden is now functionally closer to “prove you DIDN’T trade on it” — so the compliance answer is process, not intent affidavits. Career rule: if a WhatsApp forward would convict you as a chain-tippee, don’t trade the name, whatever the “conviction.”

### 2.3 The enforcement chain — how a case actually moves
1. **Surveillance:** exchange + SEBI systems flag patterns (volume-before-news, circular trading).
2. **Interim/ex-parte orders:** freeze, debar, impound — SEBI can act BEFORE final findings when markets need speed.
3. **Adjudication:** show-cause, hearing, monetary penalties.
4. **Appeal: SAT (Securities Appellate Tribunal)** — the specialist court for market cases.
5. **Supreme Court** on law points.
Plus parallel tracks: disgorgement (return illegal gains), settlement WITHOUT admission via the consent mechanism, and criminal prosecution for the worst (fraud, 1992-scam-grade). Track record lesson: cases take years but the interim orders land in days — which is why “SEBI has only sent a show-cause” is NOT the all-clear retail imagines.

### 2.4 SCORES & the grievance ladder — your actual button
Ladder, in order: (1) **Broker/entity’s own grievance desk** (they must respond), (2) **SCORES** — SEBI’s online complaint platform (status-tracked, SEBI-routed, companies/brokers must answer through it), (3) **Exchange investor-protection cells & arbitration** (money disputes with brokers — the IGF route), (4) **Online Dispute Resolution portal** (smart ODR — conciliation/arbitration online), (5) civil/criminal courts for fraud/fit cases. Escalate with documents: contract notes, ledgers, emails — SCORES moves on paper, not rage. Reality check: monetary recovery routes are arbitration/ODR; SEBI’s own mandate is market conduct, not being your recovery agent — know which war your complaint belongs to.

### 2.5 The F&O guardrail era — regulation as seatbelt design
After SEBI’s landmark study (DV canon: **9 in 10 retail F&O traders lose; average loss ≈ ₹1.1L**), the guardrails arrived: minimum index-contract lot sizes raised (₹15L notional era), weekly expiries rationalized to fewer indices per exchange, expiry-day margins hiked, upfront option-premium collection enforced, and risk disclosures printed on screens like cigarette warnings. Read the doctrine: SEBI rarely BANS retail risk — it reprices friction until only the capital-serious stay. Criticism (liquidity migration, hedge costs) is real; the regulator’s answer, consistent since 1992, is that a casino feeding on the unprotected is not “market development.” Expect this pattern — study → friction → shrink → recalibrate — to keep cycling; professionals design systems that survive guardrail seasons rather than lobbying against weather.

**Why this matters:** item 2 has ended real careers (friends-of-CFO chains are prosecuted as tippee cases), and item 4 is the difference between a recovered client and a circular-complaint hobbyist.

**🔑 Lab answers:** (1) (a) **Harshad Mehta 1992**, (b) **Ketan Parekh 2001** (T+ settlement discipline, carry-forward ban), (c) **Satyam 2009** (audit rotation, governance codes) — the market’s scar tissue, mapped (2) you can violate by: tipping it FORWARD (you become a tipper), trading yourself (tippee in possession), or having FAMILY trade on it (connected-person web); clean path = do nothing until results publish, and keep the text as evidence of your non-action — possession is presumption, silence is the only hedge (3) because information INSIDE the company becomes actionable the moment numbers freeze, days before the press release — windows close on the INFORMATION being born, not on the announcement; otherwise the golden days between preparation and publication are a compliance fiction (4) broker desk reply in writing (timeline-bound) → SCORES filing with contract notes + email admission → exchange arbitration for the MONEY (the award is enforceable) → SAT/SFC only if conduct-rule violations surface; the email admission is your crown jewel — attach it everywhere (5) populations: small-ticket retail punters (priced out — intended), brokers’ retail F&O desks (revenue hit), arbitrage/hedge desks (higher ticket friction — collateral damage); hypothesis: raise the ante and the 9-in-10 loser pool shrinks to participants with capital depth — friction as consumer protection, the recurring Indian doctrine since badla.

## ❓ Quiz

   (a) SEBI reads private chats
   (b) possession of UPSI by a connected person creates a rebuttable presumption of guilt — the practical burden flips, so process (windows, pre-clearance, trading plans) is the only real defence; innocence must be PROVABLE, not felt
   (c) insiders must disclose salary
2. The right first escalation for a wrong square-off with an emailed broker admission is:
   (a) consumer court
   (b) broker’s grievance desk in writing → SCORES with contract notes and the admission attached → exchange arbitration for the money — the ladder works when rungs are climbed in order with paper; SEBI polices conduct, arbitration writes cheques
   (c) social media outrage
3. SEBI’s F&O guardrails (bigger lots, fewer expiries) formalize which doctrine?
   (a) banning retail risk entirely

### ✅ Answers
