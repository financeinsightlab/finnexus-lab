# 🎯 EC5 · Fiscal Policy & the Union Budget — The Government's P&L, Read Like a Bond Desk
> Every February 1st, the Finance Minister reads a document that bond desks price in seconds and households feel for years: the Union Budget — the state's annual P&L (AC course skills apply, scaled to ₹45+ lakh crore) plus its borrowing plan (FI5's supply calendar). EC5 gives you the anatomy (revenue vs capital, fiscal vs revenue deficit, effective capex), the multiplier's two-way arithmetic, crowding out's interest-rate physics, the debt-GDP sustainability equation, and the bond-desk reading protocol that turns a 60-minute speech into positions.

## 🎯 Objectives
- Parse budget anatomy: receipts vs expenditure, revenue vs capital, and the four deficits (revenue, fiscal, primary, effective).
- Run multiplier math: MPC 0.75 → 4×, and why leakages (imports, taxes, savings) shrink it in India to ~1.5-2.5×.
- Trace crowding out: big government borrowing → G-Sec supply → yields → private capex — the FI5 weld.
- Test sustainability: debt-GDP stable when (g − r) discilpine holds (growth 6.5% real + inflation ≳ borrowing cost 7.2%).
- Read a budget in desk order: deficit targets → borrowing calendar → capex vs subsidies → tax changes — then asset-class impacts.

## 📘 Concepts

### 5.1 The anatomy — receipts, expenditure, and the honest splits
**Receipts:** tax revenue (GST, income tax, corporate tax, customs, excise — GST now the single biggest river, ~₹22+ L Cr/year era) + non-tax (dividends incl. RBI's record transfers, spectrum, user charges) + **capital receipts** (disinvestment + recoveries + the big one: BORROWINGS). **Expenditure:** revenue (salaries, pensions, interest — ~₹11-12L Cr of interest alone, subsidies, schemes) vs **capital** (roads, railways, Defense kit — the asset-creating slice). The honest split matters because revenue expenditure is CONSUMED while capital expenditure COMPOUNDS — two budgets with the same deficit are different countries if one builds highways and the other funds salaries. Watch **effective capex** (capex + grants for capital assets) — the quality metric desks substitute for the deficit headline.

### 5.2 The four deficits — never confuse them again
- **Revenue deficit:** revenue expenditure − revenue receipts — the state's day-to-day kitchen shortfall (target: zero, FRBM spirit).
- **Fiscal deficit:** total borrowing need = expenditure − (tax + non-tax + capital receipts); canon shape: spend ₹100, non-borrowed receipts ₹75 → FD ₹25 → on GDP ₹500 = **5% of GDP**. India's glide: ~5.9% (FY24) → sub-4.5% target by FY26. THE headline desk number.
- **Primary deficit:** fiscal deficit − interest payments — the borrowing EXCLUDING the past's bill; a shrinking primary deficit while FD stays flat signals the interest trap tightening.
- **Effective revenue deficit + capex ratio:** the quality split: a 5% FD that is 80% revenue spending is stimulus-with-a-hangover; at 30%+ capex it's investment-with-a-mortgage.

### 5.3 The multiplier — and India's leakages
Government spends ₹1L Cr → recipients spend MPC × it → recipients-of-recipients spend MPC²... **Multiplier = 1/(1 − MPC)**; at MPC 0.75 = **4×** textbook. India's REAL multiplier runs ~1.5-2.5× for capex (studies cluster) because leakages steal rounds: imports (~20%+ of demand leaks abroad), taxes (~30%), precautionary savings (no free-spending buffer), and state-execution lag (an announced highway is not a poured highway for 3-5 years). Quality ordering matters: capex multiplies more than transfers (roads crowd IN private investment via logistics gains; transfers mostly leak to consumption/imports). The countercyclical doctrine: deficits should EXPAND in recessions (automatic stabilizers + discretion) and CONTRACT in booms — the second half being the one democracies chronically forget, and bond markets eventually invoice.

### 5.4 Crowding out — the interest-rate physics
The government's borrowing calendar is FI5's supply curve: when the state needs ₹14-15L Cr/yr of fresh G-Secs, yields must clear that supply — heavy borrowing seasons push 10y yields up even with RBI on hold. Transmission of the crowd-out: G-Sec yields anchor corporate spreads (FI6) → NCD/loan costs rise → marginal private projects' DCFs fail (CF8's ₹37.4-vs-30 verdict flips at higher WACC). Escapes: RBI OMO support (buying the supply — at an inflation price tag), small-savings/SLR captive demand, and foreign index inclusion (bonds in global indices pulling passive demand). The clean summary desks use: **every fiscal deficit is somebody's asset — and somebody's interest rate.**

### 5.5 Sustainability — the g − r equation that rules everything
Debt-GDP is stable when the economy grows the denominator faster than interest grows the numerator: sustainable when **nominal growth g > effective interest r** (India: nominal ~9-10% vs borrowing cost ~7.2% → the debt ratio DRAINS unless primary deficits run too hot). India's combined government debt ~80-85% of GDP — high vs EM peers, but BORROWED IN RUPEES, held domestically, long-dated: the sovereign's three armor pieces (no FX debt blowup risk, no rollover cliff, captive buyers). Risk map: watch (1) interest/revenue share crossing ~40% (the kitchen starts feeding the past), (2) nominal growth cratering below r for years (denominator failure), (3) contingent liabilities igniting (bank recap waves, state guarantees) — FRBM exists as the self-imposed harness; markets reprice the day the harness frays in writing, not in speeches.

## 🧪 LAB — Desk reading of a budget (10 min)
Budget sheet (₹ L Cr): total expenditure 100; tax receipts 62; non-tax 9; capital receipts (non-debt) 4; interest 22; capex 22; GDP 2,000.
1. Compute all four deficits and the FD/GDP %. Is the primary deficit a trap-tightening or trap-loosening signal?
2. Quality grade: effective capex share of FD? Versus a hypothetical rival budget with same FD, capex 8 — which one do bond desks prefer long-run?
3. Multiplier application: capex rises ₹5L Cr with India's realistic multiplier 2×: direct GDP lift (₹L Cr / % of GDP)? With the 4× textbook fantasy?
4. Crowding-out trace: borrowing jumps ₹3L Cr above trend with RBI neutral: walk the five links to a private firm's shelved expansion (name the DCF dials that flip).
5. Sustainability quick-test: nominal g 10%, r 7.2%, primary deficit 2% of GDP: is the debt ratio rising, stable, or falling — and the one variable whose stall would flip the answer?

**Why this matters:** item 5 is the entire sovereign-risk conversation in one inequality; items 1–3 are what desk economists bill for on February 1st.

**🔑 Lab answers:** (1) revenue deficit: (100−22 capex) − (62+9) = 78 − 71 = **7 L Cr**; fiscal deficit: 100 − (62+9+4) = **25 L Cr = 1.25% of GDP**; primary deficit: 25 − 22 = **3 L Cr** — primary barely positive means the state's CURRENT choices are near-balanced; the borrowing mostly services the past — the interest trap is visible but stabilizing (2) capex share of FD: 22/25 = **88%** — an investment-heavy deficit, near-gold-standard composition; rival at 8/25 = 32% is consumption-dressed-as-prudence; desks prefer the 88% country — same headline FD, different decade (3) 2× → ₹10L Cr ≈ **+0.5% of GDP** (the realist's math); 4× → ₹20L Cr = +1.0% (the textbook fantasy that leakages veto — quote this version only inside economics exams) (4) ₹3L Cr extra supply → 10y G-Sec +25-40bp → NCD spreads price off the new base (FI6) → WACC 12.6% → ~13.2% → the marginal project's NPV flips (CF8's DCF canon: at 13.2% the ₹37.4 thesis decays toward market) → CFO shelves → I in C+I+G+(X−M) stalls despite G's boost: the crowd-out, complete (5) g − r = +2.8% comfortably positive, so a 2% primary deficit is **sustainable-to-stable** — the ratio grinds down slowly; flip variable: nominal GROWTH (denominator stall, e.g., real 4% + inflation 4% = 8% still fine; real 3% + deflation 1% = 4% < 7.2% → ratio climbs even with discipline): sustainability is a growth policy wearing a calculator.

## ❓ Quiz
1. Spend ₹100, non-borrowed receipts ₹75, GDP ₹500. The fiscal deficit is:
   (a) ₹25, 12.5% of GDP
   (b) ₹25 = 5% of GDP — the borrowing need and its ratio are the desk's first two numbers; quality checks (capex share, primary gap) come next because they decide whether the 5% builds highways or hangovers
   (c) ₹25, 25% of GDP
2. India's realistic capex multiplier (~2×) trails the textbook (4×) because:
   (a) RBI offsets everything
   (b) leakages - imports, taxes, precautionary savings, and execution lag - steal the later rounds; multipliers are measured in leakages, and transfer-spending leaks fastest of all
   (c) the MPC is actually 0.4 in India
3. The debt-GDP ratio stays stable whenever:
   (a) deficits stop entirely
   (b) nominal growth g exceeds effective interest r (with primary deficits modest) - India runs g ~9-10% vs r ~7.2%, so the denominator grinds the ratio down; sustainability is a growth policy wearing a calculator, and a deflationary stall flips it faster than any austerity
   (c) ratings agencies approve

### ✅ Answers
1. **(b)** — 25/500 = 5%; (a) and (c) misplaced the fraction.
2. **(b)** — (c) guesses a number; the mechanism is the leak-map.
3. **(b)** — the entire sustainability debate in one inequality.
