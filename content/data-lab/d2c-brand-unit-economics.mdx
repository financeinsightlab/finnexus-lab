## Business Question — what we set out to answer

Many D2C brands grow revenue fast but bleed cash because acquisition costs outpace lifetime value. This model simulates the full unit-economics funnel — customer acquisition cost (CAC), average order value (AOV), repeat rate, and contribution margin — to identify the combination of retention and margin that makes a brand durably profitable.

## Data Sources — where the data came from

- **Public D2C disclosures & fundraising decks**: CAC, AOV, repeat-rate benchmarks across beauty, foods, and lifestyle.
- **Platform data (Amazon/Flipkart/D2C storefront)**: Order-frequency and basket-size patterns.
- **Redseer / industry reports**: Category-level unit-economics benchmarks.

## Methodology — how we modelled it

- **Funnel build**: Traffic → conversion → AOV → margin, with a CAC function tied to scale.
- **Retention curves**: Repeat-purchase cohort curves fitted to order-frequency distributions.
- **LTV calculation**: Contribution-margin-weighted lifetime value across 24 months.
- **Sensitivity matrix**: Breakeven LTV:CAC vs. repeat rate and margin, as a heatmap.

## Key Insights

- **Retention beats acquisition**: Small gains in repeat rate move LTV more than equivalent CAC cuts.
- **Category matters**: Consumables (food, personal care) hit LTV:CAC > 3 far faster than durables.
- **Media efficiency ceiling**: CAC rises steeply past a scale threshold as paid channels saturate.
- **Margin is the keystone**: Without contribution margin above ~22%, even strong retention cannot clear the CAC hurdle.

## Limitations

CAC and retention benchmarks vary widely by category and geography; self-serve D2C data can understate true blended CAC. Model outputs are scenario ranges, not point forecasts.
