## Business Question — what we set out to answer

A handful of Indian large caps account for a disproportionate share of index returns and total market cap. This project quantifies concentration at the index and market level — using the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI), top-10 weight share, and cumulative market-cap curves — to understand how the risk profile of a broad Indian equity allocation has changed.

## Data Sources — where the data came from

- **NSE / Nifty index methodology files**: Constituent weights and reconstitution history.
- **CME & NSE historical constituent data**: Free-float market cap by stock over a decade.
- **BSE market-cap aggregates**: Whole-market concentration measures.

## Methodology — how we modelled it

- **Weight share build**: Reconstructed top-N weight share of Nifty over FY15–FY26.
- **HHI computation**: Sum of squared free-float market-cap shares at index and market level.
- **Cumulative concentration curve**: Lorenz-style curve of market cap vs. number of stocks.
- **Sector attribution**: How much of concentration is driven by financials, IT, and energy.

## Key Insights

- **Narrow leadership**: A small cluster of stocks drives an outsized share of Nifty returns — passive investors are effectively making concentrated bets.
- **Rising HHI**: Market-level concentration has trended up, reducing the effective breadth of a "broad" index.
- **Sector skew**: Concentration is largely a financials + IT + energy phenomenon.
- **Active opportunity**: Dispersion between the top and the rest creates potential for active managers in mid and small caps.

## Limitations

Free-float market-cap data has occasional methodology breaks on index rebalancing. Concentration measures are point-in-time and can shift sharply in corrections or new listings.
