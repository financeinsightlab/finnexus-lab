# Show Notes

Every 10-minute delivery is a small business with its own P&L. This episode summarises our research note that breaks the quick-commerce model down line by line — from basket size to last-mile cost.

- **The revenue stack**: Product margin, delivery fees, and the growing advertising line.
- **The cost stack**: Procurement, dark-store rent, picking labour, and the rider on the road.
- **The breakeven math**: What basket size and order density turn a contribution loss into a profit.

---

# Key Points

1. **The line-by-line model**
   - A ₹500 basket generates roughly ₹60–75 of gross margin at blended take rates of 12–15%.
   - Delivery revenue (fees + surge) covers 60–80% of last-mile cost in mature stores.

2. **Where costs hide**
   - Dark-store rent and picking labour scale with SKU count — assortment breadth is a cost, not just an attraction.
   - The last mile is the variable cost that matters; density is the only durable fix.

3. **The density flywheel**
   - More orders per store → better picking efficiency → lower cost per order → lower prices → more orders.
   - That's why the top 10 cities are profitable while expansion cities bleed.

4. **What comes next**
   - Advertising on quick-commerce apps is the next margin leg — brands will pay for the "top of cart" slot.
   - Private labels (store brands) could add 200–400 bps of gross margin at scale.
