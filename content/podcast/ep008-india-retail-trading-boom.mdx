# Show Notes

India now has the world's largest retail derivatives market — and the regulator has warned that 9 out of 10 active F&O traders lose money. Dr. Ananya Iyer, behavioural finance researcher at IIM Bangalore, joins us to understand the boom.

- **The migration**: Why a generation of savers moved from fixed deposits to options intraday.
- **The psychology**: Loss aversion, overconfidence, and the dopamine loop of the tick chart.
- **The policy response**: SEBI's margin rules, contract-size hikes, and what actually works.

---

# Key Points

1. **The scale of the shift**
   - Retail F&O participation grew from ~5 lakh active traders to over 1 crore in four years.
   - The average loss per active trader is estimated in the tens of thousands of rupees annually.

2. **Why it happens — the psychology**
   - Near-miss effects and variable rewards are the same mechanisms as slot machines.
   - Overconfidence peaks after small wins; loss-chasing compounds after small losses.
   - Easy app-based access removed the friction that used to protect savers.

3. **What the data says**
   - Studies consistently show the bottom 90% of F&O traders lose money net of costs.
   - Those who profit tend to trade less, size smaller, and hold longer horizons.

4. **The fix**
   - SEBI's measures — higher contract sizes, stricter margins — push the marginal trader out of the market.
   - The deeper fix is financial literacy: teaching risk, not just returns.

# Transcript (Abbreviated)

**Host**: Dr. Iyer, thank you for joining. India's retail F&O boom — is it democratisation or a problem?

**Dr. Ananya Iyer**: It's both. Access is democratised — that's genuinely good. But the psychology of derivatives is fundamentally hostile to the average retail participant. The data is brutal: most active traders lose money, and they keep losing because the behaviour loop is reinforcing.

> The tick chart is designed like a slot machine — variable rewards, near-misses, instant feedback. Your brain doesn't stand a chance without deliberate structure: fixed rules, position limits, and a very clear definition of what you're trying to achieve.

**Host**: What would you tell a young investor today?

**Dr. Ananya Iyer**: If you're new, start with boring — index funds, a long horizon, and a written plan. If you must trade, trade small, journal every decision, and measure your fees. The house edge in F&O isn't the exchange — it's your own impatience.
