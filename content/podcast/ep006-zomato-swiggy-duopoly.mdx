# Show Notes

The Indian food-delivery market is a duopoly — and after years of discount-driven growth, both Zomato and Swiggy are trying to prove the model prints money. This episode is a side-by-side of their strategy.

- **The take-rate story**: Delivery charges, platform fees, and advertising revenue — the three lines that moved from loss to profit.
- **The quick-commerce side bet**: How Blinkit and Instamart changed the conversation for both companies.
- **The endgame**: What consolidation, pricing power, and restaurant economics mean for the next decade.

---

# Key Points

1. **Where the money comes from now**
   - Platform fees on every order became the single biggest lever of profitability — a few rupees per order, at scale, is enormous.
   - Advertising (restaurant promotions, sponsored listings) is the highest-margin revenue line both companies are scaling.

2. **The quick-commerce halo**
   - Zomato's Blinkit is EBITDA-positive and reshaping the parent's valuation; Swiggy's Instamart is catching up.
   - Investors now price the food business as a cash cow funding the growth bets.

3. **Restaurant economics are the constraint**
   - Delivery commissions push many restaurants to thin margins; the duopoly must keep restaurants profitable or the supply side erodes.
   - Cloud kitchens and dine-in aggregation are the expansion valves.

4. **Investor takeaways**
   - The duopoly's pricing power is real — but watch for regulatory scrutiny as platform fees rise.
   - The winner isn't decided by market share; it's decided by who can raise prices without losing users.
