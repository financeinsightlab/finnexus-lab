# Show Notes

Two years ago, Ola Electric owned the EV two-wheeler narrative. Today, Bajaj and TVS have flipped the script — and the FAME subsidy phase-out has changed the game entirely. This is the state of the war.

- **Ola Electric's pivot**: From market share at any cost to service-centre fixes and a margin-first reset.
- **Bajaj's execution machine**: Chetak's climb powered by the same dealer network that sells 100,000 petrol bikes a month.
- **TVS's quiet play**: iQube's steady compounding and the export optionality nobody talks about.

---

# Key Points

1. **The market flipped**
   - Ola Electric's monthly registrations fell sharply as FAME subsidies ended; Bajaj and TVS took the top two spots in some months.
   - The subsidy phase-out exposed who had real demand — and who had subsidy-driven demand.

2. **The unit-economics gap**
   - Bajaj and TVS run EV lines through existing manufacturing and dealer infrastructure — lower fixed-cost drag.
   - Ola's vertically integrated plant is a long-term advantage if volumes return; short-term it's a cost burden.

3. **Battery is the battleground**
   - Battery cost is 40% of an EV two-wheeler's price; cell localisation will decide the next margin step.
   - Swappable-battery standards are being negotiated — the winner writes the industry's future cost curve.

4. **Investor takeaways**
   - Watch market share *with* margins, not just registrations — subsidy-seasoned numbers mislead.
   - The second-hand EV market is the tell: resale values reveal the real quality of the product.
